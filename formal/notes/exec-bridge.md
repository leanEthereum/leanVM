# The hint bridge: ISA hints against core hint atoms

Scope: what `step_at`'s `hhint` (`Mode/Step.lean`) needs at each hint the executor runs on the deployed guest, beyond the mode walk: that a witness hint's entry is its stream's next and its words are the reference's at its destination, that a computed hint's destination holds its formula, and that an allocation lands at the bump pointer the reference names. The reference is `honestX` of the honest tree `T`, whose pops match `Hints.honestPushes` stream by stream (`MainCompleteP`). Prototype: `lean_vm/LeanVM/Guest/Bridge/{Defs,Prefix,WalkPath}.lean` (not committed).

## What the data says

A survey of the generated data (Python over `Pack/*/Core.lean`, the bundles' `Cert.locs` and `Data/Advice.lean`; scripts in the session scratchpad, a measurement, not a proof). The ISA hints: 43,651 witness (`witnessStack` 42,971, `witnessHeap` 680), 763 computed, 2,629 allocation (`alloc` 1,664, `allocDyn` 965) and 1,584 plumbing (`frameAddress`, `allocFrames`, `allocArm`, `cellAddress`, `allocAdvice`). The core IR has 44,414 flow hint atoms (witness or computed) in 477 functions and 1,318 allocation atoms.

Order is the compiler's. For 970 of the 973 functions with hints, the core's flow hint atoms in body pre-order (a `match`'s arms in order, an `ite`'s zero side first) and the function's ISA flow hints in offset order pair one for one, of the same kind, a witness's or inverse's operands at the certificate's cells; the allocation atoms pair likewise with the allocation hints, in their own order, once the callee frames' `alloc`s are skipped. The three exceptions are `main`, `verify_sub` and `da_verify`, whose advised buffers are reserved early (`allocAdvice`, `exec-final.md` section 3). Allocations are hoisted: a frame's buffers are reserved before its first call (`level_residual`'s second `allocDyn`, after a `callIf` in the body, is a static `alloc` before it, its size a known constant), so allocations are out of body order with respect to flow hints and calls and need a track of their own.

Cells are the certificate's, but not for every value. `cert f v` (`Cert.locs`) places every value the compiled code reads; a value only hints touch has cell `0`. The survey finds two: `log2_ceil_in_the_exponent`'s buffer pointer (two specializations), written by `allocDyn` at cell `6` and read only by the `bitDecomposeExp` and `log2Ceil` hints. `Memory.honest` writes nothing there, so `AllocOk` fails on it as things stand.

Most functions are simple. 444 of the 477 functions with flow hints have them all among the body's leading atoms, and their ISA flow hints all at offsets up to the first jump (the **prefix class**). The other 33: `main`, `verify_tables`, `verify_sub`, `sp_wots`, `verify_sig_lean_sphincs`, `child_claim_list_digest`, `da_verify`, `main_loop2` (37,895 sites, inside its `match` arms), `main_loop4`, and 24 specializations of an `opening_queries` loop.

## Which existing facts give the correspondence

B2C's completeness walk (`B2C/Cpl*`) does not: it files a block's atoms in a book without order, maps hint atoms to nothing (`locC`), and `CplRun` is existential in the run, stating no trace. The soundness checker (`Check.lean`, `chkL`) walks the statements in order beside the segments and aligns each `ite`, `match` and call with its branch, table jump or call site, which is the alignment needed; but it accepts a hint atom without looking (`.hint _ => true`), passes items into its book lazily (its position at an atom is not the atom's pc), and its alignment lives inside a boolean proved only in the soundness direction. `Cert.locs` gives the cells for the values the code reads. The mode walk's hints are the executor's (`ctx_hintsOk`, `HintsOk`), so the bridge is stated over the advice by function (`Data.adviceFns`), with no new hint data. So: cells from `Cert.locs` plus a small extension, order from a new per-function check over data that exists. No certificate is regenerated.

## What to add

`compiles loc h h'` (`Bridge/Defs.lean`): the ISA hint `h'` is the compilation of the core hint `h` under the cell map `loc`: the same stream or computation, operands and destinations at the atom's values' cells. The prototype covers witness runs (stack, heap), `inverse`, `limbs`, `alloc`, `allocDyn`; to add: `bits`, `bitsExp`, `digitsExp`, `windowsExp`, `log2Ceil` (with `BitsDest` stack or heap) and `allocDyn` compiled to a static `alloc` when its size value is a constant.

Hint-only cells: a generated per-function list of the values whose certificate cell is below `2` with the cell their hints use, a kernel check that those cells are no certificate cell and no plumbing cell, and `honestX`'s extra writes (`X`, `exec-final.md` section 2) gaining them (the pointer's `g^base`, and the bits the `bitDecomposeExp` writes, already heap words). Simpler, if `guest-cert` is touched anyway: give those values cells in the certificate.

Order, prefix class: `prefixOk loc body code adv` (`Bridge/Defs.lean`): the rest of the body after its leading atoms has no witness hint, the advice's pcs increase from the entry, the ISA flow hints sit at offsets up to the first jump, and they compile the leading flow hints one for one (`pairsOk`). One `decide +kernel` per function, linear in its size, over its own core, certificate, code and advice. The order is then free: a frame enters at offset `0` and every instruction before the first jump falls through (`step_at`'s fall-through), so its `k`-th flow hint is its path's `k`-th (`prefix_pairs`), and the instance pops its leading flow hints' entries, then its callees' (`prefix_splice`).

Order, general (the 33, and the call alignment every frame needs): `alignOk j`, a forward pass over the code's offsets carrying a **body position** (a path into the body, a zipper, normalized past atoms that are neither hints nor control), joined at jump targets as the mode walk and `ExecCore` join. Rules: the hints at an offset consume the position's head flow hint atoms, each compiling its own; a branch `JUMP` on cell `loc c` pairs with a head `ite c z n` (fall-through gets `z ++ rest`, the target `n ++ rest`); a table jump pairs with a head `match` (the arms' starts from `Facts.arms`, arm `k` gets `arms[k] ++ rest`); a call shape (`Exec.Kind.call`, the site's return point) pairs with a head `call`, `callIf` or `dispatch` (the return point gets `rest`); a loop's closing tail call with its `callIf`; an unconditional jump carries the position to its target; the return jump needs `[ret vs]`; incoming positions agree at every join. Positions are recomputed by the kernel, not stored. The order lemma: in a frame whose path is `walk σ body = .ret es vs`, at an offset with position `p`, `walk σ (cont p) = .ret es₂ vs` with `es = es₁ ++ es₂`, so the head of `cont p` is the next event of the path. Branch decisions agree because the condition cell holds `σ c` (`FrameHolds.val` and `Fine`). Allocations: `allocOk j`, the buffer allocation hints, in offset order, compile the path's allocation atoms in body order and all precede the first callee frame's allocation; the advised families through `advSlots`.

## How the order composes along the run

Inside `Inv` (`Mode/Inv.lean`, not written), each frame of the stack carries its instance (`Tree.node f σ bufs kids`, its path `es`), its body position, its count of calls made and of buffers allocated. Globally: a list `done` of the entries popped so far, with `s.cursor.getD S 0 = (done.filter (·.1 == S)).length` for every stream, and `done ++ rest = T.pops coreProgram rd`, where `rest` is the frames' own remainders, top first: a frame's remainder is `splice rd σ es₂ (kids' pops after the calls made)`.

The cases of `InvRule`. A witness hint at the top frame: by alignment it compiles the head of the position, so its entry is the head of `rest`; the executor's streams are `honestPushes` grouped by name and `T.pops` agrees with them stream by stream, so the entry at the cursor is this one (`StreamNext`), and it moves to `done`. A computed hint: the atom is on the path, so the honest valuation's exactness (`Tree.Exact`) and the cells give `HintOk`. A non-jump instruction: the position does not move. A branch, table jump or arm's end: the position moves to the side the path takes. A call: push the callee with `kids[k]`, `k` the calls made; the caller's remainder was `kids[k]`'s pops then its rest, as `splice` reads a call event. A `callIf` not taken: a phantom, which pops nothing. A return: the callee's remainder is empty, the caller resumes at the return point with `rest`. A loop's tail call: the `callIf` is the last statement, so the caller's remainder after it is empty, and the frame is replaced by the callee's. A dispatch: a call whose callee the selector picks, as a `match`. The halt: `rest` is empty. The bump pointer: `s.nextFree` is the placement cursor of the current point (`Tree.Placed`: a frame, its buffers, then its callees in order), which a callee frame's `alloc` and each buffer allocation advance by what `Placed` adds.

## The prototype

`Bridge/Defs.lean` (102 lines, 0.8 s): `compiles`, `atomPrefix`, `flowOf`, `isaFlow`, `firstJump`, `pairsOk`, `prefixOk`. `Bridge/Prefix.lean` (209 lines, 0.9 s): the prefix class's path and pops (`prefix_path`, `prefix_splice`, `prefix_pops_leaf`), the pairing (`prefix_pairs`), and `HintOk` from the cells for a stack witness, a heap witness (`words_heap`: a buffer's words are read at `g^(b + off + k)`) and the inverse (`hintOk_stack`, `hintOk_heap`, `hintOk_inverse`). `Bridge/WalkPath.lean` (106 lines, 2.2 s, under 2 GB): `walk_path__L6_L7` is in the prefix class (`prefixOk_wp`, by the kernel); its one ISA flow hint, `witnessStack "merkle_children" 7 24` at offset `0`, compiles its first atom, child `k` (value `5 + k`) at cell `7 + k` (`wp_cells`); every instance pops exactly `("merkle_children", kids.map σ)` (`wp_pops`); and the hint meets `HintOk` from the stream's next entry and the frame's cells (`wp_hintOk`).

## Estimate

`compiles` for every kind and the hint-only cells: 300 lines and a small generator step, two days. The prefix class for its 444 functions: one generated kernel check each (about a second each), a generator of 100 lines, one day. `alignOk` and its soundness: 500 lines of definitions and 1,200 of proofs (reusing `Mode/Jump.lean`'s `JumpK` and `Mode/Edge.lean`), checked per pack for every function (calls need it everywhere), `main_loop2` in windows; two weeks. `allocOk` and the placement cursor: 400 lines, four days. The `Inv` fields and cases: 1,000 lines inside the `Inv` work, two weeks alongside it. In all about 3,500 lines and five to six weeks of one agent; the new kernel checks cost a few CPU-minutes cold.
