Researcher 35, round 5. Brief: `34-round5-brief.md`. Scripts: `scripts/35_checks.py`, `scripts/35_counts.py`, `scripts/35_flat_cubic.py`, `scripts/35_flat_list.c`. Notation: $F$ a field of characteristic 2 (think $K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$), $D \subset F$ any set of $n$ points, $y : D \to F$ a word, $k = \rho n$, $\Delta$ the largest degree of a list member ($k - 1$, or $k - 1 + \ell$), $A = an$ the agreement, $\mathrm{List}(y, A)$ the polynomials of degree $\le \Delta$ agreeing with $y$ on at least $A$ points.

## 0. Findings in one page

1. **Mechanism of the large-characteristic proofs [read].** All of them ([BCPZZ26], [Jer26], [Gab26], [DKT26], and the list half of the KoalaBear prize proofs) are one argument in two steps. Step 1, *hidden-derivative interpolation*: there is a nonzero $Q(X, Y_0, \dots, Y_s)$ of bounded weighted degree with $Q(X, P, P^{[1]}, \dots, P^{[s]}) = 0$ for every close $P$, although no derivative is received. The gain over Guruswami and Sudan comes from one fact: on the jet of a polynomial through $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ the contact form $w = Y_0 - y_\alpha - (X - \alpha) Y_1$ vanishes to order 2, not 1. With one derivative this gives the threshold $a_1(\rho) < \sqrt\rho$ ($0.6899$ at rate 1/2, $0.4688$ at 1/4, $0.3191$ at 1/8, $0.2181$ at 1/16). Step 2, *solution counting*: $Q = 0$ is a differential equation; a regular solution is determined by its initial jet through the Taylor recurrence, so the solutions form a curve (or a variety of dimension $\le s$) of controlled degree in coefficient space, and each of the $n$ agreement hyperplanes cuts it properly, which gives a list of size $O_\rho(n/\eta^2)$ (one derivative) or $n^{O(1)}$ (capacity).
2. **Where the characteristic and the points enter [read].** Step 1 is characteristic-free and uses nothing about the points. Step 2 needs $p > k - 1$ (the recurrence divides by $1, \dots, k - 1$) and $p > B_\partial$ (separability in $Y_1$). The evaluation points play no role in any list bound: the Lean theorem of the prize submission is stated for an arbitrary injection of the 262144 positions into an arbitrary field of characteristic $p$. The prime-subfield position of the KoalaBear points is used only on the correlated-agreement side (Frobenius-twisted syndromes, anchors), which the DEEP fold does not need.
3. **Step 1 transfers verbatim [proved, checked].** In characteristic 2 the same explainers exist at the same thresholds; the local rank of the contact conditions is the same number in characteristics 2, 3 and large $p$.
4. **Step 2 does not transfer, and the failure is exact [proved].** Write $P = g(X^2) + X h(X^2)$. The first-order jet $(P, P')$ is an invertible $F[X]$-linear image of $(g, h)$, so $Q(X, P, P') = 0$ is not a differential equation: it is a pair of algebraic equations $Q_0(\tau, g, h) = Q_1(\tau, g, h) = 0$ over $F(\tau)$, $\tau = X^2$ (Propositions 1 to 3). Geometrically: a word is $n$ lines in the 3-space $(\tau, u, v)$, a codeword is a section of degree $< k/2$, agreement is incidence, and an explainer is a pair of surfaces through the lines. If the two surfaces share no component the list has at most $B_{\mathrm{jet}}^2$ members, a constant. All the difficulty is in common components (*first integrals*), whose sections can form families of dimension up to $k/2$ over $F$, where the large-characteristic solution family has dimension 1.
5. **Components can still be counted, by a second interpolation on the component [proved].** Lemmas A, A+ and C bound the list members on an irreducible component by a constant unless the component contains many of the lines *and* has small weighted degree. Lemma L shows that a component whose leading $Y_1$-coefficient does not involve $Y_0$ is never of that kind. The outcome (Theorems 4 and 8): above $a_1(\rho)$ every list is $O(1)$ except for the sections of *economical first integrals*: irreducible $\hat G$ with leading coefficient in $F[X, Y_0^2] \setminus F[X]$ and weighted degree below $e^* \sum_\alpha o_\alpha$, where $o_\alpha$ is the contact order along the line of $\alpha$ and $e^* \approx \rho/a$.
6. **The obstruction is real at rate 1/4, and explicit [proved, checked].** For $D$ a subspace and the word $y = x^{n/2 - 1}$, all $n - 1$ flats of `00-lead-notes.md` section 1 are sections of one cubic surface, $\hat G = \partial E$ with $E = X Y_0^3 + (X \gamma_D(X^2) + e_0) Y_0 + e_0 X^{n/2 - 1}$, which contains $n - 1$ of the $n$ lines and has weighted degree $3k - 2$. Every codeword with more than $3n/8$ agreements with this word lies on it. So no argument of this type can give a list below $n - 1$ at rate 1/4 in $(a_1, 1/2)$, in agreement with the known lower bound, and the place where the large-characteristic proof would have cut the family (a curve in coefficient space) is exactly where characteristic 2 has a surface.
7. **At rate 1/2 the same obstruction does not exist in its simple form [proved at leading order, checked with exact integers at $n = 2^{25}$].** An economical component at rate 1/2 must have total contact $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha > 2.2\,n$ with its lines, while a surface that is smooth along its lines has at most $2n$: it must be singular along many lines (contact order 4 on a tenth of them, say). Components that are smooth along their lines, the only kind seen so far, are all cut. So: for $a > a_1(1/2) + \epsilon$, on every evaluation set in characteristic 2, $|\mathrm{List}(y, an)| = O_\epsilon(1)$ for every word whose explainers have no irreducible first-integral factor with that much singular contact. Whether such factors can exist is **[open]**; it is the one statement missing for an unconditional $H_K(0.70, 15)$ at rate 1/2 (about 210 queries instead of 218).
8. **No theorem for all words is obtained.** What is delivered is the mechanism, the exact characteristic-2 dictionary, three counting lemmas, the explicit obstruction, and the residual statement per rate (Section 8).

## 2. How the large-characteristic proofs beat Johnson

### 2.1 Step 1: hidden-derivative interpolation

Guruswami and Sudan choose $Q(X, Y_0)$ vanishing to order $m$ at every received point; $m(m+1)/2$ conditions per point against about $(mA)^2/(2\Delta)$ coefficients gives $A^2 > \Delta n$. The new idea ([Nas26] on the prize, [BCPZZ26], simplified in [Gab26] and [DKT26]) is to interpolate $Q(X, Y_0, Y_1)$ that vanishes on the *jet curve* $(X, P, P')$ of every close $P$. Since $P'(\alpha)$ is unknown, the condition at a received point $(\alpha, b)$ has to hold for a free $Y_1$. That alone would cost more than it gains. The gain is the following identity, valid for every polynomial with $P(\alpha) = b$, in every characteristic ($P'$ the formal derivative):
$$w_\alpha(X, P, P') := P(X) - b - (X - \alpha) P'(X) \equiv 0 \pmod{(X - \alpha)^2}. \tag{1}$$

**Definition.** With $T = X - \alpha$ and $w = Y_0 - b - T Y_1$, the *contact ideal* of order $m$ at $(\alpha, b)$ is $\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, b) = (T^i w^l : i + 2l \ge m) \subset F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$. It is the span of the monomials $T^i w^l Y_1^e$ with $i + 2l \ge m$, that is, the polynomials of weighted order $\ge m$ for the weights $(T, w, Y_1) = (1, 2, 0)$. The associated graded ring is the polynomial ring, a domain, so orders add under multiplication.

**Lemma 0 [proved; this is [Gab26, Claim 4.2], [BCPZZ26, Lemma 3.1], [DKT26, Section 2.1.1] in one line].** If $Q \in \mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, b)$ and $P(\alpha) = b$ then $(X - \alpha)^m \mid Q(X, P, P')$. *Proof.* Substitute: $T \mapsto X - \alpha$ and by (1) $w \mapsto$ a multiple of $(X - \alpha)^2$. $\square$

So if $Q \ne 0$ lies in $\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ for every $\alpha \in D$ and every monomial $X^x Y_0^{a} Y_1^{c}$ of $Q$ has $x + \Delta(a + c) < mA$, then $Q(X, P, P') = 0$ for every $P \in \mathrm{List}(y, A)$. Call such a $Q$ an *explainer* of multiplicity $m$. [Gab26, Definition 4.1] and [DKT26, Lemma 4.1] phrase membership in $\mathfrak{c}_m$ as "$(Y - Z)^{m - r}$ divides the coefficient of $X^r$ after the substitution $Y_0 = b + XY$", which is the same condition ($Y - Z = w/T$).

**The count [read: [DKT26, Lemma 4.1 and Section 4.3]; rederived from [Gab26, Claims 4.3, 4.4]].** Take the support $\{X^x Y_0^a Y_1^c : c \le B_\partial,\ x + \Delta(a + c) < mA\}$. It has $N(B_\partial, mA) = \sum_{t \ge 0} (\min(t, B_\partial) + 1)(mA - \Delta t)_+$ monomials. The conditions at one point have rank at most
$$R(m, B_\partial) = \sum_{s=0}^{m-1} \big[(s + 1)(B_\partial + 1) - (2s - m + 1)_+ (s + B_\partial - m + 1)_+\big],$$
with equality when the support contains the full rectangle $\{x + a < m,\ c \le B_\partial\}$ (the kernel at Taylor degree $s$ is exactly the multiples of $(U - Y_1)^{m - s}$ in a rectangle; the proof inverts no integer). With $B_\partial = \beta m \le m/2$ and $m \to \infty$: $N/(nm^3) \to \beta a^2/(2\rho) - a\beta^2/2 + \rho\beta^3/6$ and $R/m^3 \to \beta/2 - \beta^2/2 + \beta^3/3$, so an explainer exists as soon as
$$\mathrm{Margin}(\beta) = \frac{\beta}{2}\Big[\frac{a^2}{\rho} - 1 + (1 - a)\beta - \frac{2 - \rho}{3}\beta^2\Big] > 0 \quad\text{for some } \beta, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \frac{a^2}{\rho} + \frac{3(1 - a)^2}{4(2 - \rho)} > 1 .$$
At $\beta \to 0$ this is Johnson; the second term is the gain. The root is $a_1(\rho) = (3\rho + 2\sqrt{\rho(5 - \rho)(2 - \rho)})/(8 - \rho)$ for $\rho \ge \rho_c = 11 - 3\sqrt{13} \approx 0.183$, and $\sqrt{\rho/2}\,(1 + u)$ with $u^2(u + 3) = \sqrt{\rho/2}$ below ([DKT26, (31)]). `35_counts.py A`:

| rate | Johnson $\sqrt\rho$ | $a_1(\rho)$ | queries for 108 bits, Johnson to $a_1$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 0.7071 | 0.6899 | 217 to 202 |
| 1/4 | 0.5000 | 0.4688 | 108 to 99 |
| 1/8 | 0.3536 | 0.3191 | 73 to 66 |
| 1/16 | 0.2500 | 0.2181 | 54 to 50 |

With derivatives up to order $d$ the contact form is $w = Y_0 - b - \sum_{j=1}^d (-1)^{j+1} T^j Y_j$, of order $d + 1$ on jets ([BCPZZ26, (13) to (15)], with Hasse derivatives), and the thresholds decrease to capacity, slowly: [BCPZZ26] takes $d = \lceil \epsilon^{-3/\theta} \rceil$, $m = d^3$ at rate $(1 - \theta)\epsilon$; [DKT26, Theorem 1.2] takes $d = \lceil e^{1.5/\delta} \rceil$ at gap $\delta$ from capacity. This is why the explicit certificates stop close to Johnson: one derivative gives 1.7 to 3.5 percentage points, and the prize proofs at 69.15 to 69.20 bits (agreement $0.6877$ to $0.6875$, just below $a_1(1/2) = 0.6899$) already need second-order jets, multiplicity 48 and jet degree 64.

### 2.2 Step 2: counting the solutions of the differential equation

[Gab26, Section 5], [DKT26, Section 2.1.2 to 2.1.3], [Jer26, Sections 5, 6] **[read]**. Fix an irreducible factor of $Q$ and a solution $P$ with $Q_{Y_1}(X, P, P') \ne 0$ (*regular*). Differentiating $Q(X, P, P') = 0$ $j$ times expresses $P^{(j+1)}$ as a rational function of $(X, P, P')$ with denominator a power of $Q_{Y_1}$ ([Gab26, Lemma 5.1]); in Taylor coefficients $u_j$ at a center $c$ the recurrence reads $(j + 1)\, Q_{Y_1}(c, u_0, u_1)\, u_{j+1} = (\text{polynomial in } u_0, \dots, u_j)$ (Kopparty's recurrence, [DKT26, Section 2.1.2]). **This is where $p > k - 1$ is used**: the integers $1, \dots, k - 1$ must be invertible, so that $(u_0, u_1)$ on the curve $Q(c, u_0, u_1) = 0$ determines the whole polynomial. The regular solutions are then the points of a curve of degree $O(\Delta B_{\mathrm{jet}} B_\partial)$ in the coefficient space $F^k$. Agreement at position $i$ is a hyperplane; a curve of distinct polynomials lies in fewer than $k$ of them, and each other hyperplane meets a component of degree $\delta$ in at most $\delta$ points. A list member lies on at least $A - k$ proper sections, so the list has at most $\delta (n - k)/(A - k)$ members per component ([Gab26, Lemma 5.4]: $(n - k)(2k - 3) d \deg T/(a - k)$). Singular solutions satisfy $Q = Q_{Y_1} = 0$; the resultant in $Y_1$ is a nonzero ordinary equation in $(X, Y_0)$, **which uses $p > B_\partial$** (separability), and has at most $\deg_{Y_0}$ polynomial roots. Total: $|\mathrm{List}| \le C n$ ([Gab26, Theorem 1], $p > \max(d, k)$), $O_\rho(n/\eta_1^2)$ at $a = a_1 + \eta_1$ ([DKT26, Theorem 1.1], $p > \max(k - 1, B_\partial)$), $n^{O_\gamma(1)}$ at $\rho + \gamma$ ([Jer26, Theorem 1.1], prime $q \ge n$, or $p > \max(k - 1, B_{\gamma, L})$ by Section 8.4).

Both papers that discuss the hypothesis say the same thing. [DKT26, after Theorem 1.1]: "The characteristic restriction comes from reconstruction, rather than local interpolation: in characteristic $p$, both $0$ and $(X - c)^p$ solve $P' = 0$ and have the same value at $c$". [Jer26, Remark 5.8]: "If $d \ge p$, even the equation $Y_1 = 0$ has every polynomial in $X^p$ as a solution, so the solution locus has dimension growing with $d/p$", and Section 8.4: "The interpolation construction is characteristic-free".

### 2.3 The KoalaBear proofs

**List half [read: Lean statements].** `weighted_uniform_annihilator` produces, for an arbitrary injection `domain : Fin 262144 ↪ F` and an arbitrary `received`, a nonzero second-order differential polynomial $Q(X, Y_0, Y_1, Y_2)$ with jet total degree $\le 64$, a weighted total degree $\le 148$ (`jetWeight 1 4 3`), satisfying the multiplicity-48 local constraints at every received point, hence killing every polynomial of degree $\le 131072$ with at least 180265 agreements. `weighted_second_order_card` (hypothesis `[CharP F 2130706433]`, nothing else about $F$ or the points) bounds the solutions with that agreement by a regular term plus a first-order term for the exceptional (separant zero) solutions, through the resultant; the characteristic enters as `jetDegree Q (Fin.last 2) < ringChar F`. `weighted_complete_list` concludes: at most $31911086374011223 \approx 2^{54.8}$ polynomials. The write-up of the 69.20 entry gives $\Lambda \le 31973440934878498$ at agreement 180217 (relative radius $0.31253$, Johnson radius $0.2929$). So the list bound of the prize is Steps 1 and 2 with two hidden derivatives, on arbitrary points, in characteristic $p > k$. The write-up of the 69.16 entry says the charge "scales as $1/(A - 131072)^2$", the $O(n/\eta^2)$ shape with $\eta$ measured from capacity inside one finite certificate.

**MCA half [read: write-ups and docstrings only, not followed].** The quantity bounded there is the number of bad slopes $\gamma \in K = \mathbb{F}_{p^6}$ for a pair of words. Because the points lie in $\mathbb{F}_p$, the Frobenius $\varphi$ of $K/\mathbb{F}_p$ acts on words position by position and on polynomials coefficient by coefficient, and commutes with evaluation: if $u_0 + \gamma u_1$ agrees with $P$ on $S$ then $\varphi(u_0) + \varphi(\gamma)\varphi(u_1)$ agrees with $P^\varphi$ on the same $S$. The 69.15 write-up: "the ordinary and Frobenius-twisted syndromes classify the input, and in the remaining case an anchor turns distinct bad slopes into distinct base-field polynomials agreeing with one fixed word", that is, the hard case of the slope count is reduced to a list bound over $\mathbb{F}_p$. This is the only place where the position of the points (prime subfield) is used, and none of it concerns list size. [DKT26, Section 10.5] says the same: "The interpolation and component-counting mechanisms ... use distinct evaluation points, not the multiplicative-subgroup structure of this domain".

### 2.4 [Jo26]

A different mechanism (agreement-set shortening), every field and every evaluation set, $h = O(1)$ positions beyond the exact Johnson boundary, $O_{r,h}(K^6)$ bad parameters. A gain of $O(1/n)$ in relative radius; it is the "pinning points plus Johnson" dead end of the brief and I did not pursue it.

## 3. Characteristic 2: the dictionary

### 3.1 Jets are components

**Proposition 1 [proved].** Let $q = 2^e$ and write $P = \sum_{i < q} X^i g_i(X^q)$. For $0 \le j < q$ the Hasse derivative is $P^{[j]} = \sum_{i < q} \binom{i}{j} X^{i - j} g_i(X^q)$. The matrix $(\binom{i}{j} X^{i-j})_{j, i < q}$ is unitriangular over $F[X]$. So the jet $(P^{[0]}, \dots, P^{[q-1]})$ and the component vector $(g_0, \dots, g_{q-1})(X^q)$ are related by an invertible $F[X]$-linear map that does not depend on $P$. (The same holds in characteristic $p$ with $q = p^e$.)

*Proof.* $(X + T)^i g_i((X + T)^q) = (X + T)^i g_i(X^q + T^q) \equiv (X + T)^i g_i(X^q) \pmod{T^q}$. $\square$

For $e = 1$: $P = g(X^2) + X h(X^2)$, $P' = h(X^2)$. Put $\tau = X^2$, $u = Y_0 + X Y_1$, $v = Y_1$. Then $F[X, Y_0, Y_1] = F[\tau, u, v] \oplus X \cdot F[\tau, u, v]$, and on the jet of $P$ the variables $(\tau, u, v)$ take the values $(X^2, g(X^2), h(X^2))$. Let $\partial = \partial_X + Y_1 \partial_{Y_0}$; it is the total derivative, $(\partial Q)(X, P, P') = \frac{d}{dX} Q(X, P, P')$ for every $P$, because $P'' = 0$. It kills $\tau, u, v$ and sends $X$ to 1, so $\partial(Q_0 + X Q_1) = Q_1$, $\partial^2 = 0$, and $\ker \partial = F[\tau, u, v]$. I call the elements of $\ker\partial$ *first integrals* and write $\hat G(X, Y_0, Y_1) = G(X^2, Y_0 + XY_1, Y_1)$ for $G \in F[\tau, u, v]$.

**Proposition 2 (the contact ideal is a line ideal) [proved].** Let $I_\alpha = (\tau - \alpha^2,\ u + \alpha v - y_\alpha) \subset F[\tau, u, v]$, the ideal of the line $\ell_\alpha = \{\tau = \alpha^2,\ u + \alpha v = y_\alpha\}$. Write $Q = Q_0 + X Q_1 = (Q_0 + \alpha Q_1) + (X - \alpha) Q_1$ with $Q_0, Q_1 \in F[\tau, u, v]$. Then
$$Q \in \mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha) \iff Q_0 + \alpha Q_1 \in I_\alpha^{\lceil m/2 \rceil} \ \text{and}\ Q_1 \in I_\alpha^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor}.$$
*Proof.* $w = Y_0 - y_\alpha - (X - \alpha)Y_1 = u + \alpha v - y_\alpha$ and $T^2 = \tau - \alpha^2$. So $T^{2c} w^l = t_1^c t_2^l$ with $t_1, t_2$ the generators of $I_\alpha$, and $T^{2c+1} w^l = T\, t_1^c t_2^l$; the conditions $2c + 2l \ge m$ and $2c + 1 + 2l \ge m$ are $c + l \ge \lceil m/2 \rceil$ and $c + l \ge \lfloor m/2 \rfloor$. The set on the right is stable under multiplication by $T$ because $T^2 = t_1 \in I_\alpha$. $\square$

In particular for $m = 2\mu$: **$Q$ is an explainer iff both $Q_0$ and $Q_1$ vanish to order $\mu$ along each of the $n$ lines $\ell_\alpha$**, and the list is a set of polynomial sections $\tau \mapsto (g(\tau), h(\tau))$, $\deg g, \deg h \le \Delta/2$, each meeting at least $A$ of the lines. (The Frobenius $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^2$ is injective, so the $n$ lines lie in $n$ distinct planes $\tau = \alpha^2$: this is the "$x \mapsto x^2$ maps $D$ to another subspace" of the brief. Nothing is folded; the rate of the pair $(g, h)$ is $\rho/2$ and each position gives one linear form of the two symbols.) A first-order hidden-derivative argument in characteristic 2 is thus a polynomial-method argument about $n$ lines in 3-space. The whole gain over Johnson is still there, but it is not visible in these coordinates: it comes from bounding the degree in the direction $Y_1$, which is the common direction of all the lines only in jet coordinates.

For general $e$ the same computation gives: explainers of order $2^e - 1$ are $2^e$-tuples of polynomials in $(\tau, u_0, \dots, u_{2^e - 1})$, $\tau = X^{2^e}$, vanishing to order about $m/2^e$ on the $n$ codimension-2 flats $\{\tau = \alpha^{2^e},\ \sum_i \alpha^i u_i = y_\alpha\}$ (the contact form of [BCPZZ26] becomes the linear form $\sum_i \alpha^i u_i - y_\alpha$ exactly, and $T^{2^e} = \tau - \alpha^{2^e}$).

### 3.2 What replaces the differential equation

**Proposition 3 [proved].** Let $Q = Q_0 + XQ_1 \ne 0$. A polynomial $P = g(X^2) + Xh(X^2)$ satisfies $Q(X, P, P') = 0$ iff $Q_0(\tau, g, h) = 0$ and $Q_1(\tau, g, h) = 0$ in $F[\tau]$. Hence:

1. if $Q_0, Q_1$ are coprime in $F(\tau)[u, v]$, the equation has at most $\deg_{u,v} Q_0 \cdot \deg_{u,v} Q_1 \le (\deg_{Y_0, Y_1} Q)^2$ polynomial solutions of any degree (Bézout over $F(\tau)$);
2. if $G \mid \gcd(Q_0, Q_1)$ is irreducible of positive degree in $(u, v)$, every section of the surface $\Sigma_G = \{G = 0\}$ is a solution, and such families can have dimension $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ over $F$: for $G = u + c\,v + d$ with $c, d \in F$ the solutions of degree $< k$ are $P = (X + c)\, h(X^2) + d$, $h$ arbitrary.

*Proof.* $1, X$ are independent over $F(\tau)$ and $Q_i(\tau, g(\tau), h(\tau)) \in F[\tau]$ at $\tau = X^2$. $\square$

This quantifies "Hasse derivatives lose information on squares": derivatives of order $< 2^e$ are linear over the constant field $F(X^{2^e})$, an equation of order $< 2^e$ is $2^e$ algebraic equations in $2^e$ unknowns over that field, and its solution set is either finite (at most $d^{2^e}$ points) or contains the $F(X^{2^e})$-points of a positive-dimensional variety. In terms of the Taylor recurrence of Section 2.2: the factor $(j + 1)$ vanishes for $j + 1$ even, so $u_2, u_4, \dots$ are free; on a component $G(\tau, g, h) = 0$ this is the statement that $g$ is free and $h$ is algebraic over $F(\tau, g)$. In large characteristic a first-order equation has a 1-parameter family of solutions; here it has none (case 1) or an infinite-dimensional one (case 2). Case 1 is better than anything in large characteristic: a constant list. Case 2 is the whole problem.

### 3.3 Interpolation in characteristic 2

## 4. Counting the list members on a component

Throughout, $G \in F[\tau, u, v]$ is irreducible in $F(\tau)[u, v]$ and primitive, $\delta = \deg_{u,v} G \ge 1$, and $L_G$ is the set of $P \in \mathrm{List}(y, A)$ whose section lies on $\Sigma_G$. A line $\ell_\alpha$ is *contained* if $G \in I_\alpha$, i.e. $G(\alpha^2, y_\alpha + \alpha v, v) \equiv 0$. In jet coordinates: $b = \deg_{Y_1} \hat G$, $W$ the weighted degree of $\hat G$ for the weights $(1, \Delta, \Delta)$, and $o_\alpha$ the largest $o$ with $\hat G \in \mathfrak{c}_o(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ (the *contact order*; it is even, $o_\alpha = 2 j_\alpha$ with $G \in I_\alpha^{j_\alpha} \setminus I_\alpha^{j_\alpha + 1}$, and $o_\alpha \ge 2$ iff the line is contained, $o_\alpha \ge 4$ iff $\Sigma_G$ is singular along it).

**Theorem 4 (covering) [proved].** Let $Q$ be an explainer, $H = \gcd(Q_0, Q_1)$ in $F[\tau, u, v]$, and $G_1, \dots, G_r$ the irreducible factors of $H$ of positive $(u, v)$-degree. Then $\sum_i \deg G_i \le B_{\mathrm{jet}}$, each $\hat G_i$ divides $Q$, and
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le B_{\mathrm{jet}}^2 + \sum_{i=1}^r |L_{G_i}| .$$
*Proof.* Proposition 3 and Bézout for $Q_0/H$, $Q_1/H$. $\square$

A component with $b = 0$ has $|L_G| \le \deg_{Y_0} \hat G$ (its sections are roots of a bivariate polynomial). For the others there are three tools.

**Lemma A (few contained lines) [proved].** Let $\kappa = \lfloor \Delta/2 \rfloor$, let $n'$ be the number of contained lines, $\mu \ge 1$, $Z = \mu(A - n')$ and $N_\delta(Z) = \sum_{t \ge 0} \min(t + 1, \delta)(Z - \kappa t)_+$. If $N_\delta(Z) > (n - n')\,\delta\,\mu(\mu + 1)/2$ then $|L_G| \le \delta \lfloor (Z - 1)/\kappa \rfloor$. Asymptotically the hypothesis is $(a - x)^2 > (\rho/2)(1 - x)$, $x = n'/n$: a Johnson condition at rate $\rho/2$.

*Proof.* Fix a monomial order; the monomials $u^i v^j$ not divisible by the leading monomial of $G$ are independent modulo $G$ over $F(\tau)$, and there are $\min(t + 1, \delta)$ of them in degree $t$. Let $W_Z$ be the span of $\tau^x u^i v^j$ over these with $x + \kappa(i + j) < Z$; $\dim W_Z = N_\delta(Z)$ and $W_Z \cap (G) = 0$. For a line that is not contained, $G \equiv \gamma(v) \ne 0$ modulo $I_\alpha$ with $\deg\gamma \le \delta$; filtering $F[\tau, u, v]/I_\alpha^\mu$ by powers of $I_\alpha$, whose graded pieces are free over $F[v]$ of ranks $1, 2, \dots, \mu$ and on which $G$ acts as $\gamma$, gives $\dim F[\tau, u, v]/(I_\alpha^\mu + (G)) \le \delta\mu(\mu + 1)/2$. By counting there is $R \in W_Z \setminus 0$ with $R \in I_\alpha^\mu + (G)$ for all non-contained $\alpha$. For $P \in L_G$, $r(\tau) = R(\tau, g, h)$ has degree $< Z$ and vanishes to order $\mu$ at $\alpha^2$ for each of its at least $A - n'$ agreements on non-contained lines (both generators of $I_\alpha$ vanish at $\tau = \alpha^2$ along the section, and $G$ vanishes identically). So $r = 0$, and the section is a common zero of the coprime $G$ and $R$; Bézout. $\square$

**Lemma A+ (contained lines kept) [proved].** Suppose $G \notin I_\alpha^2$ for the $n_1$ contained lines. With $Z = \mu A$ and $r = \lfloor (Z - 1)/\kappa \rfloor \ge \delta + \mu$: if $N_\delta(Z) > n\,\delta\mu(\mu + 1)/2 + n_1\,\mu(r + 1 - \delta)$ then $|L_G| \le \delta r$. Asymptotically: $\delta(a^2 - \rho/2) > 2a x$.

*Proof.* As above, with the condition $R \in I_\alpha^\mu + (G)$ also on contained lines. The image of the polynomials of $(u, v)$-degree $\le r$ in $B = F[t_1, t_2, v]/I_\alpha^\mu$ is $B_r = \bigoplus_{c + d < \mu} F[v]_{\le r - d}\, t_1^c t_2^d$. It contains $G \cdot B_{r - \delta}$, and the kernel of multiplication by $G$ on $B$ is $I_\alpha^{\mu - 1}/I_\alpha^\mu$ because $G$ has order exactly 1 and the graded ring is a domain. So the number of conditions is at most $\dim B_r - \dim B_{r - \delta} + \sum_{d < \mu}(r - \delta - d + 1) \le \delta\mu(\mu + 1)/2 + \mu(r + 1 - \delta)$. $\square$

`35_counts.py C`: at rate 1/2, $a = 0.695$, a component is harmless if it contains fewer than $0.267\,n$ lines (any degree), or fewer than $0.335, 0.503, 0.670, 0.838\,n$ for degree $2, 3, 4, 5$, and always for degree $\ge 6$. At rate 1/4, $a = 0.474$: $0.147\,n$, then $0.105\,\delta\,n$, always for degree $\ge 10$.

**Lemma C (weighted degree) [proved].** Let $m \ge 1$, $B \ge 0$, $Z = mA$, and suppose $\Delta(m + B) \le Z - W$ when $b \le B$. If
$$N(B, Z) - N(B - b, Z - W) > \sum_{\alpha \in D} \big[R(m, B) - R(m - o_\alpha, B - b)\big] \tag{2}$$
(with $N = 0$ for a negative first argument or nonpositive second, $R = 0$ for nonpositive $m$) then $|L_G| \le \delta \lfloor (Z - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the support of Section 2.1 with parameters $(B, Z)$. Degrees in $Y_1$ and weighted degrees add under multiplication, so $\mathcal{S} \cap (\hat G) = \hat G \cdot \mathcal{S}'$ with $\mathcal{S}'$ the support with parameters $(B - b, Z - W)$, and $\dim \mathcal{S}/(\mathcal{S} \cap (\hat G)) = N(B, Z) - N(B - b, Z - W)$. Let $\Phi_\alpha : \mathcal{S} \to F[X, Y_0, Y_1]/(\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha) + (\hat G))$. Its kernel contains $(\mathcal{S} \cap \mathfrak{c}_m) + \hat G \mathcal{S}'$, and $\hat G Q' \in \mathfrak{c}_m$ iff $Q' \in \mathfrak{c}_{m - o_\alpha}$ (orders add). Hence $\operatorname{rank} \Phi_\alpha \le \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{S} \to F[..]/\mathfrak{c}_m) - \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{S}' \to F[..]/\mathfrak{c}_{m - o_\alpha}) \le R(m, B) - R(m - o_\alpha, B - b)$, the second rank being exactly $R$ because $\mathcal{S}'$ contains the full rectangle (Section 2.1). By (2) there is $\tilde Q \in \mathcal{S}$, not in $(\hat G)$, with $\tilde Q \in \mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha) + (\hat G)$ for all $\alpha$. For $P \in L_G$, Lemma 0 applied to the $\mathfrak{c}_m$ part gives $\tilde Q(X, P, P') = 0$, so the section is a common zero of $G$ and of whichever of $\tilde Q_0, \tilde Q_1$ is not divisible by $G$. $\square$

At leading order in $m$ (with $B = \beta m$, $W = wn$, $\bar o = \frac1n \sum_\alpha o_\alpha$, and using that $o \mapsto R(m - o, \cdot)$ is convex), (2) reads
$$b\, J(\beta) + \beta\Big[w\Big(\frac{a}{\rho} - \frac{\beta}{2}\Big) - \bar o\Big(1 - \frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\Big] > 0, \qquad J(\beta) = \partial_\beta \mathrm{Margin} = \frac12\Big(\frac{a^2}{\rho} - 1\Big) + (1 - a)\beta - \frac{2 - \rho}{2}\beta^2 . \tag{3}$$
For $a > a_1(\rho)$ the maximum of $J$ is positive, so on the interval where $J \ge 0$ the component is cut as soon as its *efficiency* $e(\hat G) := W/\sum_\alpha o_\alpha$ exceeds $e^*(\beta) = \rho(1 - \beta/2)/(a - \rho\beta/2)$. Taking the largest $\beta$ with $J \ge 0$: $e^* = 0.686$ at $(\rho, a) = (1/2, 0.692)$ and $0.672$ at $(1/2, 0.70)$; $e^* \approx 0.45$ at $(1/4, 0.48)$. Interpretation: $\hat G$ is cut unless it is itself close to being an explainer ($W < A \bar o$ means that $\hat G(X, P, P')$ is forced to vanish by its own contact; $e^* \to a$ as $a \to \sqrt\rho$).

**Lemma L (leading coefficient) [proved].** Write $\hat G = \sum_{c \le b} \hat G_c(X, Y_0) Y_1^c$ with $b \ge 1$.

1. $\hat G_b \in F[X, Y_0^2]$ (the coefficient of $Y_1^{b+1}$ in $\partial \hat G = 0$ is $\partial_{Y_0} \hat G_b$).
2. Every monomial $T^r S^q$ of $\hat G_b(\alpha + T, y_\alpha + S)$ has $r + 2q \ge o_\alpha - b$, and the monomials with $q = 0$ have $r \ge \max(o_\alpha/2,\ o_\alpha - b)$.
3. Hence either $\hat G_b \in F[X]$, and then $W \ge \sum_\alpha \max(o_\alpha/2,\ o_\alpha - b) + b\Delta$; or $\hat G_b$ involves $Y_0^2$, and then $W \ge (b + 2)\Delta$.
4. (Sudan step.) If $A - (n - n') > W - b\Delta$, with $n'$ the number of contained lines, then every member of $\mathrm{List}(y, A)$ is a $Y_0$-root of $\hat G_b$, so $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le (W - b\Delta)/\Delta$.

*Proof of 2.* Expand $\hat G$ in the basis $T^i w^l Y_1^e$, all terms having $i + 2l \ge o_\alpha$, and substitute $w = S - TY_1$: $T^i w^l Y_1^e = \sum_p \pm\binom{l}{p} T^{i + p} S^{l - p} Y_1^{e + p}$. A contribution to $T^r S^q Y_1^b$ has $p = b - e \le b$ and $i + 2l = r + 2q + p$. So $r + 2q \ge o_\alpha - b$; for $q = 0$ also $p = l \le r$, so $r + \min(r, b) \ge o_\alpha$. Part 4: $\hat G_b$ vanishes at the received points of contained lines and has weighted degree $\le W - b\Delta$. $\square$

### 4.1 Consequences of L and C

**Proposition 6 [proved at leading order in $m$ and $n$; exact integer check at $n = 2^{25}$, rate 1/2, for 150 contact profiles, `35_counts.py E`].** Let $a > a_1(\rho)$. A component with $b \ge 1$ and $\hat G_b \in F[X]$ has efficiency $e(\hat G) \ge (1 + \rho)/2$, whatever its contact profile, and is therefore cut by Lemma C as soon as $(1 + \rho)/2 > e^*$, which holds at all four rates just above $a_1$ ($0.75 > 0.69$, $0.625 > 0.46$, $0.5625 > 0.32$, $0.53 > 0.23$; here $e^*$ is taken at the largest $\beta \le 1/2$ with $J(\beta) \ge 0$).

*Proof.* Let $n'$ be the number of contained lines and $\Sigma = \sum_\alpha o_\alpha$. Lemma L part 3 with $\max(j, 2j - b) \ge j$ gives $W \ge \Sigma/2 + b\Delta$, and with $\max(j, 2j - b) \ge 2j - b$ it gives $W \ge \Sigma - b(n' - \Delta)$. With $s = \Sigma/(bn')$ and $\rho' = \Delta/n' \ge \rho$: $e \ge \max(\tfrac12 + \rho'/s,\ 1 - (1 - \rho')/s) \ge (1 + \rho')/2$, the two terms being equal at $s = 2$. $\square$

So the components that Lemma C cannot cut, the **economical first integrals**, satisfy
$$b \ge 1, \qquad \hat G_b \in F[X, Y_0^2] \setminus F[X], \qquad (b + 2)\Delta \ \le\ W \ <\ e^* \textstyle\sum_\alpha o_\alpha, \tag{4}$$
and also fail Lemmas A, A+ and the Sudan step. If $\Sigma_G$ is smooth along its lines then $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha \le 2n$ and (4) forces $(b + 2)\rho < 2e^*$, i.e. $b < 2e^*/\rho - 2$: no $b$ at rate 1/2, only $b = 1$ at rate 1/4, $b \le 3$ at rate 1/8, $b \le 5$ at rate 1/16. `35_counts.py D` confirms this with exact integers ($n = 2^{20}$, simple contact on $xn$ lines, window between the Sudan step and Lemma C): at rate 1/2 every window with $b \ge 2$ is closed, and the window for $b = 1$ ($[1.19, 1.36)\,n$ at $x = 1$) is closed by Lemma L part 3, which gives $W \ge 1.5\,n$; at rate 1/4 the windows with $b = 2, 3$ are closed and the window for $b = 1$, cut at $W \ge 3\Delta = 0.75\,n$ by Lemma L, stays open only for components containing more than about $0.8\,n$ of the lines.

## 5. The obstruction: economical first integrals

### 5.1 Total derivatives and pencils

**Proposition 7 [proved].** The first integrals with $b = 1$ are exactly the total derivatives $\partial E = E_X + Y_1 E_{Y_0}$ of bivariate polynomials $E \in F[X, Y_0]$, and the sections of $\partial E = 0$ are the $P$ with $E(X, P(X)) \in F[X^2]$. The line of $\alpha$ is contained iff $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ is a critical point of $E$.

*Proof.* If $\hat G = a Y_1 + c$ then $\partial \hat G = a_{Y_0} Y_1^2 + (a_X + c_{Y_0}) Y_1 + c_X = 0$ gives $a \in F[X, Y_0^2]$, $c \in F[X^2, Y_0]$, $a_X = c_{Y_0}$; so $a = X\phi + \psi$, $c = Y_0 \phi + \chi$ with $\phi, \psi, \chi \in F[X^2, Y_0^2]$, and $E = XY_0\phi + Y_0\psi + X\chi$ works. Conversely $\partial(\partial E) = 0$. The rest is $\frac{d}{dX}E(X, P) = (\partial E)(X, P, P')$. $\square$

More generally, for $E, E' \in F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$ the Wronskian $E' \partial E - E \partial E'$ is a first integral (because $\partial^2 = 0$), its sections are the $P$ with $E(X, P, P')/E'(X, P, P') \in F(X^2)$, and it contains the line of every received point at which both $E$ and $E'$ vanish. The roots of the pencil $E + \theta E'$, $\theta \in F$, are among its sections. **This is where the two worlds part.** In large characteristic the same Wronskian is a differential equation whose solutions are the roots of $E + \theta E'$ for *constant* $\theta$ only: a curve in coefficient space, cut by the agreement hyperplanes to $O(n)$ points. In characteristic 2 the constants are $F(X^2)$ and the solutions are the roots for every $\theta \in F(X^2)$: the sections of a surface.

### 5.2 The flat cubic

Let $D$ be an $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of dimension $N$, $n = 2^N$, $L_D(X) = \sum_{j=0}^N e_j X^{2^j}$ its subspace polynomial ($e_N = 1$, $e_0 \ne 0$), $k = n/4$, $y_\alpha = \alpha^{n/2 - 1}$, and $\gamma_D(\tau) = \sum_{1 \le j < N} e_j \tau^{2^{j-1} - 1}$, so that $L_D(X) = X^n + X^2 \gamma_D(X^2) + e_0 X$. For a hyperplane $V \subset D$, $L_V = X^{n/2} + X P_V$ with $\deg P_V < k$ and $P_V = y$ on $V \setminus 0$ (`00-lead-notes.md` section 1).

**Proposition 5 [proved; checked for $N = 4, \dots, 8$ in $\mathrm{GF}(2^{16})$, all hyperplanes, `35_flat_cubic.py`].** Let
$$G^\circ(\tau, u, v) = u(u^2 + \tau v^2) + e_0 v + u\,\gamma_D(\tau) + e_0 \tau^{n/4 - 1}, \qquad \hat G^\circ = \partial E, \quad E = XY_0^3 + (X\gamma_D(X^2) + e_0) Y_0 + e_0 X^{n/2 - 1}.$$

1. $G^\circ$ is absolutely irreducible over $F(\tau)$, of degree 3, with $b = 1$, leading coefficient $XY_0^2 + X\gamma_D(X^2) + e_0$, and weighted degree $W = 3\Delta + 1 = 3k - 2$.
2. For every hyperplane $V$, the even part of $P_V$ is the constant $c_0 = e_0/\theta_V$, where $L_D = L_V^2 + \theta_V L_V$, and the section of $P_V$ lies on $\Sigma_{G^\circ}$.
3. $\Sigma_{G^\circ}$ contains the line $\ell_\alpha$ for every $\alpha \in D \setminus 0$, with contact order exactly 2, and not for $\alpha = 0$.
4. Every polynomial of degree $< k$ with more than $3n/8$ agreements with $y$ on $D \setminus 0$ is a section of $\Sigma_{G^\circ}$.

*Proof.* Put $\mathcal{L} = X^{n/2} + XY_0$, so that $\mathcal{L}(X, P_V) = L_V$. (2) $L_D = L_V(L_V + \theta_V)$ with $\theta_V = L_V(\lambda)$, $\lambda \in D \setminus V$. Differentiating, $e_0 = \theta_V L_V' = \theta_V (P_V + XP_V')$, and $P + XP'$ is the even part of $P$; so $u = e_0/\theta_V$ on the jet. Multiply $\mathcal{L}^2 + \theta\mathcal{L} + L_D = 0$ by $u$ and substitute $\theta u = e_0$: $u\mathcal{L}^2 + e_0\mathcal{L} + uL_D = 0$. With $\mathcal{L} = \tau^{n/4} + Xu + \tau v$ (as $Y_0 = u + Xv$) the terms $e_0 X u$ cancel and the result is $\tau G^\circ$. (3) At $X = \alpha \in D \setminus 0$ and $Y_0 = y_\alpha$: $\mathcal{L} = \alpha^{n/2} + \alpha y_\alpha = 0$ and $L_D(\alpha) = 0$, whatever $u$; expanding to second order, $u\mathcal{L}^2 + e_0\mathcal{L} + uL_D \equiv e_0\alpha\, w + T^2(u^3 + e_0 Y_1 + e_1 u)$ modulo $\mathfrak{c}_3$, which is nonzero of order 2. At $\alpha = 0$, $G^\circ(0, 0, v) = e_0 v$. (1) As a polynomial in $v$, $G^\circ = \tau u v^2 + e_0 v + c(u)$; it is reducible over $\overline{F(\tau)}(u)$ iff $z^2 + z = \tau u\,c(u)/e_0^2$ has a polynomial solution $z = s_2 u^2 + s_1 u + s_0$, and comparing coefficients this forces $L_D(\sqrt\tau) = 0$. (4) $\hat G^\circ \in \mathfrak{c}_2(\alpha)$ for $n - 1$ values of $\alpha$ and $\deg_X \hat G^\circ(X, P, P') \le 3k - 2 < 2 \cdot (3n/8 + 1)$; Lemma 0. $\square$

So $\Sigma_{G^\circ}$ is an economical first integral in the sense of (4): $x = 1$, $b = 1$, $e = W/2n = 0.375 < e^*$, leading coefficient involving $Y_0^2$. It sits at the very bottom of its window: the Sudan step fails by one unit ($W - \Delta = 2k - 1$ against $A - 1 = 2k - 2$), and `35_counts.py D` gives the window $[0.7499, 0.7733)$ for $w$ at $a = 0.4999$. **[checked]** `35_checks.py C` ($n = 32$, $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$): the polynomials of the support $(m, B, A) = (4, 2, 15)$ that vanish on the jets of all 31 flats form a space of dimension 208, which is exactly the space of multiples of $\hat G^\circ$; every explainer of this word is divisible by $\hat G^\circ$, and its powers are explainers. **[checked]** `35_flat_list.c`, exhaustive over all $k$-subsets: the list of this word is exactly the $n - 1$ flats for agreement above $3n/8$ (at $n = 16$: 15 polynomials at agreement 7, none at 6, 3 at 5; at $n = 32$: 31 at agreement 15, none at 12 to 14, 20 at 11).

Consequences. (a) At rate 1/4 the first-order method cannot do better than what is true: the list in $(a_1, 1/2)$ is at least $n - 1$, and the method reduces it to the sections of one cubic. (b) The family is the pencil of Section 5.1: $E_\theta = \mathcal{L}^2 + \theta\mathcal{L} + L_D$, each member vanishing on the whole graph of $y$, with $\tau G^\circ$ its Wronskian after eliminating $\theta$; the flats are the roots for constant $\theta$. In large characteristic the incidence argument applied to this one-parameter family would give $O(n)$, the right order. (c) The smallness of $W$ is the linearized shape of $L_D$: $\gamma_D$ has degree $n/4 - 1$ instead of $n/2 - 1$. On an evaluation set that is not a subspace nothing guarantees an economical cubic for any word, but nothing excludes it either.

### 5.3 The covering theorem with all tools

**Theorem 8 [proved].** Let $F$ have characteristic 2, $D \subset F$ arbitrary, and $(m, B_\partial)$ a certificate for $(n, \Delta, A)$. Fix a cap $M$ on the multiplicities used in Lemmas A, A+ and C. For every word $y$,
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \ \le\ B_{\mathrm{jet}}^2 + B_{\mathrm{jet}} \cdot \frac{2MA}{\Delta} + \sum_{G \text{ not cut}} |L_G|,$$
where the sum is over the irreducible first-integral factors $G$ of one explainer with $b \ge 1$ to which none of Lemma A, Lemma A+, Lemma C (multiplicities $\le M$) and Lemma L part 4 applies. By Section 4.1 these factors satisfy (4) up to the leading-order evaluation of (2) and the cap; each has degree $\ge 3$, so there are at most $B_{\mathrm{jet}}/3$ of them.

*Proof.* Theorem 4; a factor with $b = 0$ contributes at most its degree, a factor cut by one of the lemmas at most $\delta \cdot 2MA/\Delta$, and $\sum\delta \le B_{\mathrm{jet}}$. $\square$

At $n = 2^{25}$, rate 1/2, $a = 0.70$: $B_{\mathrm{jet}} = 25$, and the profiles of `35_counts.py E` are cut with $M = 600$, so the first two terms are below $2^{16}$ (not optimized).

## 6. Rate by rate

**Rate 1/2.** By (4) and Proposition 6 an economical component needs total contact $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha > (b + 2)\Delta/e^* \ge 2.2\,n$, against at most $2n$ for a surface smooth along its lines. `35_counts.py E` shows where the exact inequality (2) fails at the lower bound $W = (b + 2)\Delta$, for uniform profiles (contact $2j$ on $xn$ lines, none elsewhere): contact order 4 on at least about $0.75\,n$ lines for $b = 1$, on all lines for $b = 2, 3$; contact order 6 on half the lines. For $b = 1$ and contact order 4 on all lines the coefficients $E_{Y_0} = A^2 + XB^2$ and $E_X$ of $\partial E$ have triple zeros along list members, which narrows the window to about $W \in [3A + \Delta, 4e^* n) \approx [2.57, 2.76]\,n$ (**sketch**, not written out) but does not close it. I know no example of an irreducible first integral singular along a positive fraction of the lines of a word, and no proof that there is none. **Conditional statement [proved, with the grid check of `35_counts.py E` in place of a uniform evaluation of (2)]:** if no explainer of $y$ has an irreducible first-integral factor with $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha > 2n$, then $|\mathrm{List}(y, 0.70\,n)| < 2^{16}$ at $n = 2^{25}$, on any evaluation set. This would be $H_K(0.70, 16)$, about 210 queries at level 0 instead of 218, or 204 at $a = 0.692$. It is consistent with every known lower bound (the families at $5/8$ and $9/16$ are below $a_1$).

**Rate 1/4.** The economical components smooth along their lines have $b = 1$, so by Proposition 7 they are $\partial E$ with $E$ a cubic in $Y_0$ (since $3\Delta \le W < 2e^* n < 5\Delta$), with more than about $0.8\,n$ of the received points critical. The flat cubic is one. The residual problem is: bound the number of $P$ of degree $< n/4$, close to $y$, with $E(X, P(X)) \in F[X^2]$. For the flat word the answer is exactly $n - 1$ at $n = 16, 32$. A polynomial bound here would give $H_K(0.48, O(\log n))$ at rate 1/4.

**Rates 1/8, 1/16.** $a_1 > 2\rho$, so the flats are outside the window, but the economical window is wide ($b \le 3$, $b \le 5$) and I have no example and no bound. The gain would be 73 to 66 and 54 to 50 queries.

**All rates.** The gain of the first-order method is at most 7 to 9 percent of the queries. Second-order jets (the prize needed them to go from $0.6899$ to $0.6875$ at rate 1/2) correspond to the four components of $P$ over $F(X^4)$ and four equations in four unknowns; the dictionary of Section 3 applies, the counting lemmas would have to be redone on curves in 4-space, and the gain is smaller still. This route does not lead to the $9/16$ of the brief.

## 7. The other ingredients proposed in the brief

**Difference operators $\Delta_c f = f(X + c) - f(X)$, $c \in D$ [proved: no gain by counting].** The pair $(P(X), P(X + c))$ is an invertible linear image of $(g, h)$ in $P = g(\lambda) + Xh(\lambda)$, $\lambda = X^2 + cX$ (determinant $c$), so the algebra of Section 3 is identical with $F(X^2 + cX)$ as constant field; this is the two-to-one fold $D \to D/\langle c \rangle$, rate preserved, two lines per fibre. But a "hidden shift" explainer $Q(X, P(X), P(X + c))$ has no contact identity: the jet of $P$ at $\alpha + c$ is unrelated to its jet at $\alpha$, the condition at an agreement point with a free second variable of degree $\le B$ costs the full $(B + 1)m(m + 1)/2$, and the margin is $\frac\beta2(a^2/\rho - 1 - a\beta + \rho\beta^2/3) < 0$ below Johnson. When both $\alpha$ and $\alpha + c$ are agreements the second value is known and one is back to Guruswami and Sudan for the folded code on $S \cap (S + c)$, the dead end of the brief. The derivative is the limit $c \to 0$, and the gain appears only in the limit, where the two lines of a fibre merge into one line with a tangency.

**Subspace polynomials and the tower $D \to D/U$ [proved].** Same statement with $\lambda = L_U(X)$ and $2^e = |U|$ components: $2^e$ hyperplanes in general position in each fibre. No contact, no gain.

**Evaluation points in a subfield $F_0 = \mathrm{GF}(2^{32})$ of $K$ [proved].** The Frobenius $\sigma$ of $K/F_0$ fixes the points, so $P \mapsto P^\sigma$ maps $\mathrm{List}(y)$ to $\mathrm{List}(\sigma y)$ with the same agreement sets; equivalently a $K$-word is a pair of $F_0$-words and its $K$-list is the list of the 2-interleaved code over $F_0$. This is all the "Frobenius twist" gives for lists. The prize uses it to count bad slopes (Section 2.3), which the DEEP fold has removed. Since Theorem 8 holds on every evaluation set, nothing is gained or lost by moving the points.

**Hasse derivatives.** Proposition 1 and Proposition 3.

## 8. What would have to be true

1. **Rate 1/2 (the closest).** *No irreducible $G \in F[\tau, u, v]$ of degree $\le 51$ in $(u, v)$, with $\deg_{Y_1}\hat G \ge 1$ and leading $Y_1$-coefficient involving $Y_0$, has total contact $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha > 2n$ with the lines of a word (so is singular along many of them) while having weighted degree below $0.69 \sum_\alpha o_\alpha$; or, if one exists, it has few sections close to the word.* With this, Theorem 8 gives a constant list for all words at $a = 0.70$, any evaluation set, characteristic 2. The natural attack is a version of Lemma L for singular lines: for a leading coefficient that involves $Y_0$ I only have $W \ge (b + 2)\Delta$, and a bound $W \ge 0.69\sum_\alpha o_\alpha$ is what is needed. Small cases ($b = 1$, contact 4, $E$ with $E_{Y_0} = A^2 + XB^2$, $A, B$ vanishing on the graph) can be searched by linear algebra at $n = 32, 64$.
2. **Rate 1/4.** *For $E$ cubic in $Y_0$ with more than $0.8\,n$ received points critical and $\mathrm{wdeg}(\partial E) < 0.94\,n$, at most $n^{c}$ polynomials $P$ of degree $< n/4$ with agreement $\ge 0.47\,n$ have $E(X, P(X)) \in F[X^2]$.* The flat word shows $c \ge 1$. A proof must use the agreement, since without it the sections of $\partial E = 0$ form large families.
3. **In general.** The large-characteristic proofs rest on one property that fails here: *the value of a solution at every point is an algebraic function, of bounded degree, of its initial jet*. In characteristic 2 the value at $\alpha$ of a section of $\Sigma_G$ is constrained only through the finitely many points of $\Sigma_G \cap \ell_\alpha$ (which is what Lemmas A and C use), and not at all when $\ell_\alpha \subset \Sigma_G$. Any transfer has to control the surfaces that contain many of the lines; Lemmas L and C say these surfaces must be cheap, and the flat cubic says cheap surfaces exist exactly when the word has the structure of the known lower-bound families.
