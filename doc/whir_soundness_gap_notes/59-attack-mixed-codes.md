## 0. Verdict

**Theorem M is not broken. The Key Lemma is broken, much lower than the erratum of Section 7 says, and by an exponentially large family: it fails for every $L \ge 6$ at rate exactly 1/2 and for every $L \ge 4$ at every rate below 1/2 (in particular at every later level).** Everything I tried against Theorem M itself (the spanning window, $\mu$ uniform) either reduces to the union bound of Steps 1 to 3, which is correct, or to *free configurations*, which are exactly the lists of one explicit deterministic code (the ball-folded Reed-Solomon code, Section 3) and for which I found no violating structure, only a clean statement of where one would have to live.

1. **Steps 1, 2, 3 are correct as written [proved, Section 1].** The union bound is rigorous, covers words chosen after seeing $\mu$, and sets of size exactly $\lceil an \rceil$ suffice. "More than $q^{L-1}$ members implies $L + 1$ affinely independent ones" is correct. So Theorem M is equivalent to a deterministic counting statement about Reed-Solomon codewords on the cube; the only question is that count.
2. **The Key Lemma $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}] \le 2$ is false [proved and checked exactly, Section 2]** for: $L = 4$ and $\rho \le 1/4$ ($V = \mathrm{span}(1, M_1, M_2, M_1M_2)$, $M_i$ two additive polynomials: $\Delta(V) = n$); $L = 4$ and every $\rho \le 1/2 - 2^{-a_1} - 2^{-a_2}$ (the same space times a slab polynomial); $L = 6$, 7, 8 at $\rho = 1/2$ ($N \ge 17$, 13, 9). The erratum of Section 7 states "no counterexample for $L \le 11$ at rate 1/2, $L \le 7$ at rate 1/4": both are wrong. At $N = 25$, $\rho = 1/2$, $L = 6$ one single $V$ gives $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta}] \ge q^{n/8}$. What survives as a possible target is $L \le 5$ at rate exactly 1/2 (there the same constructions miss by a relative margin of about $2^{-N/3}$ only) and $L \le 3$ at lower rates; rows 2, 3, 4 of the table of Section 2 and the "$L = 10$" of Section 7 cannot be reached through the Key Lemma as stated.
3. **These counterexamples do not touch Theorem M [proved for the thin families, sketch for the thick one, Section 3.1]:** their members have local rank at least 1 on every multiply covered window that is not free, and they have few free windows. They are artefacts of Step 2 (charging $\Delta_x(V)$ at positions where the tuple uses a small subspace of $V$). But they show that the refined count must be organized by *local rank of the differences actually present at $x$*, and that the low-rank spaces are not only the periodic ones guessed in Section 7: they are the spaces of polynomials in several additive polynomials with disjoint direction sets (grid spaces), twisted by anything.
4. **The only deterministic danger is the free configurations, and they are the lists of the ball-folded code [proved, Section 3.2].** They are self-similar (every quotient by a subspace is again a cube of dimension $c$ with windows of at least $c + 1$ points), and pair counting plus Harper settles them for every $t$ exactly when $\iota_N(k - 1)/n \le \rho^2$: for $N \le 10$ at rate 1/2 and $N \le 7$ at rate 1/4 [checked]. So no toy search at $N \le 7$ can find a free violation, and none at $N \le 10$ at rate 1/2; from $N = 11$ on pair counting fails for large $t$, and at $N = 25$ it fails already for $t = 3$ (with a common factor), as note 60 also found. I found no algebraic family whose zero sets are ball-like; the grid spaces, the only scalable structured family I know, have free pair agreement at most 0.0902 at rate 1/2 [proved for products of slabs], far below $\rho^2$.
5. **Toy data agree with the first moment [checked, Section 5].** At $N = 4$, $q = 16$ (all codewords enumerated, all triples that can reach the target): the mixed code behaves like a uniformly random linear code (minimum distance 5 to 7 against the Singleton 9; triples at agreement 12 of 16: 1039 on average against 1010 predicted and 1004 for random codes), and is *worse* than plain Reed-Solomon at this field size, as the entropy term predicts. Exact $\mathbb{E}_V[q^\Delta]$ for $L = 1$ ($N = 4$) and for $L \le 4$ at $k = N + 2$ ($N = 4, 6$; $N = 5$ for $L = 2$) equals the predicted leading term $n q^{L-1-N}$ to within 0.2 percent.
6. **Arithmetic of the tables: correct** (one rounding: $a = 0.615$, not 0.616, at rate 1/2, $L = 4$) [checked, Section 7].
7. **Tightness [proved, Section 6]:** for every linear code the Singleton part of (1) is necessary for affinely independent tuples and the pigeonhole part for lists above $q^{L-1}$; their *sum* is necessary only heuristically (true for random codes). The attack frontier of the mixed code in the accounting of note 38 is the pure pigeonhole value at every $s \ge 2$: 114, 57, 38, 28 queries at rates 1/2 to 1/16.

## 1. The proof skeleton, line by line

**Step 1 [proved correct].** For fixed $(P_i)$ and $(S_i)$ a word agreeing with $c_\mu(P_i)$ on $S_i$ exists iff at every $x$ the values $c_\mu(P_i)(x)$, $i \in I_x$, coincide, iff $\mu(x, \cdot) \perp R_x$. The vectors $\mu(x, \cdot)$ are independent and uniform on $K^C$, so the probability is exactly $q^{-\sum_x \dim R_x}$. The event does not involve the word, so the union bound over $(P_i, S_i)$ covers words chosen after seeing $\mu$. Translating all $P_i$ by the same polynomial does not change the event, so $P_0 = 0$ may be fixed and the count of tuples is at most $q^{Lk}$, as used.

**Step 2 [proved correct, and the source of the failure of Section 2 below].** $R_x$ is the image under restriction to $W_x$ of $V_x = \mathrm{span}\{P_i - P_{i'} : i, i' \in I_x\}$, of dimension $(|I_x| - 1)_+$ by affine independence, so $\dim R_x = (|I_x| - 1)_+ - \dim(V_x \cap \mathcal{I}(W_x))$. Replacing $V_x$ by $V$ is a valid bound and a very lossy one: it charges $\Delta_x(V)$ even where $|I_x| \le 1$, and even where $V_x$ meets $\mathcal{I}(W_x)$ trivially.

**Step 3 [proved correct].** $\sum_x (|I_x| - 1)_+ \ge (L+1)an - n$; at most $2^{(L+1)H(a)n}$ set systems of sets of size exactly $\lceil an \rceil$, which suffice by monotonicity; summing $q^{\Delta(\mathrm{span})}$ over ordered bases gives the number of ordered bases times $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}]$, at most $q^{Lk}\,\mathbb{E}_V$. With (1) and $\mathbb{E}_V \le 2$ this gives $2^{-129}$. The consequence "at most $q^{L-1}$ codewords" is correct (an affine space of dimension $L - 1$ over $K$ has $q^{L-1}$ points), provided $c_\mu$ is injective, which the same event with $L = 1$ gives.

**Step 4 [correct].** $\Pr[U \subseteq V] = \binom{k - u}{L - u}_q / \binom kL_q \le q^{-u(k-L)}(1 + 2/q)$.

**Step 5 [correct in its conclusion].** The count $\binom nb 2^{bN}$ does not give the stated conclusion (note 60, item 1, has the right count); the leading term $n\,q^{L-1-N}$ is exact to four digits at toy size (Section 5.1). Remark: by this term alone $L \le N$ would do; the hypothesis $L \le N - 2$ is not where the difficulty is.

**Steps 6 and 7.** Not refereed here (note 60 shows the Isoperimetric Lemma false as stated and gives the right form). What Section 2 below adds: the "direction classes" of Step 7 are not the right stratification. In the grid spaces every window has the *same* deficiency and every direction $D_x$ is a different polynomial, with $n$ classes spanning a space of dimension 4 to 8; they are non-transversal in the strongest possible sense, and no isoperimetric statement excludes them, because their deficient windows are all windows.

**Section 4 of note 57.** "A $U$-periodic polynomial sees $N - \dim U + 1$ distinct values on a window": at least that many; exactly $c + 1$ only for group-sum subspaces. Harmless.

**Section 8, dependent members [checked by hand for two cases, not in general].** Collinear $t$-tuples $P_0 + \lambda_i G$: all members coincide exactly on $Z = Z(c_\mu(G))$ and nowhere else, so $\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ = (t - 1)|Z|$ with $|Z| \le k + H(z)n/64$, $z = |Z|/n \approx \rho + H(z)/64$, and such a tuple has all agreements at least $an$ only if $a \le z + (1 - z)/t$. In that range $(t - 1)H(z) \le tH(a)$ (at rate 1/2 because $H(z) - H(a) \le |H'|\,(1 - z)/t$ with $|H'| < 0.2$; at lower rates because $H(a) > H(z)$), so the inequality of Section 8 holds for them. Quadruples in an $\mathbb{F}_2$-plane ($P_0$, $P_0 + G_1$, $P_0 + G_2$, $P_0 + G_1 + G_2$): in characteristic 2 each of the three parallel classes consists of two disjoint pairs, so a position in exactly one $Z_i = Z(c_\mu(G_i))$ has partition type 2+2 and $\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ = \sum_i |Z_i|$, which the triple inequality for $(0, G_1, G_2)$ and the bound on the common zeros $Z_0$ keep below $3k + 4H(a)n/64$: fine.

## 2. The Key Lemma fails from $L = 4$ (rates below 1/2) and $L = 6$ (rate 1/2)

**Setting.** Let $I_0, I_1, \dots, I_j$ be a partition of the directions $\{0, \dots, N-1\}$ ($I_0$ may be empty), $a_i = |I_i|$, and $M_i(X)$ the subspace polynomial of $\mathrm{span}(b_l : l \notin I_i)$: additive, of degree $n/2^{a_i}$, with $2^{a_i}$ values on $D$, and $M_i(x + b_l) = M_i(x)$ exactly when $l \notin I_i$. So a window step changes exactly one of the $M_i$: the map $(M_0, \dots, M_j)$ identifies $D$ with a grid $\prod_i \mathbb{F}_2^{a_i}$ and sends the window of $x$ to a star (the centre and, on each axis $i$, $a_i$ points). For a graph $E$ on $\{1, \dots, j\}$ put $\mathcal{V}_E = \mathrm{span}\bigl(\{1, M_1, \dots, M_j\} \cup \{M_iM_{i'} : ii' \in E\}\bigr)$, of dimension $L = 1 + j + |E|$. For $T \subseteq M_0(D)$ let $g = \prod_{t \in T}(M_0 - t)$, of degree $\tau n$ with $\tau = |T|/2^{a_0}$ ($g = 1$, $\tau = 0$ if $I_0 = \emptyset$), and $V = g \cdot \mathcal{V}_E$.

**Proposition 2.1 [proved].** $\Delta_x(V) \ge |E|$ for every $x$, and $\Delta_x(V) \ge L - 1$ for every $x$ with $M_0(x) \in T$. Hence $\Delta(V) \ge n\,\bigl(|E| + \tau j\bigr)$, while $\max\deg V = \tau n + \max_{ii' \in E}(n/2^{a_i} + n/2^{a_{i'}})$.

*Proof.* Let $u_i = M_i(x)$. For $ii' \in E$ the polynomial $g\,(M_i - u_i)(M_{i'} - u_{i'})$ lies in $V$ (expand the product) and vanishes at $x$ and at every $x + b_l$, because $b_l$ changes at most one of $M_i, M_{i'}$. These $|E|$ polynomials are linearly independent. If $M_0(x) \in T$ then $g$ vanishes at $x$ and at $x + b_l$ for $l \notin I_0$; at the remaining window points $x + b_l$, $l \in I_0$, every $M_i$ with $i \ge 1$ has the value $u_i$, so every element of $\mathcal{V}_E$ is constant there and the restriction of $V$ to $W_x$ has rank at most 1. $\square$

**Corollary 2.2 [proved].** $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}] \ge q^{\Delta(V_0) - L(k - L)}/(1 + 2/q)$ for any single $V_0$, since there are at most $(1 + 2/q)\,q^{L(k-L)}$ subspaces. With Proposition 2.1 the Key Lemma fails as soon as $|E| + \tau j \ge L\rho$ (the slack $L^2$ decides equality), under the degree condition $\tau + 2^{-a_i} + 2^{-a_{i'}} < \rho$:

**Remarks.**

1. **A thick family, not one space [proved].** For any polynomial $h$ with $\deg h + \max\deg V < k$ the space $hV$ has the same deficiencies (the vanishing elements are multiples of the same products). So the bad spaces are at least $q^{k - \max\deg V - 1}$ in number, not a handful. This matters for the plan of Section 7 of note 57 ("thin families handled by their own first moment"): the family is thick, and what saves Theorem M is only the local rank (Section 3.1).
2. **Translation-invariant spaces [proved].** If $V$ is invariant under $f(X) \mapsto f(X + d)$, $d \in D$ (equivalently closed under Hasse derivatives), then $\Delta_x(V) = \Delta_0(V)$ for every $x$: all or nothing. The spaces $\mathcal{V}_E$ are of this kind; they are spaces of polynomials of 2-weight at most 2 or 3 in the exponents. This is the structure Step 7 would have to classify. A small real instance: at $N = 5$, $k = 7$ all 32 window polynomials $\Lambda_{W_x}$ lie in a 5-dimensional invariant space (basis $\Lambda_C$, three additive-type polynomials and 1; the coordinates of $\Lambda_{W_x}$ are $(1, x, x^2, x^4, r(x))$), and 137 (over $\mathrm{GF}(2^5)$) or 72 (over $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$) of the 4495 quadruples through $x = 0$ are linearly dependent [checked, `59_toy_keylemma.py`]; at $N = 4$ and 6 there are none. Harmless there, but it is the same mechanism.
3. **Rate exactly 1/2, $L = 4, 5$ [checked].** The same constructions give $\Delta(V) = (1 + 2\tau)n$ against $4k$ and $(1 + 3\tau)n$ against $5k$ with $\tau < 1/2 - 2^{-a_1} - 2^{-a_2}$: they miss by $2(2^{-a_1} + 2^{-a_2})n$ field elements ($N = 13$: exponents $-2032$ and $-6119$). So at rate exactly 1/2 the Key Lemma for $L \le 5$ is not refuted, with a relative margin of about $2^{-N/3}$; at any rate $\rho \le 1/2 - 2^{-N/3 + 2}$ it is refuted for $L = 4$.
4. **$L \le 3$.** I found no counterexample: the constructions above give at most $\Delta/n = \tau$ ($L = 2$), $2\tau$ ($L = 3$) against $L\rho > L\tau$. For $L = 2$ the question is the pencil count of Step 6 and note 60.

## 3. Why Theorem M survives, and where the real danger is

### 3.1 Low local rank without freeness is harmless

**Lemma 3.1 [proved].** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a family of affinely independent $(L+1)$-tuples with agreement sets of size $an$. Every tuple has at least $yn$ multiply covered positions, $y = ((L+1)a - 1)/L$, and $\dim R_x = 0$ at a multiply covered position iff the members present coincide on the whole window (a free position). If no tuple of $\mathcal{F}$ has more than $fn$ free multiply covered positions, the first moment of $\mathcal{F}$ is at most $|\mathcal{F}|\,2^{(L+1)H(a)n}\,q^{-(y - f)n}$. So a family is dangerous only if it is large or has many free positions: $\log_q|\mathcal{F}|/n + (L+1)H(a)/64 \ge y - f$.

*The grid families.* For the thin family ($V = g\,\mathcal{V}_E$ fixed, tuples inside $V$): $|\mathcal{F}| \le q^{L^2}$ and $f \le \iota_N(k-1)/n \le 0.35$ by Harper alone, against $y \ge 0.51$ at the thresholds of (1): exponent about $-0.05n$ field elements at worst, in fact much less. For the thick family ($hV$, $h$ arbitrary, Remark 1 of Section 2) at $N = 25$, $L = 6$, $a = 0.5866$: $\log_q|\mathcal{F}|/n \le 0.109$, entropy 0.107, $y \ge 0.518$, so it is harmless if $f < 0.30$; Harper alone gives only $f \le 0.345$, and the family is excluded by the structure of its zero sets, $Z(h) \cup \{M_0 \in T\} \cup Z(\varphi)$: a slab point is interior only if its out-of-slab neighbours along $I_0$ are zeros of $h\varphi$, which bounds the free positions by about $\deg h/n + 2^{-a_i - a_{i'}}$, around 0.11 **[sketch, not written out]**. The same computation with $\min(|I_x| - 1, c + 1)$ is the lead's remark for the periodic family of Section 7. **So the counting statement that Theorem M needs is: the number of (tuples, set systems) with $\sum_x \dim R_x = r$ is at most about $q^{r - \text{margin}}$; the Key Lemma is the wrong surrogate for it, a correct one must use $\dim(V_x \cap \mathcal{I}(W_x))$ with $V_x$ the span of the differences present at $x$, and for the thick grid family Harper alone misses by $0.04n$ field elements, so the free positions have to be counted through the structure of the zero sets, not bounded by isoperimetry only.**

### 3.2 Free configurations are the lists of the ball-folded Reed-Solomon code [proved]

Define the folded code $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ball}} = \{(P|_{W_x})_{x \in D}\} \subseteq (K^{N+1})^D$. Two members coincide freely at $x$ iff their folded symbols at $x$ are equal, and $c_\mu$ is a random $K$-linear projection of each folded symbol. So a free configuration with member agreements $S_i$ is exactly a folded word with the members in its list, and every list of $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ball}}$ is a list of $\mathcal{C}_\mu$ for every $\mu$. Its pair agreement is at most $\iota_N(k - 1)$ (the largest interior of a set of fewer than $k$ points; Harper). It is an additively folded Reed-Solomon code with overlapping windows, in characteristic 2, where the linear-algebraic list decoding arguments for folded codes fail precisely because of periodic polynomials.

**Self-similarity [proved].** Let $U \subseteq D$ be any subspace of codimension $c$, $\pi : \mathbb{F}_2^N \to \mathbb{F}_2^c$ the quotient. The window maps to $\{0\} \cup \{\pi(e_l)\}$, which spans, so it has at least $c + 1$ points, with equality iff the $\pi(e_l)$ take only the values 0 and a basis (group-sum type with a zero group). So the free problem at dimension $N$ and rate $\rho$ contains the free problem at every dimension $c \le N$, same rate, Hamming-ball windows, on the domain $L_U(D)$, and nothing worse. The lists that note 57 Section 10 lifts through local windows are killed here only by the isoperimetry of the small cube, which is *better* for the defender than that of the large one.

**Where pair counting ends [proved; numbers checked, `59_tables.py`, Harper's function verified by exhaustion at $N = 4$].** If every pair has free agreement at most $\varphi n$ then $\sum_x \binom{c_x}{2} \le \binom t2 \varphi n$, and by convexity $\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ \le (t - 1)\rho n$ for every $t$ as soon as $\varphi \le \rho^2$ (if $c(c-1) = t(t-1)\varphi$ and $\varphi \le \rho^2$ then $c \le 1 + (t-1)\rho$). ### 3.3 The scalable structured family: grid spaces [proved for products of slabs]

Polynomials in $M_1, \dots, M_j$ are the one family where many codewords have large structured common zero sets. A product of slabs $\psi = \prod_i \prod_{t \in T_i}(M_i - t)$ has degree $\sum_i \tau_i n$, zero set of density $1 - \prod(1 - \tau_i)$, and its interior consists of the points with two coordinates in their $T_i$, plus the points with one coordinate in the interior of $T_i$ inside the $a_i$-cube (self-similarity again). With many thin slabs and $\sum \tau_i = \rho$ the first part has density at most $1 - e^{-\rho}(1 + \rho)$: 0.0902 at rate 1/2, 0.0265 at rate 1/4, below $\rho^2$ in both cases, so by the convexity argument above free configurations built from slab products satisfy every $t$-wise inequality. The second part is the same problem inside the $a_i$-cube. For the multilinear space of Section 2 ($L = 8$) a difference $\psi$ is free at $u$ iff $\psi$ and its three partial difference quotients vanish at $u$, which for monomials happens on a fraction of at most $\sum_{i < i'} 2^{-a_i - a_{i'}}$ of the positions [checked by hand for monomials only]. The grid spaces defeat the Key Lemma because they have many *half-deficient* windows; they have few free ones.

## 4. Other lines of attack, all negative

0. **Local windows (note 57, Section 10).** The lift of quotient lists is one line (a function of $L_W$ is constant on every window inside a coset of $W$) and was confirmed at toy size by note 64, Section 2; I did not repeat it. With the spanning window the same lift lands in the quotient cube with Hamming-ball windows (Section 3.2).
1. **Linearized members [proved].** For an additive polynomial $A$, $A|_{W_x} = A(x)\mathbf{1} + (0, A(b_0), \dots, A(b_{N-1}))$, so $c_\mu(A)(x) = \langle \mu'(x), (A(b_l))_l \rangle$ with $\mu'(x)_l = \mu(x, b_l) + v_l(x)\sum_{c}\mu(x, c)$, and $\mu'(x)$ is uniform on $K^N$ and independent over $x$. The subcode of additive polynomials (dimension $N - 1$ or less) is a uniformly random linear code: no structure survives, although the conditions at different positions look correlated.
2. **Frobenius and the Carlitz structure [nothing found].** The window is defined through the basis, and the only maps that preserve the window structure are the translations by $D$ (and coordinate permutations, which are not field maps). Frobenius and multiplication by $x$ move the basis: $x \cdot D \ne D$, and $b_l \mapsto x b_l = b_{l+1}$ shifts the directions but maps $b_{N-1}$ out of $D$. I found no polynomial identity that makes a Hamming ball, or any set with thin vertex boundary, the zero set of a structured low-degree polynomial; subspaces and their cosets have empty interior, unions of cosets have the interior of their image in the quotient cube, quadrics $\{Q = 0\}$ have as interior the flat $\{x : B(x, e_l) = Q(e_l)\ \forall l\}$ of codimension $\mathrm{rank}\,B$ [proved, one line each].
3. **Common factors [proved].** A family $P_0 + g \cdot (\ldots)$ with $Z(g) = B$ gets $|\mathrm{int}\,B|$ free positions for all members at the price of $|B| > |\mathrm{int}\,B|$ dimensions per member: always a loss for the adversary. This is the same computation as for collinear members.
4. **Products of low-degree pieces.** They are the grid spaces of Section 2: fatal for the Key Lemma, harmless for the theorem.
5. **Probability far above the first moment.** By Step 1 the probability of each configuration is exact, so the truth can only be *below* the first moment; the toy data of Section 5 show it is close to it.

## 6. Lower bounds, and what Theorem M would leave

**Singleton part [proved, every linear code].** Take any $k - L$ coordinates $Z$; the subcode vanishing on $Z$ has dimension at least $L$; take $c_0 = 0$ and $L$ independent members of it, split the other $n - k + L$ coordinates into $L + 1$ nearly equal parts and let the word follow $c_i$ on part $i$. So $L + 1$ affinely independent codewords at agreement $k - L + \lfloor (n - k + L)/(L+1) \rfloor$ exist in every linear code: the term $\rho + (1 - \rho)/(L+1)$ of (1) is necessary up to $L^2/n$.

**Pigeonhole part [proved, every code].** The average list size at agreement $an$ is at least $q^{k - n}\binom{n}{an}(q-1)^{(1-a)n}$, so lists exceed $q^{L-1}$ (and then contain $L + 1$ affinely independent members) whenever $a < \rho + H(a)/64 - (64(L-1) + O(\log n))/(64n)$: 0.5156, 0.2630, 0.1339, 0.0681 at rates 1/2 to 1/16.

**The sum [heuristic].** (1) asks for the sum of the two terms. For a uniformly random linear code the first moment is tight up to the overcount of set systems, so the sum is the truth there; for an arbitrary linear code I can prove only the maximum of the two. So "(1) is best possible for any linear code over a 64-bit alphabet" is proved in the weak form (each term separately) and conjectured in the strong form.

**What a proof would leave.** Theorem M bounds lists by $q^{L-1}$, and the DEEP fold pays for that with points, so the relevant comparison is with the point where lists exceed any budget, the pigeonhole value: 114 queries at rate 1/2. With (1): $L = 15$ gives 124 (gap 10 queries, 8.8%), the largest allowed $L = N - 2 = 23$ gives $a = 0.5364$, 121 queries (gap 6%), at the price of lists up to $q^{22}$ (about 50 points a level). The residual gap is the Singleton term $(1 - \rho)/(L+1)$ and nothing else.

**The frontier of note 38 for the mixed code [proved modulo note 38's attack model].** The structured lists that set the plain Reed-Solomon frontier at small $s$ (nested half-supports: $2^{96}$ at 9/16, $2^{227}$ at 17/32, $2^{475}$ at 33/64) are free configurations whose agreement sets are unions of flats; in the mixed code only their cube interiors survive, which are empty or tiny. Pigeonhole lists exist in every code. So for the mixed code the frontier is the pigeonhole value for every $s \ge 2$ (a list of $2^{11}q^{s-1}$ through a column needs $a < \rho + H(a)/64 - (64(s-1) + 11)/(64n)$, the same to six digits): **114, 57, 38, 28 queries** at rates 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, against 119, 60, 39, 29 for plain Reed-Solomon at $s = 2$ generic. Unchanged at $s \ge 4$.

## 8. Seed, lanes, extension field

1. **One fixed public seed is the right choice [proved under the theorem].** Theorem M is a statement about the code, with failure probability $2^{-128}$ over $\mu$, once. With a fixed nothing-up-my-sleeve seed there is nothing to grind. If the seed depends on anything the prover controls (the statement, a commitment, a salt), the prover gets one independent code per oracle query and the bound becomes $Q \cdot 2^{-128}$, a loss of $\log_2 Q$ bits that the "+130" of (1) would have to absorb ($+130 + \log_2 Q$, costing $\log_2 Q/(64(L+1)n)$ in $a$: nothing). So a statement-dependent seed is affordable but buys nothing; a per-level or per-size seed is harmless. What must not happen is $\mu$ depending on the committed word.
2. **Words chosen after $\mu$:** covered by the union bound (Section 1).
3. **Windows do not wrap:** $D + C = D$.
4. **Interleaved lanes with the same $\mu$ [proved].** Theorem M does not extend verbatim to the interleaved code with $K$-affine independence of the tuples of polynomials: the first moment over $m$ lanes has $q^{Lkm}$ tuples while $\dim R_x \le N + 1$ whatever $m$ is. Concretely the members $0, (G, 0, \dots), (0, G, 0, \dots), \dots$ are $K$-linearly independent and behave like a collinear family: they exist at agreement $z + (1 - z)/(L+1)$ with $z = \rho + H(z)/64$, that is 0.5459 for $L = 15$ at rate 1/2, just below the 0.5468 of (1) but for a reason unrelated to its proof. The interleaved statement must go through the generic interleaving reduction (researcher 61), which bounds list sizes, and its factor 2.
5. **$E$-valued lanes at later levels [proved].** With $\mu \in K$ the code over $E$ is the extension of scalars of $\mathcal{C}_\mu$, that is the two-lane interleaved code. For a difference $G = G_1 + \theta G_2$ the probability of a random agreement is $q^{-2}$ if $G_1|_{W_x}, G_2|_{W_x}$ are independent and $q^{-1}$ if not, so the $K$-rational directions $eP$, $e \in E$, $P \in K[X]$, keep the entropy term at $H(a)/64$, not $H(a)/128$, at every level. The list budget over $E$ is $q^{L-1} = |E|^{(L-1)/2}$ through the reduction.
