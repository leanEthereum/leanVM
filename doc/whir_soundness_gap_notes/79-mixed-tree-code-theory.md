# 79. Mixed tree code V1: theory (researcher 79, round 18)

**Summary (researcher 79, round 18, 2026-11-21; labels proved / sketched / conjectured / false on every statement; nothing here is refereed).** No reason was found that V1 is false with uniformly random partner maps, and no proof was found that it satisfies the generalized Singleton bound beyond classes of two. **Proved.** (1) Theorem 2.2: for the generic mixed tree code (T2 twiddles and all $\mu$ algebraically independent), every $d \ge 0$ and every window system with $x \in W_x$, with no other condition on the partner maps: with one functional per leaf the incidence rows of a list of distinct codewords are independent; hence V1 is MDS, any $t$ distinct codewords have at most $(t-1)k - 1$ leaves where two of them agree, and every pattern has $w \le Lk - 1 + \sum_x (c_x - 2)_+$ (the proof is a derivative lemma in the private coefficients, Lemma 2.1, which says that every component of a relation is a free agreement on a whole window, plus note 74's Theorem 4.2). (2) The exact local law (Lemma 3.1): $d$ private coefficients pay $\min(\kappa_x, d)$ of the $\kappa_x = c_x - 1$ conditions of a leaf and never separate functionals; the plain tree code is the same construction with $d = 1$ on sibling windows, which is why note 74 stopped at classes of three. (3) The criterion (3.1): $w \le Lk - s + (w_{\rm free} + \dim \mathfrak F - \mathrm{rank}\, U_\Phi)$, so GS($L$) for generic V1 follows from an inequality about the *plain* code alone, $\mathrm{rel}(\Psi) \le \beta_W(\Psi)$: the number of relations of a plain pattern is at most the weight it loses to the boundary of the windows (for $d = 1$, its total variation along $\pi$). (4) Theorem 5.1: for generic $\mu$ on any MDS base code, if every set of at most $k - 1$ leaves contains at most half as many whole windows as leaves (condition (W2), $\gamma \le 1/2$, which uniform maps have from about $d = 6$ and no $d = 1$ map has), three codewords satisfy $w \le 2k - 1$ on the part of the solution variety where the window directions are the three differences. (5) Over GF($2^{64}$) with uniform $\mu$ and any fixed twiddles (Proposition 6.1, Theorem 6.2): the probability that a tuple satisfies a pattern is exactly $\prod_x q^{-r_x}$, bounded by $q^{-w} q^{w_{\rm free}(M)}$, losslessly for classes up to $d + 2$ members and for full classes; if $\mathcal K = q^{-Lk} \sum_{M} q^{w_{\rm free}(M)} \le C^n$ over lists $M$ (hypothesis KH), then except with probability $2^{-130}$ no word has $L + 1$ codewords at agreement $\tau = (1 + L\rho)/(L+1) + H(\tau)/64 + \log_2 C / (64 (L+1))$: at rate 1/2 and $C = 4$, $0.691, 0.621, 0.574, 0.547$ for $L = 2, 4, 8, 16$. Unconditionally only the pair statement: on note 73's good event, two codewords of V1 agree on fewer than $(\rho + 2\varepsilon_0 + 2/64) n + 2$ leaves, which is worse than the plain code. **False.** (a) Fixed XOR masks (and any partner maps sending blocks to blocks at some scale) do not remove the lifts: the staircase survives with $N$ replaced by $N/2^d$, for instance $\binom{31}{16}$ codewords at $1/2 + 1/64 - 2/n$ for $d = 1$ (Proposition 4.2, checked by encoding up to $n = 256$). (b) GS(2) for $d = 1$ and *every* $n$-cycle: a cycle that walks through the blocks of a lift carries three codewords of weight $2k - 1 + (k/2 - 4)$ (exact ranks at $k = 8, 16, 32$); so any theorem needs a hypothesis tying the maps to the tree, and for $d = 1$ it cannot be expansion. (c) The reading "full rank because each leaf has $d$ private coefficients for its $d$ functionals": false as an argument, for the reason in (2). **Sketched or open.** GS(2) and beyond for generic V1 with block-scattering maps (true on all $5^8$ patterns at $(4,8)$ for twelve maps with $d = 1$; proved to hold for the lift patterns; the missing lemma is a statement about the plain tree code: a plain pattern with relations is a lift up to about rel rows); window directions that depend on the parameters (the four block lines of a lift) are covered by no argument here; KH is open for $L \ge 2$, its local part is a transfer-matrix computation done only for $d = 1$ ($C \le 2.62$ a triple leaf), its global part is the same missing lemma with a budget. The coarse events of the twiddles that dominate the plain first moment are harmless in V1 one at a time (the top levels up to $N = 4$ still need a checked condition: three equal top twiddles have probability $2^{-126}$). For the prize ($\tau \le 0.63$, $L \ge 4$) the count as it stands needs $d \ge L - 1 \ge 3$ partner maps; whether fewer suffice is not decided.

## 1. Setup and notation

Plain tree code (T2, notes 68 and 74): $k = 2^v$, $n = 2^{v+r}$, leaf $y$ has row $g_y = \bigotimes_{i<v}(1, x_i(y)) \in K^k$. Windows: every leaf $x$ has a window $W_x = \{x, \pi_1(x), \dots, \pi_d(x)\}$ ($d+1$ distinct leaves unless said otherwise), $P_x = W_x \setminus \{x\}$ its partners, $W(T) = \bigcup_{x \in T} W_x$, $\partial T = W(T) \setminus T$, and for a set $Y$ of leaves $\mathrm{int}\,Y = \{x : W_x \subseteq Y\}$. Mixed code V1: row $h_x = g_x + \sum_a \mu_{x,a}\, g_{\pi_a(x)}$, so $c' = A c$ with $A = I + \sum_a \mathrm{diag}(\mu_{\cdot,a}) P_{\pi_a}$. $F_x = \mathrm{span}(g_y : y \in W_x)$ is the flat of leaf $x$; $h_x$ is a point of $F_x$ with $d$ private coordinates.

Lists and patterns as in note 74 Section 1: $M \in K^{L \times k}$ has the differences $d_l = m_l - m_0$ as rows, $\delta_y = M g_y^T \in K^L$ is the vector of plain values of the differences at $y$, a pattern gives each leaf a class $C_x$ and $\Lambda_x = \mathrm{span}(e_l - e_{l'} : l, l' \in C_x)$, $\kappa_x = \dim \Lambda_x = c_x - 1$, $w = \sum_x \kappa_x$. The mixed incidence system is $\lambda^T M h_x = 0$ ($\lambda \in \Lambda_x$), its row space is $R = \sum_x \Lambda_x \otimes h_x$, its solution space $S = R^\perp$. $S$ is *genuine for $\lambda$* if $\lambda^T M \ne 0$ for some $M \in S$; a list (all $d_l$ nonzero and distinct) is genuine for every $e_l - e_{l'}$. GS($L$) is the statement: every pattern carried by $L + 1$ distinct codewords has $w \le Lk - 1$ (equivalently, no word has $L + 1$ codewords with total agreement $\ge n + Lk$).

"Generic" below: the twiddles are algebraically independent over the prime field, and the $\mu_{x,a}$ are algebraically independent over the field $K_{\rm tw}$ generated by the twiddles. $F$ is the field generated by everything. The window matrix of a leaf: $N_x(M) = \Lambda_x^T [\delta_y]_{y \in W_x}$, a $\kappa_x \times (d+1)$ matrix (in a basis of $\Lambda_x$); the mixed conditions at $x$ read $N_x(M)\,(1, \mu_x)^T = 0$.

## 2. The generic statement for classes of two (proved), and the derivative lemma for the mixed code

**Lemma 2.1 (derivative lemma for V1; proved, any characteristic, any windows containing their centre, any base code whose rows do not involve the $\mu$).** Let $\sum_{x \in T} \ell_x \otimes h_x = 0$ be a relation among the mixed rows, $\ell_x \in \Lambda_x \otimes F$. Then for every $j$ and every $y \in W_j$: $\ell_j \otimes g_y \in R_T := \sum_{x \in T} \Lambda_x \otimes h_x$. Hence every solution $M$ of the mixed system satisfies $\ell_j^T \delta_y = 0$ on the whole window of $j$: **the components of relations are free agreements** (a combination of the plain differences that vanishes on a whole window). *Proof.* The $g_y$ do not involve $\mu$. Clear denominators and differentiate the identity in $\mu_{j,a}$: $\ell_j \otimes g_{\pi_a(j)} + \sum_x \partial \ell_x \otimes h_x = 0$, and $\partial \ell_x \in \Lambda_x \otimes F$ because $\Lambda_x$ is defined over the prime field. For $y = j$: $\ell_j \otimes g_j = \ell_j \otimes h_j - \sum_a \mu_{j,a}\, \ell_j \otimes g_{\pi_a(j)}$ and $\ell_j \otimes h_j \in R_T$. $\square$ Unlike note 74's Lemma 2.3 the component of a relation at a leaf is always a pure tensor $\ell_x \otimes h_x$, and the lemma gives one plain row for each of the $d + 1$ leaves of the window.

**Theorem 2.2 (one functional per leaf; proved).** Generic V1 on the generic T2 tree code, any $d \ge 0$, any windows with $x \in W_x$ (the partner maps need not be permutations, and nothing is asked of them). Let $X$ be a set of leaves and $\lambda_x \in K_0^L \setminus 0$ for $x \in X$, $K_0$ a field over which twiddles and $\mu$ are algebraically independent. If the solution space $S$ of $\{\lambda_x^T M h_x = 0\}_{x \in X}$ is genuine for every $\lambda_x$, the rows $\lambda_x \otimes h_x$ are linearly independent, so $|X| = Lk - \dim S \le Lk - 1$.

*Proof.* Suppose not and let $T \ne \emptyset$ be the union of the supports of the relations. Here $\Lambda_x = \langle \lambda_x \rangle$, so by Lemma 2.1 $\lambda_x \otimes F_x \subseteq R_T$ for every $x \in T$, in particular $\lambda_x \otimes g_x \in R_T$ and every $M \in S$ solves the plain system $\{\lambda_x^T M g_x = 0\}_{x \in T}$. That plain system has one functional per leaf on distinct leaves, its solution space contains $S$ and is therefore genuine for each $\lambda_x$, so by note 74's Theorem 4.2 (refereed in note 75) its $|T|$ rows are independent. They lie in $R_T$, which is spanned by $|T|$ dependent rows: $|T| \le \dim R_T < |T|$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.3 (proved).** In the generic mixed tree code, for any windows: (a) the code is MDS; (b) $Q_t$: among $t$ distinct codewords the number of leaves where at least two agree is at most $(t-1)k - 1$; (c) every pattern carried by $L + 1$ distinct codewords has $w \le Lk - 1 + \sum_x (c_x - 2)_+$; (d) three codewords: $w \le 5k/2 - 2$ (the argument of note 74, Theorem 4.4). So V1 is never worse than the plain tree code on these counts, whatever the partner maps; all excess over GS sits at leaves where three or more members agree, as before. What mixing is supposed to add is the removal of that excess, which is Sections 3 to 5.

**Remark 2.4 (Rado form; proved).** For one functional per leaf the mixed rows are points with private generic coordinates on the flats $\lambda_x \otimes F_x$, which are defined over $K_{\rm tw}(K_0)$, so by Rado's theorem (generic points on flats, Lovasz 1977) $\dim \sum_{x \in X} \lambda_x \otimes h_x = \min_{J \subseteq X} \big(\dim \sum_{x \in J} \lambda_x \otimes F_x + |X \setminus J|\big)$: the rank is governed by the plain systems $\{\lambda_x \otimes g_y : x \in J, y \in W_x\}$, which put up to $d + 1$ functionals on a plain leaf. Theorem 2.2 only uses the sub-system $y = x$.

## 3. Classes of three or more: exact local accounting, and the reduction of GS($L$) to an inequality on the plain code

**Lemma 3.1 (what a leaf's private coefficients pay; proved, any base code, any field).** Fix the plain code and $M$. Over uniform $\mu_x \in K^d$ the mixed conditions at $x$ hold with probability $q^{-r_x(M)}$ if they are solvable and $0$ otherwise, where $r_x(M) = \mathrm{rank}\, N_x(M)$ ($= $ the rank of the partner columns when solvable), and the events at different leaves are independent. So $r_x \le \min(\kappa_x, d)$: **$d$ private coefficients pay at most $d$ of the $\kappa_x = c_x - 1$ conditions of a leaf; the other $\kappa_x - r_x$ are conditions on the plain codewords at the window** (the left kernel $\Phi_x(M) = \{\varphi \in \Lambda_x : \varphi^T \delta_y = 0\ \forall y \in W_x\}$ has dimension $\kappa_x - r_x$; I call $\varphi \in \Phi_x(M)$ a *free functional* and $w_{\rm free}(M) = \sum_x \dim \Phi_x(M)$ the free weight). For $\kappa_x \le d + 1$ a generic window matrix has full rank $\kappa_x$ or is unsolvable; for $\kappa_x \ge d + 2$ every solvable $M$ has a deficient window. *Proof.* The conditions are $N_x(M)(1, \mu_x)^T = 0$, an affine system in $\mu_x$ alone. $\square$ The plain T2 tree code is itself this construction with $d = 1$: its leaf row $g_p \otimes (1, t_x)$ is a point with one private coordinate on the flat $g_p \otimes K^2$, the "window" being the sibling pair, which does not expand at all. This is the exact reason note 74's Theorem 2.5 stops at classes of three and the lifts exist: private coefficients never separate functionals at a leaf, they pay conditions, and everything they do not pay is decided by how the free functionals of different leaves overlap on the plain code.

**Proposition 3.2 (dimension count; proved, generic $\mu$, any base code).** Let a pattern have solution space of dimension $s \ge 1$ for generic $\mu$, and let $\Sigma$ be the closure of the set of solutions $M$ swept by a component of the incidence variety $\{(M, \mu)\}$ that dominates the $\mu$-space. Then for generic $M \in \Sigma$: $\dim \Sigma \ge s + \sum_x r_x(M)$, hence $w = \sum_x r_x + w_{\rm free}(M) \le \dim \Sigma - s + w_{\rm free}(M)$. If $\mathfrak F$ is the closure of the family of free profiles $\Phi(M) = (\Phi_x(M))_x$, $M \in \Sigma$, and $U_\Phi = \sum_x \Phi_x \otimes F_x$ the plain system "$\Phi_x$ at every leaf of $W_x$", then $\dim \Sigma \le \dim \mathfrak F + Lk - \mathrm{rank}\, U_{\Phi}$ for generic $\Phi \in \mathfrak F$, so
$$ w \;\le\; Lk - s + \big(w_{\rm free}(\Phi) + \dim \mathfrak F - \mathrm{rank}\, U_\Phi\big). \tag{3.1}$$
*Proof.* Over the open set of $\mu$ where $\dim S(\mu) = s$ the incidence variety is a vector bundle, so exactly one component dominates and it has dimension $dn + s$. Its fibre over a generic $M \in \Sigma$ lies in the fibre of the whole incidence variety, a product of affine spaces of dimensions $d - r_x(M)$ (Lemma 3.1), so $dn + s \le \dim \Sigma + dn - \sum r_x$. The map $M \mapsto \Phi(M)$ sends $\Sigma$ onto a dense subset of $\mathfrak F$ and its fibre over $\Phi$ is orthogonal to $U_\Phi$. $\square$

**Corollary 3.3 (reduction; proved in the direction stated).** The generic mixed code satisfies GS($L$) if no free configuration has $w_{\rm free}(\Phi) + \dim \mathfrak F - \mathrm{rank}\,U_\Phi \ge s$ on a dominating component (by (3.1); the converse is not claimed, because $\dim \Sigma \le \dim \mathfrak F + Lk - \mathrm{rank}\,U_\Phi$ can be strict and a component of the right dimension need not dominate, as a cycle of $\pi$ made of deficient leaves shows: there the product of the $\mu$ around the cycle is forced to be 1). In particular GS($L$) follows from the **interior inequality**: for every family $\mathfrak F$ of full free profiles $\Phi(M)$ of lists $M$ of the *plain* code (full: Remark 3.4), $\mathrm{rank}\, U_\Phi \ge w_{\rm free}(\Phi) + \dim \mathfrak F$. For profiles that are sub-classes (graphic, $\dim \mathfrak F = 0$): the plain pattern $\Psi = \Psi(\Phi)$, $\Psi_y = \sum_{x : y \in W_x} \Phi_x$, must have rank at least the weight $\sum_x \dim \bigcap_{y \in W_x} \Psi_y$ of its window interior. With $\mathrm{rel}(\Psi) = w(\Psi) - \mathrm{rank}(\Psi)$ (note 74, Proposition 4.1: relations between dual codewords supported on the members' agreement sets) and the **boundary weight** $\beta_W(\Psi) = w(\Psi) - w_{\rm int}(\Psi) \ge 0$ this reads
$$ \mathrm{rel}(\Psi) \;\le\; \beta_W(\Psi) \qquad \text{for every plain pattern } \Psi \text{ carried by a list.} \tag{3.2}$$
For $d = 1$, $\beta_W(\Psi) = \sum_x \dim \Psi_x / (\Psi_x \cap \Psi_{\pi(x)})$: the total variation of the plain pattern along the cycles of $\pi$.

**What is known about the two sides (proved facts about the plain generic tree code).** $\mathrm{rel}(\Psi) \le \sum_y (\psi_y - 1)_+$ (note 74, Theorem 4.2: rank at least the support) and $\mathrm{rel}(\Psi) \le \sum_l (a_l - k)_+$ with $a_l$ the size of member $l$'s agreement set (MDS: no dual codeword of weight $\le k$); both are attained by lifts, and every deficient plain pattern found in notes 69, 74, 75 is a lift, block-aligned at its split level. A lift at a scale of $N$ blocks with $L + 1$ members has $\mathrm{rel} \le (L - 1)(n/N - 1) + s - 1$ and block-aligned weight about $Lk$, so (3.2) holds for it as soon as a fraction $f \ge ((L-1) n/N - L + s)/(Lk - n/N) \approx (L - 1)/(L \rho N)$ of the leaves of every block-class (the blocks on which one difference vanishes, of density at most $\rho - 1/N$) have a partner outside the class and outside the zeros of $B$: at rate 1/2 any fraction above $2/N$, and uniform partners give $f \ge 1 - (\rho - 1/N)^d - O(1/N) \ge 1/2$ (**proved** for the lift patterns themselves; for their sub-patterns, where the adversary keeps the leaves whose partners stay inside and uses the zeros of $B$ as jokers, rel falls at least as fast as the weight kept, by the dual-distance bound: **sketched**, with one case estimated, $N = 4$, $L = 2$, $d = 1$, where the kept agreement sets have about $0.94 k < k$ leaves, so that rel is 0). **So for the lifts, mixing with $d = 1$ already suffices in the generic model; what is missing for a theorem is the statement that plain deficiency is always lift-like** (note 74 observed that every deficient kernel it found is spanned by products; this is needed as a theorem, with a count of rows), or a hypothesis on the windows strong enough to beat the crude bounds on rel, which is Section 5.

**Three sanity checks of the count (proved).** (i) A set closed under the partner maps, $W(T) = T$, has $\beta_W = 0$ on patterns constant on orbits: there V1 is the plain code (also noted by researcher 78). (ii) $d = 1$, $\pi$ one $n$-cycle, a plain three-member pattern made of four arcs of the cycle (common zeros $Y_0$, and one arc for each difference, two of them adjacent to $Y_0$) has $\beta_W = 3$, so it gives a mixed list of weight $2k - 1 + (\mathrm{rel} - 3)$: with $d = 1$ a plain pattern with $\mathrm{rel} \ge 4$ supported on few arcs of $\pi$ is fatal, and an adversarially chosen $\pi$ (one that walks through the blocks of a lift one after the other) breaks GS(2) with excess $k/2 - 4$. (iii) Collinear lists ($M$ of rank one) have $w \le L(k-1)$ by MDS alone.

**Remark 3.4 (two cautions on reading (3.1); proved).** (a) $\Phi$ is the *full* free profile of the generic solution. For $\kappa_x \ge d + 2$ it has dimension at least $\kappa_x - d$ at every leaf and its family has positive dimension; an isolated full class then gives equality in (3.1), $(\kappa - d) + (\kappa - d) d - (\kappa - d)(d + 1) = 0$, so small $d$ is not by itself an obstruction (patterns made of full classes only are settled by MDS for every $d$). (b) Proposition 3.2 gives $\dim \Sigma \ge s + \sum r_x$ in general (the dominating component's fibre over $M$ is a closed subset of the affine space of Lemma 3.1); only this direction is used.

**Remark 3.5 (locality; proved, any MDS base code).** If $|W(T)| \le k$, the mixed rows $\{\Lambda_x \otimes h_x\}_{x \in T}$ are independent for generic $\mu$, whatever the pattern: the $g_y$, $y \in W(T)$, are independent and $h_x = g_x + \sum_a \mu_{x,a} g_{\pi_a(x)}$ is unitriangular on $T$ at $\mu = 0$. So every relation has a support whose windows cover at least $k + 1$ plain leaves, and a violation of GS($L$) needs free functionals on a set $T$ with $|W(T)| > k$: the adversary cannot use small configurations, only ones on the scale of the code.

## 4. Target (C): what the partner maps must satisfy (proved unless marked)

**Proposition 4.1 (transfer of lists through interiors; proved, any $\mu$).** If the plain code has codewords $c_0, \dots, c_L$ agreeing with a word $u$ on sets $S_0, \dots, S_L$, then V1 has the codewords $A c_l$ agreeing with the word $A u$ on $\mathrm{int}\, S_l$ at least. So V1 has every list of the plain code whose agreement sets have large window interiors, at the agreement of the interiors. *Proof.* $(Ac)(x)$ depends on $c|_{W_x}$ only. $\square$

**Proposition 4.2 (fixed XOR masks do not remove the lifts; proved by construction, checked by encoding).** Let $\pi_a(x) = x \oplus m_a$ for fixed masks $m_1, \dots, m_d$ (any $\mu$). Fix a split level $i$, $N = n/2^i$ blocks, and let $H$ be the span of the block parts $\mathrm{bl}(m_a)$, $|H| \le 2^d$. Every union of $H$-cosets of blocks is closed under the partner maps. Hence the one-sided staircase of note 74 (Proposition 6.1) survives with blocks replaced by $H$-cosets: $\binom{N/|H| - 1}{\rho N/|H|}$ codewords of V1 at agreement at least $\rho + 1/N - 2^d/n$ with one word, for every $N$ with $N/|H| \ge 4$; at $N = 64$, $d = 1$, rate 1/2: $\binom{31}{16} = 3 \cdot 10^8$ codewords at $1/2 + 1/64$, and lists of unbounded size at every fixed agreement above $\rho$ as $n$ grows. If the masks do not touch the top bits (every $m_a < 2^{i}$), blocks are preserved outright and all lifts at split levels above the masks survive unchanged. The same holds for any partner maps that send blocks to blocks at some scale (affine maps of the index that preserve the top bits as a set, the sibling map, permutations inside half trees). *Proof.* Members $A_S \otimes B$ with $S$ a union of $H$-cosets and the zeros of $B$ chosen as a union of cosets of $\langle m_a \rangle$ inside the blocks of one $H$-coset outside every $S$; apply Proposition 4.1. $\square$ *Check* (`scripts/79_xor.c`, GF($2^{64}$), by encoding, mask flipping the tree bit): $(k,n) = (32, 64)$, $N = 16$: 35 codewords at agreement 34 each, sum 1190 against the GS bound $n + 34k - 1 = 1151$; $(64, 128)$, $N = 32$: 6435 codewords at 66; $(128, 256)$, $N = 32$: 6435 at 134 (sum 862290 against 823807). The same members under a uniformly random partner permutation: agreement at most 22 to 25 of 64 and at most 82 of 256, below the dimension, as the lead predicted ($\varphi^{d+1}$).

**Proposition 4.3 (what $d = 1$ can and cannot do; proved unless marked).** (a) For $d = 1$ the boundary weight is a total variation along the cycles of $\pi$ (Corollary 3.3), every arc of a cycle has boundary one, and a union of cycles is closed. So $\pi$ should be one $n$-cycle (no fixed points, no short cycles: at toy size these are what makes deficiency depend on the draw, as researcher 78 measures), and even then GS(2) fails for the $n$-cycles that walk through the blocks of a lift block by block (Section 3, check (ii)). (b) With $d = 1$ and a uniformly random $\pi$ the lifts are harmless in the generic model at every scale $N \ge 4$ (Section 3: proved for the lift patterns, sketched for their sub-patterns; exact check at $N = 4$ in Section 5, `79_adv.c`). (c) Exhaustive check (`scripts/79_scan.c`, all $5^8$ three-member patterns at $(k, n) = (4, 8)$ over GF($2^{64}$)): the plain tree code has the 48 violations of GS(2) of note 69 (weight 8); V1 with $d = 1$ has **none** for 9 uniformly random permutations (several with fixed points), for one 8-cycle, for the mask $x \oplus 4$ and for bit reversal, and has the same 48 for the sibling map. (At this size a surviving lift needs three sibling pairs and a fixed point closed under $\pi$.)

**What the maps must satisfy, in order of necessity.** (1) No block structure at any scale $N \le N_{\max}$ (the scales where lifts can exceed the budget, $N \le 64$ for $\varepsilon \ge 1/64$): for every block-class $U$ (a union of blocks on which one difference of a lift vanishes, density at most $\rho$) a fraction bounded below of the leaves of $U$ must have a partner outside $U$. Uniform block-to-block counts (each ordered pair of blocks joined by $n/N^2$ leaves, for one of the maps) give interior fraction at most $\varphi$ of $U$ for every $U$ of density $\varphi$ (exactly $\varphi$ for $d = 1$; about $\varphi^d$ for $d$ independent uniform maps); this is achieved **deterministically** for all $N \le \sqrt n$ by $\pi(x) = $ (bit reversal of $x$), or by exchanging the top and bottom halves of the index bits, composed with any fixed-point-free map on the low bits to break the involution (whether such a structured map can also be a single $n$-cycle, as $d = 1$ wants, was not examined); an XOR mask or a rotation of the index by one position is not enough (for a rotation by one, the sets of blocks $\{q : \text{Hamming weight of } q \le j/2\}$ have most of their leaves with partners inside, by the poor expansion of de Bruijn graphs: sketched, not computed). (2) No small closed sets and no small-boundary sets on which a plain pattern can have $\mathrm{rel} > \beta_W$: for the generic statement this is (3.2); for the statement over GF($2^{64}$) the first moment needs in addition $\kappa_x \le d + 1$ at every leaf for its local bound to be lossless, and $|\partial T| \ge \dim \bigcap_{x \in T} \Lambda_x - O(1)$ for the sets $T$ that matter (Section 6); an involution or a 2-cycle is harmless for the lifts but makes the expected number of solutions be dominated by an event of probability $1/q$ in $\mu$ (two proportional rows), so the maps should have no closed pair. (3) For a proof that does not rest on a classification of plain deficiency: expansion of every set, condition (W2) of note 62 (every $Z$ with $|Z| \le k - 1$ contains at most $\gamma |Z|$ whole windows), which random maps satisfy with $\gamma = 1/2$ only from $d = 6$ on at rate 1/2 (note 62, Theorem E) and which no map with $d = 1$ satisfies.

## 5. Target (A) for three members: what is proved under an expansion hypothesis, and what is not

Notation for $L = 2$ and a generic solution $M$ of rank two (collinear lists are settled by MDS): $V$ is the row space of $M$, $\xi(y) = [\delta_y] \in \mathbb P^1$ the direction of the plain values at $y$ (undefined on the common zeros $Y_0$), and for a direction $c$, $v_c \in V$ is the combination vanishing exactly on $Z_c = Y_0 \cup \xi^{-1}(c)$, $|Z_c| \le k - 1$. The three differences are the *marked* directions. A pair leaf with class direction $\lambda$ is free iff $W_x \subseteq Z_\lambda$; a triple leaf is free ($r_x = 0$) iff $W_x \subseteq Y_0$, and has $r_x = 1$ iff $W_x \subseteq Z_c$ for one direction $c = c(x)$, marked or not, with at least two leaves of $W_x$ outside $Y_0$.

**Theorem 5.1 (GS(2) on the marked part of the solution variety; proved, generic $\mu$, any MDS base code).** Let the windows satisfy (W2) with $\gamma \le 1/2$: every set $Z$ of leaves with $|Z| \le k - 1$ has $|\mathrm{int}\, Z| \le |Z|/2$. Then V1 with generic $\mu$ on any MDS code (in particular on the generic tree code, or on Reed-Solomon on the subspace domain) has no three distinct codewords with $w \ge 2k$ whose generic solution has rank two and all its window directions $c(x)$ among the three marked ones. *Proof.* The profile is one of finitely many, $\dim \mathfrak F = 0$, and by (3.1) with $s \ge 1$ a pattern with $w \ge 2k$ needs $\mathrm{rank}\, U_\Phi \le w_{\rm free} - 1$. Let $T_3$ and $T_\lambda$ be the leaves with free space $K^2$ and $\langle \lambda \rangle$, $Y_0$ the plain leaves that carry two independent functionals of $U_\Phi$ (it contains $W(T_3)$ and the intersections $W(T_\lambda) \cap W(T_{\lambda'})$), $Y^\lambda = W(T_\lambda) \setminus Y_0$, and $y_0, z_\lambda, t_3, t_\lambda$ the sizes. The solution $M$ vanishes on $Y_0$ and $v_\lambda$ on $Y_0 \cup Y^\lambda$, so $y_0 + z_\lambda \le k - 1$. For two directions $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2$ the rows $\lambda_j \otimes g_y$, $y \in Y_0 \cup Y^{\lambda_j}$, lie in the complementary summands $\lambda_j \otimes K^k$ and are independent in each (MDS, at most $k - 1$ rows), so $\mathrm{rank}\, U_\Phi \ge 2 y_0 + \sum_\lambda z_\lambda - \min_\lambda z_\lambda \ge 2 y_0 + \tfrac23 \sum_\lambda z_\lambda$. From (W2): $T_3 \cup T_\lambda \subseteq \mathrm{int}(Y_0 \cup Y^\lambda)$, so $t_3 + t_\lambda \le (y_0 + z_\lambda)/2$ and $w_{\rm free} = 2 t_3 + \sum_\lambda t_\lambda \le \sum_\lambda (t_3 + t_\lambda) \le \tfrac32 y_0 + \tfrac12 \sum_\lambda z_\lambda \le \mathrm{rank}\, U_\Phi$. $\square$ The tree structure is not used: on this part of the variety the expansion of the windows does all the work, as in note 62 (its Lemma 5.3), and the theorem is as true for the window-mixed Reed-Solomon code.

**What Theorem 5.1 does not cover (open).** Triple leaves whose window direction is not marked. Let their direction classes be $T^1, \dots, T^m$ with directions $c_1, \dots, c_m$, and $b = \dim \mathfrak F$ the transcendence degree of $(c_j)$ over $K_{\rm tw}$. For a class whose direction is free (in a transcendence basis) note 74's Theorem 4.2 applies with $K_0 = \mathbb F_2(c)$ and the class costs $|W(T^j)| - 1 \ge |T^j|$ as it should whenever its windows leave it (sketched: the count of the leaves of $W(T^j)$ outside the common zeros was not completed for $d \ge 2$). For a class whose direction is algebraic over the twiddles and the other directions nothing is available: this is exactly the situation of the four block lines of a lift, whose cross-ratio is a function of the top twiddles, and of note 68's 4.4. For $d = 1$ every triple leaf is free or has a direction ($r_x \le 1$), and (W2) with $\gamma \le 1/2$ fails for every map (an arc of $m$ leaves contains $m - 1$ windows); with uniform maps it needs about six of them (note 62, Theorem E). **So the generic GS(2) for V1 is proved only on the part of the solution variety with marked directions and only for strongly expanding windows; for $d = 1$ it rests on the toy-size evidence (Proposition 4.3 (c), and researcher 78).** The honest summary of target (A): the pair statement (Theorem 2.2) for every $d$ and every window system; the exact criterion (3.1), (3.2); and the identification of the missing lemma, which is a statement about the plain tree code alone: *a plain pattern with $\mathrm{rel} > 0$ is, up to a number of rows comparable to rel, a lift* (then (3.2) follows for block-scattering maps by the count of Section 3).

**Does a larger $d$ help in the generic model?** Not through Lemma 3.1: the count (3.1) balances for every $d$ (Remark 3.4). It helps through $\beta_W$: every plain pattern loses to the boundary a fraction of its weight that tends to 1 as $d$ grows, for sets that are not closed, while rel does not depend on $d$. And $d + 1 \ge k$ makes $h_x$ a generic vector, for which GS($L$) holds for every $L$ (Brakensiek, Gopi, Makam). Between these, a threshold "GS($L$) for $d \ge d_0(L)$ and all window systems of a given expansion" is plausible and not proved.

**Check of Section 3 (ii) by exact rank (`scripts/79_adv.c`, GF($2^{64}$)).** The three-member lift at $N = 4$ (plain weight $5k/2 - 2$, kernel of dimension 1), V1 with $d = 1$ and the $n$-cycle that walks block 0, the zeros of $B$, block 1, the leftover leaf, block 2: the interior pattern has weight $5k/2 - 5$ and carries a list: 15, 35, 75 at $k = 8, 16, 32$, that is $2k - 1 + (k/2 - 4)$ as computed. With a uniformly random $n$-cycle the interior pattern of the same lift has weight 6, 20, 43 to 46 and full rank (kernel dimension exactly $2k - w$): no deficiency, as (3.2) predicts.

## 6. Target (B): the statement over GF($2^{64}$)

Here the twiddles are fixed (any plain code) and $\mu$ is uniform in $K^{dn}$. $\mathcal L$ is the set of $M$ with all differences nonzero and distinct.

**Proposition 6.1 (first moment over $\mu$, exact identity and bound; proved).** For every $M$: $\Pr_\mu[M \in S(\mu)] = \prod_x p_x(M)$, $p_x(M) = q^{-r_x(M)}$ if the conditions at $x$ are solvable and $0$ otherwise (Lemma 3.1; the leaves are independent because each has its own $\mu_x$). Since $r_x = \mathrm{rank}\,N_x(M)$ when solvable, $p_x(M) \le q^{-\kappa_x} |\Phi_x(M)|$, with equality when solvable, and
$$ \mathbb E_\mu |S(\mu) \cap \mathcal L| \;\le\; q^{-w} \sum_{M \in \mathcal L} q^{\,w_{\rm free}(M)} \;=\; q^{-w} \sum_{\varphi \in \prod_x \Lambda_x} \#\{M \in \mathcal L : M \perp U_\varphi\}, \qquad U_\varphi = \mathrm{span}\{\varphi_x \otimes g_y : y \in W_x\}. $$
A pattern that carries a list has at least $q - 1$ points of $\mathcal L$ in $S$, so $\Pr_\mu[\text{the pattern carries a list}] \le q^{Lk - w} (q-1)^{-1} \mathcal K(\Lambda)$ with $\mathcal K(\Lambda) = q^{-Lk} \sum_{M \in \mathcal L} q^{w_{\rm free}(M)} \ge 1$. The bound is lossless leaf by leaf when $\kappa_x \le d + 1$ (a generic square or wide window matrix is unsolvable or of full rank, and $q^{-\kappa_x}$ is its true price) and loses $q^{\kappa_x - d - 1}$ at a leaf with $\kappa_x \ge d + 2$ (there a generic $M$ is unsolvable, yet is counted at $q^{-(d+1)}$). A full class ($\kappa_x = L$) can always be taken out first as a shortening, exactly. *This is the precise form of the lead's "probability exactly $q^{-\mathrm{rank}}$", and the answer to "what $d$ for classes of size $c$": the local count is exact for $c \le d + 2$ and for $c = L + 1$.*

**Theorem 6.2 (conditional list bound; proved from the hypothesis).** Suppose that for the plain code and the windows at hand $\mathcal K(\Lambda) \le C^n$ for every pattern with $L + 1$ members (hypothesis KH($L$, $C$)). Then, except with probability $2^{-130}$ over $\mu$, no word of $E^n$ has $L + 1$ codewords of V1 at agreement $\tau n$ each, for every $\tau$ with
$$ 64\big((L+1)\tau - 1 - L\rho\big) \;\ge\; (L+1) H(\tau) + \log_2 C + \frac{131}{n}, \quad\text{that is}\quad \tau = \frac{1 + L\rho}{L+1} + \frac{H(\tau)}{64} + \frac{\log_2 C}{64 (L+1)} + o(1). $$
*Proof.* Cut the agreement sets to size $\lceil \tau n \rceil$: at most $2^{(L+1) H(\tau) n}$ patterns, each of weight $w \ge (L+1)\tau n - n$; the incidence rank is a rank over $K$, so words over $E$ cost nothing; sum Proposition 6.1. $\square$ At rate 1/2 with $C = 4$ (`scripts/79_tau.py`): $\tau = 0.691$ for $L = 2$ (Johnson is 0.707), $0.647$ for $L = 3$, $0.621$ for $L = 4$, $0.574$ for $L = 8$, $0.547$ for $L = 16$, that is 203, 173, 158, 136, 124 level-0 queries at 108 bits against 217; at rate 1/4: $0.461$, $0.422$, $0.351$, $0.310$ for $L = 3, 4, 8, 16$ (97, 87, 72, 64 queries against 108), and nothing for $L \le 2$. $C = 1$ instead of 4 moves $\tau$ by $0.010$ at $L = 2$ and $0.003$ at $L = 8$.

**What KH needs: the strata that remain.** $\mathcal K = \sum_\varphi q^{-\mathrm{rank}\,U_\varphi}$ over the $\varphi$ whose system has a list solution, and $\mathrm{rank}\,U_\varphi = \sum_y \psi_y(\varphi) - \mathrm{rel}(U_\varphi)$ with $\psi_y$ the dimension of the span of the $\varphi_x$ whose window contains $y$.
1. *Local part, $\mathrm{rel} = 0$* (in particular every $\varphi$ supported on $T$ with $|W(T)| \le k$, for an MDS plain code, Remark 3.5): the sum $\sum_\varphi q^{-\sum_y \psi_y(\varphi)}$ is a partition function on the window hypergraph that does not see the code. **Computed for $d = 1$ (proved):** along an arc of triple leaves ($L = 2$) it is a transfer matrix with states $\varphi \in K^2$ and weight $q^{-\dim \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle}$, which aggregates to $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & q \\ 1/q & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ on (zero, nonzero) up to $O(1/q)$, spectral radius $(3 + \sqrt 5)/2$: so $C \le 2.62$ per triple leaf and $1 + O(q^{-1/2})$ per pair leaf (matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1/q & 1 \end{pmatrix}$), against a true value of 1 (the arc's rows are always independent). The loss is the unsolvable matrices counted at $q^{-\kappa}$. **For general $d$ and $\kappa_x \le d + 1$ (sketched):** each leaf contributes $1 + q^{\kappa_x - d - 1}$ on its own, and the overlaps are controlled by the vertex boundaries: a family $\varphi_x = a_x \varphi$ on $T$ contributes $q^{\dim \bigcap_T \Lambda_x - 1 - |\partial T|}$, so one needs $|\partial T| \ge \dim \bigcap_{x \in T} \Lambda_x - 1$ for all $T$ up to a $2^{O(n)}$ count, true for arcs when $\kappa \le 2$ and for expanding windows when $\kappa \le d + 1$. I expect $C \le 4$ for $d \ge L - 1$; this is a finite computation on uniform matrices and I did not carry it out for $d \ge 2$.
2. *Global part, $\mathrm{rel}(U_\varphi) > 0$:* functional systems supported on window closures with $|W(T)| > k$ that have relations in the plain code. This is where the tree code enters, and it is the same object as in (3.2), now with a budget: a system with rel relations costs $q^{\mathrm{rel}}$, affordable up to $\mathrm{rel} \le \varepsilon' n$ at a price $\varepsilon'/(L+1)$ in $\tau$, provided the number of such $\varphi$ is at most $q^{(\text{their codimension})}$. For $L = 1$ this is the nullity excess of the set $W(T)$ and note 73's theorem bounds it by $\varepsilon_0 n$; so **the pair statement for V1 over GF($2^{64}$) is proved from note 73**: on the good event of note 73's theorem for the twiddles (rank above $\min(|Z|, k) - \varepsilon_0 n$ for every set), except with probability $2^{-130}$ over $\mu$, any windows containing their centres, two codewords of V1 agree on fewer than $(\rho + 2\varepsilon_0 + 2/64)\,n + 2$ leaves (the term of $T$ is at most $q^{k + 2\varepsilon_0 n - |\partial T|}$, there are at most $2^{|X|}$ sets $T$ and $2^n$ sets $X$). This is weaker than the bound for the plain code: mixing does not help distance in this accounting. For $L \ge 2$ nothing bounds rel: the lifts have $\mathrm{rel} \approx (L-1) n/N$ on block-aligned systems, and what is needed is that systems on window closures have few relations, for all functionals $\varphi \in K^L$, special directions included (a lift with four or more block lines has its relations only at one value of a cross-ratio: a jump of about $n/N$ on a locus of codimension one, which the sum over $\varphi$ sees at full price unless the windows scatter the blocks).
3. *The degenerate strata of the lead's Route 1, named.* (a) Leaves with $r_x < \kappa_x$ in the local regime: paid at par, item 1. (b) Free agreements on window interiors of the pairwise zero sets: bounded by $\beta_W$ against rel, item 2; they are dropped by no step above (every $\varphi$ is in the sum). (c) Parameter-dependent directions: in the sum over $\varphi$ they are ordinary terms, so over a finite field there is no separate "generic subspace" issue, but their count is what item 2 must control. (d) Closed or nearly closed sets of windows ($|\partial T| \le 1$ with more than one window through each boundary leaf): there the mixed rows are dependent with probability $q^{-(|\partial T| + 1)}$ and the *expectation* is dominated by that rare event in $\mu$ (a 2-cycle of a partner map: probability $1/q$, gain $q^{\kappa}$). Arcs of one cycle are not of this kind (their rows are bidiagonal, always independent). So for $d = 1$ the map must be one $n$-cycle (no fixed point, no short cycle; then no such set exists). For $d \ge 2$ a set is of this kind only if every leaf of its boundary lies in at least two of its windows, which uniform maps do not produce at small size (sketched, no count made); a 2-cycle of one map among $d \ge 2$ is harmless.

**Is the first moment dominated by rare coarse events? (the lead's question; proved for the events named, sketched in general).** In the plain tree code a single coincidence in the top twiddles at a scale of $N$ blocks (probability about $2^N/q$) gives a family of dimension $n/N$ vanishing on $k$ leaves, and the expected number of codewords with $k + n/N - 1$ zeros is dominated by it. In V1 the same family vanishes for free only on the interior of its $k$ leaves, about $k \rho^d$ of them for block-scattering maps, and pays one coincidence in $\mu$ for every other zero: it can reach $k \rho^d + n/N - 1$ zeros, below $k$. A top codeword with $b$ excess zero blocks (probability about $q^{-b}$) reaches the dimension only if $(\rho + b/N)^{d+1} \ge \rho - 1/N$: at rate 1/2 and $d = 1$, $b \ge 0.2 N$, probability $q^{-0.2N}$, which is negligible from $N = 8$ on once the levels up to $N = 4$ are fixed by a checked condition (at $N = 4$, $b = 2$: three equal top twiddles, probability $2^{-126}$, not negligible). So for V1 the coarse events of the twiddles are harmless one at a time, which the plain code is not; what the first moment over $\mu$ sees instead is item 2, a property the twiddles must have with probability $1 - 2^{-130}$, and the rare events of $\mu$ of item 3 (d).

**Route 2 (level by level).** The mixing lives at the leaves only, so an induction on the levels is an induction for the plain code, and its target is item 2: a bound on the relations of multi-functional systems on window closures. Its $L = 1$ case is note 73. For $L \ge 2$ the invariant cannot be "every system has few relations" (false: lifts); it has to be restricted to systems whose supports are not block-aligned at any scale, and the natural induction step does not preserve that restriction (a scattered set of leaves rounds up to an arbitrary set of parents). I see no proof.

**Largest $L$ as a function of $d$.** With the local count of Proposition 6.1: classes up to $d + 2$, so $L \le d + 1$ ($d = 1$: three members, $\tau \approx 0.69$ at rate 1/2, barely below Johnson; $d = 3$: $L = 4$, $\tau \approx 0.62$; $d = 7$: $L = 8$, $\tau \approx 0.57$), conditional on KH. Whether smaller $d$ suffices for larger $L$ is not decided by anything here: the generic count (3.1) balances for every $d$, and the loss for $\kappa_x \ge d + 2$ is a loss of the bound, not a list.

## 9. Self-referee on the four known failure modes, and what to do next

1. *Free agreements dropped.* They are the central object here: Lemma 2.1 shows that relations are made of them, (3.1) carries $w_{\rm free}$ explicitly, and the sum of Proposition 6.1 runs over all free functionals. No step discards a leaf whose window lies in a zero set.
2. *First moments over tuples whose structure changes the probability.* Proposition 6.1 uses the exact probability $q^{-r_x(M)}$ of each $M$; the structure of $M$ enters through $w_{\rm free}(M)$ and is what KH has to bound. Nothing is proved about KH for $L \ge 2$.
3. *Dependent events multiplied as if independent.* The only product taken is over leaves for a fixed $M$ and uniform $\mu$, where independence is exact (each leaf has its own $\mu_x$). No product is taken over the twiddles, and none over $M$.
4. *Generic subspaces that depend on the parameters.* Theorem 2.2 uses note 74's Theorem 4.2 only with the functionals $\lambda_x$ of the pattern, which are constants. Theorem 5.1 uses only MDS, for constant (marked) functionals. Every place where a functional depends on the twiddles or on $\mu$ (unmarked window directions, Section 5; special $\varphi$ in item 2 of Section 6) is listed as open, not argued.

Weak points a referee should look at first: the proof of Proposition 3.2 (uniqueness of the dominating component and the fibre bound); the claim that (3.1) with a full profile is the right inequality when $\kappa_x \ge d + 2$ (Remark 3.4); the sketched parts of Section 3 on sub-patterns of lifts.

**Next steps (suggestions).** (i) For researcher 78: measure (3.2) directly, that is $\mathrm{rel}(\Psi)$ against $\beta_W(\Psi)$ for the interior patterns of every deficient plain pattern of notes 69 and 74 and of their sub-patterns, for $d = 1$ uniform maps at $(8,16)$ to $(32, 64)$; and search for a three-member violation in V1 whose triple leaves have unmarked window directions (none exists at $(4,8)$). (ii) The missing lemma on the plain tree code: a pattern with rel relations contains a lift pattern up to $O(\mathrm{rel})$ rows; note 74's kernel classification is the evidence. (iii) The constant $C$ of the local part for $d = 2, 3$: a transfer-matrix or small-field enumeration. (iv) A design decision that the theory already supports: partner maps must be chosen for their block-to-block statistics at every scale $N \le 64$ and for the absence of closed sets, not merely "from the oracle"; a uniformly random permutation has both properties with overwhelming probability only for the first, and has fixed points and 2-cycles with constant probability, which at $d = 1$ are closed sets.
