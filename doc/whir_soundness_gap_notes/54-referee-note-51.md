## 2. Lemma B of note 40

### 2.1 The statement that is true

**Lemma B$'$ [proved].** Let $E \in F[X, Y]$ with $a = E_Y \ne 0$ and $c = E_X \ne 0$ having no common factor in $F[X, Y]$. This holds when $\hat G = c + aY_1$ is irreducible in $F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$, in particular when $G$ is absolutely irreducible and primitive (note 52, Section 2.3 (a)). Let the received points $p_\alpha$ have pairwise distinct $\alpha$ and contact orders $o_\alpha = 2j_\alpha$. Then the intersection number of $a = 0$ and $c = 0$ at $p_\alpha$ is at least $o_\alpha^2 - o_\alpha - 1$ when $o_\alpha \ge 2$, and
$$\sum_{\alpha :\ o_\alpha \ge 2}(o_\alpha^2 - o_\alpha - 1) \ \le\ \deg_X\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c) \ \le\ D_c(W - \Delta) + D_aW - \Delta D_aD_c .$$
The right side is nondecreasing in $D_c$ as soon as $W \ge (D_a + 1)\Delta$, which always holds, so $D_c$ may be replaced by an upper bound: with $D_a = 2K$, $(2K + D_c)W \ge \Delta D_c(1 + 2K) + \sum_j n_j\beta_j$, the form (B) of note 51 ($5W - 9\Delta \ge \sum$ for a cubic).

### 2.2 The proof, line by line

1. *No common factor.* A common factor $h \in F[X, Y]$ of $a$ and $c$ divides $\hat G$, which is irreducible of $Y_1$-degree 1, so $h$ is a unit. Note 40 and note 51 (step (i)) prove less: coprimality in $F(X)[Y]$, from irreducibility over $F(X)$. That is enough for $\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c) \ne 0$ and not enough for step 4.
2. *Degree.* $\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c)$ is homogeneous of degree $D_c$ in the coefficients $a_k$ and $D_a$ in the $c_k$, and isobaric: if $a_k, c_k$ have weight $k$, every term has weight $D_aD_c$ (check: $\mathrm{Res}(a_1Y + a_0, c_1Y + c_0) = a_1c_0 - a_0c_1$). With $\deg a_k \le W - \Delta - k\Delta$ and $\deg c_k \le W - k\Delta$ every term has degree at most $D_c(W - \Delta) + D_aW - \Delta D_aD_c$. Correct. **[checked]** the bound is attained in most computed instances (for instance $214 \le 214$, $428 \le 428$).
3. *Local number.* By Lemma N, $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j - 1}$. Then every monomial $T^rS^q$ of $E$ with $r + q < 2j$ has both exponents even (a monomial with $r$ odd gives the monomial $T^{r-1}S^q$ of $c$, of degree below $2j - 1$; likewise for $q$ odd). With $E_d$ the part of degree $d$: $a_{2j-1} = \partial_SE_{2j}$, $c_{2j-1} = \partial_TE_{2j}$, and Euler in characteristic 2 gives $Tc_{2j-1} + Sa_{2j-1} = 2j\,E_{2j} = 0$, so $a_{2j-1} = T\gamma$, $c_{2j-1} = S\gamma$ with $\gamma$ a form of degree $2j - 2$; and $Tc + Sa = \sum_{d\ \mathrm{odd}}E_d \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j+1}$ because the odd parts of degree below $2j$ vanish. If $\gamma = 0$, both multiplicities are at least $2j$ and the number is at least $4j^2$. If $\gamma \ne 0$, both multiplicities are exactly $2j - 1$, so after $S = Ts$ the quotients $\tilde a = a/T^{2j-1}$, $\tilde c = c/T^{2j-1}$ are the strict transforms, $\tilde a|_{T=0} = \gamma(1, s) \not\equiv 0$, $\tilde c + s\tilde a = T\Phi$, and Noether's formula gives $I_{p_\alpha}(a, c) = (2j - 1)^2 + \sum_q I_q(\tilde a, \tilde c)$ with $I_q(\tilde a, \tilde c) = I_q(\tilde a, T\Phi) \ge I_q(\tilde a, T) = \mathrm{ord}_q\gamma(1, s)$. In the other chart ($T = St$) the same with $\tilde a' + t\tilde c' \in (S)$ covers $s = \infty$. The form $\gamma$ has $2j - 2$ zeros on the exceptional line, so $I \ge (2j - 1)^2 + 2j - 2 = o^2 - o - 1$. Correct. Noether's formula needs that $a$ and $c$ have no common component through $p_\alpha$: step 1.
4. *From intersection numbers to the resultant.* For $a, c$ without common factor in $F[X, Y]$, the order of $\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c)$ at $X = \alpha$ is the total intersection number of the two curves, homogenized in $Y$, along the fibre of $\mathbb{A}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ over $\alpha$ (standard: the valuation of the resultant of two binary forms over a discrete valuation ring is the length of their common zero scheme). It is therefore at least the sum of the affine intersection numbers above $\alpha$, with equality when one leading coefficient does not vanish at $\alpha$. This is the step that fails when $X - \alpha$ divides both $a$ and $c$: the intersection numbers on that fibre are infinite and the resultant is not zero.

### 2.3 The hypothesis cannot be dropped

**Counterexample [proved; computed by `54_lemmaB_thm4.py`, first lines of the output].** $E = X^3Y$: $a = X^3$, $c = X^2Y$, coprime in $F(X)[Y]$, $\hat G = X^2(Y_0 + XY_1)$. At the point $(0, 0)$ both lie in $\mathfrak{m}^3$, the contact order is 4, $o^2 - o - 1 = 11$; $D_a = 0$, $D_c = 1$, $W = \Delta + 3$, $\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c) = X^3$, and the right side of the lemma is $D_c(W - \Delta) = 3$. More generally, multiplying a first integral by $(\tau - \alpha^2)^e$ raises $j_\alpha$ by $e$ and $W$ by $2e$: the left side grows like $4e^2$ and the right side like $2e(D_a + D_c)$.

This is not an artificial case for the linear algebra that all the numerical checks of notes 40, 45, 51 use: when the imposed contact at a point exceeds what the shape allows (Lemma M$'$ below), the solutions of smallest $W$ carry the factor $(X - \alpha)^2$. **[checked]** of 215 potentials, 79 have content, and for 29 of those the inequality of Lemma B fails before the content is divided out (for instance a cubic at $n = 8$, $\Delta = 4$ with contact 6 imposed on two lines: $W = 22$, $\sum = 83 > 74$; after division $W = 18$, the two lines have contact 4, $\sum = 47 \le 54$). After division there is no violation. The checks of note 40 (`40_bezout.py`: contact 4 and 6 on quintics) were inside the allowed range, which is why they did not see this.

**Use in note 51.** The survivor $G^\star$ of Theorem S is an irreducible element of $F[\tau, u, v]$, hence primitive, and its profile is the profile of that primitive polynomial. So (B) applies to it. Theorem 2.1 is not affected.

### 2.4 The last sentence of the lemma in note 40

"For $W < 6\Delta$ the middle term is at most $9W - 25\Delta$" is the value at $(D_a, D_c) = (4, 5)$, and the middle term is monotone in $D_a$ only when $W \ge \Delta D_c$: the sentence needs $W \ge 5\Delta$. **Counterexample [checked, instance C5]:** the flat word $\alpha^{n/2 - 1}$ of note 35 on a subspace, $n = 32$, $\Delta = 7$: the primitive cubic has $W = 22 < 6\Delta$, contact 2 on 31 lines, $\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c)$ of degree exactly 31 (it is $\prod_{\alpha \ne 0}(X - \alpha)$ up to a constant), and $9W - 25\Delta = 23 < 31$; the correct bound is $5W - 9\Delta = 47$. Nine potentials of my suite violate the sentence. No use of it that I found is affected: note 40 applies it to contact 4 on all lines at rate 1/2, where the bound on $W$ implied by the correct inequality, $(11n + \Delta D_c(1 + D_a))/(D_a + D_c)$, is smallest at $(4, 5)$; note 45 applies it to quintics with $K = 2$; note 51 does not use it.

## 3. Proposition 1 and Theorem 4 of note 45, the extension, and Lemma M$'$

### 3.1 The proofs

**Proposition 1 [proved].** Contact $\ge 2j$ iff $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j-1}$: one direction is Lemma N; conversely $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}^{2j-1}$ gives $E \in F[T^2, S^2] + \mathfrak{m}^{2j}$ (step 3 above), $\partial$ kills $F[T^2, S^2]$, $\mathfrak{m}^{2j}[Y_1] \subseteq \mathfrak{c}_{2j}$ (a monomial $T^rS^q$ becomes $\sum_p\binom qpT^{r+p}w^{q-p}Y_1^p$, of order $r + 2q - p \ge r + q$), $\partial$ lowers the order by at most one ($\partial T = 1$, $\partial w = 0$), and a first integral of order $\ge 2j - 1$ has order $\ge 2j$ (note 35, Proposition 2). So the criterion of note 40 holds for every $j$, not only $j \le 3$. Then $a = (\mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B})^2 + T\mathcal{B}^2$ and $c = (\mathcal{C} + \zeta_\alpha\mathcal{B})^2 + S\mathcal{B}^2$, the two terms have orders of different parity, and orders add in the local ring. Correct, and it does not need $E$ to be a quintic.

**Theorem 4 and its extension [proved].** Degree: $\deg\mathcal{A}_k \le (W - (2k + 1)\Delta)/2$, $\deg\mathcal{B}_k \le (W - 1 - (2k + 1)\Delta)/2$, $\deg\mathcal{C}_k \le (W - 2k\Delta)/2$ (in the coefficient of $Y^{2k}$ of $a$ the terms $\mathcal{A}_k^2$ and $X\mathcal{B}_k^2$ have degrees of different parity, so neither cancels), and each term of the determinant takes one entry per row and per column: $(3W - 8\Delta - 1)/2$. Local orders: adding $\xi_\alpha$, $\zeta_\alpha$ times the row of $\mathcal{B}$ to the two other rows and replacing the column of $1$ by its combination with the columns of $Y, Y^2$ that passes from $(1, Y, Y^2)$ to $(1, S, S^2)$ does not change the determinant; a conic $q_0 + q_1S + q_2S^2$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}^k$ iff $\mathrm{ord}_Tq_i \ge k - i$; so the entries have orders at least $(j - i)_+$, $(j - 1 - i)_+$, $(j - i)_+$ in column $i$, and for $j \ge 2$ every term has order at least $3j - 1 - 3 = 3j - 4$. Correct as written in note 51, for every $j \ge 2$, and it is a statement about any quintic potential, primitive or not.

### 3.3 Lemma M$'$: contact orders of a primitive first integral with $b = 1$

**Lemma M$'$ [proved here; checked].** Let $G$ be a primitive first integral with $b = 1$ and contact order $2j \ge 2$ on the line of $\alpha$. Then $j \le K$ or $2j - 1 \le D_c$; that is, $j_\alpha \le \max(K, \lfloor (D_c + 1)/2 \rfloor)$. Moreover if $j_\alpha > K$ then $X - \alpha$ divides $E_Y$ and $D_c \ge 2j_\alpha - 1$.

*Proof.* $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j-1}$ (Lemma N). Restricted to the fibre $X = \alpha$, $a(\alpha, Y)$ vanishes at $y_\alpha$ to order at least $2j - 1$, hence at least $2j$ because it is a polynomial in $(Y - y_\alpha)^2$; so $a(\alpha, Y) = 0$ if $2j > 2K$. Likewise $c(\alpha, Y) = 0$ if $2j - 1 > D_c$. If both vanish, $X - \alpha$ divides $\hat G$, so $\tau - \alpha^2$ divides $G$. $\square$

### 4.3 Convexity, and what "closed" means

**[proved]** For one pair $(m, B)$ and one linear bound $\ell$, $g_{m,B,\ell}(x) = \sum_j x_j\psi(j) - \Phi(n\ell(x))/n$ is convex: the first term is linear and $\Phi(W) = N(B, mA) - N(B - 1, mA - W)$, extended to real $W$ with the positive parts, is concave and nondecreasing (it stays so when $n\ell(x)$ exceeds $mA$ or $W_{\max}$, where it is constant or simply not attained by any first integral). So the set where this pair and this bound certify the cut, $\{g < 0\}$, is convex, and a cell lies in it iff its vertices do. The union of these sets over pairs and bounds is not convex, and "not cut" is not a convex condition: this is why a covering is needed, and the only correct rule is that a cell is closed when one pair and one bound give $g < 0$ at all its vertices. For an actual first integral with profile in such a cell, $\Phi(W) \ge \Phi(n\ell(x)) > \sum_j n_j\psi(j)$ because $W \ge n\ell(x)$, and the side condition of Lemma C holds because it is imposed at $W_{\max} \ge W$.

**The script does exactly this.** In `51_profiles.py`: `try_cell` ranks the pairs (candidate, bound) by the value of $g$ at the centre of the cell and accepts a pair only if `all(self.g(self.cands[ci], self.bounds[bi], p) < 0 for p in profs)` over all vertices, one candidate and one bound at a time; candidates are kept only if `D * (m + B) <= m * A - Wmax`; `vertices` returns the corners of the dyadic box inside the simplex and the intersections of the box edges with the hyperplane $\sum_{j \ge 1}x_j = 1$, which are all the vertices of the cell, with integer coordinates in units of $n/256$; `g` is evaluated with exact rationals; `bounds_for` is the list of Section 2.1 of the note; the cells left open are those of side $1/256$ for which none of the 12 best pairs works, so the open region is an outer approximation; `window` takes each bound at its worst corner and the credit $\sum n_j\psi(j)$ at its largest corner. Nothing weaker than the rule above is used. **[computed]** Rerun: identical output to `51_results.txt` for $a = 0.7065$ at the four sizes and for $a = 0.705$ at $n = 2^{25}$ (107 s per run at $0.7065$).
