## 0. Summary

**What is proved about generic lists, the best upper bound, and the frontier.** Proved for the generic tree code: (i) with one pairwise difference kept at each leaf, the incidence rows of any list of distinct codewords are linearly independent (Theorem 4.2, by induction on the levels with a derivative lemma), so any $L + 1$ distinct codewords agree pairwise on at most $Lk - 1$ leaves in all, and every pattern has weight at most $Lk - 1 + \sum_x (c_x - 2)_+$: the whole excess over the generalized Singleton bound sits at leaves where three or more members agree; (ii) for three members the largest weight is exactly $5k/2 - 2$ (Theorem 4.4), so note 69's three codewords at agreement $3n/4 - 1$ are optimal at rate 1/2; (iii) for more members a linear-programming bound ($13k/3$ for four members, $6.42 k$ for five), against $(3L - 1)k/2 - L$ reached by lifts when $n \ge Lk$; (iv) for any MDS code the excess plus the kernel dimension minus one is exactly the number of linear relations between dual codewords supported on the agreement sets (Proposition 4.1). **Best upper bound on list sizes: none below the Johnson radius.** A weight bound uniform in the rate cannot give one below agreement $3\rho/2$ (proved); the conjecture that fits everything known is at most $\binom{N-1}{\rho N} + nN$ codewords at agreement $\rho + 1/N$ ($\log_2 \Lambda \approx H(\rho)/\varepsilon$ at $\varepsilon = 1/N$). **The lower bounds move up** (one-sided staircase, Proposition 6.1, proved and checked by encoding up to $n = 256$): all zeros of the lower factor in one block give $\binom{N-1}{\rho N}$ codewords at agreement $\rho + 1/N - 1/n$, twice the distance from capacity of note 69's balanced staircase. ## 1. Setup and notation

Tree code as in notes 68 and 69: $k = 2^v$, $n = 2^{v+r}$, leaf $j$ has row $g_j = \bigotimes_{i<v}(1, x_i(j))$, $x_i(j) = t_i(p_i(j), j_i)$, variant T2 (every node has its own twiddle). "Generic" means: the twiddles are algebraically independent over the prime field. A list is $L+1$ distinct messages $m_0, \dots, m_L$; $d_l = m_l - m_0$; $M \in K^{L \times k}$ has rows $d_1, \dots, d_L$. For a leaf $x$, $\delta_x = M g_x^T \in K^L$ is the vector of the values of the differences at $x$. A pattern gives each leaf a class $C_x \subseteq \{0..L\}$, empty or of size $c_x \ge 2$; $\Lambda_x \subseteq K^L$ is the span of $e_l - e_{l'}$, $l, l' \in C_x$ ($e_0 = 0$), of dimension $\kappa_x = c_x - 1$ (0 for an empty class). The incidence system is $\lambda^T M g_x = 0$ for all $x$ and $\lambda \in \Lambda_x$; its row space is $R = \sum_x \Lambda_x \otimes g_x \subseteq K^L \otimes K^k$, the weight is $w = \sum_x \kappa_x$, the kernel has dimension $Lk - \dim R$. A pattern *carries a list* if its kernel has a point with $0, d_1, \dots, d_L$ pairwise distinct.

One level: split the message by the bottom variable, $M = (M_0, M_1)$ with $M_b \in K^{L \times k/2}$. A leaf $x = (p, b)$ under the pair $p$ has $g_x = g_p \otimes (1, t_x)$, $g_p$ the row of the tree code of the upper $v - 1$ layers at $p$ and $t_x$ the leaf's own bottom twiddle, so $\delta_x = M_0 g_p + t_x M_1 g_p$.

## 2. Task 1: the one-level statements (items 2 and 3 of the brief)

Throughout this section $K_1$ is the field generated by the twiddles of layers $\ge 1$, the bottom twiddles $t_x$ (one per leaf, T2) are algebraically independent over $K_1$, $F = K_1(t)$, $V = K^L \otimes K^{k/2}$, $U_x = \Lambda_x \otimes g_p \subseteq V$ (dimension $\kappa_x$, defined over $K_1$), $z_x = (1, t_x)$, so that $R = \sum_x U_x \otimes z_x \subseteq V \otimes F^2$ and a solution is a pair $M = (M_0, M_1)$ with $\langle M_0, u \rangle + t_x \langle M_1, u \rangle = 0$ for $u \in U_x$.

**Lemma 2.1 (item 2; proved).** Let $M$ be any solution (any twiddles, generic or not) and $x = (p, b)$ a leaf. The map $\phi_x : \Lambda_x \to K^2$, $\lambda \mapsto (\lambda^T M_0 g_p, \lambda^T M_1 g_p)$ has image in the line $\{(a, b) : a + t_x b = 0\}$. Hence $\Lambda_x^M := \ker \phi_x$ is $\Lambda_x$ or a hyperplane of it; in the second case $t_x = -\lambda^T M_0 g_p / \lambda^T M_1 g_p$ for every $\lambda \in \Lambda_x \setminus \Lambda_x^M$, a function of $M$ and of the upper twiddles only. *Proof.* $\lambda^T \delta_x = \lambda^T M_0 g_p + t_x \lambda^T M_1 g_p = 0$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda_x$; a linear map into a line has a kernel of codimension at most one. $\square$ The statement is correct as the lead wrote it. Note that it is a statement about one solution: for a space $\mathcal S$ of solutions the common kernel $\bigcap_{M \in \mathcal S} \Lambda_x^M$ can have any codimension up to $\min(\kappa_x, \dim \mathcal S)$.

**Lemma 2.2 (the easy half of item 3; proved, for any twiddles).** For every choice of subspaces $\Lambda'_x \subseteq \Lambda_x$ (over any extension field, depending on anything), with $\kappa'_x = \dim \Lambda'_x$ and $U' = \sum_p (\Lambda'_{p0} + \Lambda'_{p1}) \otimes g_p$: $\dim R \le \Phi(\Lambda') := 2 \dim U' + \sum_x (\kappa_x - \kappa'_x)$. *Proof.* $R \subseteq U' \otimes F^2 + \sum_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ with $Q_x$ a complement of $\Lambda'_x \otimes g_p$ in $U_x$. $\square$ Call $\Lambda'$ *exact* if equality holds. $\Phi$ is submodular on the product of the subspace lattices ($\dim \sum_x (A_x + B_x) + \dim \sum_x (A_x \cap B_x) \le \dim \sum A_x + \dim \sum B_x$), so the exact tuples form a sublattice and there is a least one.

**Lemma 2.3 (derivative lemma; proved, any characteristic).** If $\sum_x u_x \otimes z_x = 0$ with $u_x \in U_x \otimes F$, then for every leaf $j$: $u_j \otimes (0, 1) = -\sum_x \partial_j u_x \otimes z_x \in R$, where $\partial_j$ is the formal derivative in $t_j$ and $\partial_j u_x \in U_x \otimes F$ because $U_x$ is defined over $K_1$. So every solution kills $u_j$ in both slices: $M_0 u_j = M_1 u_j = 0$. With Hasse derivatives $D_j^{(a)}$ in place of $\partial_j$ the same computation gives $D_j^{(a)} u_j \otimes (0,1) \in R$ for all $a \ge 0$, and $(D_i u_j + D_j u_i) \otimes (0,1) \in R$ for $i \ne j$. *Proof.* Clear denominators and differentiate the identity; $\partial_j z_x$ is $(0,1)$ for $x = j$ and $0$ otherwise. $\square$

**Proposition 2.4 (exactness criterion; proved).** Let $C_x \subseteq U_x \otimes F$ be the space of $x$-components of relations (the projection of $\ker(\bigoplus_x U_x \to V \otimes F^2)$). (a) Every exact tuple (over any extension of $F$) contains $C_x$ for all $x$, and $U' \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$ for it. (b) Let $\tilde C_x$ be the smallest subspace of $U_x$ defined over $K_1$ whose extension contains $C_x$ (the $K_1$-hull; it is spanned by the values of $C_x$ at sufficiently many specializations of $t$). If $(\sum_x \tilde C_x) \otimes F^2 = \sum_x \tilde C_x \otimes z_x$ (condition $\star$), then $\tilde C$ is exact and it is the least exact tuple, so the recursion of item 3 holds with subspaces that are functions of the upper twiddles only. (c) If any exact tuple exists, the least one is defined over the perfect closure of $K_1$ (it does not depend on the bottom twiddles) and contains $\tilde C$; it can be strictly larger than $\tilde C$ (instance below). *Proof.* (a) If $\Lambda'$ is exact the sum $U' \otimes F^2 \oplus \bigoplus_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ is direct and equal to $R$; a relation $\sum u_x \otimes z_x = 0$ projects to a relation of the $Q_x$ parts modulo $U' \otimes F^2$, which is trivial, so $u_x \in \Lambda'_x \otimes g_p$. (b) Under $\star$, a relation of the quotients $U_x / \tilde C_x$ modulo $(\sum \tilde C) \otimes F^2$ lifts to a relation of the $U_x$, whose components lie in $C_x \subseteq \tilde C_x$. (c) $\Phi$ does not involve $t$, and the least minimizer of a submodular function on a lattice is unique, hence fixed by every automorphism of an algebraic closure of $F$ over $K_1$; with (a) it contains $C_x$, hence its hull. $\square$

**Theorem 2.5 (item 3, hard half: proved in two cases, FALSE for arbitrary subspaces, exact at toy size for patterns).** The identity $\dim R = \min_{\Lambda'} \Phi(\Lambda')$:

- **proved** when $\kappa_x \le 1$ for every leaf (two-member classes only, or $L = 1$): this is the matroid union theorem, and Lemma 2.3 proves it directly (take $\Lambda'_x = \Lambda_x$ on the leaves in the support of some relation and $0$ elsewhere; then $\star$ is Lemma 2.3);
- **proved** when at most two leaves have $\kappa_x \ge 2$ (in the dual form $N = \ker \sigma$, $S = N \cap TN$: a functional $\psi \perp N + TN$ vanishes on every one-dimensional component in the support of $S$ by Lemma 2.3, and two remaining components are separated by the two equations $\psi(n) = \psi(Tn) = 0$; induction on the dimension after restricting to the hull);
- **false for arbitrary subspaces $U_x$ in place of $\Lambda_x \otimes g_p$** (exact computation, `scripts/74_star.c`, `74_star_one.py`, `74_segre.py`). Counterexample: five planes $U_1, \dots, U_5$ of $K^5$ whose Pluecker vectors are linearly dependent and which are otherwise in general position (Segre's five associated lines of $\mathbb P^4$: every plane meeting four of them meets the fifth). Then $\sum_i U_i \otimes (1, t_i)$ has rank 9 for generic $t$ (20 of 20 random constructions over GF($2^{64}$), against rank 10 with a random fifth plane), the single relation has components that move with $t$ and whose hulls are the whole $U_i$, so by Proposition 2.4 (a) and (c) no tuple is exact (hulls computed on the instance found over $\mathbb F_2$; for it $\min \Phi = 10$). This is a square system with identically vanishing determinant and no shrunk subspace (commutative rank below non-commutative rank). It was found by a random search over subspaces of $\mathbb F_2^N$: 3 instances in 800000 where $\Phi(\tilde C)$ exceeds the rank; in each the sub-system formed by the hulls has rank one below $\Phi$ of every tuple containing them. The first is the five associated planes (Pluecker rank 4, checked); the other two have five subspaces of dimensions $(4,3,2,3,2)$ in $K^7$ and $(3,2,3,3,2)$ in $K^6$ and were not analysed.
- **exact at the stated sizes for patterns**: on every pattern of the table below the hull $\tilde C$ is exact. No pattern realizing a Segre-type configuration was found; none can occur for three members at one level from triple leaves alone, because the planes $K^2 \otimes g_p$ decouple into two copies of the MDS system. Whether one occurs at larger size is **open**.

**Correction to the brief.** For $\kappa_x \ge 2$ the statement is not a consequence of matroid union or of Lovasz's theorem as I can reconstruct it: the $\kappa_x$ functionals of a leaf share one twiddle. The exact dictionary is: $\dim R$ is the commutative rank of the linear matrix $[\sigma; \sigma T]$ ($\sigma : \bigoplus_x U_x \to V$ the sum, $T$ the multiplication by $t_x$ on $U_x$); $\min \Phi$ is the bound from graded shrunk subspaces; with $N = \ker \sigma$ the claim reads $\dim (N \cap TN) = \max_{X' \text{ graded}} (2 \dim (N \cap X') - \dim X')$; and $N \cap TN = \mathrm{Hom}(P_t, \rho)$ for the general representation $P_t$ of dimension $(2; 1, \dots, 1)$ of the star quiver and the fixed representation $\rho = (N \to U_x)_x$. Lemma 2.3 is Schofield's vanishing of $\mathrm{Ext}(P,P) \to \mathrm{Ext}(P, \rho)$ on the image of a general homomorphism, valid in characteristic 2 because the solutions are rational in $t$. Schofield's formula needs both representations general; here $\rho$ is special, and Segre's configuration shows that it matters.

**Why the recursion does not iterate (proved obstruction).** The pushed-up system $\sum_p (\Lambda'_{p0} + \Lambda'_{p1}) \otimes g_p$ has subspaces that depend on the twiddles of layer 1 and above (in note 69's smallest example $\Lambda'_x$ at the triple leaf is the line $\lambda$ with $\lambda \otimes g_0 \in \langle e_1 \otimes g_1, e_2 \otimes g_2, (e_1 + e_2) \otimes g_3 \rangle$). Lemma 2.3 needs subspaces independent of the twiddles one differentiates, so the same theorem cannot be applied one level up: item 3 is a one-level identity, not a recursion with a closed class of subspaces. This is note 68's 4.4 seen from the other side.

## 3. Task 2: classification of the kernels of the deficient orbits

**Definition used (so that "spanned by lifts" is a computable statement).** Fix a split level $i \in \{1, \dots, v\}$, $k_{\rm up} = k/2^i$, and write a leaf's row as $g_x = g_{q(x)} \otimes \beta_x$ with $\beta_x \in K^{2^i}$ the row of the bottom code and $q(x)$ its block. A *lifted solution at level $i$* is a kernel element $M = A \otimes B$, $A \in K^{L \times k_{\rm up}}$, $B \in K^{2^i}$. **Lemma 3.1 (proved).** $A \otimes B$ is a solution iff for every leaf $x$ either $B(x) := \beta_x \cdot B = 0$ or $\lambda^T A g_{q(x)} = 0$ for all $\lambda \in \Lambda_x$. For generic twiddles the bottom code is MDS of dimension $2^i$, so $B \ne 0$ has a zero set $Z$ of at most $2^i - 1$ leaves, and the lifted solutions at level $i$ are exactly $\bigcup_Z \mathcal A_i(Z) \otimes W_Z$, where $W_Z = \{B : B|_Z = 0\}$ and $\mathcal A_i(Z)$ is the kernel of the top system with block constraints $\Lambda^{\rm up}_q = \sum_{x \in q \setminus Z} \Lambda_x$. Their span is $\mathcal S^{\rm lift}_i = \sum_{|Z| = 2^i - 1} \mathcal A_i(Z) \otimes B_Z$ (because $W_{Z'}$ is spanned by the $B_Z$, $Z \supseteq Z'$, and $\mathcal A_i(Z') \subseteq \mathcal A_i(Z)$). Level $i = v$ is the trivial top code: all differences are multiples of one codeword with $k - 1$ zeros; such solutions exist for every MDS code. So the test is finite: enumerate $Z$, solve the top system, and compare $\mathcal S^{\rm lift} = \sum_{i=1}^{v} \mathcal S^{\rm lift}_i$ with the kernel $\mathcal S$. (`scripts/74_classify.c`.)

## 4. Task 3: upper bounds on the weight of a generic list

"Generic" in this section: all twiddles algebraically independent (T2). A space $\mathcal S$ of solutions is *genuine for a vector $\lambda \in K^L$* if some $M \in \mathcal S$ has $\lambda^T M \ne 0$; a list of distinct codewords is genuine for every difference $e_a - e_b$.

**Proposition 4.1 (duality; proved, for any MDS code).** Let $S_l$ be the set of leaves whose class contains $l$ ($a_l = |S_l|$), $S = \bigcup_l S_l$, and $D_l = C^\perp(S_l)$ the dual codewords supported in $S_l$ ($\dim D_l = (a_l - k)_+$). The relations among the rows of the incidence system are the tuples $(\mu_0, \dots, \mu_L)$, $\mu_l \in D_l$, $\sum_l \mu_l = 0$. Hence $\mathrm{rel} = \sum_l \dim D_l - \dim \sum_l D_l$, the kernel has dimension $s = Lk - w + \mathrm{rel}$ (equal to $\dim C^\perp(S) / \sum_l C^\perp(S_l)$ when all $a_l \ge k$), and the excess over the generalized Singleton bound is exactly $E := w - (Lk - 1) = \mathrm{rel} - (s - 1)$. *Proof.* A relation is a family $\nu_x \in K^{C_x}$ with zero sum, $\sum_x \nu_{x,l}\, g_x = 0$ for each $l \ge 1$; put $\mu_l(x) = \nu_{x,l}$. $\square$ So "$E$ expressed by the structure" has an exact answer: $E + s - 1$ is the number of independent linear relations between dual codewords supported on the agreement sets. For the one-sided staircase $D_S = \alpha^S \otimes B^\perp$ with $\alpha^S$ the dual codeword of the top code on $S \cup \{q_0\}$: all block moments at $q_0$ lie in one hyperplane.

**Theorem 4.2 (one functional per leaf; proved).** Let $X$ be a set of leaves and $\lambda_x \in K_0^L \setminus 0$, $x \in X$, vectors with entries in a field $K_0$ over which the twiddles are algebraically independent (for instance differences $e_a - e_b$). Let $\mathcal S$ be the solution space of $\{\lambda_x^T M g_x = 0\}_{x \in X}$ in the generic tree code (any $v \ge 0$, any number of trees). If $\mathcal S$ is genuine for every $\lambda_x$, then the rows $\lambda_x \otimes g_x$ are linearly independent; in particular $|X| = Lk - \dim \mathcal S \le Lk - 1$.

*Proof.* Induction on $v$. For $v = 0$ every row is $\lambda_x \otimes 1$, so $\lambda_x^T M = 0$ on $\mathcal S$ and genuineness forces $X = \emptyset$. Let $v \ge 1$ and suppose the rows are dependent. Let $T \ne \emptyset$ be the union of the supports of the relations. The spaces $U_x = \langle \lambda_x \otimes g_p \rangle$ do not depend on the bottom twiddles, so Lemma 2.3 applies: for a relation with $u_x \ne 0$, $u_x \otimes (0,1)$ is a combination of the rows of its support. As $U_x$ is a line, $U_x \otimes K^2 \subseteq R_T := \sum_{y \in T} U_y \otimes z_y$ for every $x \in T$, hence $R_T = U_T \otimes K^2$ with $U_T = \sum_{x \in T} U_x$, and $|T| > \dim R_T = 2 \dim U_T$ because the rows of $T$ are dependent. Every $M \in \mathcal S$ therefore has both slices $M_0, M_1$ orthogonal to $U_T$. Choose for each pair $p$ meeting $T$ one leaf $x(p) \in T \cap p$ and put $\lambda_p = \lambda_{x(p)}$. The upper system $\{\lambda_p^T N g_p = 0\}$ lives on the tree code of the upper $v - 1$ layers, whose twiddles are independent over $K_0$; its solution space contains the slices of all $M \in \mathcal S$, so it is genuine for every $\lambda_p$ ($\lambda^T M \ne 0$ implies $\lambda^T M_0 \ne 0$ or $\lambda^T M_1 \ne 0$); and it has at least $|T|/2 > \dim U_T \ge \dim \sum_p \langle \lambda_p \otimes g_p \rangle$ rows, so they are dependent. This contradicts the induction hypothesis. $\square$

## 5. The shape of generic lists, and the conjectured list bound

**Column-space tree (the lead's item 4; proved facts).** For a solution $M$ and a node $q$ at level $i$ let $M_q = M (g_q \otimes I) \in K^{L \times 2^i}$ be the partial evaluation of the differences at $q$ and $C_q$ its column space, the span of the $\delta_x$, $x$ under $q$. (a) $C_q = C_{q0} + C_{q1}$. (b) The agreement partition of a leaf is the partition of $\{0..L\}$ cut out by $C_x = \langle \delta_x \rangle$, and it refines along every path: an agreement at a leaf not already forced by $C_q$ at an ancestor $q$ needs the twiddle of one edge below $q$ to be a root of a polynomial determined by $M_q$ and the path (Lemma 2.1 at that edge: for a constant $\gamma$ and $t$ transcendental over the field of $M_q$, $\gamma^T (M_q^0 + t M_q^1) = 0$ forces $\gamma^T M_q^0 = \gamma^T M_q^1 = 0$). (c) Theorem 4.2 is the statement that this accounting is tight when each leaf is charged one agreement: one coincidence a leaf, $Lk - 1$ available. (d) Excess comes only from leaves charged more than once, and Proposition 4.1 measures it by relations of dual codewords.

**The three shapes seen (exact at the stated sizes).** (1) Products $A \otimes B$ at a split level: all blocks at that level have $\dim C_q \le 1$ with a common lower factor; a block agreement costs the top code one condition and is worth $2^i$ leaves; the $2^i - 1$ zeros of $B$ are shared. All deficient kernels of the exhaustive scans (Section 3). (2) A product on a sub-list plus members tied to it by at most $k$ ordinary conditions each (384 orbits at (4,8), four members). (3) Sums of two products $A \otimes b + A' \otimes b'$, neither a solution alone: note 69's pattern at $(16, 32)$, `02 02 02 02 | . . . . | . . . . | 012 . 012 012 | 012 . 012 012 | 01 01 01 01 | 01 12 12 12 | 012 012 012 012`. Checked here (`scripts/74_two_dim.py`): kernel of dimension 1, level-2 flattening of rank 2, and the lower-factor space $W$ contains $b$ vanishing on the three triple leaves of block 3 and $b'$ vanishing on those of block 4 (the evaluations at each triple are proportional on $W$). So three leaves of a block impose the two conditions $A' g_q = 0$ of one leaf: the saving is again "zeros of a lower factor are shared", used twice. Its weight is $32 = Lk$, excess 1, against excess 7 for the product at level 3. In all three shapes a member gains over the generalized Singleton count only through whole blocks of a top code and through the zeros of lower factors, at most one block's worth in total per factor.

**Conjecture 5.1 (structure; conjectured, evidence Sections 3, 4, 6).** In the generic tree code let $L + 1$ distinct codewords agree with a word on at least $k + g$ leaves each, $g \ge 1$. Then all but at most $(n - k)/g$ of them lie in lifted families $\{A \otimes B\}$ at split levels with blocks of at least $g$ leaves ($2^i \ge g$, that is $N = n/2^i \le n/g$), in which the members are distinct codewords of the top code $[N, \rho N]$ with block agreement at least $\rho N$ around a common block word, and the excess $g$ comes from the zeros of $B$ and from leaves of blocks outside the member. ($(n - k)/g$ is the generalized Singleton count for the members not explained by a lift.)

**Conjecture 5.2 (list bound; conjectured).** For the generic tree code of rate $\rho$, every word and every power of two $N \le n/2$: at most $\binom{N - 1}{\rho N} + nN$ codewords at agreement $k + n/N$ or more (that is $\varepsilon \ge 1/N$). The proved lower bounds it is measured against (Proposition 6.1): $\binom{N-1}{\rho N}$ codewords at agreement $k + n/N - 1$, and $\binom{N-2}{\rho N}$ at agreement $k + n/N$ once $n \ge N \binom{N-2}{\rho N}$ (one free block); so at $\varepsilon = 1/N$ the conjectured bound is attained up to the factor $(1 - \rho)$ and the additive term, and there $\log_2 \Lambda \approx H(\rho)/\varepsilon$ (note 69's lower bound had $(1 - \rho) H(\rho)/\varepsilon$ at its best points). Between these points the statement is deliberately not sharp: just above $\varepsilon = 1/N$ it only gives the bound for $2N$, while the best known lists are the tails of the $N$-family, $\max_f \min\big(\binom{N-1-f}{\rho N},\, f n / (N j)\big)$ codewords at agreement $k + n/N + j - 1$. At rate 1/2 the conjecture reads $\Lambda \le 2^{28.2}$ at $\varepsilon = 2/64$, $2^{59.7}$ at $1/64$, $2^{251}$ at $1/256$; and it is **proved** that the budget $2^{300}$ of the application is exceeded at every agreement below $1/2 + 1/512$ ($\binom{511}{256} \approx 2^{507}$ codewords at $k + n/512 - 1$). **Proved part:** the lower bounds, the optimality for three members (Theorem 4.4), and the limits of weight bounds (end of Section 4). **Not proved:** any upper bound below the Johnson radius.

**What a proof would have to do (sketched).** Theorem 4.2's induction handles one functional per leaf because the pushed-up system keeps subspaces that do not depend on the twiddles. With $\kappa_x \ge 2$ the pushed-up subspaces are the hulls $\tilde C_x$, which depend on the upper twiddles, and the one-level identity itself fails for arbitrary subspaces (Segre). A proof must therefore carry a class of twiddle-dependent subspaces closed under the push-up; the data say the right class is "subspaces $\lambda$ with $\lambda \otimes g_q$ in the span of block rows of a top code", that is, hyperplanes $B^\perp$ of lower factors (in the dual form of Proposition 4.1: block moments of all members confined to a common subspace at the zero block).

## 7. Task 5: from generic twiddles to random twiddles in GF($2^{64}$)

**7.1 What "generic" hides (proved).** Every generic statement above says that some minor of an incidence matrix is a nonzero polynomial $\Delta$ in the twiddles. A row is multilinear in the $v$ twiddles of its path, a twiddle of level $i$ occurs in at most $2^i$ rows of a tree, so $\deg \Delta \le v \cdot (\text{number of rows})$ and for uniform twiddles $\Pr[\Delta = 0] \le v\,|X|/q$ for one system of Theorem 4.2 (Schwartz and Zippel): about $2^{-35}$ at $k = 2^{23}$, $L = 2$. The true order for one elementary event is $c/q$ (note 69 measured $c$ between 1 and 3.4 for $k \times k$ minors); either figure is useless for a union over patterns. The passage therefore needs, as in note 68 Sections 4 and 5, a count of **coincidences**: by Lemma 2.1 a solution that does not exist generically needs twiddles equal to slopes determined by the solution and the twiddles above, one unit of probability $q^{-1}$ each, and what matters is how many distinct coincidence events exist at each scale and how much list each one buys.

**7.3 Consequence for the application (proved mechanism, estimated probabilities).** A list bound $2^{300}$ at agreement $(\rho + \varepsilon) n$ for all words contains the statement "every set of $(\rho + \varepsilon) n$ leaves has nullity at most 4" (a nullity of 5 is $q^5$ codewords around zero), which is note 68's distance statement with $\nu = 5$. For block-aligned sets the nullity jumps from 0 to $n/N - g$ with a single coincidence, so the tolerance buys nothing against K1. Precisely, a K1 event $(b, N)$ gives nullity $b\,n/N - \varepsilon n$ on a set of $(\rho + \varepsilon) n$ leaves, so it is fatal exactly when $b > \varepsilon N$, and the bound can hold with probability $1 - 2^{-130}$ only if every K1 event with $b > \varepsilon N$ is either excluded by a check or has probability below $2^{-130}$. The largest $b$ whose probability $c \binom{N}{N/2} q^{-b}$ is still above $2^{-130}$ at rate 1/2 is $b_{\max}(N) \approx (N + 130)/64$: 2 at $N \le 64$, 3 at $N = 128$, 5 at 256, 9 at 512. Hence: (a) at $\varepsilon = 2/64$: the top codes with $N \le 16$ must be MDS, the top $[32, 16]$ code must have no codeword with 17 zero blocks (probability about $2^{-99}$ if unchecked; $5.6 \cdot 10^8$ rank conditions), and from $N = 64$ on nothing needs checking ($b = 3$ at $N = 64$ has probability $2^{-132}$). **So $c_0 = 2$ in note 68's target is exactly what "top levels checked to $N = 32$, random below" allows, and it is attained only if nothing but K1 matters.** (b) For $\varepsilon < 2/64$ the scale $N = 64$ needs the same condition with $b = 2$ ($1.8 \cdot 10^{18}$ block sets, probability $2^{-68}$ if unchecked), then $\varepsilon \ge 3/128$ is the limit with random twiddles from $N = 128$ on, $5/256$ from 256 on, and $1/64 + 2/N$ in general: $\varepsilon = 1/64$ itself fails at every scale with probability about $2^{-66}$. A structured top (Reed-Solomon on a subspace, MDS at every depth) removes these conditions at any $N$ but brings back that code's own lists at the top scales, which are the open problem this programme started from.

**7.4 What has to be proved, in order.**
1. (deterministic, open) Conjecture 5.1 or 5.2 for generic twiddles.
2. (probabilistic, within reach of note 68's Lemma 5.1) A random version of Theorem 4.2. 3. (open, the lead's Section 5 item (2)) The slacks of different levels must not add up: an invariant sensitive to scale. Nothing here helps beyond the remark that a K1 event at scale $N$ is only harmful when $b/N \ge \varepsilon$, so that at rate 1/2 only the scales $N \le 130/(64\varepsilon - 1)$ count ($N \le 130$ at $\varepsilon = 2/64$): a bounded number of levels, not $v$.
