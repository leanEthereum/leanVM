# 57. Window-mixed Reed-Solomon codes: a randomized code for which the list bound near capacity may be provable (lead, 2026-11-21)

Status of everything here: **derived by the lead by hand, unrefereed, partly a sketch.** Labels: [exact] an identity I checked, [sketch] an argument with gaps named, [target] a statement to prove. This note changes the question. Rounds 5 to 10 tried to prove list bounds for plain Reed-Solomon codes on a subspace domain and stopped at the same wall each time: the real domain over a 64-bit field carries only $64N$ bits of "genericity", against $2^{\Theta(n)}$ configurations to exclude, so nothing transfers from the formal model, and in characteristic 2 genuine structured lists exist. The DEEP-fold framework needs only a list bound for the committed code, tolerates lists up to about $2^{35(s-10)}$ ($s$ out-of-domain points a level), and puts no constraint on the code beyond: linear, fast to encode, each symbol a short combination of evaluations of the message polynomial (so the verifier's weights stay tensor structured). So the code itself can be randomized, with public randomness from the random oracle, and then a first-moment argument has $64rn$ random bits to spend instead of $64N$.

## 1. The code

$K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$, $q = |K|$, $D = \{x_v : v \in \mathbb{F}_2^N\}$ the $\mathbb{F}_2$-span of a basis $b_0, \dots, b_{N-1}$, $n = 2^N$, $k = \rho n$, $\mathrm{RS} = \{(P(x))_{x \in D} : \deg P < k\}$. Fix offsets $C = \{0, b_0, \dots, b_{N-1}\} \subset D$ ($r = N + 1$ points; the *window* of $x$ is $W_x = x + C$, the Hamming ball of radius 1 around $x$ in the coordinates of $D$). Let $\mu : D \times C \to K$ be uniform and independent (derived from the random oracle with a public seed). The **window-mixed code** is
$$\mathcal{C}_\mu = \Bigl\{ c_\mu(P) = \Bigl(\sum_{c \in C} \mu(x, c)\,P(x + c)\Bigr)_{x \in D} : \deg P < k \Bigr\}.$$
Encoding: one additive NTT, then $r$ multiply-adds a symbol along strides $2^s$ (about twice the multiplications of the NTT itself; see Section 6). The symbol at $x$ is a combination of $r$ point evaluations with public coefficients, so a query still yields linear claims on the message with tensor-structured weights ($r$ times more of them). Lanes are mixed with the same $\mu$, so folding lanes commutes with mixing.

## 2. The target theorem

**Theorem M [target].** Let $L \ge 1$ with $L \le N - 2$ and $a \in (\rho, 1)$ with
$$64\,\bigl((L+1)a - 1 - L\rho\bigr)\,n \ \ge\ (L+1)\,H(a)\,n + 130 . \tag{1}$$
Then with probability at least $1 - 2^{-128}$ over $\mu$: no word $w \in K^D$ has $L + 1$ affinely independent polynomials $P_0, \dots, P_L$ of degree $< k$ with $c_\mu(P_i)$ agreeing with $w$ on at least $an$ positions each. Consequently every word has at most $q^{L-1} = 2^{64(L-1)}$ codewords at agreement $an$ (a set of more than $q^{L-1}$ polynomials is not contained in an affine space of dimension $L - 1$).

Inequality (1) reads $a \ge \rho + \frac{1 - \rho}{L+1} + \frac{H(a)}{64} + o(1)$: the generalized Singleton bound for lists of size $L$ plus the pigeonhole slack of a 64-bit field. The list bound $q^{L-1}$ is exactly what the large budget of the DEEP fold pays for: $L = 8$ costs about 23 out-of-domain points a level, $L = 15$ about 36.

## 3. Proof skeleton

**Step 1, exact probability [exact].** Fix affinely independent $P_0, \dots, P_L$ and sets $S_0, \dots, S_L$. A word agreeing with $c_\mu(P_i)$ on $S_i$ for all $i$ exists iff for every $x$ and all $i, i'$ in $I_x = \{i : x \in S_i\}$: $\sum_c \mu(x, c)(P_i - P_{i'})(x + c) = 0$. For fixed $x$ this says that $\mu(x, \cdot) \in K^C$ is orthogonal to $R_x = \mathrm{span}\{(P_i - P_{i'})|_{W_x}\}$. The vectors $\mu(x, \cdot)$ are uniform and independent over $x$, so the probability is exactly $q^{-\sum_x \dim R_x}$. (This exactness, with conditions linear in the randomness and independent across positions, is what random evaluation points do not have: there each position has one random element, at most 64 bits of gain, whatever the number of members covering it.)

**Step 2, deficiency [exact].** Let $V = \mathrm{span}\{P_i - P_0\}$ (dimension $L$). Then $\dim R_x = (|I_x| - 1)_+ - \delta_x$ with $\delta_x \le \Delta_x(V) := \dim\bigl(V \cap \mathcal{I}(W_x)\bigr)$, $\mathcal{I}(W)$ the polynomials of degree $< k$ vanishing on $W$. Put $\Delta(V) = \sum_x \Delta_x(V)$.

**Step 3, union bound [exact].** $\sum_x (|I_x| - 1)_+ \ge (L+1)an - n$, there are at most $2^{(L+1)H(a)n}$ set systems, and summing over ordered bases of $V$:
$$\Pr[\text{bad}] \ \le\ 2^{(L+1)H(a)n}\ q^{-((L+1)a - 1)n}\ q^{Lk}\ \mathbb{E}_V\bigl[q^{\Delta(V)}\bigr],$$
$V$ uniform among the $L$-dimensional subspaces of $K[X]_{<k}$. So Theorem M follows from (1) and:

**Key Lemma [target].** $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}] \le 2$ for $L \le N - 2$, at $N = 25$ to $28$, $q = 2^{64}$, every rate.

**Step 4, the expansion [exact].** $q^{\Delta(V)} = \prod_x |V \cap \mathcal{I}(W_x)|$ is the number of families $(D_x)_{x \in D}$ with $D_x \in V \cap \mathcal{I}(W_x)$. Exchanging the sums, and using that a fixed $u$-dimensional space lies in a random $V$ with probability about $q^{-u(k - L)}$:
$$\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}] \ \le\ \sum_{u = 0}^{L} q^{-u(k - L)}\ \#\bigl\{(D_x)_x : D_x \in \mathcal{I}(W_x),\ \dim \mathrm{span}(D_x) = u\bigr\}\,(1 + o(1)).$$
The term $u = 0$ is 1. Everything else must be small.

**Step 5, rank one [exact up to the count of sets by boundary].** A rank-one family is $D_x = a_x G$ with $a_x \ne 0$ exactly on a set $T$ and $G$ vanishing on $T + C$. Reed-Solomon is MDS, so the number of $G$ (up to scalars) vanishing on a set $Z$ is $q^{k - |Z| - 1}$ for $|Z| < k$ and 0 otherwise, exactly, on every domain. The term is $q^{L-1}\sum_{T \ne \emptyset} q^{|T| - |T + C|} = q^{L-1}\sum_T q^{-|\partial T|}$, $\partial T$ the vertex boundary of $T$ in the $N$-cube. Every nonempty $T$ with $|T + C| < n/2$ has $|\partial T| \ge N$ (Harper), and the number of sets with boundary $b$ is at most $\binom nb 2^{bN}$, so the term is at most about $n\,q^{L - 1 - N}$: negligible for $L \le N - 1$. The same count handles families whose direction classes are linearly independent (rank $u$ with exactly $u$ classes): each class contributes a factor $q^{L - |\partial T_a|}$.

**Step 6, pencils [sketch].** Rank two with at least three direction classes: $V \supseteq U = \langle D_1, D_2 \rangle$ and the deficient windows are those on which the rational function $\xi = D_1/D_2$ is constant (up to common zeros). Write $U = g \cdot \langle a, b\rangle$ with $a, b$ coprime of degree at most $m$. (i) For a prescribed pattern (clusters $B_1, \dots, B_p$ of points, $\xi \equiv t_i$ on $B_i$, $|Y| = \sum |B_i|$) the pairs $(a, b)$ of degree $\le m$ are the short vectors of the rank-two $K[X]$-lattice $\{(a, b) : a + \tau b \equiv 0 \bmod \Lambda_Y\}$, $\tau$ the interpolant of the step function; by reduction theory there are $q^{2m + 2 - |Y|}$ of them when $|Y| \le 2m + 1$ (transversal, exact), and when $|Y| > 2m + 1$ there is at most one primitive $\xi$ of degree $\le m$ (two rational functions of degree $\le m$ agreeing on more than $2m$ points are equal). (ii) In the transversal range the term is a product of factors $q^{1 - |\partial J_i|}$ as in Step 5. (iii) In the non-transversal range the number of deficient windows of $\xi$ must be bounded outright: the fibres of $\xi$ on $D$ have at most $m$ points each, a window is deficient only if it lies in one fibre (or in the zero set $B$ of $g$, which costs $|B|$), and the exponent to control is $I + |\mathrm{int}\,B| - |B| - k - m$. With $B' = B \cup$ (points of a fibre with a neighbour in another fibre), $B'$ is a vertex separator of the cube whose parts have at most $m$ points, and the claim needed is the purely combinatorial

**Isoperimetric Lemma [target].** In the $N$-cube, $N = 25$ to 28, if a vertex set $B'$ separates the rest into parts of at most $m$ points each, then $m + |B'| \ge (1/2 + \sigma)\,2^N$ for an explicit $\sigma > 0$ (I expect $\sigma$ about 0.05 or more; a single Hamming ball gives exactly $0.500$ at density 0.345, but then the other half must be split again). 

Nothing in Steps 1 to 6 uses any property of the evaluation points beyond: they are distinct, they are indexed by the cube, and Reed-Solomon is MDS. No genericity. This is why it could hold on the real domain.

**Step 7, rank $u \ge 3$ with more than $u$ classes [open].** Expected shape of the argument, checked by the lead only for $u = 3$ with isolated directions: non-transversality of the conditions means that the rank-$u$ lattice $\{G \in K[X]^u : \langle\lambda(X), G\rangle \equiv 0 \bmod \Lambda_Y\}$ has an unbalanced reduced basis, the short sublattice has rank $u - 1$, and its generalized cross product is a map $D \to \mathbb{P}^{u-1}$ of degree at most the sum of the short degrees that is constant on every deficient window. Then the same isoperimetric lemma applies with $m$ replaced by that sum. To be proved in general, by induction on $u$.

## 4. Why the windows must span, and what happens if they do not

With windows inside blocks (offsets $b_0, \dots, b_{t-1}$ only), take $U = \mathrm{span}(b_0..b_{t-1})$ and any polynomial $g$: the members $P_0 + g \cdot c(L_U(X))$ restrict on each block to the line $P_0 + s\,g$, so their window matrices have rank 1, the family is a code with one random coefficient a position, and for $g = 1$ it is the periodic subcode, whose list problem is that of plain Reed-Solomon on the quotient domain: unprotected. With the full Hamming ball this cannot happen: a function that is constant on windows in all $N$ directions is constant, and a $U$-periodic polynomial sees $N - \dim U + 1$ distinct values on a window, so the mixed code restricted to the periodic subcode is a mixed code on the quotient domain: the construction is self-similar at every scale. The known structured lists of Reed-Solomon die for the same reason: the agreement set of a list member becomes its interior in the cube, and a hyperplane coset has empty interior, a quadric of density 3/4 has interior density 1/4.

## 5. What has to be checked first (assignments)

1. **Prove** the Key Lemma: Steps 5 and 6 with all constants, the Isoperimetric Lemma, and Step 7 in general. Or find the family of subspaces $V$ that breaks it.
2. **Attack**: deterministic bad configurations of the mixed code; exact computation of $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}]$ and of true list sizes at toy sizes (small $N$, small $q$), against the prediction.
3. **Protocol and cost**: does the DEEP-fold soundness theorem (paper and Lean) go through for a linear code given by position functionals instead of an evaluation set; the interleaving reduction; where $\mu$ comes from and what the $2^{-128}$ over the oracle means for round-by-round soundness; exact tables of $a$, queries, list budget and points at all levels; prover and verifier cost; cheaper windows (the proof only needs the offsets' Cayley graph to have a good vertex-isoperimetric profile and at least $L + 1$ neighbours, so about $L + 2$ well-chosen integer strides may replace the $N + 1$ cube directions).

## 6. Cost, first estimate

Per lane and symbol: $r = 26$ multiply-adds in $K$ along strides, against about $N/2 = 12.5$ multiplications in the additive NTT. So the encoding of a level costs about three times today's NTT, before any optimization; the Merkle hashing, the sumchecks and the rest of the prover are unchanged. The verifier evaluates $r$ weights a query instead of one and derives $r$ coefficients from the oracle (two Keccak blocks a query). Against that: about 43% fewer queries at level 0 if $L = 15$.

## 7. Erratum by the lead, same day: the Key Lemma as stated fails for large $L$; Theorem M is not affected

Let $U$ be a subspace of codimension $c$ of the cube of "group-sum" type (the $N$ basis directions partitioned into $c$ groups, $U$ the vectors whose coordinate sum over each group is 0) and $\ell = L_U(X)$: degree $n/2^c$, $2^c$ values on $D$, and only $c + 1$ distinct values on every window. Then $V_u = \mathrm{span}(1, \ell, \dots, \ell^{u-1})$, admissible while $u \le \rho 2^c$, has window rank at most $c + 1$ at every window, so $\Delta(V_u) \ge (u - c - 1)n$, against a cost of about $uk$ for one space. At rate 1/2 with $c = 5$ this breaks $\mathbb{E}_V[q^{\Delta(V)}] \le 2$ for $12 \le u \le 16$, hence for every $L \ge 12$ (at rate 1/4 from about $L = 8$). It is an artefact of Step 2, which charges the deficiency of the whole $V$ at every position: the members $P_0 + Q(\ell)$ form a subcode of dimension $2^{c-1}$ on which every position still contributes rank $\min(|I_x| - 1, c + 1)$, and the first moment of that family alone is hugely negative. So:

- for small $L$ (I know no counterexample for $L \le 11$ at rate 1/2, $L \le 7$ at rate 1/4) the Key Lemma as stated is the target: $L = 10$ at rate 1/2 would give $a \approx 0.561$, about 130 queries, lists of at most $q^9$;
- for larger $L$ the union bound has to use the true rank $\dim(V_x|_{W_x})$ of the span $V_x$ of the differences of the members covering $x$, stratified by local rank, with thin families (few spaces, low local rank) handled by their own first moment or by a Johnson-type bound inside the family, and with the freedom to choose which $L + 1$ members of a list of more than $q^{L-1}$ to bound. What that needs in the end is a counting theorem for tuples of Reed-Solomon codewords with prescribed window ranks, or a classification of spaces of low local rank (guess: sums of $g$-twisted spaces of polynomials in periodic functions).

## 8. A ladder with constant list size: $t$-wise inequalities and convexity (lead, same day)

The affine-independence budget of Theorem M is not the only way to conclude, and for small $L$ it is not the best. Suppose the first moment is proved for tuples of rank at most $t - 1$ (the counting lemma for subspaces of dimension at most $t - 1$). Then with probability $1 - 2^{-128}$ every $t$ members of every list, with agreement sets $S_1, \dots, S_t$ and cover counts $c_x$, satisfy the **$t$-wise inequality**
$$\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ \ \le\ (t - 1)k + \frac{t\,H(a)\,n + 130}{64}$$
(for affinely independent members this is the first moment; for dependent ones it should follow from the inequalities of lower rank and the distance, as it does for collinear triples, where all pairwise agreements lie in one zero set of size at most $k$ plus a few, and for coplanar quadruples; to be proved in general). A family of $M$ sets of density $\alpha$ all of whose $t$-subfamilies satisfy $\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ \le B$ has $n\,g_t(\alpha) - O(t^2 n/M) \le B$ with $g_t(p) = tp - 1 + (1 - p)^t$, by Jensen ($g_t$ is convex and the cover count of a random $t$-subfamily at $x$ is hypergeometric). So above the threshold $g_t(\alpha) = (t-1)\rho + tH(\alpha)/64$ the list has **constant** size: no affine-dimension budget, no extra out-of-domain points. For $t = 3$ this is the three-wise Johnson bound of note 44, which is already machine-checked (`PeriodDescent.threewise_johnson`); the role that the period conjecture played there is played here by the first moment for pencils.

## 9. The first rung needs no isoperimetry and only local windows (lead, same day, sketch)

Note 61 shows that spanning windows are expensive for the verifier (the weight of a query costs about $\kappa^2/2$ products instead of $\kappa$) and that the proposal is only worth its complexity with local windows. For the first rung ($t = 3$) local windows seem to suffice, by an argument simpler than Step 6, because the loss is capped by the pair distance.

*Pairs, any window containing 0 [sketch, short].* Two distinct codewords of the mixed code agree on at most $k + (n + z)/64 + 2$ positions with probability $1 - 2^{-128}$: the free agreements of $d = P - P'$ are the $x$ with $d$ vanishing on $W_x$, a set $T$ with $T + C \subseteq Z(d)$, the number of $d$ with $Z(d) \supseteq T + C$ is $q^{k - |T + C|} \le q^{k - |T|}$, and there are at most $2^n$ sets $T$ and $2^n$ agreement sets. Call the excess $\varepsilon_2 n$, $\varepsilon_2 \approx 0.024$.

*Triples.* For an affinely independent triple with cover counts $c_x$ the exact probability is $q^{-\text{gain}}$ with gain $= u_2 n + 2u_3 n - |J_3|$ up to zero-deficient positions, where $u_j n$ is the number of positions covered exactly $j$ times and $J_3$ is the set of triple-covered positions whose window is pencil-deficient ($\xi = d_1/d_2$ constant on the window). Since triple-covered positions are in particular agreements of one pair, $|J_3| \le u_3 n \le k + \varepsilon_2 n$. Write $\langle d_1, d_2 \rangle = g\langle a, b\rangle$, $a, b$ coprime of degree at most $m$, and let $Y$ be the union of the closures of the deficient clusters, $p$ their number.

- Case A, $|Y| \le 2m + 1$: the number of pairs $(a, b)$ with the pattern is $q^{2m + 2 - |Y| + p}$ exactly (balanced lattice), the number of $g$ is at most $q^{k - m}$, so loss minus cost is at most $|J_3| - |Y| + p + (m + 2 - k) \le \sum_{\text{clusters}} (|J_a| - |\overline{J_a}| + 1)$. A cluster costs nothing unless it equals its own closure, which for Hamming-ball windows inside blocks of $2^{t'}$ points happens only for whole blocks: the excess is at most one field element per block, $64 n / 2^{t'}$ bits in all.
- Case B, $|Y| > 2m + 1$: the primitive $\xi$ is unique given its values on at most $p_0 \le (2m + 2)/r + 1$ clusters, so the number of pencils is at most $q^{k - m + p_0}$ and loss minus cost is at most $|J_3| - (k + m - p_0) \le \varepsilon_2 n + 2$.

So every triple satisfies $u_2 + 2u_3 \le 2\rho + 3H(a)/64 + \varepsilon_2 + 64/2^{t'} \cdot (1/64) + o(1)$, and with the three-wise Johnson bound the list is constant above $\alpha^2(3 - \alpha) = 2\rho + 3H/64 + \varepsilon_2 + 2^{-t'}$: about 0.68 to 0.69 at rate 1/2 (about 195 to 200 queries against 217), with windows of 8 or 9 neighbouring points and no isoperimetric input. Collinear triples satisfy the same inequality by the pair bound. Windows of two points do not work: both positions of a pair share one window, the cluster equals its closure, and the loss is two for a cost of one.

The same mechanism (the loss of a rung is capped by the inequalities of the lower rungs, and non-transversal families are determined by few values) should give the higher rungs with local windows; the families that defeated the naive Key Lemma (members $P_0 + g\,c(L_U)$, rank one on every window) satisfy the $t$-wise inequality with a slack of $(t - 1)\varepsilon_2$ because each of their multiply covered positions is an agreement of a pair. To be worked out.

## 10. Erratum to Section 9 (lead, same day): local windows do NOT work; the first rung needs the spanning window and the isoperimetry after all

Section 9 dropped the zero-deficient positions ("gain ... up to zero-deficient positions"). They are the whole difficulty. A position where a difference of two members vanishes on the entire window is a **free agreement**: it has probability 1 and no first moment sees it. With a window set $C$, a polynomial that is constant on the cosets of $W = \mathrm{span}(C)$ (a function of $L_W$) is constant on every window, so on the $W$-periodic polynomials the mixed code is plain Reed-Solomon on the quotient domain $D/W$, each symbol repeated and scaled: **every list of plain Reed-Solomon on $D/W$, at the same rate and agreement, lifts to the mixed code with all its agreements free.** For a local window ($W$ spanned by $t'$ low directions) the quotient has $2^{N - t'}$ points, and the statement $H_3$ for the mixed code contains the three-wise inequality for plain Reed-Solomon on that quotient, which is Conjecture P there: unproved, and the original problem at a smaller size. So local windows protect nothing against structured lists, and the protection of any window set is exactly its ability to make free agreements rare: a free agreement set is the interior, for the window structure, of a zero set of size below $k$.

What survives:

1. **Pairs** (any window containing 0): two codewords agree on at most $k + \varepsilon_2 n$ positions. Correct as written, because the free agreements of one difference lie in its zero set.
2. **Triples, spanning cube window.** The free agreements of a triple are the interiors of three fibres of $\xi = d_1/d_2$ (disjoint sets of fewer than $k$ points each), and the claim needed is the purely combinatorial $\sum_i |\mathrm{int}\,F_i| \le 2k - (\text{margin})$: trivial with room at rate 1/2 (the fibres are disjoint, so the total is at most $(1 - \beta)n$), and true at lower rates as soon as sets of density $\rho$ lose a third of their points to the boundary, which holds in the 25-cube (about 0.44 at density 1/4, more below). Mixed free and random agreements are handled by Cases A and B of Section 9 with the free positions put back in the pattern. So $H_3$, and constant lists above about 0.67 at rate 1/2, is still the first theorem to prove, **for the cube window**, and the isoperimetric input is essential.
3. **Higher rungs, cube window.** The new crux is deterministic: bound $\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+$ over the free agreements of $t$ members, that is over interior-closed coincidence patterns of $t$ Reed-Solomon codewords. Pairwise bounds plus isoperimetry give effective pairwise free agreement $(1 - \beta)k$, which by itself excludes violations only above about $\sqrt{\rho(1 - \beta)}$ (0.587 at rate 1/2 in the 25-cube): enough for $t \le 5$ at rate 1/2, not beyond. Beyond that one needs to use that the coincidence pattern is realized by Reed-Solomon codewords (a higher-order MDS statement restricted to interior-closed patterns, to be proved through uniqueness of low-degree maps plus isoperimetry, as for pencils), or a window structure with thinner interiors.
4. **Cost.** Note 61's verdict stands for the spanning window: about the same trade as halving the rate. Stride windows protect partially: a window with strides $2^j$ leaves the cosets of a subspace only through carries, and a subspace containing the runs of coordinates above each stride keeps an interior fraction of about $\prod_j (1 - 2^{-(h_j + 1)})$; with 8 strides spread over 25 scales that is at most about 0.34, with 5 strides about 0.83. Whether a partial protection is provably enough is open (it shifts the plain Reed-Solomon problem on quotients to a higher agreement).
