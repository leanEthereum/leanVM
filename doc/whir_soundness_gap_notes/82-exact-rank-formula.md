# 82. An exact rank formula for the incidence system of the generic tree code T2

**The exact formula, where it is proved, and what is open.** **Formula (C\*).** For a pattern $\mathcal P$ of the generic T2 tree code let $\mathcal Q^*$ be the pattern one level up whose space at a bottom pair $p$ is the set of functionals $\theta$ with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$ that kill both bottom slices of every solution at $p$ ($\theta \otimes g_p \otimes F^2$ lies in the row space), and $\Delta_p$ the span of the difference vectors $N g_p$ of the solutions $N$ of $\mathcal Q^*$. Then $r(\mathcal P) = 2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*) + \sum_x \mathrm{rank}(\Lambda_x|_{\Delta_{p(x)}})$, equivalently (C') $r(\mathcal P) = \min_{\mathcal Q} [2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) + \sum_x (r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q \vee \Lambda_x@p(x)) - r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q))]$ over the patterns one level up, and iterated up the tree $r(\mathcal P) = k \dim \Theta + \sum_{i < v} 2^i \sum_{u \text{ of height } i} \rho^{(i)}_u$: a generalized Singleton count in which a node of height $i$ counts $2^i$ times and costs the rank of its space on the difference vectors of the solutions one level up, not its dimension. **The class** in which the recursion closes is not the class patterns and not the partition patterns (24 exact failures at $(2,4)$ with four members): it is the **binary patterns**, where a node carries any subspace of the even-weight code of length $L + 1$ over $\mathbb F_2$ (several disjoint groups of agreeing members, and spaces such as the all-ones vector). **Proved:** the upper bound for every $\mathcal Q$ (Lemma 1.1); (C\*) when at most two leaves have a class of three or more members, or more generally a full part not spanned by binary functionals (Theorem 4.2: this contains the all-pairs case and puts the proved cases of note 74 in iterable form); (C') for every binary pattern at $(2, 4)$ and every class pattern at $k = 2$ (by citation: tame four-subspace theory, Brakensiek, Gopi and Makam); the one-level identity of note 74 when at most **four** leaves have a class of three or more, for arbitrary subspaces, sharp (Theorem 4.4); for three members the rank is $\sum_{c<c'} \min(k, |A_c \cup A_{c'}|) - \min(k, |A_1 \cup A_2 \cup A_3|) - n_T$ with one unknown integer $n_T$, the number of copies of "three lines in a plane" in the triple of agreement spans, and the functionals that depend on the upper twiddles are the points of a conic attached to that part (Proposition 4.3); and, by exhaustive exact computation in characteristic 2 (both sides are rigorous bounds), (C') for **all 390625 three-member patterns at $(4, 8)$**, all 65536 four-member binary patterns at $(2, 4)$, and all 142 million five-member systems on five and six leaves at $k = 2$. **Exact at the stated sizes:** (C') or (C\*) on about 760000 further patterns with 3 to 6 members at $(4,8)$, $(8,16)$, $(16,32)$, $(4,16)$, among them every deficient orbit of notes 69 and 74, note 76's first four-member pattern where splitting fails, and note 80's pattern (the formula gives 16, the rank); no failure for class patterns. **Structure theorem** (Theorem 5.1, Corollaries 5.2, 5.3; unconditional in part (i), proved wherever (C\*) is): all $2^i$ slices of every solution solve a binary pattern $\mathcal Q^{(i)}$ on the top code at scale $n/2^i$, so every solution is a sum of tensors $A \otimes B$ with $A$ a solution of $\mathcal Q^{(i)}$ (the correct form of "spanned by lifts": the summands need not be solutions); inside, the leaves impose independent conditions; the deficiency is at most twice the upper deficiency plus the number of functionals depending on the upper twiddles that the upper solutions absorb; where the upper difference vectors at a pair span a line, every solution has a rank-one block there with a common lower factor that vanishes at the leaf, whatever the size of its class; and a pattern carrying a list has $w \le 2 d^{\rm up} - 1 + \sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \Delta_{p(x)}^\perp)$. **False:** the closed recursion for six members at rate 1/4. At $k = 2$ with five leaves the partition pattern $\Lambda_{012}, \Lambda(03/14), \Lambda(23/15), \Lambda(05/24), \Lambda_{345}$ has rank 9 where every count gives 10, by **Pappus's theorem** (this is Segre's configuration of note 74, found here among graphic planes); and a class pattern at $(8, 32)$, six codewords around one word with $w = 43 > Lk = 40$, is the lift of that configuration: a new kind of lift (of a pairwise-agreement configuration, not of a list around a word), for which (C\*) holds at the two lower levels and fails at the top. There are 132 such five-leaf atoms among graphic planes: 60 of Pappus type and 72 that exist in characteristic 2 only (five double pairs; rank 10 in odd characteristic). **Conjectural:** (C\*) for every class pattern with the true ranks one level up; the closed recursion for at most five members at every rate and for any number of members at rate 1/2 (where the scale with top dimension 2 has four nodes and no such atom exists). **Smallest open case:** three members, three or more leaves carrying all three members, in different bottom pairs, whose relation components depend on the upper twiddles; it is settled at $(4, 8)$ by the exhaustive computation, so the first sizes without a proof are $(8, 16)$ and $(4, 16)$.

## 1. Setup and the class of systems

Notation of notes 68, 74, 76. Generic tree code T2, $k = 2^v$, $n = 2^{v+r}$, leaf row $g_x = g_p \otimes (1, t_x)$, $p$ the bottom pair of $x$. A *partition pattern* gives each leaf $x$ a set partition $\pi_x$ of the members $\{0..L\}$; $\Lambda_x = \Lambda(\pi_x) \subseteq K^L$ is the span of the differences $e_a - e_b$, $a \sim_{\pi_x} b$ ($e_0 = 0$), of dimension $\kappa_x = L + 1 - |\pi_x|$. A pattern of notes 69, 74 is the case where $\pi_x$ has at most one block that is not a singleton. Sums of such spaces are such spaces: $\Lambda(\pi) + \Lambda(\pi') = \Lambda(\pi \vee \pi')$ (join of partitions). The row space is $R(\mathcal P) = \sum_x \Lambda_x \otimes g_x$, the rank $r(\mathcal P) = \dim R(\mathcal P)$ (generic twiddles). For a pattern $\mathcal Q$ on the upper code (one partition per bottom pair) write $r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$, and $\mathcal Q \vee \pi@p$ for $\mathcal Q$ with $\pi$ joined in at the pair $p$.

**Lemma 1.1 (graphic upper bound; proved, trivial).** For every partition pattern $\mathcal P$ and every upper partition pattern $\mathcal Q$: $r(\mathcal P) \le B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) := 2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) + \sum_x \big( r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q \vee \pi_x@p(x)) - r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) \big)$. *Proof.* Put $X = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) \otimes K^2$. Then $R \subseteq X + \sum_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ with $Q_x$ a complement of $(\Lambda_x \otimes g_p) \cap R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$ in $\Lambda_x \otimes g_p$, and $\dim Q_x = \dim (R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) + \Lambda_x \otimes g_p) - \dim R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$. $\square$ This is Lemma 2.2 of note 74 for the tuple $\Lambda'_x \otimes g_p = (\Lambda_x \otimes g_p) \cap R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$, which is not graphic in general (it depends on the upper twiddles), while the bound itself only involves ranks of partition patterns one level up.

**Candidate (C) (tested in Section 3; false as stated from four members on, see Finding 3.1; (C') and (C\*) are its corrected forms).** $r(\mathcal P) = \min_{\mathcal Q} B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q)$, the minimum over all upper partition patterns. If true this is a combinatorial recursion closed in the class of partition patterns, with base $v = 0$: $r = L + 1 - |\bigvee_{\rm trees} \pi_{\rm root}|$. It contains the generalized Singleton count ($\mathcal Q = 0$ gives $w$), the generic-matrix formula of Brakensiek, Gopi and Makam ($\mathcal Q_p = $ one fixed partition $P$ of the members at every pair gives $(L + 1 - |P|) k + w(\mathcal P / P)$), and the lifts (worked by hand on `. 012 | 12 12 | 02 02 | 01 01`: $\mathcal Q = (\,\cdot\,, 12, 02, 01)$ gives $2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7$).

## 2. Reductions (each is tagged)

**2.1 Matrix-space dictionary (proved, restating note 74).** One level: $R = \sum_x U_x \otimes z_x$, $U_x = \Lambda_x \otimes g_p$, $z_x = (1, t_x)$. With $\sigma : \bigoplus_x U_x \to V$ the sum and $\pi_x$ the projections, $R$ is the image of the linear matrix $A_0 + \sum_x t_x A_x$, $A_0 = (\sigma, 0)$, $A_x = (0, \sigma \pi_x)$, and $\min_{\Lambda'} \Phi(\Lambda')$ is its non-commutative rank: the image of a subspace $X \subseteq \bigoplus U_x$ under the whole matrix space is $\sigma(X) \oplus \sum_x \pi_x(X)$, so graded $X$ suffice. $A_x$ has rank $\kappa_x$: the space is spanned by $A_0$ and rank-one matrices exactly when every class is a pair, which is why that case is matroid union and the others are not.

**2.2 The generic-matrix case is the case $k = 2$ (proved, by citation).** For $k = 2$ the rows are $\Lambda_x \otimes (1, t_x)$ with independent $t_x$, which is a generic $2 \times n$ matrix up to column scalings. For a generic $k \times n$ matrix the rank of a pattern with agreement sets $S_l$, all of size at least $k$, is $\min_P [(L + 1 - |P|) k + w(\mathcal P / P)]$ over the partitions $P$ of the members, $\mathcal P / P$ the pattern with the members of each part merged (this is the generic intersection formula of Brakensiek, Gopi and Makam, $\dim \bigcap_l W_{A_l} = \max_P [\sum_j |\bigcap_{l \in P_j} A_l| - (|P| - 1) k]$, put through Proposition 4.1 of note 74: $\mathrm{rank} = w - \sum_l (a_l - k) + (n - k) - \dim \bigcap_l H_{Z_l}$, $Z_l$ the complement of $S_l$). So for generic matrices the commutative rank equals the non-commutative rank on graphic systems, with a graphic minimizer $\Lambda'_x = \Lambda_x \cap \Lambda(P)$. The tree code differs from this by the lifts only at $k \ge 4$.

**2.3 Dual form at rate 1/2 (proved, from Proposition 4.1 of note 74 and Lemma 1.1 of note 76).** $r(\mathcal P) = w - \sum_l (a_l - k)_+ + (n - k) - \dim \bigcap_{l : a_l \ge k} G^{\rm sw}_{Z_l}$, where $G_Z = \mathrm{span}(g_x : x \in Z)$ in the sibling-exchanged tree code, again generic. So an exact rank formula for every pattern is the same thing as a formula for $\dim \bigcap_l G_{Z_l}$ for arbitrary sets of at most $k$ leaves: a "generic intersection formula for tree codes". For three members it is the same as $\dim (I(A) + I(B) + I(C))$ for three sets of leaves, $I(Z)$ the codewords vanishing on $Z$ (any rate): $\dim \ker = \dim I(A) + \dim I(B) + \dim I(C) - \dim (I(A) + I(B) + I(C))$ with $A, B, C$ the sets where the three differences are required to vanish (the kernel is the space of triples $(d_1, d_2, d_3) \in I(A) \times I(B) \times I(C)$ with zero sum); the dual form is Proposition 4.3.

**2.4 Derivative lemma at every node (proved; contains Lemma 2.3 of note 74 and Lemma 2.3 of note 76).** Let $u$ be any node of the tree at height $i$, $\hat u$ its parent, $t_u$ its twiddle, so that $g_x = g_{\hat u} \otimes (1, t_u) \otimes \beta_x$ for the leaves $x$ below $u$ ($\beta_x \in K^{2^i}$ the row of the subtree of $u$, free of $t_u$) and no other row involves $t_u$. For a relation $\sum_x \nu_x \otimes g_x = 0$, $\nu_x \in \Lambda_x \otimes F$, put $\rho'_u = \sum_{x \le u} \nu_x \otimes \beta_x \in F^L \otimes F^{2^i}$. Then $g_{\hat u} \otimes K^2 \otimes \rho'_u \subseteq R$. *Proof.* Differentiate in $t_u$ (formal derivative, any characteristic; the $\nu_x$ are rational in the twiddles): $g_{\hat u} \otimes (0,1) \otimes \rho'_u = -\sum_x \partial_u \nu_x \otimes g_x \in R$, and $g_{\hat u} \otimes (1, t_u) \otimes \rho'_u$ is a combination of rows. $\square$ In words: the part of a relation below any node $u$, read as a functional on the restriction of the list to the block of $u$, kills both halves of the restriction of every solution to the parent block.

**2.5 The top level at rate 1/2 is tame (proved by citation: Dlab and Ringel's theory of tame hereditary algebras over an arbitrary field; checked numerically in Section 4).** Top down, $R = \sum_{q=1}^{4} z_q \otimes R_q$, $R_q$ the row space of the subtree under the child $q$ of a root ($\dim R_q = w_q$), free of the four top twiddles. The general representation of dimension $(2; 1,1,1,1)$ is the regular simple of a homogeneous tube of $\tilde D_4$ with transcendental parameter, so for every fixed $\rho$: $\hom(P_t, \rho) = $ sum of the defects of the preinjective summands of $\rho$ $= \max_{\rho' \subseteq \rho} \langle \delta, \underline{\dim} \rho' \rangle$. Hence **at the top level the identity $\dim R = \min_{R'_q \subseteq R_q} [2 \dim \sum_q R'_q + \sum_q (w_q - \dim R'_q)]$ is a theorem for arbitrary subspaces**, in contrast with five arms (Segre). With three arms it reads $\dim R = \sum_q w_q - \dim \bigcap_q R_q$. The same statement holds for any system with four leaves, which is the code with $k = 2$ at rate 1/2 (used in Theorem 4.2 (d)). For the top-down recursion it is not exploited further: the minimizing $R'_q$ are not row spaces of patterns.

**2.6 Schofield's reduction for a fixed target (sketched: a translation of the derivative lemma into the language of general representations, not used in the proofs below).** Let $I_t \subseteq \rho_F$ be the trace of $P_t$ (the sum of the images of all homomorphisms) and $\tilde I$ its hull over $K_1$. The derivative lemma says $\mathrm{Ext}(P_t, I_t) \to \mathrm{Ext}(P_t, \rho)$ is zero. Hence $\hom(P_t, \rho) = \langle \alpha, \underline{\dim} \tilde I \rangle + \mathrm{ext}(P_t, \tilde I)$ and $\mathrm{Ext}(P_t, \tilde I) \cong \mathrm{Ext}(P_t, \tilde I_F / I_t)$: the one-level identity holds iff $\mathrm{Ext}(P_t, \tilde I_F / I_t) = 0$, and it holds whenever the trace is defined over $K_1$. In Segre's example $\tilde I_F / I_t$ is five points of a conic on $\mathbb P^1 \times \mathbb P^1$ (the quotient system is five pure tensors $\bar u_i \otimes z_i$ in $F^2 \otimes F^2$ spanning a 3-space, i.e. $\bar u_i = \gamma(z_i)$ for a projectivity $\gamma$).

**3.3 Reductions found on the way to a proof (each proved; none closes the general case).**

*(a) Full parts.* For a leaf $j$ put $E_j = \{u \in U_j \otimes F : u \otimes F^2 \subseteq R\}$. Then $R \cap (U_j \otimes F^2) = U_j \otimes z_j + E_j \otimes (0,1)$, and every exact tuple is contained in $(E_j)$. **Constancy criterion (proved):** if $E_\Sigma = \sum_j E_j$ is stable under all $\partial_i$ (for instance defined over $K_1$), the tuple $(E_j)$ is exact. *Proof.* Take a relation modulo $E_\Sigma \otimes F^2$: $\sum_x q_x \otimes z_x = \sum_a e_a \otimes y_a$ with $(e_a)$ a basis of $E_\Sigma$; differentiate in $t_i$: $q_i \otimes (0,1) = -\sum_x \partial_i q_x \otimes z_x + \sum_a (\partial_i e_a \otimes y_a + e_a \otimes \partial_i y_a) \in R$, so $q_i \in E_i$. $\square$ **The full-part tuple is not exact in general, even for three members (exact example):** one triple leaf at $g$ and five pair-class leaves at five other vectors of $W = K^2$: no relation, rank $7 = w$, the unique solution restricted to the block of the triple leaf is $\nu^\perp \otimes z_j^\perp$ with $\nu$ a line moving with $t_j$, so $E_j = \nu \otimes g$ and $\Phi(E) = 8$. So the minimizer has to be built from relations, not from containment.

*(b) The pencil of one leaf.* Let $F'$ be the field without $t_j$, $R_{\ne j}$ the rows of the other leaves, $N_j = (U_j \otimes F'^2) \cap R_{\ne j}$. Then $\dim R = \dim R_{\ne j} + \kappa_j - \dim (N_j \cap (U_j \otimes z_j))$, and $N_j \cap (U_j \otimes z_j)$ is the kernel of the matrix pencil $\Psi_0 + t_j \Psi_1 : U_j \to (N_j^\perp)^*$. Its right minimal indices say how the relation component at $j$ moves with $t_j$; the span of the coefficients of a polynomial kernel vector $\nu(t_j) = \sum_i \nu_i t_j^i$ satisfies $\nu_i \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$ (from $(\nu_i, \nu_{i-1}) \in N_j$ for all $i$): the Hasse-derivative statement of note 74 with its reason. For a triple leaf of a three-member system ($\kappa_j = 2$) the cases are: $\dim N_j \le 1$, or $\dim N_j = 2$ and $N_j \ne \nu_0 \otimes F'^2$: no relation through $j$; $N_j \supseteq \nu_0 \otimes F'^2$ with $\dim N_j \le 3$: component $\nu_0 \otimes g_p$, free of $t_j$; $\dim N_j = 3$ with $N_j^\perp$ spanned by a form of rank 2: the component moves with $t_j$ and $U_j \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$; $N_j$ everything. In all cases $\dim R = \min(\dim R_{\ne j} + 2 - \dim E_j^-,\ \dim (R_{\ne j} + U_j \otimes F^2))$ with $E_j^-$ the full part of $U_j$ in the system without $j$.

*(c) Projective invariance.* $\mathrm{PGL}_2(K_1)$ acts on the twiddles by simultaneous Moebius maps and $R_{g \cdot t} = (1 \otimes g) R_t$; so relation components, full parts and hulls are invariant: they depend on cross-ratios of the $t_x$ only. With at most three leaves involved everything is constant. A moving component needs four twiddles; Segre's failure is a projectivity $z_i \mapsto \bar u_i$ between twiddles and directions.

*(d) Three members are a three-arm problem in the member factor.* For three members and any depth, $r(\mathcal P) = \sum_{c=1}^{3} \dim G_{A_c} - \dim (G_{A_1} \cap G_{A_2} \cap G_{A_3})$, $A_c$ the set of leaves where the two members of the pair $c$ agree and $G_A = \mathrm{span}(g_x : x \in A)$, $\dim G_A = \min(|A|, k)$ (apply a generic element of $\mathrm{GL}_2$ to the member space: the rows become $\mu_c \otimes G_{A_c}$ with three generic $\mu_c$). So the three-member rank problem at any rate is exactly: the dimension of the intersection of three spans of evaluation vectors.

The example in (a) is exact: `scripts/82_fullpart.py` (rank 7, block form of rank one, $\nu \otimes g \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$, $\nu$ different at two values of $t_j$).

**3.4 The canonical certificate: the binary full part (exact at the stated sizes; `scripts/82_big.c`).** For a pattern $\mathcal P$ let $\mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)$ be the upper binary pattern with $\mathcal Q^*_p = \{\theta \in \mathbb F_2^L : \theta \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R(\mathcal P)\}$: the functionals with constant coefficients that kill both bottom slices of every solution at the pair $p$. It is read off the kernel of the system. **Statement (C\*):** $r(\mathcal P) = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P))$. ## 4. What is proved about (C\*)

Notation: $Y^* = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*) \subseteq V$, a subspace defined over $K_1$ with $Y^* \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$; $E_x$ the full part of the leaf $x$ (3.3 (a)); $\Xi_p = \{\nu \in K_1^L : \nu \otimes g_p \in Y^*\}$, whose binary vectors are exactly $\mathcal Q^*_p$.

**Proposition 4.1 (relations modulo the binary full part; proved, any characteristic, any constant subspaces $\Lambda_x$).** Let $\sum_x u_x \otimes z_x \in Y^* \otimes F^2$ with $u_x \in U_x \otimes F$. Then (i) $u_x \in E_x$ for every $x$; (ii) $u_x \in Y^*$ at every leaf whose full part is spanned by vectors $\theta \otimes g_p$ with $\theta$ binary, in particular at every leaf with $\kappa_x = 1$; (iii) if at most two leaves have a full part not contained in $Y^*$, all $u_x \in Y^*$. *Proof.* (i) $Y^*$ does not involve the bottom twiddles, so $\partial_j$ of the identity gives $u_j \otimes (0,1) = \partial_j(\text{right side}) - \sum_x \partial_j u_x \otimes z_x \in R$, and $u_j \otimes z_j \in R$. (ii) A binary $\theta$ with $\theta \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$ lies in $\mathcal Q^*_p$ by definition. (iii) Move the components that lie in $Y^*$ to the right; what is left is $\bar u_j \otimes z_j + \bar u_{j'} \otimes z_{j'} = 0$ in $(V / Y^*) \otimes F^2$ with $z_j, z_{j'}$ independent, so $\bar u_j = \bar u_{j'} = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 ((C\*) in the proved cases; proved).** If every relation modulo $Y^* \otimes F^2$ has its components in $Y^*$, then $R = Y^* \otimes F^2 \oplus \bigoplus_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ ($Q_x$ a complement of $U_x \cap Y^*$ in $U_x$) and $r(\mathcal P) = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*) + \sum_x \mathrm{rank}(\Lambda_x|_{\Delta_p})$, $\Delta_p = \Xi_p^\perp = \mathrm{span}\{N g_p : N \in \ker \mathcal Q^*\}$. By Proposition 4.1 this holds, and (C\*) is a theorem, (a) when every $\kappa_x \le 1$, for arbitrary binary lines, not only differences (matroid union with the minimizer identified as an upper binary pattern); (b) when at most two leaves have $\kappa_x \ge 2$; (c) when at most two leaves have a full part that is not spanned by binary functionals, whatever the class sizes. The weaker (C') (some binary $\mathcal Q$ reaches the rank, not necessarily $\mathcal Q^*$) is a theorem (d) for all binary patterns at $(2, 4)$, any number of members, by 2.5 (four arms: the identity of note 74 holds for arbitrary subspaces and its least minimizer is defined over the perfect field $\mathbb F_2$, so $\Theta = \sum_x \Lambda'_x$ is binary); (e) for class patterns at $k = 2$, any number of leaves and members, by 2.2. (That an exact $\mathcal Q$ makes $\mathcal Q^*$ exact is not proved; it held on every instance.) So in these cases the minimizer of note 74's identity is not only a tuple of subspaces depending on the upper twiddles: **its sum is the row space of a binary pattern one level up**, which is what makes the formula iterate. *Proof of the first sentence.* If $\sum_x q_x \otimes z_x \in Y^* \otimes F^2$ with $q_x \in Q_x \otimes F$, then $q_x \in Y^* \cap Q_x = 0$; and $\dim Q_x = \kappa_x - \dim (\Lambda_x \cap \Xi_p)$ is the rank of the restriction $\Lambda_x \to \Delta_p^*$. $\square$

**What is missing for a proof of (C\*) in general (sharp form).** After Proposition 4.1 the statement is: *a relation modulo $Y^* \otimes F^2$ supported on three or more leaves whose full parts contain functionals depending on the upper twiddles has its components in $Y^*$.* With exactly three such leaves the relation is $\bar u \otimes (c_1 z_1 + c_2 z_2 + c_3 z_3) = 0$ with one constant vector $\bar u \in \bar U_1 \cap \bar U_2 \cap \bar U_3 \ne 0$ in $V / Y^*$, that is $\nu_1 \otimes g_{p_1} \equiv \nu_2 \otimes g_{p_2} \equiv \nu_3 \otimes g_{p_3} \not\equiv 0$ modulo $Y^*$ with $\nu_i \in \Lambda_{x_i} \otimes K_1$ not binary; (C\*) says this never happens for class patterns (then $(Y^* + \langle \nu_1 \otimes g_{p_1} \rangle) \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$ would have to contain a binary pure tensor outside $Y^*$). **Smallest open case: three members, three leaves with triple classes in three different pairs.** It is true at $(4, 8)$ by the exhaustive computation and has no counterexample in 400000 patterns at $(8, 16)$ and $(16, 32)$.

Status of the two cited cases: (d) rests on the theory of tame hereditary algebras over an arbitrary field (Dlab and Ringel: preprojective, regular and preinjective modules of $\tilde D_4$, the regular ones supported at closed points of $\mathbb P^1_{K_1}$, while $P_t$ sits at the transcendental point given by the cross-ratio of the four twiddles); I did not re-derive that theory, so (d) is **proved by citation**, and checked: 400000 random binary systems with 6 and 7 members on four leaves, 233037 with dependent rows, no failure, against one failure in 300000 on five leaves. (e) is **proved by citation** (Brakensiek, Gopi and Makam), the translation of 2.2 checked numerically as above.

**Proposition 4.3 (three members: one integer, and the origin of the functionals that depend on the upper twiddles; proved, any depth, any rate).** Let $A_c$, $c = 1, 2, 3$, be the set of leaves where the two members of the pair $c$ agree ($A_c \cap A_{c'} = D$, the leaves with all three), $G_c = \mathrm{span}(g_x : x \in A_c)$ in the message space $X = K^k$, $\ell_c \in \mathbb F_2^2$ the difference of the pair $c$. Then $R = \sum_c \ell_c \otimes G_c$. Decompose the triple of subspaces $(X; G_1, G_2, G_3)$ into indecomposables (nine types over any field: $(1;000)$, $(1;100)$ and its two images, $(1;110)$ and its two images, $(1;111)$, and three lines in a plane $(2;111)$), and let $n_T$ be the multiplicity of the last one and $K^2 \otimes T$ its isotypic part, with $G_c \cap (K^2 \otimes T) = m_c \otimes T$, $m_3 = m_1 + m_2$. (a) $n_T$ is the failure of inclusion and exclusion, $n_T = \sum_c \dim G_c - \sum_{c < c'} \dim (G_c \cap G_{c'}) + \dim \bigcap_c G_c - \dim \sum_c G_c$, and since every other term is known by the MDS property,
$$r(\mathcal P) = \sum_{c < c'} \min(k, |A_c \cup A_{c'}|) - \min(k, |A_1 \cup A_2 \cup A_3|) - n_T(\mathcal P).$$
So the rank of a three-member pattern is one unknown integer, the number of copies of "three lines in a plane" in $(G_1, G_2, G_3)$. (Check: on `. 012 | 12 12 | 02 02 | 01 01` the formula gives $12 - 4 - n_T$ and the rank is 7, so $n_T = 1$; for three sets of one leaf at $k = 2$ it gives $6 - 2 - 1 = 3$.) (b) For $\nu \in K^2$ not one of the three $\ell_c$: $\nu \otimes h \in R$ iff $h \in G_{(2)} \oplus \phi(\nu) \otimes T$, where $G_{(2)} = \sum_{c < c'} G_c \cap G_{c'}$ and $\phi$ is the linear map with $\phi(\ell_c) = m_c$; for $\nu = \ell_c$: iff $h \in G_c + G_{(2)}$. *Proof.* (a) Count dimensions type by type. (b) $R$ splits along the decomposition; on a summand of type $(1;100)$ it is $\ell_1 \otimes X_i$, on the types with two or three subspaces equal to $X_i$ it is $K^2 \otimes X_i$, and on $K^2 \otimes T$ it is spanned by $\ell_c \otimes m_c \otimes T$, which is $S^2(K^2) \otimes T$ after identifying the two planes by $\phi$; a tensor $\nu \otimes h$ is symmetric iff $h = \phi(\nu) \otimes \tau$. $\square$ Reading: **the functionals $\nu$ that depend on the upper twiddles (the lines $\lambda$ of note 74's obstruction, the non-binary part of the hull) are exactly the points of a conic: $\nu$ is tied by the projectivity $\phi$ to the position of the block in the part of the message space where the three agreement spans are three lines in a plane.** The same statement one level up (for $Y^* = \sum_c \ell_c \otimes W^*_c$) describes $\Xi_p$. What (C\*) adds for three members is a statement about how the planes $g_p \otimes F^2$ sit relative to $G_{(2)}$ and $T$; I could not turn this into a proof, and it is the natural next step (see the smallest open case above).

**Theorem 4.4 (the one-level identity of note 74 with at most four large leaves; proved, the four-arm step by the citation of 2.5; sharp).** Let the $\Lambda_x$ be arbitrary subspaces with constant entries (over $K_1$; no pattern structure needed). Put $\mathcal Q^K_x = \{u \in U_x \text{ defined over } K_1 : u \otimes F^2 \subseteq R\}$ and $Y^K = \sum_x \mathcal Q^K_x$ (the rational full part inside the leaves' own spaces; it may depend on the upper twiddles). If at most four leaves have a full part $E_x$ that is not spanned by vectors defined over $K_1$, in particular **if at most four leaves have $\kappa_x \ge 2$**, then $R = Y^K \otimes F^2 \oplus \bigoplus_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ and $\dim R = \min_{\Lambda'} \Phi(\Lambda') = 2 \dim Y^K + \sum_x \dim U_x / (U_x \cap Y^K)$. With five such leaves the identity is false for arbitrary subspaces (Segre) and for partition patterns of six members (Pappus, Finding 3.2). This replaces "at most two leaves" in Theorem 2.5 of note 74. *Proof.* Let $\sum_x q_x \otimes z_x \in Y^K \otimes F^2$ with $q_x \in Q_x \otimes F$, $Q_x$ a complement of $U_x \cap Y^K$ defined over $K_1$. As in Proposition 4.1 (i), $q_x \in E_x$; where $E_x$ is spanned by rational vectors, $E_x \subseteq Y^K \otimes F$ and $q_x = 0$. The remaining leaves form a set $J$ of at most four, and $\sum_{i \in J} \bar q_i \otimes z_i = 0$ is a relation of the system $(\bar U_i)_{i \in J}$ of constant subspaces of $V / Y^K$. A system with at most four leaves has an exact tuple $\bar\Lambda'_i \subseteq \bar U_i$ defined over $K_1$ (two leaves: no relation at all; three: $\bar\Lambda'_i = \bar U_1 \cap \bar U_2 \cap \bar U_3$; four: 2.5, the subrepresentation of largest defect is a direct summand over $K_1$), it contains the components of every relation, and $\bar\Lambda'_i \otimes F^2 \subseteq \bar R$ (Proposition 2.4 (a) of note 74). A rational vector $e \in U_i$ over $\bar e \in \bar\Lambda'_i$ then has $e \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$, so $e \in \mathcal Q^K_i \subseteq Y^K$ and $\bar e = 0$. Hence $\bar\Lambda' = 0$, the relation is trivial, $q_i \in Q_i \cap Y^K = 0$. $\square$ The tuple $U_x \cap Y^K$ depends on the upper twiddles, so this is the identity of note 74 and not yet (C\*): (C\*) asks in addition that the count does not change when $Y^K$ is replaced by the binary $Y^*$, that is, that the rational vectors $\nu \otimes g_p$ of the full parts that relations use lie in the span of binary pure tensors $\theta \otimes g_{p'}$ of $R$ (Proposition 4.3 says where they come from for three members).

## 5. The structure theorem that (C\*) gives

**Theorem 5.1 (structure of the solutions; part (i) proved unconditionally, parts (ii) to (iv) proved for every pattern for which (C\*) holds, in particular in the cases of Theorem 4.2).** Let $\mathcal P$ be a binary pattern, $\mathcal Q^* = \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)$, $d^{\rm up} = \dim \ker \mathcal Q^*$, and $\Delta_p = \mathrm{span}\{N g_p : N \in \ker \mathcal Q^*\} \subseteq K_1^L$ the space of difference vectors of the upper solutions at the pair $p$. (i) Every solution $M = (M_0, M_1)$ of $\mathcal P$ has both slices in $\ker \mathcal Q^*$; so its block at the pair $p$ lies in $\Delta_p \otimes K^2$, and $\ker \mathcal P \subseteq \ker(\mathcal Q^*) \otimes K^2$. (ii) Inside $\ker(\mathcal Q^*) \otimes F^2$ the leaf $x$ imposes exactly $\rho_x = \mathrm{rank}(\Lambda_x|_{\Delta_{p(x)}}) = \dim \Delta_p - \dim(\Delta_p \cap \Lambda_x^\perp)$ conditions, and the conditions of different leaves are independent: $\dim \ker \mathcal P = 2 d^{\rm up} - \sum_x \rho_x$. (iii) The deficiency is $\delta(\mathcal P) := w - r = \sum_x (\kappa_x - \rho_x) - 2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*)$, and $\kappa_x - \rho_x = \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \Delta_p^\perp) = \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \mathcal Q^*_p) + \epsilon_x$ with $\epsilon_x \ge 0$ the number of independent functionals of the leaf, modulo the binary ones, that depend on the upper twiddles and kill every upper solution at $p$. Hence $\delta(\mathcal P) \le 2\, \delta^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*) + \sum_x \epsilon_x$, with equality iff both leaves of every pair contain $\mathcal Q^*_p$. (iv) $\epsilon_x > 0$ forces $\dim \Delta_p \le L - 1 - \dim \mathcal Q^*_p$ strictly below what binary functionals explain, and the extreme case is $\dim \Delta_p = 1$: **every solution has a block of rank one at $p$, $\delta_p \otimes (b_0, b_1)$ with a fixed direction $\delta_p$, the leaf's condition is the single equation $b_0 + t_x b_1 = 0$ (the lower factor vanishes at $x$) whatever the size of its class, and $\epsilon_x = \kappa_x - 1 - \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \mathcal Q^*_p)$.** In general a leaf whose class has $\kappa_x$ functionals costs only $\rho_x \le \min(\kappa_x, \dim \Delta_p)$. *Proof.* (i) is the definition of $\mathcal Q^*$. (ii) is Theorem 4.2 read on the solutions: $\ker \mathcal P$ is the annihilator of $R = Y^* \otimes F^2 \oplus \bigoplus_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ and $\dim Q_x = \rho_x$. (iii) $w - r = \sum_x \kappa_x - 2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*) - \sum_x \rho_x$; a binary $\theta \in \Lambda_x$ kills $\Delta_p$ iff $\theta \in \mathcal Q^*_p$; and $\sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \mathcal Q^*_p) \le 2 \sum_p \dim \mathcal Q^*_p$. (iv) is (ii) for $\dim \Delta_p = 1$. $\square$

**Corollary 5.2 (iterated form; conditional on (C\*) at every level of the chain).** Put $\mathcal Q^{(0)} = \mathcal P$, $\mathcal Q^{(i+1)} = \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal Q^{(i)})$ (a binary pattern on the nodes of height $i + 1$), $d_i = \dim \ker \mathcal Q^{(i)}$, $\Theta = \sum_{\rm roots} \mathcal Q^{(v)}_{\rm root}$. Unconditionally, all $2^i$ slices of every solution along the bottom $i$ variables solve $\mathcal Q^{(i)}$: $\ker \mathcal P \subseteq \ker(\mathcal Q^{(i)}) \otimes K^{2^i}$ for every $i$, so **every solution is a sum of tensors $A \otimes B$ with $A \in \ker \mathcal Q^{(i)}$ a solution of a binary pattern on the top code at scale $n / 2^i$** (this is the corrected form of "spanned by lifts": the $A \otimes B$ need not be solutions themselves, which is what happens in note 74's sums of two products at $(16, 32)$ and in its 384 orbits with an attached member). Under (C\*): $d_v = L - \dim \Theta$, $d_i = 2 d_{i+1} - \sum_{u \text{ of height } i} \rho^{(i)}_u$, hence
$$\dim \ker \mathcal P = k\,(L - \dim \Theta) - \sum_{i=0}^{v-1} 2^i \sum_{u \text{ of height } i} \rho^{(i)}_u, \qquad r(\mathcal P) = k \dim \Theta + \sum_{i=0}^{v-1} 2^i \sum_{u \text{ of height } i} \rho^{(i)}_u .$$
**This is the exact rank formula in closed form: a generalized Singleton count in which a condition at a node of height $i$ is counted $2^i$ times, and a node costs the rank of its space on the difference vectors of the solutions one level up instead of its dimension.** The generalized Singleton bound is the case $\mathcal Q^{(i)} = 0$ for $i \ge 1$ ($\rho_x = \kappa_x$, $r = w$); the formula of Brakensiek, Gopi and Makam is the case $\mathcal Q^{(i)}_u = \Lambda(P)$ for all $i \ge 1$; the lifts of note 69 are the case $d_i = 1$ at the split level $i$, where the $2^i - 1$ zeros of the lower factor cost one condition each whatever their classes.

**Corollary 5.3 (weight of a list in terms of its block structure; conditional on (C\*) at the bottom level).** If $\mathcal P$ carries a list ($\dim \ker \mathcal P \ge 1$), then $\sum_x \rho_x \le 2 d^{\rm up} - 1$ and $w = \sum_x \rho_x + \sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \Delta_p^\perp) \le 2 d^{\rm up} - 1 + \sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \Delta_{p(x)}^\perp)$. With $n_j$ the number of leaves whose space has rank exactly $j \ge 1$ on $\Delta_p$: $\sum_j j\, n_j \le 2 d^{\rm up} - 1$. For $d^{\rm up} = 1$ (one upper solution $A$, all blocks of rank one) this is: exactly one leaf is not explained by the top list, and $w \le 1 + \sum_x \dim (\Lambda_x \cap \delta_{p(x)}^\perp)$, which is Proposition 4.6 of note 74 at split level 1 with equality. For general $d^{\rm up}$ it bounds the number of leaves that are zeros of lower factors, counted with the rank they see, by twice the dimension of the upper solution space minus one.

## 6. Where the closed recursion stops: lifts of pairwise-agreement configurations, and Pappus (exact at the stated sizes; mechanism proved)

**Finding 6.1 (a new kind of lifted list; exact at the stated size, mechanism proved).** The lifts of note 69 are lifts of a top list around a word (a class pattern of the top code). A lift only needs a top solution of a **partition pattern**: at a top node the members may agree in several disjoint groups, and the leaves of the block take one group each. Example, six members, rate 1/4, $(k, n) = (8, 32)$, top code $[8, 2]$, blocks of four leaves: `012 012 012 012 | 03 03 14 14 | 23 15 23 15 | 05 24 24 05 | 345 345 345 345 | 012345 . . . | . 012345 . . | . . 012345 .` (`82_tmp/pappus_lift.txt` is written by the commands of Section 8). It is a class pattern, so it is six codewords around one word, each agreeing on 11 leaves; $w = 43 > Lk = 40$; rank 39. Its solution is $A \otimes B$ with $B \in K^4$ vanishing on the three leaves with all six members and $A$ the solution of the Pappus pattern of Remark 3.5 on the top code, which exists only because of Pappus's theorem (ten equations of rank nine in ten unknowns).

**The chain of binary full parts on this example (`VERBOSE=1 82_big`):** at $(8, 32)$: $w = 43$, rank 39, $\mathcal Q^*$ is the Pappus pattern with every node doubled (weight 20, rank 18), $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 2 \cdot 18 + 3 = 39$: (C\*) holds. At $(4, 16)$: that pattern has rank 18, its $\mathcal Q^*$ is the Pappus pattern (weight 10, rank 9), $B = 18$: (C\*) holds. At $(2, 8)$: the Pappus pattern has rank 9, $\mathcal Q^* = 0$, $B = 10$: **(C\*) fails**. So for class patterns with six members at rate 1/4 the one-level statement (C\*) survives at the bottom, with the true upper ranks, and the closed form of Corollary 5.2 does not: the chain ends in an atom whose rank is given by an incidence theorem of the plane and not by any count of shrunk subspaces.

So in the characteristic of the code there are atoms that are not incidence theorems of every plane; I did not find the geometric statement behind them (in characteristic 2 each double pair contains the all-ones vector of its four members, which is how the three matchings of Finding 3.1 arise, but here the minimum over binary $\Theta$ does not see the dependency). Lifted to a class pattern the second kind has pair classes only outside the zeros of the lower factor, so it stays inside the generalized Singleton bound; the Pappus kind does not (Finding 6.1). Both kinds satisfy Segre's condition in characteristic 2 (checked by hand on the two examples): the Pluecker vector of a graphic plane is the edge set of a triangle or of a 4-cycle on the members, and the five edge sets have sum zero modulo 2; with signs the sum vanishes for the Pappus kind only, which is why the other kind disappears in odd characteristic.

Exhaustive at $k = 2$ (`82_k2.c multi`; computer-assisted theorems, since for $k = 2$ the bound has no upper rank in it): for **four members** every multiset of 5, 6, 7 or 8 proper nonzero binary subspaces (8568, 27132, 77520, 203490 systems) and for **five members** every multiset of 5 (11238513 systems, 2113150 with rank below $\min(w, 2L)$) and every multiset of 6 (131115985 systems, 9313105 with rank below $\min(w, 2L)$; `82_k2 multi 5 6`, run separately, about ten minutes) satisfies $r = \min_\Theta$. So the atoms need six members, in characteristic 2 as well.

## 7. What remains, in order

1. **(C\*) for three members (conjectured; smallest open case above).** After Propositions 4.1 and 4.3 it is a statement about three subspaces $G_1, G_2, G_3$ of the message space, each spanned by evaluation vectors: the planes $g_p \otimes F^2$ that meet the "three lines in a plane" part must do so through binary pure tensors. A proof would give an exact, purely combinatorial rank formula for every three-member pattern at every depth and rate, closed in a class of five spaces a node, and with it the exact value of $\dim(G_{A_1} \cap G_{A_2} \cap G_{A_3})$ for any three sets of leaves.
2. **That an exact $\mathcal Q$ makes $\mathcal Q^*$ exact (conjectured; held on every instance).** It would merge (C') and (C\*).
3. **Four and five members (conjectured, no failure found):** the closed recursion over binary patterns (16 and 67 spaces a node). The exhaustive checks at $k = 2$ say that no incidence theorem of the plane enters before six members.
4. **Six and more members.** Classify the atoms at $k = 2$ beyond five leaves and six members (on five leaves with six members they are the 60 Pappus configurations and the 72 configurations special to characteristic 2 of Section 6; the latter are not understood), and decide whether atoms exist at rate 1/2. At rate 1/2 the first possible place is $(4, 8)$; 120000 random patterns with six members found none.
5. **From the structure theorem to a list bound (not attempted).** Corollary 5.2 turns a list into a chain of binary patterns $\mathcal Q^{(i)}$ on the top codes with solution spaces of dimensions $d_i$, $d_i = 2 d_{i+1} - \sum_u \rho^{(i)}_u$, $d_0 \ge 1$. A bound on list sizes needs a bound on how much weight a node can carry for a given $\rho$, that is on $\dim(\Lambda_u \cap \Delta^\perp)$, in terms of the number of members; for $d_{i+1} = 1$ this is the count of note 74 (Proposition 4.6), and the open part is $d_{i+1} \ge 2$ (the sums of two products at $(16, 32)$ and the staircases).
6. **Random twiddles over GF($2^{64}$).** Everything here is generic. The formula is a statement about which minors vanish identically, so a version with slack needs the degrees of the nonvanishing minors along the chain; not looked at.

## 8. Scripts

All in `doc/whir_soundness_gap_notes/scripts/`, C with `69_gf.h` (GF($2^{64}$)), no dependency; `82_run.sh` builds everything into `82_bin/` and regenerates `82_output.txt` (about fifteen minutes on one core; `FIVE=1 ./82_run.sh` adds the five-member tables at $(4, 8)$, about ten more minutes; `82_bin/82_k2 multi 5 6` and `82_bin/82_k2_classify planes 6 graphic` are run by hand).

- `82_rank.c`: partition patterns. `table v r m out.bin` (all ranks at depth $v$, $2^r$ trees, $m$ members), `all`, `rand`, `file`: the exact rank against $\min_{\mathcal Q} B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q)$ with the table one level up.
- `82_binary.c`: the same for binary patterns (generated from `82_rank.c`; the leaf alphabet is the set of subspaces of $\mathbb F_2^L$). In its output a space is printed by a basis, each vector as the even set of members it is the indicator of (`01+23` is the double pair, `0123` the all-ones vector of four members).
- `82_big.c`: (C\*) with upper ranks computed on demand: the binary full part $\mathcal Q^*$ from the kernel, then coordinate descent if needed. `rand`, `pert` (a random pattern one layer up, every leaf doubled, some leaves redrawn), `file ... [lift]`; `VERBOSE=1` prints $w$, $Lk$, the rank, $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*)$ and $\mathcal Q^*$.
- `82_k2.c`: the case $k = 2$: `rand`, `multi m n` (every multiset of $n$ proper nonzero binary subspaces), `planes 6 graphic` (every five-subset of the 65 graphic planes of six members: the 132 Pappus-type systems); `ONLY_GRAPHIC_THETA=1` restricts $\Theta$ to partitions.
- `82_k2_classify.c`: `82_k2.c planes` printing all 132 failing systems with their number of triangles; its output is `82_planes6_output.txt`.
- `82_pentagon.py`: the two kinds of atoms modulo a large prime (Pappus: rank 9; five double pairs: rank 10).
- `82_fullpart.py`: the example of 3.3 (a).
- Patterns used in the text are written to `82_tmp/` by `82_run.sh`: `p76.txt`, `p80.txt`, `basic.txt`, `pappus.txt`, `pappus_mid.txt`, `pappus_lift.txt`.
