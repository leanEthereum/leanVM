## 1. Re-derivation of the conditional statement

I re-derived the definitions and inequality (2) of note 35 and agree with note 41 on every point. Three remarks that matter below.

**1.1 The local rank [checked].** `40_checks.py A` computes over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$, for $1 \le m \le 8$ and $0 \le b \le m$, the space of polynomials of $Y_1$-degree $\le b$ in the contact ideal $\mathfrak{c}_m$ (truncated at degree $m + 2$ in $(T, S)$): the rank of the conditions on the rectangle is $R(m, b)$, the closed sum of note 35.

**1.2 The leading-order form of (2), with its $b$ term.** With $B = \beta m$, $W = wn$, $\bar o = \frac1n \sum o_\alpha$, a component is cut by Lemma C at leading order iff $bJ(\beta) + \beta[w(a/\rho - \beta/2) - \bar o(1 - \beta/2)] > 0$ for some $0 < \beta < \min(1/2, a/\rho - 1)$ (the second bound is the side condition $\Delta(m + B) \le mA - W$). So the exact threshold is
$$w_C(a, b, \bar o) = \min_{\beta} \frac{\bar o\,(1 - \beta/2) - bJ(\beta)/\beta}{a/\rho - \beta/2},$$
not $e^* \bar o$ with a fixed $\beta$: the minimizing $\beta$ depends on $b$ and $\bar o$ ($0.327$ for $(b, \bar o) = (1, 4)$, $0.158$ for $(2, 4)$ at $a = 0.70$). `40_window.py` prints this next to the exact integer threshold at $n = 2^{25}$. For contact on all lines the exact threshold decreases with $m$ towards the leading-order value (for $(1, 4)$: $2.6887$ at $m = 480$, $2.6876$ at $m = 960$, against $2.6865$; for $(2, 4)$: $2.6134$ at $m = 480$ against $2.6098$), in agreement with the values $2.688, 2.613, 2.521$ of note 41 **[checked]**. For profiles concentrated on half of the lines the exact threshold at small $m$ is lower than the leading-order value by up to $0.05\,n$, because $R(m, \cdot) - R(m - o, \cdot)$ is smaller than its linearization in $o$ (the convexity that Lemma U of note 41 uses); the tables below use the exact integers.

**1.3 Proposition 6 of note 35 at leading order, uniformly in the profile [proved at leading order].** For a leading coefficient in $F[X]$, Lemma L gives $\bar o \le 2(w - b\rho)$ and $\bar o \le w + b(1 - \rho)$. The left side of the criterion is decreasing in $\bar o$, and with $\bar o$ at the smaller bound it is piecewise linear in $w$ with slopes $\beta(a/\rho - 2 + \beta/2) < 0$ and $\beta(a/\rho - 1) > 0$ on the two sides of $w = b(1 + \rho)$. So its minimum over all profiles is $b\{J(\beta) + \beta[(1 + \rho)a/\rho - 2 + \beta(1 - \rho)/2]\}$, which at $(\rho, a, \beta) = (1/2, 0.70, 0.3)$ is $0.065\,b > 0$. This is the uniform statement behind the 150 profiles of note 35; the exact-integer version is Lemma U of note 41.

## 2. New constraints

### 2.1 Lemma N: the coefficients of a first integral at a received point

**Lemma N [proved; checked `40_checks.py A`].** Let $Q \in F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$ have $Y_1$-degree $\le b$ and lie in the contact ideal $\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$. Then every coefficient $Q_c(X, Y_0)$ of $Q = \sum_c Q_c Y_1^c$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{m - b}$. This holds in every characteristic. If $Q = \hat G$ for a first integral $G \in I_\alpha^j$, every coefficient lies in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{\max(j,\ 2j - b)}$.

*Proof.* Substitute $S = Ts$. Then $w = S - TY_1 = T(s - Y_1)$, and a generator $T^i w^l Y_1^e$ of $\mathfrak{c}_m$ ($i + 2l \ge m$) becomes $T^{i + l}(s - Y_1)^l Y_1^e$. So the coefficient $\Psi_N(s, Y_1)$ of $T^N$ in $Q(\alpha + T, y_\alpha + Ts, Y_1)$ is divisible by $(s - Y_1)^{m - N}$ for $N < m$. It has $Y_1$-degree $\le b$ and $(s - Y_1)^{m - N}$ is monic of degree $m - N$ in $Y_1$, so $\Psi_N = 0$ when $m - N > b$. The coefficient of $T^N s^q Y_1^c$ in $Q(\alpha + T, y_\alpha + Ts, Y_1)$ is the coefficient of $T^{N - q} S^q$ in $Q_c$: all monomials of $Q_c$ of total degree $N < m - b$ vanish. For the second claim, $G = \sum_{c + d \ge j} \gamma_{cd}(v)\,t_1^c t_2^d$ with $t_1 = T^2$, $t_2 = S - TY_1$, and each term lies in $(T, S)^{2c + d}[Y_1] \subset \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^j[Y_1]$; and $\hat G \in \mathfrak{c}_{2j}$. $\square$

**Corollary N [proved].** Let $G$ be a first integral with $b \ge 1$ and $P$ a polynomial of degree $\le \Delta$ with agreement set $S_P$. Either $\hat G_b(X, P(X)) = 0$, or
$$\sum_{\alpha \in S_P} \max(j_\alpha,\ 2j_\alpha - b) \ \le\ W - b\Delta .$$
If $\hat G_b$ involves $Y_0$, the first case occurs for at most $\deg_{Y_0} \hat G_b \le \delta - b$ polynomials $P$ (sections of $G$ or not). So if the sum of the $A$ smallest values of $\max(j_\alpha, 2j_\alpha - b)$ exceeds $W - b\Delta$, the whole list has at most $\delta - b$ members.

*Proof.* $\hat G_b(X, P(X))$ has degree $\le W - b\Delta$ and vanishes at $\alpha \in S_P$ to the order of $\hat G_b$ at $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$. $\square$

For contact 4 this is Section 4.3 of note 41 ($\max(2, 4 - b) = 3, 2, 2$ for $b = 1, 2, 3$). For contact 6 it gives $5, 4, 3$ and for contact 8 it gives $7, 6, 5$, which note 41 does not have. All $Y_1$-coefficients satisfy the same bound, not only the leading one; I found no use for the others.

### 2.2 Lemma M: the multiplicity along a line is at most the degree

**Lemma M [proved].** If $G$ is irreducible in $F(\tau)[u, v]$ and primitive, then $j_\alpha \le \delta$ for every $\alpha$; hence $o_\alpha \le 2\delta \le 2W/\Delta$.

*Proof.* $G \in I_\alpha^j$ implies that $G(\alpha^2, u, v)$, of degree $\le \delta$, is divisible by $(u + \alpha v - y_\alpha)^j$. If $j > \delta$ it is zero, so $\tau - \alpha^2$ divides $G$. $\square$

### 2.3 First integrals with $b = 1$

By Proposition 7 of note 35, $\hat G = \partial E = E_X + Y_1 E_{Y_0}$ for some $E \in F[X, Y_0]$, determined modulo $F[X^2, Y_0^2]$. Write $a = E_{Y_0}$, $c = E_X$.

**Proposition 9 [proved; part 1 checked for $j = 1, 2, 3$ by `40_checks.py B`].**

1. (Criterion.) $\partial E$ has contact order $\ge 2j$ at $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ iff $E \in F[T^2, S^2] + \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j}$, that is, iff the Taylor coefficients $e_{rq}$ of $E$ at the point with $r + q < 2j$ and $(r, q)$ not both even vanish: $(3j^2 + j)/2$ linear conditions (2 for $j = 1$, 7 for $j = 2$, 15 for $j = 3$).
2. (Normal form.) The first integrals with $b \le 1$ are exactly $G = \mathsf{C}(\tau, q) + v\,\mathsf{A}(\tau, q) + u\,\mathsf{B}(\tau, q)$ with $q = u^2 + \tau v^2$ and $\mathsf{A}, \mathsf{B}, \mathsf{C} \in F[\tau, q]$ arbitrary; in jet coordinates $q = Y_0^2$, $a = \mathsf{A} + X\mathsf{B}$, $c = \mathsf{C} + Y_0\mathsf{B}$, $E = Y_0\mathsf{A} + X\mathsf{C} + XY_0\mathsf{B}$, and $E_{XY_0} = \mathsf{B}$. Over a perfect field $\mathsf{A}, \mathsf{B}, \mathsf{C}$ are squares of polynomials in $(X, Y_0)$.
3. (Contact 4.) At a point of contact $\ge 4$: $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^3$, $E_{XY_0} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^2$, the leading forms are $a_3 = T\gamma$ and $c_3 = S\gamma$ with $\gamma = e_{31}T^2 + e_{13}S^2$ the leading form of $E_{XY_0}$, and $Tc + Sa \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^5$. In the fibre, $(Y_0 - y_\alpha)^4$ divides $a(\alpha, Y_0)$ and $(Y_0 - y_\alpha)^3$ divides $c(\alpha, Y_0)$. For general $j$: $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j - 1}$, $a_{2j-1} = T\gamma$, $c_{2j-1} = S\gamma$ with $\gamma$ a form of degree $2j - 2$ in $F[T^2, S^2]$.
4. (Shape in the window.) If $W < 6\Delta$ then $E = \sum_{k \le 5} e_k(X) Y_0^k$ modulo squares, $a = e_5 Y_0^4 + e_3 Y_0^2 + e_1$, and $e_3$ vanishes at every $\alpha$ of contact $\ge 4$. If $e_5 = 0$ and the contact is 4 on all lines, every list member with more than $(W - \Delta - n)/2$ agreements is a root of $a$.
5. (The case $E_{XY_0} = 0$.) Then $a = \mathsf{A}_0^2$ and $c = \mathsf{C}_0^2$ are squares of polynomials that lie in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^2$ at every point of contact $\ge 4$, and a list member with $s$ agreements on such points is a root of $\mathsf{A}_0$ unless $W \ge 4s + \Delta$. With contact 4 on all lines this is $W \ge 4A + \Delta = 3.3\,n$ at $a = 0.70$: closed.

*Proof.* (1) By the remark after Lemma 0 of note 35, the contact order of $Q$ is the generic order of vanishing of $Q(X, P, P')$ over polynomials (or power series) $P$ through the point (the lowest part $\sum_{i + 2l = o} a_{il}(c_1) c_2^l\,T^o$ is a nonzero polynomial in the free Taylor coefficients $c_1, c_2$ of $P$). Here $(\partial E)(X, P, P') = \frac{d}{dX}E(X, P(X))$, so the contact order is $\ge 2j$ iff the coefficients of $T^1, T^3, \dots, T^{2j - 1}$ in $E(\alpha + T, P(\alpha + T))$ vanish for all $P$. If $E \in F[T^2, S^2] + \mathfrak{m}^{2j}$ they do, since $S(T)^2$ is even in $T$. The converse is the linear algebra of `40_checks.py B` for $j \le 3$ (for $j = 2$ by hand: the coefficient of $T^3$ is $e_{30} + e_{21}c_1 + e_{12}c_1^2 + e_{03}c_1^3 + e_{11}c_2 + e_{01}c_3$). (2) is the proof of Proposition 7 of note 35 read backwards. (3) $E = e_{00} + e_{20}T^2 + e_{02}S^2 + E_4 + E_5 + \dots$ by (1); differentiate. Euler: $TE_T + SE_S = \sum_d d\,E_d = E_5 + E_7 + \dots$, which also gives $Tc_3 + Sa_3 = 4E_4 = 0$. The fibre statement is the restriction to $T = 0$, where $a$ is even in $S$. (4) $e_3(\alpha)$ is the coefficient of $S^2$ in $a(\alpha, y_\alpha + S)$. If $e_5 = 0$ then $a = \Lambda \tilde a$ with $\Lambda = \prod_\alpha (X - \alpha)$ dividing $e_3$ and $e_1$ (from $a(\alpha, Y_0) = 0$), and $\tilde a(X, P)$ has degree $\le W - \Delta - n$ and double zeros at the agreements. (5) $a_X = c_{Y_0} = 0$ puts $a, c$ in $F[X^2, Y_0^2]$; a square in $\mathfrak{m}^3$ has its root in $\mathfrak{m}^2$. $\square$

### 2.4 Lemma B: Bézout for the singular scheme of the foliation

For $b = 1$ the surface $\Sigma_G$ contains the line of a point $(x_0, y_0)$ iff $a(x_0, y_0) = c(x_0, y_0) = 0$. The lines of the word are among them, with a multiplicity that grows with the contact order, and the number of common zeros of two coprime curves is bounded.

**Lemma B [proved; checked `40_bezout.py`].** Let $\hat G = \partial E$ be absolutely irreducible with $b = 1$. Then $a = E_{Y_0}$ and $c = E_X$ are coprime, the intersection number of the curves $a = 0$ and $c = 0$ at a received point of contact order $o = 2j \ge 2$ is at least $(2j - 1)^2 + 2j - 2 = o^2 - o - 1$, and
$$\sum_{\alpha :\ o_\alpha \ge 2} (o_\alpha^2 - o_\alpha - 1) \ \le\ \deg_X \mathrm{Res}_{Y_0}(a, c) \ \le\ D_c(W - \Delta) + D_a W - \Delta D_a D_c \ \le\ \frac{W(W - \Delta)}{\Delta},$$
with $D_a = \deg_{Y_0} a$, $D_c = \deg_{Y_0} c$. For $W < 6\Delta$ the middle term is at most $9W - 25\Delta$.

*Proof.* $\hat G = c + Y_1 a$ is linear in $Y_1$, and $(u, v) \mapsto (Y_0, Y_1)$ is linear over $F(X)$, so $G$ is absolutely irreducible iff $a$ and $c$ are coprime in $F(X)[Y_0]$. The coefficient of $Y_0^k$ has degree $\le W - \Delta - k\Delta$ in $a$ and $\le W - k\Delta$ in $c$; the resultant is isobaric of weight $D_a D_c$, which gives the degree bound, and $(W - \Delta - \Delta D_a)(W - \Delta D_c) \ge 0$ gives the last inequality. The multiplicity of the root $X = \alpha$ of the resultant is at least the sum of the intersection numbers at the points above $\alpha$. Local number: blow up the received point, $S = Ts$. By Proposition 9 part 3, $a = T^{2j-1}\tilde a$, $c = T^{2j-1}\tilde c$ with $\tilde a|_{T = 0} = \gamma(1, s)$, and $\tilde c - s\tilde a = T\,\Phi$ because $Tc + Sa \in \mathfrak{m}^{2j+1}$ (in characteristic 2, $- s = s$). So the intersection number is $(2j - 1)^2 + \sum_q I_q(\tilde a, \tilde c)$ over the points $q$ of the exceptional line, and $I_q(\tilde a, \tilde c) = I_q(\tilde a, T\Phi) \ge I_q(\tilde a, T) = \mathrm{ord}_q\,\gamma$. The same computation in the other chart covers $s = \infty$. The zeros of the form $\gamma$ on the exceptional line number $2j - 2$. If $\gamma = 0$ then $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}^{2j}$ and the number is at least $4j^2$. $\square$

**[checked]** `40_bezout.py` on the first integrals found by `40_scale` (it also re-verifies the 7 Taylor conditions, the multiplicity 3 of $a$ and $c$, and the weighted degree independently of the C code): for a random word at $n = 32$ ($W = 89$) the resultant has degree 422 $\le 9W - 25\Delta = 426$ and the multiplicity of every $\alpha \in D$ is exactly 11; for the family of Section 5 at $n = 32, 64, 128$ the degree is exactly $9W - 25\Delta$ and the multiplicities are 11 at every nonzero $\alpha$ and 23 at $\alpha = 0$; with contact order 6 imposed (15 conditions per point, `J=3`) on a random word at $n = 16$ and $n = 32$ the multiplicity of every $\alpha$ is exactly $29 = 6^2 - 6 - 1$ and the degree is $516 \le W(W - \Delta)/\Delta = 521$, respectively $1016 \le 1046$.

**Consequences.** With contact 4 on all $n$ lines and $W < 6\Delta$: $11n \le 9W - 25\Delta$, so $W \ge (11n + 25\Delta)/9 = 2.611\,n$ at rate 1/2. This is an existence bound: it does not involve $a$. With contact $o$ on $xn$ lines and none elsewhere it gives $x(o^2 - o - 1) \le 2w(w - 1/2)$, which together with $w < w_C$ excludes contact 6 and 8 at $a = 0.70$ and contact 4 on fewer than $0.95\,n$ lines (Section 3). The lemma is consistent with the flat cubic at rate 1/4 ($n - 1 \le (3\Delta + 1)(2\Delta + 1)/\Delta \approx 1.5\,n$). I found no useful analogue for $b \ge 2$: the natural one (two specializations $\hat G(X, Y_0, \lambda Y_0 + \mu)$, curves of weighted degree $W$ with multiplicity $\max(j, 2j - b)$ at the received points) would give at best $\sum_\alpha \max(j_\alpha, 2j_\alpha - b)^2 \le W^2/\Delta$, which is far from the window.

Other pairs give less. Write $a = \mathsf{A}_0^2 + X\mathsf{B}_0^2$ with $\mathsf{B}_0^2 = E_{XY_0}$. At a point of contact 4, $a = (\mathsf{A}_0 + \sqrt\alpha\,\mathsf{B}_0)^2 + T\mathsf{B}_0^2$ with $\mathsf{A}_0 + \sqrt\alpha\,\mathsf{B}_0 \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^2$ and $\mathsf{B}_0 \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha$, so the curves $a$ and $\mathsf{B}_0$ meet at least 4 times there, and when they are coprime $4n \le (W - \Delta)^2/(2\Delta)$: $W \ge \Delta + \sqrt{8n\Delta} = 2.5\,n$ at rate 1/2 **[proved]**. The vanishing of $e_3$ on $D$ (Proposition 9 part 4) gives the same $W \ge 3\Delta + n$ when $e_3 \ne 0$.

### 2.5 Why I see no slack in Lemma C at a singular line **[leading order, not a theorem]**

A natural hope was that a line of multiplicity $j$ costs Lemma C as if all $j$ sheets of $\Sigma_G$ through it carried sections, while fewer do. The tangent cone of $G$ along $\ell_\alpha$, $\sum_{c + d = j} \gamma_{cd}(v)\,\sigma^d$ (a section through the line at $v = P'(\alpha)$ has $\sigma$ equal to its second Hasse derivative at $\alpha$), has degree at most $\min(b, j)$ in $\sigma$ **[proved: the coefficient of $T^N$ in the proof of Lemma N vanishes for $N < 2j - b$]**. So for $b = 1$, $j = 2$ exactly one sheet carries sections; the other is tangent to the fibre $\tau = \alpha^2$ (the fibre of $\hat G$ at $X = \alpha$ contains the line three times). But the number of conditions of Lemma C for $(o, b) = (4, 1)$, $R(m, B) - R(m - 4, B - 1) \approx 3Bm - B^2 + m^2/2$, is already the number of conditions for vanishing to order $m$ along that single sheet: writing a function on $\Sigma_G$ as $Q^*/a^B$ with $Q^* = \sum_e Q_e c^e a^{B - e}$ bivariate, one needs $Q^* \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{3B + m}$, and the coefficient of $T^{3B + k}$ of $Q^*$ on the exceptional line is a polynomial of degree $\le 3B + k$ in the slope that vanishes to order $2(B - k)_+$ at the one bad slope (the double root of $\gamma$), which gives $\sum_{k < m}(3B + k + 1) - \sum_{k < B} 2(B - k) = 3Bm - B^2 + m^2/2$. The cost of a singular line is the pole of the higher Taylor coefficients of the sections at the bad slope, not a second sheet. I did not find an interpolation space that avoids it.

## 4. What kind of problem the residual is

**The curve.** For $b = 1$, a polynomial $P$ is a section of $\partial E$ iff $E(X, P(X)) \in F[X^2]$ (note 35, Proposition 7), that is, iff $(Y, Z) = (P, \theta)$ with $\theta^2 = E(X, P)$ is a point of the affine curve $C_E : Z^2 = E(X, Y)$ over $K = F(X)$ with polynomial coordinates. In the window $E$ is a quintic in $Y$, with $e_5 \ne 0$ in the cases that matter (Proposition 9 part 4). Over the perfect closure of $K$ the curve is rational: $E = \epsilon(Y)^2 + Y\omega(Y)^2$, so $Y = ((Z + \epsilon)/\omega)^2$. Its plane model has arithmetic genus 2. For two rational points $(P, \theta)$, $(P', \theta')$ the divisor $2[P] - 2[P']$ is the divisor of $(Y - P)/(Y - P')$, because the function field is a purely inseparable extension of degree 2 of $K(Y)$ and a rational point is totally ramified over its image: the classes of the rational points differ by 2-torsion **[proved]**. The received points of contact 4 are the points where $Z^2 = E$ has a singularity of the form $z^2 = TS\,\ell(T, S)^2 + (\text{order} \ge 5)$, whose weighted blow-up has for exceptional curve the cuspidal curve $z_1^2 = s\,\ell(1, s)^2$ of arithmetic genus 1 (those of contact 2 are $z^2 = TS$); a list member is a section through $A$ of them **[local computation, not used elsewhere]**. At rate 1/4 the same dictionary gives a cubic, a quasi-elliptic fibration, and the flats are $n - 1$ of its sections.

**What the literature says [read].** [V91, Theorem 3]: over a function field $K$ in one variable over a finite field, a curve whose genus relative to $K$ differs from its absolute genus has finitely many $K$-rational points. The proof of its Theorem 1 bounds the number of points by a constant times the number of elements of a Riemann-Roch space $L(D)$ of $K$ whose degree depends on the coefficients: exponential in their height. [V95]: for $p > 2$ and $q = p^n$ the curves $x - (t + t^{q + 2} + \dots)x^p = y^p$ over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ have absolute genus 0, relative genus $(p - 1)(p - 2)/2$ independent of $n$, and at least $p^{2^n/2n}$ rational points; for $p = 3$ they are quasi-elliptic and the result says that the Mordell-Weil rank is unbounded ([V95, Remark 2], which cites Ito for characteristic 2; I did not read Ito). I did not verify that $C_E$ has positive relative genus for the family of Section 5 (it needs regularity, which should follow from $\gcd(a, c) = 1$), so I do not claim finiteness from [V91].

**Reading.** The number of sections of an economical $b = 1$ component is finite but nothing in the general theory bounds it polynomially in $n$; the two classical arguments (the derivation $P' = c(X, P)/a(X, P)$, and $\delta^p = 0$) are exactly Step 2 of the large-characteristic proofs and give nothing here because the foliation is 2-closed (it has the first integral $E$): this is Proposition 3 of note 35 again. A proof of (S) through sections has to use the height bound $\deg P \le \Delta$ and the passage through $A$ singular points. I did not find one.

## 5. An explicit singular first integral of efficiency $11/16$

**Observation 12 [checked: `40_scale` for $N = 5, \dots, 9$; `40_bezout.py` for $N = 5, 6, 7$; several random subspaces at $N = 5, 7, 8$; not proved for general $N$].** Let $D$ be a random $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of $\mathrm{GF}(2^{16})$ of dimension $N \in \{5, \dots, 9\}$, $n = 2^N$, $\Delta = n/2 - 1$, and $y_\alpha = \alpha^{3n/4 - 1}$. Among the polynomials $E$ whose total derivative has contact order $\ge 4$ at all $n$ received points, the smallest weighted degree of $\partial E$ is $W = 11n/4 - 2$, attained by one $E$ up to scalars and squares. It has degree 5 in $Y_0$, $\deg e_5 = n/4 + 3$, and $\gcd(E_{Y_0}, E_X) = 1$, so $\partial E$ is absolutely irreducible with $b = 1$ and leading coefficient $E_{Y_0}$ involving $Y_0$.

The support of $E$ is structured: at $n = 64$ the coefficient of $Y_0^5$ is $X^3$ times a linearized polynomial of degree $n/4$ (exponents $4, 5, 7, 11, 19$), the coefficient of $Y_0^4$ is the single monomial $X^{3n/4 + 3}$, and the exponents of the other coefficients are of the form constant plus $2^i$ (so $W = 5\Delta + \deg e_5 = 5(n/2 - 1) + n/4 + 3$). The word $X^{n/2}L_V(X)$ with $V \subset D$ of size $n/4$ has $W_{\min} = 11n/4 - 3$ at $n = 32, 64, 128$.

**Where it stands against Lemma C [exact integers, `40_window.py 3`, $n = 2^{25}$, $m \in \{240, 480, 960\}$].** Efficiency $11/16 - 1/(2n)$. The cut of Lemma C for contact 4 on all lines, $b = 1$, is at $2.7526\,n$ for $a = 0.6905$, $2.7491\,n$ for $a = 0.691$, $2.7428\,n$ for $a = 0.6919$, $2.7224\,n$ for $a = 0.6949$, $2.6876\,n$ for $a = 0.70$. So the family is cut at the three certified agreements of Theorem 8', and is not cut for $a_1 = 0.6899 < a < 0.6907$ (leading order $w_C = 2.75$ at $a \approx 0.6907$). At $a = 0.6905$ the exact threshold was computed for 13 multiplicities between 20 and 1440: it decreases from $3.26\,n$ to $2.7521\,n$ and stays above $2.75\,n$, towards the leading-order value $2.7511\,n$. Every polynomial with agreement above $11n/16$ is a section (Proposition 11), and Corollary N kills only agreements above $0.75\,n$. I could not decide whether it is a fixed component of an explainer space for such $a$ (the smallest certificates at toy sizes have more than $6 \cdot 10^4$ unknowns), nor compute its sections.

**Its list [checked, `40_list`].** At $n = 32$ on the random subspace used above, information-set decoding with $2 \cdot 10^7$ random 16-subsets finds no polynomial of degree $< 16$ with agreement $\ge 18$ with $X^{23}$; a member with agreement 19 would have been found with probability $1 - e^{-32}$ and one with agreement 18 with probability $0.994$. So this singular component exists without any close codeword: it is an example for the second alternative of the residual ("few sections"), not a threat. It is not one of the known list families: those sit at agreements $5n/8$ and $9n/16$, where this word has no codeword at $n = 32$; and the word of the $5/8$ family, $X^{n/2}L_W$ with $|W| = n/8$, has degree $5n/8 < A$, so a curve of low degree passes through its graph, and in the scan it has no coprime solution with $W \le 11n/4 - 4$.

**What it changes.** (i) Existence statements of the form "no absolutely irreducible first integral with contact 4 on all lines has efficiency below $c$" are false for $c > 11/16$ on subspace domains. Note 35, Section 8 item 1 asks for $c \ge 0.69$; the needed constant is $e^*(a)$: $0.672$ at $a = 0.70$, $0.681$ at $0.6949$, $0.686$ at $0.6919$, all below $11/16$. (ii) The gap between $11/16$ and $e^*(0.6919) = 0.6857$ is $0.002$: at the smallest certified agreement the family misses the window by $0.007\,n$ in $W$. (iii) For $a_1 < a < 0.6907$ a statement of type (S) is in doubt unless the family is not fixed; a proof of (S) by an efficiency bound must fail there.

## 6. A parameter count for $b = 1$, contact 4 on all lines **[heuristic]**

For $2.5 \le w \le 3$ the polynomials $E$ modulo squares with $W(\partial E) \le wn$ have $(4.5w - 6)n + O(1)$ coefficients; the conditions number $7n$. For a random word a solution is expected iff $4.5w - 6 > 7$, $w > 26/9$, which the table of Section 5 confirms. Counting the $n$ values of the word as unknowns of a nonlinear system, solutions are not excluded for $w \ge 8/3 = 2.667$, a set of words of dimension about $(4.5w - 12)n$. The window at $a = 0.70$ is $[2.611, 2.688)$: it meets $[2.667, 2.688)$. At $a \ge 0.704$ the cut $w_C$ is below $8/3$. This is not a proof of anything: determinantal loci can be empty, and the structured examples sit at $2.75$, above the count. It says that Lemma B ($2.611$) is not sharp and that a bound $W \ge 2.688\,n$ would have to see more than the number of conditions.

## 7. The exact remaining case

At $n = 2^{25}$ to $2^{28}$, rate 1/2, $a = 0.70$, certificate $(18, 5)$, Theorem 8' of note 41 holds with the class $\mathcal{G}'$ of Proposition 10. To make it unconditional it suffices to bound, for every word, the sections with agreement $\ge A$ of the fixed, absolutely irreducible first-integral components with leading coefficient involving $Y_0$ of the following kinds, or to show there are none:

1. $b = 1$: $\hat G = \partial E$, $E$ a quintic in $Y_0$ with $E_{XY_0} \ne 0$ (and $e_5 \ne 0$ when the contact is 4 on all lines), containing at least about 95 percent of the lines and singular along at least about 15 percent of them when the contact orders are at most 4 (profiles mixing contact 6 or more with lower contact were not tabulated), with $\sum_\alpha (o_\alpha^2 - o_\alpha - 1) \le W(W - \Delta)/\Delta$ and $W < w_C\,n$. Hard core: contact 4 on all lines, $W \in [2.611, 2.688)\,n$, where the sections are all the list and are $F(X)$-points of $Z^2 = E$ through at least $A$ of its $n$ singular points of type $z^2 = TS\ell^2$.
2. $b = 2$: contact 4 on at least about $0.9\,n$ lines ($W \in [2.4, 2.613)\,n$ at $x = 1$), or contact 6 or 8 on at least about half of the lines (table of Section 3.1); no tool beyond Corollary N and Lemma C.
3. $b = 3, 4, 5$: contact 6 or more on a large fraction of the lines (table of Section 3.1 for $b = 3$; I did not tabulate $b = 4, 5$, for which note 41 states the same).

A proof must fail at rate 1/4, must fail when $e^*(a)$ is replaced by $11/16$ on subspace domains (the family of Section 5), and for $b = 1$ should be tested on that family and on the random-word solutions at $W \approx 26n/9$.

**Routes that I tried and that did not close it.** One-sheet interpolation (Section 2.5); Bézout on other pairs of curves and genus bounds (Section 2.4); the discriminant of $G$ in $u$ (sketch, not written up and not used: it should vanish to order $j(j - 1)$ at $\tau = \alpha^2$, which would bound $\sum_\alpha j_\alpha(j_\alpha - 1)$ by about $w(w - 1/2)n$, weaker than Lemma B where both apply); peeling a fixed component $t$ times from the explainer space (sketch: it cuts the sections with agreement above $A + t(oA - W)/(m - to)$, never the ones at $A$); for $b = 1$, building $E$ from the fourth power of the ideal of the graph points (sketch: elements of low weighted degree force a curve of weighted degree below $A$ through the graph, hence a list of one; the family of Section 5 is not of this kind, its low degree comes from cancellation against squares).

**Routes that remain.** (a) The closed form of the family of Section 5 and its generalizations: if $11/16$ is the minimum efficiency of absolutely irreducible first integrals with $b = 1$ and contact 4 on all lines, the hard core of case 1 closes for every $a \ge 0.691$, including all three certified agreements. The numerical evidence is the scan of Section 0 item 6; the parameter count of Section 6 speaks against it. (b) A second-order version of Lemma C (jets of order 2 and the four components over $F(X^4)$): the first-order windows have relative width 3 percent and second-order explainers exist below $a_1$. (c) For case 1, heights on the fibration $Z^2 = E$ with its $n$ elliptic-type singular points.

## 8. Reproduction
