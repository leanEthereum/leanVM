## 0. Verdict in one page

**Not closed.** Statement (S) of note 41 is neither proved nor refuted. There is no unconditional list bound below the Johnson radius in this note. What is new relative to note 40:

1. **A normal form that makes the $b = 1$ residual explicit [proved].** Every $E$ is uniquely $\Theta^2 + Y\mathcal{A}^2 + X\mathcal{C}^2 + XY\mathcal{B}^2$, and for a quintic $E$ the three polynomials $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C}$ are conics in $Y$. The first integral $\partial E$ has contact order $\ge 2j$ at $p_\alpha$ iff $\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{j-1}$, $\mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{j}$, $\mathcal{C} + \zeta_\alpha\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{j}$ (Proposition 1). For contact 4: three conics through the $n$ received points, mutually tangent there. A polynomial $P = \gamma^2 + X\eta^2$ is a section iff $\eta\,\mathcal{A}(X, P) + \gamma\,\mathcal{B}(X, P) + \mathcal{C}(X, P) = 0$. ## 2. The square-root normal form

$F[X, Y]$ is free over $F[X^2, Y^2] = F[X, Y]^2$ with basis $1, Y, X, XY$. So every $E \in F[X, Y]$ is uniquely
$$E = \Theta^2 + Y\mathcal{A}^2 + X\mathcal{C}^2 + XY\mathcal{B}^2, \qquad \Theta, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C} \in F[X, Y],$$
and $a = E_Y = \mathcal{A}^2 + X\mathcal{B}^2$, $c = E_X = \mathcal{C}^2 + Y\mathcal{B}^2$, $E_{XY} = \mathcal{B}^2$. Only $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C})$ matters for $\partial E$. If $E$ has $Y$-degree at most 5 modulo squares, $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C}$ have $Y$-degree at most 2: $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{A}_2Y^2 + \mathcal{A}_1Y + \mathcal{A}_0$ and so on, with $\mathcal{A}_k, \mathcal{B}_k, \mathcal{C}_k \in F[X]$. The weighted degree of $\partial E$ is $W = \max_k\big(2\deg\mathcal{A}_k + (2k + 1)\Delta,\ 2\deg\mathcal{B}_k + 1 + (2k + 1)\Delta,\ 2\deg\mathcal{C}_k + 2k\Delta\big)$ (the three parity classes do not cancel).

**Proposition 1 [proved; checked, `45_lattice.py`: on every basis vector of 20 solution spaces, and the two linear systems have the same nullity].** Let $j \ge 1$. The first integral $\partial E$ has contact order $\ge 2j$ at $p_\alpha$ iff
$$\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{\,j - 1}, \qquad \mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{\,j}, \qquad \mathcal{C} + \zeta_\alpha\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{\,j}.$$
For $j = 2$: $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C}$ vanish at $p_\alpha$ and their gradients there are proportional, $\nabla\mathcal{A} = \xi_\alpha\nabla\mathcal{B}$, $\nabla\mathcal{C} = \zeta_\alpha\nabla\mathcal{B}$: seven conditions, and the three curves are mutually tangent at $p_\alpha$ (or singular).

*Proof.* The contact order is $\ge 2j$ iff $a = E_S$ and $c = E_T$ lie in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j - 1}$: if $\partial E = c + Y_1a$ lies in the contact ideal of order $2j$, its two coefficients lie in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j - 1}$ by Lemma N of note 40; conversely $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j - 1}$ says that every monomial of $E$ of degree $< 2j$ in $(T, S)$ has both exponents even, $E \in F[T^2, S^2] + \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j}$, and then the contact order is $\ge 2j$ by the direct half of note 40, Proposition 9 part 1. Now $a = (\mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B})^2 + T\mathcal{B}^2$. The first term has even order $2k$, $k = \mathrm{ord}(\mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B})$, the second has odd order $2l + 1$, $l = \mathrm{ord}\,\mathcal{B}$ (orders add because the graded ring is a domain), so the order of $a$ is $\min(2k, 2l + 1)$, and it is $\ge 2j - 1$ iff $k \ge j$ and $l \ge j - 1$. The same with $c = (\mathcal{C} + \zeta_\alpha\mathcal{B})^2 + S\mathcal{B}^2$. $\square$

The count of conditions is $2\binom{j+1}{2} + \binom{j}{2} = (3j^2 + j)/2$, the number of note 40.

**Section equation [proved].** Write a polynomial as $P = \gamma^2 + X\eta^2$ ($\gamma^2$ its even part, $\eta^2 = P'$). Then $E(X, P) \equiv \gamma^2\mathcal{A}_P^2 + X\big(\eta\mathcal{A}_P + \gamma\mathcal{B}_P + \mathcal{C}_P\big)^2 + X^2\eta^2\mathcal{B}_P^2$ modulo squares, with $\mathcal{A}_P = \mathcal{A}(X, P)$ and so on. So $P$ is a section of $\partial E$ iff
$$\Gamma_P := \eta\,\mathcal{A}(X, P) + \gamma\,\mathcal{B}(X, P) + \mathcal{C}(X, P) = 0 . \tag{1}$$
This is the square root of the surface equation $G = \mathsf{C} + v\mathsf{A} + u\mathsf{B}$ of note 40, Proposition 9 part 2. At an agreement $\alpha$ of contact 4, $(\eta(\alpha), \gamma(\alpha), 1)$ is orthogonal to $(\xi_\alpha, 1, \zeta_\alpha)$ and $\Gamma_P$ has a double zero; $\deg\Gamma_P \le W/2$, which is Lemma 0 again.

**Consequences for a list member [proved].** Let $\partial E$ have contact 4 on all lines and let $P$ be a section with agreement set $S$. Then $\mathcal{A}_P = \Lambda_S\mathfrak{a}_P$, $\mathcal{B}_P = \Lambda_S\mathfrak{b}_P$, $\mathcal{C}_P = \Lambda_S\mathfrak{c}_P$ with $\Lambda_S = \prod_{\alpha \in S}(X - \alpha)$, $\mathfrak{c}_P = \eta\mathfrak{a}_P + \gamma\mathfrak{b}_P$, $\mathfrak{a}_P^2 + X\mathfrak{b}_P^2 = \Lambda_S r_P$ where $a(X, P) = \Lambda_S^3 r_P$, and $(\mathfrak{a}_P, \mathfrak{b}_P, \mathfrak{c}_P)(\alpha) = \mathfrak{b}_P(\alpha)\,(\xi_\alpha, 1, \zeta_\alpha)$ on $S$. (Each of $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C}$ vanishes on the graph; the rest is Proposition 1 and (1).) Three further facts, none of which I could turn into a count:

- *Rigidity.* The Zariski tangent space of the scheme of polynomial sections of degree $\le \Delta$ at $P$ is $\{\delta : (a(X, P)\,\delta)' = 0\}$, that is, $\delta \in \mathrm{sqfree}(a(X, P)) \cdot F[X]^2$. For a list member the squarefree part of $a(X, P)$ is divisible by $\Lambda_{S'}$, $S'$ the agreements at which $\mathcal{B}(X, P(X))$ has a simple zero (there $a(X, P)$ has order exactly 3), so if $|S'| > \Delta$ the section is an isolated reduced point.
- *Differences (the analogue of Lemma D of note 43).* If $P_1 \ne P_2$ are sections, every root of $P_1 - P_2$ of odd multiplicity is a common root of $a(X, P_1)$ and $a(X, P_2)$: it is a common agreement, or a common root of $r_{P_1}$ and $r_{P_2}$. (Because $(P_1 - P_2)\cdot\mathrm{DD}$ is a square, where the divided difference $\mathrm{DD}$ of $E$ equals $a(X, P_1)$ where $P_1 = P_2$.)
- *The resultant.* $a(X, P)$ divides $R_E = \mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c)$ (note 43, Lemma T), and $R_E = \Lambda^{11}\rho$ with $\deg\rho \le 9W - 25\Delta - 11n$ (note 40, Lemma B), so the roots of $r_P$ outside $D$ are roots of one polynomial $\rho$ of degree below $0.69\,n$.

**Proposition 2 (the section equation of a quintic is quadratic over $\mathbb{F}_2$) [proved].** Let $E$ be a quintic and $V = \{(g, h)\} = F[\tau]^2$, $P = g(X^2) + Xh(X^2)$. The map $\Gamma : V \to F[\tau]$, $\Gamma(g, h) = h\,\mathsf{A}(\tau, q) + g\,\mathsf{B}(\tau, q) + \mathsf{C}(\tau, q)$, $q = g^2 + \tau h^2$, whose zeros are the sections, satisfies: $\beta(v, v') := \Gamma(v + v') + \Gamma(v) + \Gamma(v') + \Gamma(0)$ is $\mathbb{F}_2$-bilinear. Hence for three sections $v_0, v_1, v_2$: $v_0 + v_1 + v_2$ is a section iff $\beta(v_1 - v_0, v_2 - v_0) = 0$.

*Proof.* $q$ is additive in $(g, h)$, and $\mathsf{A}, \mathsf{B}, \mathsf{C}$ have degree $\le 2$ in $q$, so $q \mapsto \mathsf{A}(\tau, q) - \mathsf{A}(\tau, 0) = \mathsf{A}_2q^2 + \mathsf{A}_1q$ is additive. So $\Gamma$ is a sum of products of two additive maps, of additive maps and of a constant. $\square$

For a cubic ($q$-degree $\le 1$) the same structure, in the coordinate $1/(P + s)$, is the group of note 43; for $Y$-degree 7 it is lost. So the quintics of the rate 1/2 window are the last case in which the sections are the zeros of an $\mathbb{F}_2$-quadratic system, and, as note 43 Section 4.3 found for the cubic, such a system does not bound anything by linear algebra at the real sizes.

## 3. The lattice of quintic potentials and its conservation law

Let $R_5$ be the free $F[\tau]$-module of polynomials of $Y$-degree $\le 5$ modulo $F[X^2, Y^2]$, with basis $X, XY^2, XY^4, Y, Y^3, Y^5, XY, XY^3, XY^5$, and $w(E) = \mathrm{wdeg}(\partial E)$. On a basis element times $f(\tau)$, $w = 2\deg f + w_i$ with $(w_i) = (0, 2\Delta, 4\Delta, \Delta, 3\Delta, 5\Delta, \Delta + 1, 3\Delta + 1, 5\Delta + 1)$, and $w$ of a sum is the maximum. For a contact profile $(j_\alpha)$ with all $j_\alpha \le 3$ let $M \subseteq R_5$ be the set of $E$ whose $\partial E$ has contact $\ge 2j_\alpha$ on the line of $\alpha$. It is an $F[\tau]$-submodule (the condition $E \in F[T^2, S^2] + \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j}$ is stable under multiplication by $\tau = \alpha^2 + T^2$).

**Theorem 2 (conservation law) [proved; checked `45_lattice.py`, `45_b2.py`: $n = 16, 32$, the word $X^{3n/4 - 1}$, two random words, the zero word, a random word on a random evaluation set].** $M$ has $F$-codimension $\kappa = \sum_\alpha (3j_\alpha^2 + j_\alpha)/2$ in $R_5$, and it has a basis $E_1, \dots, E_9$ with $w(\sum\phi_iE_i) = \max_i(2\deg\phi_i + W_i)$, $W_i = w(E_i)$, $W_1 \le \dots \le W_9$ (the successive minima). They satisfy
$$\sum_{i=1}^{9} W_i = 24\Delta + 3 + 2\kappa .$$
For contact 4 on all lines, $\sum W_i = 24\Delta + 3 + 14n$, which is $26n - 21$ for $\Delta = n/2 - 1$ and $26n + 123$ for $\Delta = n/2 + 5$.

*Proof.* The conditions at one point are $(3j^2 + j)/2$ independent linear forms in the Taylor coefficients of order $< 2j \le 6$, which polynomials of $Y$-degree $\le 5$ realize freely, and the conditions at different $\alpha$ are independent by Hermite interpolation in $X$; this gives the codimension. The reduced basis is the standard reduction of a lattice over $F[\tau]$ for a degree function with $w(\tau E) = w(E) + 2$. For $W$ large, $\dim\{E \in M : w(E) \le W\} = \sum_i(\lfloor (W - W_i)/2 \rfloor + 1) = \sum_i(\lfloor (W - w_i)/2 \rfloor + 1) - \kappa$; taking $W$ even and $W$ odd gives first that the $W_i$ and the $w_i$ have the same number of odd terms and then the identity. $\square$

**Corollary 2.1 [proved].** For every word on every evaluation set there is $E \ne 0$ in $R_5$ with contact $\ge 4$ on all lines and $w(E) \le (24\Delta + 3 + 14n)/9 = 26n/9 + O(1)$. So first integrals with $b \le 1$, contact 4 on all lines and efficiency at most $13/18 = 0.7223$ exist for every word. This is the value $26n/9$ of note 40 Section 6, and for random words all nine minima are within 2 of the mean (`45_results.txt`: $89, 90, \dots, 91$ at $n = 32$, mean $90.1$).

**Proposition 3 (uniqueness) [proved].** Let $\partial E_1$ be absolutely irreducible with contact $\ge 4$ on all lines and $w(E_1) < 4A$, and let $E'$, of $Y$-degree $d'$ modulo squares, have contact $\ge 4$ on all lines and $w(E') < 4A$. Then either $E' \in F[\tau, q]\,E_1$ modulo squares, or $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le d' + 4$. In particular, if the list has more than 9 members, every element of $M$ of weighted degree below $4A$ is an $F[\tau]$-multiple of $E_1$: $W_2 \ge 4A$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 0 of note 35 every list member is a section of both, so $P' = c_1/a_1 = c'/a'$ at $Y = P$ and $P$ is a root of $c_1a' - c'a_1$, of $Y$-degree $\le d' + 4$. If this polynomial is zero, $a' = \phi a_1$ and $c' = \phi c_1$ with $\phi \in F(X)[Y]$ because $a_1, c_1$ are coprime in $F(X)[Y]$, $\phi$ is a polynomial by Gauss, and $0 = \partial(\phi\,\partial E_1) = (\partial\phi)\,\partial E_1$ gives $\phi \in F[X^2, Y^2]$. $\square$

With Theorem 2 this describes the shape of a hard-core word: $W_1 < w_C n$, $W_2 \ge 4A$, and $W_2 + \dots + W_9 = 24\Delta + 3 + 14n - W_1$. It is not a contradiction ($2.69 + 8 \times 2.8 < 26$).

## 4. The determinant

For a quintic $E$ let $\mathbf{E}(X)$ be the matrix with rows $(\mathcal{A}_0, \mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2)$, $(\mathcal{B}_0, \mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{B}_2)$, $(\mathcal{C}_0, \mathcal{C}_1, \mathcal{C}_2)$, so that $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C})^{\mathsf T} = \mathbf{E}\,(1, Y, Y^2)^{\mathsf T}$. Call $E$ *degenerate* if $\det\mathbf{E} = 0$.

**Theorem 4 (determinant bound) [proved; checked `45_det.py`, $n = 16, 32$: multiplicity exactly 2 at every $\alpha$ and $\deg\det\mathbf{E}$ within 1 of the bound on random words].** Let $E$ be a quintic and $W = w(E)$.

1. $\deg\det\mathbf{E} \le (3W - 8\Delta - 1)/2$.
2. If $\partial E$ has contact $\ge 4$ on the line of $\alpha$ then $(X - \alpha)^2$ divides $\det\mathbf{E}$; if it has contact $\ge 6$ then $(X - \alpha)^5$ divides it.
3. Hence, if $\partial E$ has contact $\ge 4$ on $n_4$ lines, of which $n_6$ with contact $\ge 6$, then $\det\mathbf{E} = 0$ or
$$W \ \ge\ \frac{4n_4 + 6n_6 + 8\Delta + 1}{3} .$$
With contact 4 on all lines and $\Delta = n/2 + 5$: $W \ge (8n + 41)/3$.

*Proof.* (1) $\deg\mathcal{A}_k \le (W - (2k + 1)\Delta)/2$, $\deg\mathcal{B}_k \le (W - 1 - (2k + 1)\Delta)/2$, $\deg\mathcal{C}_k \le (W - 2k\Delta)/2$, and every term of the determinant takes one entry in each row and each column. (2) Fix $\alpha$, $y = y_\alpha$, $e = (\xi_\alpha, 1, \zeta_\alpha)^{\mathsf T}$. In characteristic 2, $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{A}_2S^2 + \mathcal{A}_1S + \mathcal{A}(X, y)$, so a unimodular constant change of columns turns $\mathbf{E}$ into $[v_0, v_1, v_2]$ with $v_0 = (\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C})(X, y)$, $v_1 = (\mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{C}_1)$, $v_2 = (\mathcal{A}_2, \mathcal{B}_2, \mathcal{C}_2)$. By Proposition 1 with $j = 2$, restricted to the line $Y = y$ and to the $Y$-derivative: $v_0 = T\,\mathcal{B}_X(p_\alpha)\,e + O(T^2)$ and $v_1 = \mathcal{B}_1(\alpha)\,e + O(T)$. So $\det[v_0, v_1, v_2] = T\,\mathcal{B}_X\mathcal{B}_1\det[e, e, v_2] + O(T^2) = O(T^2)$. With $j = 3$: $v_0 = \beta_0e + O(T^3)$ with $\beta_0 = O(T^2)$, $v_1 = \beta_1e + O(T^2)$ with $\beta_1 = O(T)$, $v_2 = \beta_2e + O(T)$, and every term of the expansion of the determinant that contains at most one $e$ is $O(T^5)$. (3) Count the roots. $\square$

Remarks. (i) Contact 2 gives nothing: there $v_0(\alpha) = \mathcal{B}(p_\alpha)e$ need not vanish. (ii) The bound is sharp up to $O(1)$ as a statement about $\det\mathbf{E}$: on random words $\deg\det\mathbf{E} = (3W - 8\Delta - 1)/2$ or one less, with $n$ double roots on $D$ and $(3W - 8\Delta)/2 - 2n \approx n/3$ other roots at $W = 26n/9$. (iii) Tests: random words ($W_1 = 2.78\,n$ at $n = 32$, non-degenerate, bound $2.59\,n$); with contact 6 imposed on a random word at $n = 16$ the multiplicity of $\det\mathbf{E}$ is exactly 5 at every $\alpha$. The families of Sections 6 and 7 are degenerate; they have $W = 2.75\,n$ and $2.70\,n$, above $8n/3$, so they do not test the dichotomy sharply. No non-degenerate potential below the bound occurred in any run.

**Proposition 4.2 (the degenerate case) [proved].** Let $E$ be a degenerate quintic whose $\partial E$ has contact $\ge 4$ on all lines, with $W < 4A$, $2A \le n + \Delta$ and $W < n + 4\Delta$ (all true in the window at rate 1/2). Then one of the following holds.

1. $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le 2$.

## 6. The closed form of the 11/16 family

**Lemma 6.0 [proved].** Let $\psi \in F[X]$ interpolate the word on $D$, $\Lambda = \prod_{\alpha \in D}(X - \alpha)$, and $a \in F[X][Y^2]$ of $Y$-degree $\le 4$. Then $a \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^3$ for all $\alpha \in D$ iff $a = e_5(Y - \psi)^4 + m_3\Lambda\,(Y - \psi)^2 + \nu\Lambda^3$ with $e_5, m_3, \nu \in F[X]$. For such $a$, $E = (Y - \psi)\,a$ has multiplicity $\ge 4$ at every received point, so $\partial E$ has contact $\ge 4$ on all lines, $E_Y = a$, and $E$ is degenerate with $\tilde\psi = \psi$.

*Proof.* Expand $a$ in powers of $Y - \psi$ (only even powers occur). At $\alpha$, $Y - \psi = S + O(T)$; the coefficient of $S^2$ at $T = 0$ must vanish, so $\Lambda$ divides the middle coefficient, and then the part without $S$ is $a(X, \psi) + O(T^3)$, so $\Lambda^3$ divides $a(X, \psi)$. $E_Y = a + (Y - \psi)a_Y = a$. $\square$

So the whole question for potentials of this type is the degree of $a(X, 0) = e_5\psi^4 + m_3\Lambda\psi^2 + \nu\Lambda^3$, and the cost of $E_X = \psi'a + (Y - \psi)a_X$.

**Theorem 6 [proved for the contact and the degrees; coprimality and minimality checked, `45_family.py`: $n = 16, 32, 64$, three subspaces of $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ each; equality with the unique solution of the linear system at $n = 16, 32$].** Let $D$ be an $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of dimension $N \ge 4$, $L_D = X^n + \epsilon X^{n/2} + M$ with $M = \sum_{j \le N - 2} e_jX^{2^j}$, $H = X^{n/2}$, $\psi = X^{3n/4 - 1}$, and
$$a = X^3M\,(Y - \psi)^4 + X(\epsilon M + e_{N-2}^2)\,L_D\,(Y - \psi)^2 + \frac{M}{X}\,L_D^3, \qquad E = (Y - \psi)\,a .$$

1. $\partial E$ has contact order $\ge 4$ on all $n$ lines of the word $\alpha^{3n/4 - 1}$.
2. $X\,a(X, 0) = M_-^2H^4 + e_{N-2}^2\epsilon H^4 + \epsilon M^2H^3 + (\epsilon^3 + e_{N-2}^2)MH^3 + M^3H^2 + \epsilon^2M^2H^2 + \epsilon M^3H + M^4$ with $M_- = M - e_{N-2}X^{n/4}$, of degree $9n/4$ when $e_{N-3} \ne 0$; the even part of $a(X, 0)$ has degree $3n/2$, and the even part of $X^3M$ is $e_0X^4$.
3. $\mathrm{wdeg}(\partial E) \le \max(n/4 + 3 + 5\Delta,\ 5n/4 + 1 + 3\Delta,\ 9n/4 - 1 + \Delta,\ 3n/4 + 2 + 4\Delta)$ for $\Delta \ge n/2 - 1$, with equality when $\epsilon\,e_{N-2}\,e_{N-3} \ne 0$. All four terms equal $11n/4 - 2$ for $\Delta = n/2 - 1$, and the last one is always attained ($e_0 \ne 0$), so $\mathrm{wdeg}(\partial E) = 11n/4 - 2$ exactly on every subspace; for $\Delta = n/2 + 5$ the value is $11n/4 + 28$.
4. **[checked]** $E_Y$ and $E_X$ are coprime; for $\Delta = n/2 - 1$ this $E$ is, up to a scalar and squares, the only quintic potential with contact 4 on all lines and weighted degree $\le 11n/4 - 2$.

*Proof.* (1) is Lemma 6.0 ($M/X$ is a polynomial). (2) $X^4\psi^4 = H^6$, $X\psi^2 = H^3/X$, so $X\,a(X, 0) = M(H^6 + L_D^3) + (\epsilon M + c)L_DH^3$ with $c = e_{N-2}^2$; expand with $L_D = H^2 + \epsilon H + M$: the coefficient of $H^5$ is $\epsilon M + \epsilon M + c$ and $M^2H^4 = cH^5 + M_-^2H^4$, so the terms of degree $5n/2$ cancel exactly for this $c$. Odd exponents come only from the term $e_0X$ of $M$. (3) $E_X = \psi'a + (Y - \psi)a_X = \psi'\mathcal{A}^2 + Y\mathcal{B}^2$ because $\psi = X\psi'$; the four terms are $Y_1e_5Y^4$, $Y_1e_3Y^2$, $Y_1e_1$ and $\psi'e_0X^4Y^4$, and the other terms of $E_X$ ($Y\mathcal{B}^2$ and the rest of $\psi'\mathcal{A}^2$, whose coefficients have degrees $n + 2$ and $3n/2$ by (2)) are smaller. $\square$

So the low weighted degree is the linearized shape of $L_D$ twice: $X^{3n}$ reduces to degree $9n/4$ modulo $L_D^3$ after the correction by $m_3$, and $\psi = X\psi'$ removes the large part of $E_X$. On a subspace with $e_{N-3} = 0$ the $a$-part is cheaper ($\deg a(X, 0) = 2n + n/8 - 1$ in one of the runs at $n = 64$) but $W$ stays at $11n/4 - 2$ because of the term $\psi'e_0X^4Y^4$ of $E_X$.

## 8. The ideas of the brief that gave nothing

### 8.1 Fixedness for all certificates

**[proved]** If $Q$ is any polynomial with $Q(X, P, P') = 0$ for every list member (an explainer of any certificate $(m, B)$, of any support shape, or with poles at places outside $D$ of total order below $mA$), then either $\hat G$ divides $Q$ or $|L_G| \le \delta\,\deg_{u,v}Q$ (Proposition 3 of note 35). The syzygy module has rank 3 and total degree $6\Delta + 4|S_0 \cup S_1|$, so if it is balanced $W \ge 3\Delta + \tfrac43|S_0 \cup S_1| \ge (5\Delta + 8A)/3 = 2.700\,n$ at $a = 0.70$, above the cut $2.688\,n$ by $0.012\,n$. So a hard-core word with two list members needs an unbalanced syzygy module; this is the same numerology as everywhere near the Johnson radius and I could not prove balance.

## 9. The theorem as it stands

**Theorem 9 [proved, given Theorem 8' of note 41 and Proposition 10 of note 40; the finite inequalities by `45_window.py 5` with the functions of `41_counts.py`, exact integers].** Let $F$ be a perfect field of characteristic 2, $n \in \{2^{25}, 2^{26}, 2^{27}, 2^{28}\}$, $D \subset F$ any $n$ points, $\Delta = n/2 + 5$, $a \in \{0.70, 0.703\}$, $A = \lceil an \rceil$, $y$ any word, $\mathbb{E}$ its explainer space for the certificate $(18, 5)$ (which exists at both agreements, $B_{\mathrm{jet}} = 25$), and $\mathcal{G}'$ the class of note 40, Proposition 10: the fixed components of $\mathbb{E}$ that are absolutely irreducible first integrals with $1 \le b \le 5$, leading $Y_1$-coefficient involving $Y_0$, total contact above $2n$, and its conditions (v), (vi). Let $\mathcal{G}'' \subseteq \mathcal{G}'$ be the set of members that satisfy in addition: if $b = 1$ and $W < 6\Delta$ (so that $\hat G = \partial E$ with $E$ a quintic modulo squares), with contact $\ge 4$ on $n_4$ lines of which $n_6$ with contact $\ge 6$, then either $E$ is degenerate with $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ independent ($E \equiv (Y - \tilde\psi)E_Y$ over $F(X)$, Proposition 4.2 case 2), or $\det\mathbf{E} \ne 0$ and $W \ge (4n_4 + 6n_6 + 8\Delta + 1)/3$. Then
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \ \le\ C_a + \sum_{G \in \mathcal{G}''} |L_G|, \qquad C_{0.70} = 2750, \quad C_{0.703} = 2775,$$
and $|\mathcal{G}''| \le 8$. Moreover:

(a) at $a = 0.703$, a member of $\mathcal{G}''$ with $b = 1$ and contact order exactly 4 on all lines that is not a degenerate quintic is cut by Lemma C with $(m, B) = (960, 257)$, so $|L_G| \le \delta\lfloor (960A - 1)/\Delta \rfloor = 1349\,\delta$ ($6745$ for a quintic): at $a = 0.703$ the non-degenerate hard core is closed, and with at most five quintic components the total stays below $2^{16}$;

(b) at both agreements, if a member with $b = 1$, contact $\ge 4$ on all lines and $W < 4A$ exists and the list has more than 9 members, then it is, up to $F[\tau]$-multiples and squares, the only quintic potential with contact $\ge 4$ on all lines and weighted degree below $4A$.

*Proof.* Theorem 8' and Proposition 10 give the statement with $\mathcal{G}'$ and $C = 2750$ at $a = 0.70$. At $0.703$ the certificate and the cuts of Theorem 8' hold with the same pairs $(m, B)$ (re-evaluated by the script for the four sizes; the left side of (2) is nondecreasing in $A$), with $\lfloor (79A - 1)/\Delta \rfloor = 111$, so $C = \max_t\,(25 - t)^2 + 111t = 2775$. A member of $\mathcal{G}' \setminus \mathcal{G}''$ is $\partial E$ with $E$ a quintic that is either non-degenerate with $W$ below the bound of Theorem 4, which is impossible, or degenerate with $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ dependent. In the second case, if the contact is $\ge 4$ on all lines, Proposition 4.2 case 1 gives $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le 2$; if not, I do not use the determinant clause: to stay on proved ground, read "degenerate with $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ independent" in the definition of $\mathcal{G}''$ as "degenerate" whenever some line has contact below 4. (a): by Theorem 4 a non-degenerate quintic has $W \ge (8n + 41)/3$, and a member that is not a quintic has $W \ge 6\Delta$; both are above the exact threshold of Lemma C for the profile "contact 4 on all lines" at $m = 960$ ($2.66582\,n$); the left side of (2) is nondecreasing in $W$ and the side condition of Lemma C holds up to the largest weighted degree $18A - 1$ of a factor (checked by the script); Lemma C gives $\delta\lfloor (mA - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$. (b) is Proposition 3. $\square$

**Query counts.** $t = \lceil 108/\log_2(n/A) \rceil$: 210 at $0.70$, 213 at $0.703$, 217 at the Johnson radius. None of them is obtained unconditionally.

**Checks against what is known.** Theorem 9 is conditional and bounds nothing by itself, so it cannot contradict the lower bounds of note 21 (families at $5/8$, $9/16$ and $5/8 + \epsilon$, all below $0.6899$) or the exhaustive data of note 18 (lists of at most 4 at the relevant agreements for $n = 16$). The unconditional statements of this note are structural: Theorem 2 (verified exactly in every run: ten words for the rank 9 lattice, five for the rank 13 lattice), Theorem 4 (verified in every run; consistent with the random-word minima $2.78\,n$ and with note 40's table $89, 182, 366, 736$, which are all at most $(26n - 21)/9$ as Corollary 2.1 requires), Theorem 6 and Theorem 7. The only list statement, Theorem 7 item 4, concerns agreement above $0.675\,n$ with a monomial word of degree $0.7\,n$ on a field domain, where no family is known, and passed the exhaustive low-degree test at $n = 64$.

## 10. The exact remaining case

At $n = 2^{25}$ to $2^{28}$, rate 1/2, certificate $(18, 5)$, an unconditional bound $|\mathrm{List}(y, an)| < 2^{16}$ on every evaluation set in characteristic 2 would follow from a bound on the sections with agreement $\ge A$ of the fixed, absolutely irreducible first-integral components of the following kinds, or from their non-existence.

1. **$b = 1$, degenerate.** $E \equiv (Y - \tilde\psi)\,a$ with $\tilde\psi \in F(X)$ interpolating the word and $a = \mathcal{A}^2 + X\mathcal{B}^2 \in F[X, Y^2]$ of $Y$-degree 4 with triple points on the graph; sections are the solutions of $\tilde\eta\,\mathcal{A}(X, P) = \tilde\gamma\,\mathcal{B}(X, P)$, $P - \tilde\psi = \tilde\gamma^2 + X\tilde\eta^2$. Hard core: contact 4 on all lines and $W \in [2.611, 2.688)\,n$ at $a = 0.70$ ($[2.611, 2.666)\,n$ at $0.703$). The two explicit families are of this kind with $\tilde\psi$ a polynomial, at $W = 2.75\,n$ (all subspaces) and $2.70\,n$ (field domains), and for the second the equation has no polynomial solution. A proof for this case has to show that (2) has few polynomial solutions of degree $\le \Delta$ through $A$ points, as Theorem 7 item 3 does when $\mathcal{B} \in F[X]$; the case where $\mathcal{B}$ involves $Y$ is open.
2. **$b = 1$, non-degenerate.** Contact 4 on all lines: $W \in [(8n + 41)/3,\ 2.6876\,n)$ at $a = 0.70$, a window of relative width $0.8$ percent, **empty at $a \ge 0.703$**. There $E_1$ is unique (Proposition 3), $\det\mathbf{E} = \Lambda^2\rho$ with $\deg\rho < 0.032\,n$, and the other eight minima of the lattice average at least $2.914\,n$. Mixed profiles: the ten cells of Section 4 at $a = 0.70$ (contact 4 on at least half of the lines).
3. **$b = 2$.** As in note 40: contact 4 on at least about $0.9\,n$ lines with $W \in [2.4, 2.613)\,n$, or contact 6 or 8 on at least half of the lines. New here: the 10 conditions per line, the conservation law (mean $2.92\,n$, genuine $b = 2$ minima of mean $3n$), and no example at $n = 16, 32$.
4. **$b = 3, 4, 5$.** Untouched (contact 6 or more on a large fraction of the lines, note 40 Section 3.1).

Tests that a proof must pass: fail at rate 1/4 (flat cubic); fail when the cut is replaced by $27/40$ on field domains and by $11/16$ on all subspace domains; for the non-degenerate case, be consistent with $\det\mathbf{E}$ having exactly $n$ double roots on $D$ for random words at $W = 26n/9$.

**Routes that I tried and that did not close it.** Fixedness across certificates and places (Section 8.1); one-sheet and tangent-cone counts (8.2); a Plotkin or list-recovery count (8.3); bounding the higher minima of the lattice from above by potentials built from list members (the natural ones, $X^\epsilon Y^\epsilon\Lambda_{S^c}^4(Y - P)^4$, have $W \ge 3.2\,n$, and Theorem 2 then gives nothing); the three-section identity $\det\mathbf{E}\cdot\prod_{i<j}(P_i - P_j) = \Lambda_{S_0}\Lambda_{S_1}\Lambda_{S_2}\cdot\det[\mathfrak{a}_i; \mathfrak{b}_i; \mathfrak{c}_i]$ (true, and weaker than Theorem 4); Mason and Stothers on the two-member identity $\epsilon^2[e_5\Lambda_I\epsilon^2 + \Lambda_{I^c}\tilde e_3] = \Lambda_{S_1 \setminus I}^3r_1 + \Lambda_{S_2 \setminus I}^3r_2$ (the radical count leaves a margin of $0.4\,n$).

**Routes that remain.** (a) For the degenerate case, the structure of equation (2): it is the section equation of a potential $\delta\,\Omega$ with $\delta = \sqrt{Y - \tilde\psi}$ and $\Omega = \mathcal{A} + \sqrt X\,\mathcal{B}$, and when $\mathcal{B}$ does not involve $Y$ it forces agreement or $\eta = 0$ at every point. (b) A determinant for $b = 2$. (c) An argument that the syzygy module of Section 8.3 is balanced, which would exclude two list members on a hard-core component at $a = 0.70$ with a margin of $0.012\,n$.
