# 86. Proof of the rank formula (C*) of note 82 for class patterns of the generic T2 tree code

**Result.** The formula (C\*) of note 82 is now **proved** (computer-assisted proof, characteristic 2) for three members and every pattern at $(k, n) = (4, 8)$ and $(8, 16)$ (rate 1/2) and at $(4, 16)$ (rate 1/4), and for four members (all binary patterns) at $(4, 8)$; and it is **false** from $(16, 32)$ on: a three-member class pattern at $(16, 32)$, rate 1/2, has weight 30, rank 29, and $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) \ge 30$ for every binary pattern $\mathcal Q$ one level up (proved with a certificate; referee 87 found the same pattern type independently), so (C\*) and (C') fail there for every number of members $\ge 3$, and a proof at every size does not exist. **Technique:** a reduction, proved for any number of members, of (C\*) to *saturated* patterns (those that contain every binary functional of their own full part: their binary full part is the pattern of sibling meets, and that pattern is again saturated) and then to *reduced* patterns (for three members: an upper pattern doubled, plus lonely triple leaves), by the derivative lemma: in a saturated pattern only a leaf that exceeds its sibling by two dimensions can carry a relation. This leaves $6^{n/2}$ patterns for three members and $31^{n/2}$ for four, checked exhaustively with a chain of certified generic ranks. **True at every size (proved):** for a saturated pattern, in particular for the agreement pattern of any list, the deficiency splits exactly as $w - r = \sum_i 2^i (b^{(i)} + \Delta^{(i)})$, with $b^{(i)}$ the term of the binary recursion and $\Delta^{(i)}$ the number of relations among the large nodes of height $i$ modulo the meet pattern above; $0 \le \Delta \le \sum_x e_x - e_{(1)} - e_{(2)}$ with $e_x \le \rho_x - 1$; for three members $\Delta \le \ell - 2$, $\ell$ the number of lonely triples with a non-binary line in their full part, and this is attained ($\ell = 5$, $\Delta = 3$ at $(32, 64)$); and $r \ge 2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \#\{x : \Lambda_x \not\subseteq \Lambda_{x'}\}$, so the rank of a saturated pattern is at least its weight divided by the largest excess of a node over its sibling. **What replaces the binary pattern:** the tuple of all functionals with coefficients in the field of the upper twiddles that kill the solutions at a node (one subspace of $K^L$ per node, no longer one of finitely many states). With it the one-level formula is exact on all 116 violating three-member patterns found at $(16, 32)$, on three at $(32, 64)$, and on note 87's five and six member failures at $(8, 16)$ with their saturations; but the scalar recursion does not close on such tuples: at the level where a line that is not binary is born, the upper object is an intersection of binary row spaces (114 of 116 cases) or a sum of such, its dimension follows from the binary rank function by the modular law, and the class closed under the recursion is the lattice generated by the binary row spaces under sum and intersection. **Smallest cases still open:** for (C\*): three members at $(8, 32)$, four members at $(8, 16)$, five members at $(4, 8)$; for the corrected one-level statement: a system with five or more leaves whose full part moves with the bottom twiddles (no instance is known).

## 1. Setup, and the reduction to saturated patterns

Notation of note 82: generic T2 tree code, $k = 2^v$, leaf row $g_x = g_p \otimes z_x$, $z_x = (1, t_x)$, $p = p(x)$ the bottom pair, $K_1$ the field of the twiddles of layers $\ge 1$, $F = K_1(t)$, $V = K^L \otimes K^{k/2}$, $U_x = \Lambda_x \otimes g_p$, $R = R(\mathcal P) = \sum_x U_x \otimes z_x$, $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q)$ the bound of Lemma 1.1 of note 82, $\mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)_p = \{\theta \text{ binary} : \theta \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R\}$, $Y^* = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*)$. (C\*) is $r(\mathcal P) = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P))$. Binary patterns throughout (for three members these are the class patterns: $0$, three lines $\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell_3 = \ell_1 + \ell_2$, and $K^2$).

**Definition 1.1.** A binary pattern is *saturated* if $\mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)_p \subseteq \Lambda_{p0} \cap \Lambda_{p1}$ for every bottom pair $p$ (then equality holds: a binary $\theta$ in both sibling spaces has $\theta \otimes g_p \otimes z_{p0}$ and $\theta \otimes g_p \otimes z_{p1}$ in $R$). The *saturation* $\mathcal P^{\rm sat}$ of $\mathcal P$ has $\Lambda^{\rm sat}_{pb} = \Lambda_{pb} + \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)_p$. The *meet pattern* $\mathcal P^\wedge$ is the upper pattern with $\Lambda_{p0} \cap \Lambda_{p1}$ at $p$.

**Lemma 1.2 (saturation; proved, any number of members, binary patterns).** (a) $R(\mathcal P^{\rm sat}) = R(\mathcal P)$, $\mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P^{\rm sat}) = \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)$, $\mathcal P^{\rm sat}$ is saturated and $(\mathcal P^{\rm sat})^\wedge = \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)$. (b) $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) \le B(\mathcal P'; \mathcal Q)$ when $\Lambda_x \subseteq \Lambda'_x$ for all $x$. (c) (C\*) for $\mathcal P^{\rm sat}$ implies (C\*) for $\mathcal P$. (d) For a saturated pattern (C\*) reads $r(\mathcal P) = 2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x \dim \big( (U_x + Y^*)/Y^* \big)$ with $Y^* = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge)$. *Proof.* (a) The added rows $\theta \otimes g_p \otimes z_{pb}$ lie in $R$; the rest is the definition. (b) Each term $r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q \vee \Lambda_x@p) - r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) = \dim (R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) + U_x)/R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$ is monotone in $U_x$. (c) $r(\mathcal P) \le B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) \le B(\mathcal P^{\rm sat}; \mathcal Q^*) = r(\mathcal P^{\rm sat}) = r(\mathcal P)$, the first step by Lemma 1.1 of note 82. (d) Definition. $\square$

**Lemma 1.3 (three members, saturated: only lonely triples can carry a relation; proved).** Let $\mathcal P$ be a saturated three-member pattern, $Y = Y^* = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge)$. Call a leaf $x$ a *lonely triple* if its class is the triple and its sibling's class is empty. Let $\sum_x u_x \otimes z_x \in Y \otimes F^2$ with $u_x \in U_x \otimes F$. Then $u_x \in Y \otimes F$ for every leaf that is not a lonely triple, and for a lonely triple $u_x$ lies in the full part $E_x = \{u \in U_x \otimes F : u \otimes F^2 \subseteq R\}$, which is $0$ or a line $\nu_x \otimes g_p$ with $\nu_x \in \mathbb P^1(F)$ not binary. *Proof.* Proposition 4.1 (i) of note 82 gives $u_x \in E_x$. Pair leaf with class $c$: $E_x \ne 0$ means $\ell_c \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$, so $\ell_c \in \mathcal Q^*_p$ and $u_x \in Y$. Triple leaf with a sibling of class $c$: $\ell_c \otimes g_p \in Y$; if $E_x$ were larger than $\ell_c \otimes g_p \otimes F$ then $K^2 \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$ and the sibling would be a triple. Two sibling triples: $U_x \subseteq Y$. Lonely triple: $E_x$ is not $U_x \otimes F$ (saturation) and contains no binary vector (saturation). $\square$

**Corollary 1.4 (the core statement; proved equivalence).** (C\*) holds for all three-member patterns at depth $v$ (given rate) if and only if the following holds for all *reduced* saturated patterns, that is, saturated patterns in which every bottom pair is (class, same class) or (empty, triple): **(Core)** *if $\sum_{x \in J} u_x \otimes z_x \in Y \otimes F^2$ with $J$ the set of lonely triples and $u_x \in K^2 \otimes g_{p(x)} \otimes F$, then every $u_x \in Y \otimes F$*, where $Y = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$ and $\mathcal Q = \mathcal P^\wedge$. *Proof.* By Lemma 1.2 it suffices to treat saturated patterns, for which (C\*) is the absence of relations modulo $Y \otimes F^2$ with a component outside $Y$ (Theorem 4.2 of note 82). By Lemma 1.3 such a relation is a relation of the sub-pattern $\mathcal P'$ that keeps $\mathcal P^\wedge$ doubled and the lonely triples; $\mathcal P'$ is saturated with the same meet pattern, because $R(\mathcal P') \subseteq R(\mathcal P)$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.5 (the meet of a saturated pattern is saturated; proved, binary patterns, any number of members).** If $\mathcal P$ is saturated then $\mathcal P^\wedge$ is saturated as a pattern on the upper code, hence so are all iterated meets $\mathcal P^{\wedge i}$, whose space at a node $u$ of height $i$ is $\bigcap_{x \le u} \Lambda_x$. *Proof.* If $\theta \otimes g_{\hat p} \otimes F_1^2 \subseteq R^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge)$ for a node $\hat p$ of height 2, then $\theta \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) \otimes F^2 \subseteq R(\mathcal P)$ for both children $p$ of $\hat p$, so $\theta \in \mathcal P^\wedge_p$ for both. $\square$ So for a saturated pattern the chain of binary full parts of Corollary 5.2 of note 82 is the chain of meets, and (C\*) at every level would read, for three members: $w - r = \sum_i 2^i \cdot \#\{$lonely triple nodes $u$ of height $i$ with $K^2 \otimes g_{\hat u} \cap R(\mathcal P^{\wedge (i+1)}) \ne 0\}$.

**Lemma 1.6 (reduction for any number of members; proved).** For binary patterns of $m$ members, (C\*) at a given size follows from (C\*) for the *reduced* patterns: those in which every bottom pair $(\Lambda_0, \Lambda_1)$, with $M = \Lambda_0 \cap \Lambda_1$, has each $\Lambda_b$ equal to $M$ or of dimension at least $\dim M + 2$. *Proof.* As Corollary 1.4: in a saturated pattern a leaf with $\dim \Lambda_x / M \le 1$ has a full part equal to $M \otimes g_p$ (if it were larger it would be $U_x \otimes F$, and saturation would put $\Lambda_x$ in the sibling), so its relation components lie in $Y$; replacing such leaves by $M$ gives a reduced saturated pattern with the same meet pattern that carries the same relation. $\square$ For three members the reduced pairs are the 5 doubled ones and (empty, triple); for four members there are 31.

So for three members the unknown is the position of the planes $\bar U_p = (K^2 \otimes g_p + Y)/Y$, $p \in J$, in $\bar V = V/Y = (\ker \mathcal Q)^*$: the transposes of the evaluation maps ${\rm ev}_p : \ker \mathcal Q \to K^2$, $N \mapsto N g_p$, of the solutions of the upper pattern at the nodes that carry a lonely triple. (Core) says: under saturation, $\sum_{p \in J} \bar U_p \otimes z_p$ is a direct sum in $\bar V \otimes F^2$.

## 2. The formula is false at $(16, 32)$: a three-member counterexample

Levels: leaves are level 0, bottom pairs are the nodes of level 1, their parents level 2. $V_i = K^2 \otimes K^{k/2^i}$, $g_u$ the row of a node $u$ of level $i$ in the code of the layers $\ge i$, so that a leaf $x$ below a level-2 node $u$ has $g_x = g_u \otimes \beta_x$ with $\beta_x \in K^4$ the row of $x$ in the local code of the block of $u$ (the four $\beta_x$ of a block are a basis of $K^4$).

**Theorem 2.1 ((C\*) and (C') fail for three members at $(16, 32)$; proved, the lower bounds on ranks by exact computation at a draw, which is a certificate).** Let $\mathcal Q_2$ be the pattern `012 02 . . 12 . . 01` on the code $(4, 8)$ of level 2 (nodes $0..7$), and $\mathcal P$ the pattern at $(16, 32)$ whose blocks of four leaves are: `012 012 012 012` under node 0, `02 02 02 02` under node 1, `012 012 . 012` under node 2, `. 012 . 012` under node 3, `12 12 12 12` under node 4, nothing under nodes 5 and 6, `01 01 01 01` under node 7. Then $w(\mathcal P) = 30$, $r(\mathcal P) = 29$, $\mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P)$ is `012 012 02 02 012 . . . 12 12 . . . . 01 01` (level 1), $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 30$, and $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) \ge 30$ for every binary pattern $\mathcal Q$ one level up. *Proof.* (a) $r(\mathcal Q_2) = 5$ (five rows, independent at a draw), so $\bar V_2 = V_2 / R(\mathcal Q_2)$ has dimension 3 and the images of the planes $K^2 \otimes g_2$ and $K^2 \otimes g_3$ meet: there are $\nu, \mu \in K_2^2$, not both zero, with $\nu \otimes g_2 + \mu \otimes g_3 \in R(\mathcal Q_2)$. (b) The 20 rows of the leaves under the nodes 0, 1, 4, 7 span $R(\mathcal Q_2) \otimes K^4$. Modulo this space the five vectors $\nu \otimes g_2 \otimes \beta_x$ ($x$ the three triple leaves under node 2) and $\mu \otimes g_3 \otimes \beta_x$ ($x$ the two triple leaves under node 3), which are independent combinations of the other ten rows, lie in $\bar\lambda \otimes K^4$, $\bar\lambda$ the class of $\nu \otimes g_2$, a space of dimension at most 4. So the 30 rows satisfy a nontrivial relation and $r \le 29$ for generic twiddles; the rank is 29 at a draw (`86_cex.py`, three seeds, and `82_big`), hence $r = 29$. (c) At the draw the binary full part is the stated $\mathcal Q^*$; a binary $\theta \otimes g_p \otimes F^2$ outside $R$ at the draw is outside $R$ generically (the rank of $R$ at the draw is already the generic one), so the generic $\mathcal Q^*$ is contained in it, and it contains the stated pattern because that is the meet pattern. (d) If $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) = r$ for a binary $\mathcal Q$, then $\mathcal Q \le \mathcal Q^*$ and every relation has its components in $R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) \subseteq R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*) = R(\mathcal Q_2) \otimes F_1^2 + K^2 \otimes g_2 \otimes z_4$ (Proposition 2.4 (a) of note 74; $z_4$ the level-1 vector of the child of node 2 with two triple leaves). The relation of (b) has the component $\nu \otimes g_2 \otimes z_5$ at the lonely triple under node 2, and $\nu \otimes g_2 \otimes z_5 \notin R(\mathcal Q_2) \otimes F_1^2 + K^2 \otimes g_2 \otimes z_4$ because $\nu \otimes g_2 \notin R(\mathcal Q_2)$ (the two planes have dimension 2 modulo $R(\mathcal Q_2)$ at the draw, and $\nu$, $\mu$ are not binary) and $z_5 \ne z_4$. So no binary $\mathcal Q$ is exact. (e) Independently, `86_cex.py` enumerates the 8000 binary $\mathcal Q \le \mathcal Q^*$: the least $B$ is 30. $\square$

**Mechanism (proved).** Cut the code at level 2: $R(\mathcal P) = R(\mathcal Q_2) \otimes K^4 + \sum_{x} K^2 \otimes g_{u(x)} \otimes \beta_x$ over the nine triple leaves under the nodes 2 and 3. A solution is $M \in W_2 \otimes K^4$, $W_2 = \ker \mathcal Q_2$ of dimension 3. Let $N_a$ span the solutions vanishing at node 2, $N_b$ those vanishing at node 3, $N_c$ a third basis vector; $M = N_a \otimes y_a + N_b \otimes y_b + N_c \otimes y_c$. The three triple leaves under node 2 say $y_b, y_c \perp B_2$ (the span of their three local rows), the two under node 3 say $y_a, y_c \perp B_3$: **the component $y_c \in K^4$ is hit by $3 + 2 = 5$ conditions, one more than its dimension**, while the binary count charges $2 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 2 = 10$ independent conditions. In the row space: the planes of the two nodes share the line $\bar\lambda$ (the functional that reads off the $N_c$ coordinate), and $\bar\lambda \otimes (B_2 + B_3) = \bar\lambda \otimes K^4$ has dimension 4, not 5. The functional is not binary and $R(\mathcal Q_2) + \lambda$ contains no binary pure tensor $\theta \otimes g_s$ outside $R(\mathcal Q_2)$ (`86_coincide.py`), so no binary pattern at any level sees it.

**Corollary 2.2 (a list not explained by binary patterns; exact at the stated size).** Adding the pair classes `01` and `02` at two empty leaves under node 5 gives a pattern with $w = 32 = Lk$, rank 31, a solution space of dimension one, the same $\mathcal Q^*$ and $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 32$ (`86_tmp/cand2.txt`, first line, through `82_big`); no binary $\mathcal Q$ is exact, by the argument of Theorem 2.1 (d), which applies word for word (same relation, same $\mathcal Q^*$). The solution is a list of three distinct codewords that agree with a word on 19, 18 and 18 of the 32 leaves (`86_list.py`): a list at $w = Lk$ whose existence no chain of binary patterns explains (the binary recursion predicts full rank, that is, no list with this agreement pattern). It is not a large list: the lift of note 69 at the same size has agreement $20/32$ for each member. So parts (ii) to (iv) of Theorem 5.1 of note 82 and its Corollaries 5.2 and 5.3 (which are conditional on (C\*)) do not hold in general at $k \ge 16$; part (i) is unconditional and stands.

**Corollary 2.3 (more members; proved).** A three-member pattern is a pattern of $m \ge 3$ members in which the other members are in no class, with the same row space $R \subseteq K^2 \otimes X \otimes F^2$. If $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) = r$ for a binary $\mathcal Q$ of $m$ members, then $R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$, so every $\mathcal Q_p \subseteq \mathbb F_2^2$ and $\mathcal Q$ is a three-member pattern, which Theorem 2.1 excludes. So (C') and (C\*) fail at $(16, 32)$ for every number of members $\ge 3$: **the conjecture of note 82 that the closed recursion holds for any number of members at rate 1/2 is false, and so is the one for at most five members.**

**How common (exact at the stated size, `86_reduced 4 1 5 struct 200000`).** Among 200000 random patterns at $(16, 32)$ built from a random pattern two levels up (every node of level 2 quadrupled, a random subset of the four leaves made triples under the empty nodes, a few pair leaves raised to triples), 54 violate (C\*), each time by one: $r = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) - 1$, with $(w, r)$ among $(30, 29)$, $(31, 30)$, $(32, 31)$. On all 54 (`86_hull.py`): each has exactly three lonely triples whose full part is a line that is not binary and does not move when the bottom twiddles are redrawn, in two blocks of level 2. A second search over 1.5 million random reduced patterns at $(16, 32)$ (`86_reduced 4 1 11 redrand 1500000`) finds 61 more, of the same kind. The same structured search finds no violation in 300000 patterns at $(8, 16)$ (where Theorem 3.1 says there is none) and none in 300000 at $(8, 32)$ (not a proof). Uniformly random patterns almost never have this shape, which is why note 82's 93000 patterns at $(16, 32)$ did not see it.

## 3. The formula is true below that size: $(8, 16)$ and $(4, 16)$ (computer-assisted proof)

**Theorem 3.1 ((C\*) for three members at $(8, 16)$, $(4, 16)$ and $(4, 8)$; computer-assisted proof, characteristic 2).** For every three-member pattern at $(k, n) = (8, 16)$, $(4, 16)$ and $(4, 8)$, $r(\mathcal P) = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal P))$. *Proof.* By Corollary 1.4 it suffices to check the reduced patterns: an arbitrary pattern $\mathcal Q$ one level up, doubled, plus one triple leaf in each pair of a subset $J$ of the pairs where $\mathcal Q$ is empty; there are $6^{n/2}$ of them. `scripts/86_reduced.c` checks $r = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*)$ on all of them, with a certified chain: at the top level ($k = 1$) ranks have no twiddle; at each level below, the rank at the draw is a lower bound on the generic rank and $B$ computed with generic upper ranks is an upper bound, so equality on every pattern of a level certifies that the drawn ranks of that level are generic, and they are the upper ranks of the next level. At $(8, 16)$: all 625 patterns at $(2, 4)$, all 390625 at $(4, 8)$, all 1679616 reduced patterns at $(8, 16)$ (978318 with dependent rows): no failure. At $(4, 16)$: all 390625 patterns at $(2, 8)$, all 1679616 reduced patterns (1668417 with dependent rows): no failure. If the drawn $\mathcal Q^*$ is exact it is contained in the generic one, and it contains it by the argument of Theorem 2.1 (c). $\square$ This settles the smallest open case of note 82. **So at rate 1/2 the formula holds for three members exactly up to $k = 8$.**

**Theorem 3.2 (four members at $(4, 8)$; computer-assisted proof).** (C\*) holds for every binary pattern of four members at $(4, 8)$, in particular for every class pattern. *Proof.* Lemma 1.6 reduces to the $31^4 = 923521$ reduced binary patterns; `scripts/86_reduced_m.c 4 2 1` checks them with the same certified chain (all 65536 binary patterns at $(2, 4)$ first): no failure. $\square$

## 4. What is true at every size: the level decomposition of the deficiency, and an inequality (proved)

Throughout this section $\mathcal P$ is a **saturated** binary pattern with any number of members (the agreement pattern of an actual list is closed under its own row space, hence saturated; for an arbitrary pattern apply everything to $\mathcal P^{\rm sat}$, which has the same row space). Notation: $M_p = \Lambda_{p0} \cap \Lambda_{p1}$, $Y = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge)$, $\bar V = V/Y$ (the dual of the solution space of the meet pattern), $\bar U_x$ the image of $U_x$, $c_x = \dim \Lambda_x - \dim M_{p(x)}$ (what the leaf has beyond what it shares with its sibling), $\rho_x = \dim \bar U_x$, $b_x = c_x - \rho_x = \dim(U_x \cap Y) - \dim M_p \ge 0$, $E_x$ the full part of the leaf, $\bar E_x$ its image in $\bar U_x \otimes F$, $e_x = \dim \bar E_x$, and $\mathcal S = \{(u_x) \in \bigoplus_x \bar U_x \otimes F : \sum_x u_x \otimes z_x = 0 \text{ in } \bar V \otimes F^2\}$ the relations modulo $Y \otimes F^2$, $\Delta = \dim \mathcal S$.

**Theorem 4.1 (level decomposition of the deficiency; proved).** (a) $r(\mathcal P) = 2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x \rho_x - \Delta$. (b) With $\delta = w - r$: $\delta(\mathcal P) = 2\, \delta(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x b_x + \Delta$. (c) $\mathcal P^\wedge$ is saturated (Lemma 1.5), so for the chain of meets $\delta(\mathcal P) = 2^v \delta_{\rm top} + \sum_{i < v} 2^i \big( b^{(i)} + \Delta^{(i)} \big)$, where $b^{(i)}$, $\Delta^{(i)}$ are the quantities $\sum_x b_x$, $\Delta$ of the pattern $\mathcal P^{\wedge i}$ at height $i$ and $\delta_{\rm top} = \sum_{\rm roots} \dim \Theta_j - \dim \sum_j \Theta_j$, $\Theta_j$ the meet over a whole tree (zero for a list of distinct codewords). (d) (C\*) holds for $\mathcal P$ iff $\Delta = 0$; the closed binary formula of Corollary 5.2 of note 82 holds for $\mathcal P$ iff $\Delta^{(i)} = 0$ at every height. *Proof.* $R = Y \otimes F^2 + \sum_x U_x \otimes z_x$ because $M_p \otimes g_p \otimes z_{p0}$ and $M_p \otimes g_p \otimes z_{p1}$ span $M_p \otimes g_p \otimes F^2$; so $\dim R = 2 \dim Y + \dim \sum_x \bar U_x \otimes z_x = 2 \dim Y + \sum_x \rho_x - \Delta$. (b) follows from $w(\mathcal P) = 2 w(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x c_x$. (d): $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 2 \dim Y + \sum_x \rho_x$ since $\mathcal Q^* = \mathcal P^\wedge$. $\square$ So **all of the difference between the true rank and the binary recursion is the number $\Delta^{(i)}$ of relations, modulo the meet pattern one level up, among the leaves (nodes) of each height**; the terms $b^{(i)}$ are what the binary recursion already counts (the "conic" functionals of note 82).

**Theorem 4.2 (where the relations can sit, and the inequality; proved).** (i) $\mathcal S \subseteq \bigoplus_x \bar E_x$ (derivative lemma). (ii) If $c_x \ge 1$ then $\rho_x \ge 1$ and $e_x \le \rho_x - 1$, and $\bar E_x$ contains the image of no binary $\theta \in \Lambda_x \setminus M_p$; in particular $e_x = 0$ unless $c_x \ge 2$ (a leaf, or node, that exceeds what it shares with its sibling by at least two dimensions: call it *large*). (iii) Every nonzero relation has at least three nonzero components. Hence
$$2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x \rho_x - \Big( \sum_x e_x - e_{(1)} - e_{(2)} \Big)_+ \ \le\ r(\mathcal P) \ \le\ 2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x \rho_x = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*),$$
$e_{(1)}, e_{(2)}$ the two largest $e_x$; and, more crudely, $r(\mathcal P) \ge 2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \sum_x (\rho_x - e_x) \ge 2\, r^{\rm up}(\mathcal P^\wedge) + \#\{x : \Lambda_x \not\subseteq \Lambda_{x'}\}$. *Proof.* (i) Proposition 4.1 (i) of note 82. (ii) If $\rho_x = 0$ then $U_x \subseteq Y$, $U_x \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$, and saturation gives $\Lambda_x \subseteq \Lambda_{x'}$, $c_x = 0$. $(U_x \cap Y) \otimes F \subseteq E_x$, so $\bar E_x = \bar U_x \otimes F$ would mean $E_x = U_x \otimes F$ and again $c_x = 0$. A binary $\theta$ with $\theta \otimes g_p \in E_x$ lies in $\mathcal Q^*_p = M_p$. If $\rho_x \le 1$ then $e_x = 0$. (iii) $u_1 \otimes z_1 + u_2 \otimes z_2 = 0$ with $z_1, z_2$ independent forces $u_1 = u_2 = 0$. The projection of $\mathcal S$ that forgets the components of two leaves is therefore injective, which gives the left inequality; and if $C_x$ is a complement of $\bar E_x$ in $\bar U_x \otimes F$, the sum $\sum_x C_x \otimes z_x$ is direct by (i), which gives the crude ones ($\rho_x - e_x \ge 1$ when $c_x \ge 1$). $\square$

**Corollary 4.3 (iterated; proved).** For a saturated pattern, with $c_u$, $\rho_u$, $e_u$ the same quantities for the nodes $u$ of height $i$ in $\mathcal P^{\wedge i}$: $\sum_{i} 2^i \sum_{u} (\rho_u - e_u) \le r(\mathcal P) - 2^v \dim \Theta \le \sum_i 2^i \sum_u \rho_u$, and $r(\mathcal P) \ge 2^v \dim\Theta + \sum_i 2^i N_i$ with $N_i$ the number of nodes of height $i$ whose meet space is strictly larger than that of their parent. Since $w = 2^v \sum_j \dim \Theta_j + \sum_i 2^i \sum_u c_u$, this gives, when no two members agree on a whole tree ($\Theta_j = 0$, as for a list of distinct codewords), $r(\mathcal P) \ge w(\mathcal P) / c_{\max}$, $c_{\max} = \max_u c_u \le L$: the rank of a saturated pattern is within the factor $c_{\max}$ of its weight, and the loss sits only at large nodes. For three members $c_{\max} = 2$ and the large nodes are the lonely triple nodes (maximal blocks on which all three members agree, whose sibling block has no common pair).

**Theorem 4.4 (three members; proved).** Let $\ell$ be the number of lonely triple leaves with $\rho_x = 2$ and $E_x \ne 0$ (a line $\nu_x \otimes g_p$, $\nu_x$ not binary: *lined* leaves). Then $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) - (\ell - 2)_+ \le r(\mathcal P) \le B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*)$. If every line $\nu_x$ is defined over $K_1$ (does not move with the bottom twiddles), then $\Delta = \ell - 2 \dim \mathrm{span}\{\bar\lambda_x\}$, $\bar\lambda_x$ the class of $\nu_x \otimes g_p$ in $\bar V$. If at most four lined leaves have a moving line, the same holds with the rational lines. With exactly three lined leaves, $\Delta = \dim(\bar U_{x_1} \cap \bar U_{x_2} \cap \bar U_{x_3}) \in \{0, 1\}$. *Proof.* $e_x \le 1$ by Theorem 4.2 (ii), which gives the first statement. Rational case: $\bar Y' = \sum_x \bar\lambda_x$ has $\bar Y' \otimes F^2 \subseteq \bar R$, a relation modulo $\bar Y' \otimes F^2$ has its components in $\bar E_x \subseteq \bar Y'$, so $\bar R = \bar Y' \otimes F^2 \oplus \bigoplus_x (\bar U_x / \bar\lambda_x) \otimes z_x$ and $\dim \bar R = 2 \dim \bar Y' + \sum_x \rho_x - \ell$. Four moving lines: Theorem 4.4 of note 82. Three leaves: move $z_1, z_2, z_3$ to $(1,0), (0,1), (1,1)$ by $\mathrm{GL}_2$; a relation is $u_1 = u_2 = u_3$. $\square$ **The bound $\ell - 2$ is attained (exact at the stated size):** at $(32, 64)$, the pattern `86_tmp/cand5.txt`, first line (level-3 pattern `012 02 . . 12 . . 01`, the blocks of eight leaves under its nodes 2 and 3 equal to `012 012 012 012 . 012 . 012` and `012 012 . 012 . 012 . 012`) has $w = 62$, rank 59, $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 62$, five lined leaves with one common line: $\Delta = 3 = \ell - 2$. The second and third lines of that file have $(\ell, \Delta) = (6, 2)$ and $(7, 1)$.

## 5. What replaces the binary pattern one level up

**5.1 The corrected one-level statement (K).** For a system of subspaces $\Lambda_x \subseteq K_1^L$ that do not involve the bottom twiddles, let $\Xi_p = \{\nu \in K_1^L : \nu \otimes g_p \otimes F^2 \subseteq R\}$: **all** functionals with coefficients in the field of the upper twiddles that kill both bottom slices of every solution at the pair $p$ (not only those with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$), $Y^K = \sum_p \Xi_p \otimes g_p$, $\Delta_p = \Xi_p^\perp \subseteq K_1^L$ (the span of the difference vectors at $p$ of the slices of the solutions, taken over $K_1$). Statement (K): $r = 2 \dim Y^K + \sum_x \mathrm{rank}(\Lambda_x|_{\Delta_{p(x)}})$. This is formula (C\*) word for word with the binary pattern $\mathcal Q^*$ replaced by the tuple $\Xi$; it is note 74's one-level identity with its largest minimizer, and it is a theorem when at most four leaves have a full part not spanned by vectors over $K_1$ (Theorem 4.4 of note 82; the four-arm step is re-proved in note 87). The upper object is one subspace of $K^L$ per node, so the data per node is finite-dimensional (a point of a Grassmannian of $K^L$; for three members: nothing, everything, or one point of the projective line), but it is no longer one of finitely many states: the coordinates are rational functions of the upper twiddles.

**Tests of (K) (exact at the stated sizes; `86_hull.py`, `86_hull_m.py`: $\Xi_p$ is computed as the common kernel over three draws of the bottom twiddles).** (K) holds, and (C\*) fails, on all 54 violating patterns of the structured search and all 61 of the random reduced search at $(16, 32)$ (each time $\dim Y^K = \dim Y^* + 1$: three lonely triples with a rational line that is not binary, none with a moving line), on the three patterns at $(32, 64)$ of Theorem 4.4, and on the four failures of note 87 with five and six members at $(8, 16)$ and on their saturations ($w = 32, 31, 32, 38$, ranks $31, 30, 31, 37$; $\dim Y^K - \dim Y^* = 2, 1, 2, 2$, with a non-binary rational functional at 7, 4, 7 and 7 of the 8 pairs). No failure of (K) was found.

**5.2 The class of such tuples is closed, the scalar recursion is not (exact at the stated sizes; `86_chain.py`).** The tuple $\Xi$ is again a system on the upper code, so one can ask for the chain $Y_0 = R(\mathcal P), Y_1 = Y^K(\mathcal P), Y_2 = Y^K(Y_1), \dots$ and for (K) at every level. The derivative lemma needs node spaces that do not involve the twiddles of the level at which it is applied, and $\Xi_p$ may involve the twiddles of its own level. Call a line of $\Xi$ at level $h$ *born at level $h$* if it moves with the twiddles of level $h$. Results: on the basic lift `. 012 | 12 12 | 02 02 | 01 01`: level 0 exact, $Y_1$ has node spaces of total dimension 4 (the three pairs and one born line at the node of the lonely triple) and dimension 3; (K) at level 1 would give 4: **defect 1 at level 1**, harmless because the born line is redundant ($Y_1$ is also the row space of the binary pattern). On the counterexample of Theorem 2.1 and on all 54 violating patterns: (K) is exact at levels 0 and 1 (the lines of $Y_1$ do not move with the level-1 twiddles: they are inherited from level 2), and **at level 2 the defect is exactly 1**: $Y_2$ has the binary rows of a pattern $\mathcal Q_2$ and two born lines $\nu$, $\mu$ at two nodes $a, b$, of dimension $r(\mathcal Q_2) + 1$ and not $r(\mathcal Q_2) + 2$. On note 87's patterns with five and six members at $(8, 16)$: level 0 exact, and at level 1 the node spaces of $Y_1$ have total dimension 21, 15, 21, 23 for $\dim Y_1 = 14, 12, 14, 16$: **defects 7, 3, 7, 7 at level 1**, with 9, 4, 9, 9 non-binary dimensions born there. So: (K) is exact for a system whose node spaces are constant at its level; at a level where lines are born the dimension of the upper object is smaller than (K) predicts by the number of *birth relations*, the relations that define the born lines (in Theorem 2.1: $\nu \otimes g_a + \mu \otimes g_b \in R(\mathcal Q_2)$).

**5.3 What an exact closed recursion has to carry (proved reformulations; the closed form is conjectural).** (a) On 114 of the 116 violating three-member patterns at $(16, 32)$ (`86_birth.py`) the upper object at the level of birth is an **intersection of two binary row spaces**, $Y_2 = R(\mathcal Q_2 + a) \cap R(\mathcal Q_2 + b)$ ($\mathcal Q_2$ the binary part of $Y_2$, $\mathcal Q_2 + a$: the triple added at the node $a$; ranks $5, 7, 7, 8$, so dimension $7 + 7 - 8 = 6 = r(\mathcal Q_2) + 1$ by the modular law). Dually, the slices of the solutions span $\ker(\mathcal Q_2 + a) + \ker(\mathcal Q_2 + b)$: **a sum of solution spaces of binary patterns, which is not the solution space of a binary pattern.** On the other two, three nodes carry a born line and $Y_2$ is $R(\mathcal Q_2)$ plus the plane spanned by the three components of the single relation $u_1 + u_2 + u_3 \in R(\mathcal Q_2)$ among the three planes (a sum of intersections, not one intersection). In general (proved, linear algebra): if $T$ is a set of nodes of a binary pattern $\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal N_S$ the space of relations among the planes of the nodes of $S \subseteq T$ modulo $R(\mathcal Q)$, $n_S = \sum_{u \in S} r(\mathcal Q + u) - r(\mathcal Q + S) - (|S| - 1)\, r(\mathcal Q)$ its dimension, then the span of $R(\mathcal Q)$ and of all components of these relations has dimension $r(\mathcal Q) + \sum_{u \in T} (n_T - n_{T \setminus u}) - n_T$: **the dimension of the upper object at the level of birth is a function of the binary rank function at that level**, and a line is born at $T$ exactly where that rank function fails to be modular ($n_T > 0$) beyond what binary pure tensors explain. (b) So the class that is closed under the recursion is the **lattice generated by the binary row spaces under sum and intersection** (equivalently by the binary pure tensors $\theta \otimes g_u$), with its dimension function; binary patterns are its join-sublattice. The corrected conjecture is: $r(\mathcal P) = \min_Y [2 \dim Y + \sum_x \dim (U_x + Y)/Y]$ over the elements $Y$ of that lattice one level up (the bound holds for every subspace $Y$: Lemma 1.1 of note 82). Dimensions of iterated intersections are again ranks of binary patterns with more members: $\dim(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap A_3) = \sum_c \dim A_c - \dim \sum_c \ell_c \otimes A_c$, and $\sum_c \ell_c \otimes R(\mathcal Q_c)$ is the row space of a binary pattern with member space $\mathbb F_2^2 \otimes \mathbb F_2^L$. So the recursion closes only on **all numbers of members at once**: the three-member problem at depth $v$ calls problems with up to $2L + 1$ members at depth $v - 1$. I have no bound on the depth of the lattice words that occur; in the 116 three-member examples they are the words of (a), two levels up. (c) An equivalent finite-state description of these examples: a pattern with one extra symbol $\varepsilon$ (a *virtual pair*): the leaves under $a$ and $b$ whose class is the triple also carry $\varepsilon$, the count below the level of birth is the binary one with four pair symbols, and at the level of birth the two nodes carrying $\varepsilon$ count for one. (d) Exact and trivial, for orientation: the relations of $\mathcal P$ are the pairs $(a, b)$ of relations of the binary sibling-join pattern $\mathcal P^\vee$ one level up with $(a_p, b_p) \in \{(\nu_0 + \nu_1, t_{p0} \nu_0 + t_{p1} \nu_1) : \nu_b \in \Lambda_{pb}\}$ at every pair; so the object that determines everything below is the *code of relations* of a binary pattern (a subspace of $\bigoplus_p K^{\dim \Lambda^\vee_p}$, at most $L$ coordinates per node), and (C') is the restriction of note 74's formula $\max_{X'} [2 \dim(N \cap X') - \dim X']$ to binary $X'$.

## 6. The mechanism at every scale (proved), and how large the loss can be

Cut the code at level $i \ge 1$: a leaf $x$ below a node $u$ of level $i$ has $g_x = g_u \otimes \beta_x$, $\beta_x \in K^{2^i}$ its row in the local code of the block of $u$; the $2^i$ local rows of a block are a basis, and the local rows of leaves in different blocks are rows of a generic tree code of dimension $2^i$, hence MDS.

**Proposition 6.1 (coincidence bound; proved, any number of members).** Let $Y \subseteq V_i$ be any subspace, $\lambda \in V_i \setminus Y$, and $n_\lambda$ the number of leaves $x$ with $\lambda \in \Lambda_x \otimes g_{u(x)} + Y$. Then $r(\mathcal P) \le 2^i \dim Y + \sum_x \dim (\Lambda_x \otimes g_{u(x)} + Y)/Y - (n_\lambda - 2^i)$. *Proof.* $R \subseteq Y' \otimes K^{2^i} + \sum_x Q_x \otimes \beta_x$ with $Y' = Y + \lambda$ and $Q_x$ a complement of $(\Lambda_x \otimes g_u) \cap Y'$; passing from $Y$ to $Y'$ costs $2^i$ and saves one for each of the $n_\lambda$ leaves. $\square$ A functional on the upper solutions that more than $2^i$ leaves offer makes the count drop by the excess. For a binary $\lambda = \theta \otimes g_u$ the leaves are below one node (at most $2^i$ of them) unless another node gives the same functional modulo $Y$, which is a deficiency of a binary pattern at level $i$ and is what the binary recursion counts. For a line that is not binary and lies in the planes of two nodes $a$, $b$ (three members), $n_\lambda$ is the number of triple leaves below $a$ and $b$, at most $2(2^i - 1)$ if neither block is full: **the loss against the binary count can reach $2^i - 2$ for one line at level $i$**. Observed (exact at the stated sizes): $3 + 2 - 4 = 1$ at $(16, 32)$, $i = 2$ (Theorem 2.1); $6 + 5 - 8 = 3$, $5 + 5 - 8 = 2$, $5 + 4 - 8 = 1$ at $(32, 64)$, $i = 3$ (`86_tmp/cand5.txt`). With $3 + 3$ triple leaves at $(16, 32)$ the line is absorbed: the binary full part contains a triple under each of the two nodes, the two remaining lonely triples see it as one of note 82's conic functionals, and (C\*) holds with rank $30 = w - 2$ (`86_tmp/cand3.txt`).

**Why $k \le 8$ is safe for three members and $k = 16$ is not (exact at the stated sizes).** A line that is not binary, common to the planes of two empty nodes of a pattern $\mathcal Q$ modulo $R(\mathcal Q)$, with no binary pure tensor in $R(\mathcal Q) + \lambda$ outside $R(\mathcal Q)$, does not exist on the code $(2, 4)$ (all 625 patterns, `86_coincide.py 1 1 all`), nor for four-member class patterns there (`86_coincide4.py 4 1 1 all`: 485094 pairs of extended nodes), nor in 30000 random patterns on $(2, 8)$; on $(4, 8)$ it is common (2859 pairs of nodes in 20000 random patterns): it needs an upper solution space of dimension 3 and two nodes where the solutions take all difference vectors. Two levels below such a pair of nodes there is room for $n_\lambda > 4$. At $(8, 16)$ the line would have to be shared by three lonely triples one level up, and Theorem 3.1 says that then a binary pure tensor always appears. For five and six members note 87 finds violations already at $(8, 16)$, with lines born one level up (5.2).
