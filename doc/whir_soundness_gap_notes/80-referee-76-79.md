## 1. Theorem 4.1 (splitting): confirmed for T2, any number of trees

**Re-derivation.** A leaf $(p, b)$ has rows $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (1, t_{p,b})$, $\lambda \in \Lambda_{(p,b)}$, with $\Lambda$ constant and $t_{p,0}, t_{p,1}$ independent variables (T2). Scaling a row by $1/t$ does not change the rank, the scaled matrix is polynomial in $t_{p,\chi(p)}$ and $\tau = 1/t_{p,1-\chi(p)}$, and the rank over the function field is at least the rank at $t = 0$, $\tau = 0$; there the rows are $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (1, 0)$ and $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (0, 1)$, which read the slices $M_0$ and $M_1$ separately, with the upper twiddles untouched and still generic. Both leaves of a pair to the same side: both twiddles to $0$, or both to $\infty$, give $(\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1}) \otimes g_p$ on that side. Nothing uses the number of trees. Correct.

**Exact check (`scripts/80_match.c`, output `80_match_output.txt`).** Random choice $\chi$, exact ranks of $\mathcal P$, $\mathcal P^0$, $\mathcal P^1$ (the upper code is built from the same upper twiddles): $\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P) \ge \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P^0) + \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P^1)$ in all 27962 patterns (3 to 6 members; $(k, n) = (2, 4), (4, 8), (8, 16), (16, 32)$; one third random classes, one third block-constant with a block of common zeros, one third staircases), with equality reached at every size. The classes tested are cliques (graphic spaces of one class); general constant subspaces are covered by the proof only.

**Referee: variant T1.** The split form holds for T1 as well: put $t_{p,0} = c_p \in \{0, 1\}$, so that the leaf $b = c_p$ has row $(1, 0)$ and its sibling $(1, 1)$; in the coordinates $(M_0, M_0 + M_1)$ the specialized matrix is block diagonal, and the upper code is the generic T1 code. The merge variant is not obtained this way (the possible limits of a pair are the slices $M_0$, $M_0 + M_1$ at $t = 0, 1$ and $M_1$ twice at $t = \infty$, three different slices), so for T1 only bichromatic splits are proved. Checked: the same 27963 patterns with T1 twiddles, no violation; and no three-member pattern with $\mathrm{rank}_{\rm T1}$ below the full recursion $r_v$ (merges included) among all $5^8$ at $(4, 8)$ and $4 \cdot 10^6$ at $(8, 16)$ (Section 8).

## 2. Corollary 4.2 (matching bound): confirmed; exactly which matchings

**Statement refereed.** Rate 1/2 (two trees), T2, constant subspaces: $\mathrm{rank} \ge \sum_{x \in T_0} \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\mu(x)})$ for every bijection $\mu$ from the leaves of tree 0 to the leaves of tree 1. No condition on $\mu$: the tree structure of neither side enters.

**Re-derivation.** The graph with the sibling edges and the edges $\{x, \mu(x)\}$ is 2-regular, its cycles alternate the two kinds, a $\mu$-edge changes tree, so each cycle has an even number of $\mu$-edges and hence of sibling edges, and the leaves can be coloured with sibling pairs bichromatic and $\mu$-pairs monochromatic. With $\chi(p)$ the leaf of colour 0, $\mu$ restricts to bijections $\mu^0, \mu^1$ between the pairs of the two trees, Theorem 4.1 (split form only) gives $\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P) \ge \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P^0) + \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P^1)$, and the induction closes at $v = 0$, where the code is $\{(m, m)\}$ and the rank is $\dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_y)$. Correct.

**Exact check (`80_match.c`).** For each of the 27962 patterns of Section 1: 20 uniformly random bijections (559240 in all) and the best bijection by the Hungarian method: the rank is never below the bound; it equals the best bound in 22217 patterns. Lifted patterns are included (block-constant classes, staircases); at $(8, 16)$ and $(16, 32)$ this is the test asked for.

**Which matchings are allowed (sharp).** Allowed: all $k!$ bijections between the two trees. Not allowed: arbitrary perfect matchings of the $n$ leaves. Control in the same program: 20 uniformly random perfect matchings of all $n$ leaves per pattern give a bound above the rank in 1923 of 559240 cases, for instance at $(8, 16)$ the lift `12 12 12 12 | 02 02 02 02 || 012 012 012 . | 01 01 01 01` (rank 15) with the matching $(10,11)(7,0)(2,8)(3,5)(6,12)(1,13)(9,14)(4,15)$ (bound 16). Matchings inside a tree are covered only in the form of Corollary 4.3.

**Referee: T1.** The induction uses only bichromatic splits, so by Section 1 Corollary 4.2 holds for the generic T1 code too. Checked on 27963 patterns with T1 twiddles (559260 random bijections and the best one): no violation.

**Referee: more than two trees.** The statement and the proof are for two trees. With $2^r$ trees the natural statement is about systems of $k$ groups with one leaf of each tree and the bound $\sum_{\rm groups} \dim \sum_{x} \Lambda_x$; the same induction proves it exactly when, at every level, the graph "groups joined by sibling pairs" is bipartite (for instance when all the bijections are the same level-swap isomorphism), and not otherwise. The smallest system outside this class (four trees, $k = 4$, the three pairings of four groups all used) shows no violation in 600000 patterns of 3 to 6 members, T2 and T1 (`80_match4.c`, `80_match4_output.txt`): open, neither proved nor refuted. Restricting a pattern to two of the trees gives the two-tree bound on those $2k$ leaves at any rate, trivially.

**Consequence 2 (Hall form): confirmed, and made exact for three members (referee).** Koenig's theorem applied to "no bijection has all pairs spanning" gives the three alternatives of the note. For three members the best bound of Corollary 4.2 has a closed form: with $l_j, f_j$ the numbers of leaves of tree $j$ with at most one member and with all three, and $n_\lambda$ the number of leaves with exactly the pair $\lambda$,
$$\max_\mu \sum_{x \in T_0} \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\mu(x)}) \;=\; 2k - \max\big(0,\; l - f,\; l_0 - f_1,\; l_1 - f_0,\; \max_\lambda (l + n_\lambda - k)\big).$$
*Proof.* A transportation problem on the five leaf types; by LP duality and integrality the minimum loss is the maximum over the vertices of the dual polyhedron, which does not depend on $k$; a complete enumeration finds exactly these seven vertices (`80_r3.py`, part D; the normalized potentials lie in $[-2, 2]$, the scan covers $[-6, 6]$), and the formula agrees with the Hungarian value on all 33391 profiles with $k \le 6$ (part C). $\square$ Consequences 3 and 4 of the note (the 35-member staircase, random-like sub-lists) were not refereed.

## 3. Corollary 4.3 (sibling and cut bounds): confirmed for T2 at rate 1/2; the written proof does not apply to T1

**Re-derivation (T2).** Specialize the twiddle of the second child of every chosen node $u$, and every twiddle below it, to the corresponding twiddle of the first child's subtree through the isomorphism $\iota_u$: the rows of $x$ and $\iota_u(x)$ become $\Lambda_x \otimes g_x$ and $\Lambda_{\iota_u(x)} \otimes g_x$, the $k$ leaves $x$ that survive keep generic twiddles, and their rows are independent because the code is MDS, so the specialized rank is $\sum \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\iota_u(x)})$. Nodes of different heights may be mixed as long as they partition the leaves. Correct. Two restrictions that the statement should carry: (i) $\iota_u$ must be a tree isomorphism (otherwise the paths of twiddles cannot be identified); (ii) rate 1/2 is used (with more trees there are $n/2 > k$ surviving rows, which are not independent).

**The proof is T2 only.** In T1 the two twiddles of a pair differ by 1 and cannot be made equal. **Referee: the statement is nevertheless true for T1** (sibling bound, and cuts at one height). Send $t_p \to \infty$ for a set $P_\infty$ of $k/2$ pairs (both rows of the pair become $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (0, 1)$) and $t_p = 0$ for the other $k/2$ pairs $P_0$ (rows $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (1, 0)$ and $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (1, 1)$). The specialized row space contains $\sum_{P_\infty} (\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1}) \otimes g_p \otimes (0, 1)$, of dimension $\sum_{P_\infty} \dim(\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1})$ because any $k/2$ rows of the upper code $[k, k/2]$ are independent, and modulo the slice $K^L \otimes K^{k/2} \otimes (0, 1)$ the rows of $P_0$ project onto $\sum_{P_0} (\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1}) \otimes g_p$, of dimension $\sum_{P_0} \dim(\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1})$ for the same reason. For a cut at height $h$ identify the two lower subtrees first (allowed in T1) and do the same with the twiddle of the node. Mixed heights in T1: not examined.

**Exact check (`80_match.c`).** Sibling bound on all patterns of Section 1, and at every height $h = 2, \dots, v + 1$ one cut bound with a random level-swap isomorphism in every node (81943 cut bounds for T2, 81946 for T1; $h = v + 1$ pairs the two trees): no violation for T2 and none for T1; the sibling bound equals the rank in 4318 (T2) and 4388 (T1) of about 27960 patterns. Mixed heights were not tested.

## 4. Lemma 1.1 (the dual of a rate-1/2 tree code): confirmed

**Re-derivation.** One tree is $T = \mathrm{diag}(T_a, T_b)(X \otimes I)$ with $X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t_a \\ 1 & t_b \end{pmatrix}$ (the message split by its top variable). $X^{-1} = (t_a + t_b)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} t_b & t_a \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ in characteristic 2, so $X^{-T} = (t_a + t_b)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t_b \\ 1 & t_a \end{pmatrix} J$, the exchanged matrix times the swap $J$ of the two coordinates. By induction $T_a^{-T} = \Delta_a T_a^{\rm sw} P'$ and the same $P'$ for $T_b$, and $(I \otimes P')(X^{\rm sw} J \otimes I) = (X^{\rm sw} \otimes I)(J \otimes P')$, so $T^{-T} = \Delta\, T^{\rm sw} P$ with $P = J \otimes P'$ the complement of every index bit, the same for every tree. Then $C^\perp = \{(y_0, y_1) : T_0^T y_0 + T_1^T y_1 = 0\} = \{(T_0^{-T} z, T_1^{-T} z)\} = \Delta \cdot C^{\rm sw}$ (the common $P$ is absorbed in $z$). Only $t(p, 0) \ne t(p, 1)$ is used, so the lemma holds for T1 and T2 and for any specialization, as stated; it is specific to two trees, as stated.

**Exact check (`scripts/80_dual.c`, output `80_dual_output.txt`).** $\sum_x g_x[e]\, \Delta_x\, g^{\rm sw}_x[e'] = 0$ for all $e, e'$ and $\mathrm{rank}\, G^{\rm sw} = k$, for $v = 1, \dots, 6$ (up to $(64, 128)$), T2 and T1; for T2 the same sum without $\Delta$ is not zero (control), for T1 it is ($\Delta = 1$ there). The remark "the supports of dual codewords are the supports of codewords of a generic tree code" follows (the exchanged twiddles are again independent). Lemma 1.2 (secant form) is the usual syndrome correspondence and is correct: for $|E| \le n - a \le n - k$ the parity-check columns on $E$ are independent, so a codeword at agreement at least $a$ and a support with all coefficients nonzero determine each other.

## 5. Lemma 2.1 (monotonicity in $n$ of the largest list at agreement $\tau n$): confirmed

**Re-derivation.** The top code at blocks of two leaves of the generic $[2n, n]$ tree code is the generic $[n, n/2]$ tree code (layers 1 and above, independent twiddles). For a list $c_0, \dots, c_m$ of the top code around a word $u$, the messages $A_l \otimes B$ with $B = (1, 0)$ encode to $x \mapsto c_l(\text{block of } x)$, the word to $x \mapsto u(\text{block of } x)$; each member keeps its agreement set, with every block replaced by its two leaves, so agreement $\tau n$ becomes $2\tau n = \tau \cdot 2n$, and distinct members stay distinct. Hence $\lambda_\tau(n) \le \lambda_\tau(2n)$. The definition of $\lambda_\tau$ does not depend on the field over which the word is taken (the existence of a list for a pattern is a rank condition, and the fields are infinite). Correct; "(S8) is $\sup_n \lambda_{5/8}(n) \le \Lambda_8$" follows.

**Exact check (`80_lem23 lift`, output `80_lem23_output.txt`).** All 162 five-member patterns of note 76 at $n = 16$, lifted to $(16, 32)$ by copying each class to the two leaves of a pair: every one carries five distinct codewords, each member at agreement at least $20 = 5 \cdot 32/8$ (my own incidence system; a draw of the twiddles of the larger code). So $\lambda_{5/8}(32) \ge 5$ by this route as well. Lemma 2.2 (full blocks) was not refereed.

## 6. Lemma 2.3 (top-down derivative lemma): confirmed for T2

**Re-derivation.** With $g_x = (1, t_q) \otimes g'_x$ for $x$ in the top block $q$, a relation reads $\sum_q (1, t_q) \otimes \rho_q = 0$, $\rho_q = \sum_{x \in q} \nu_x \otimes g'_x$. In T2 the variable $t_q$ occurs in the rows of block $q$ only, and $\partial/\partial t_q$ is a derivation of the function field that kills the constant spaces $\Lambda_x$ and the $g'_x$; applied to the relation it gives $(0, 1) \otimes \rho_q + \sum_x (\partial_q \nu_x) \otimes g_x = 0$ with $\partial_q \nu_x \in \Lambda_x \otimes F$, so $(0, 1) \otimes \rho_q \in R$; and $(1, t_q) \otimes \rho_q = \sum_{x \in q} \nu_x \otimes g_x \in R$, hence $(1, 0) \otimes \rho_q \in R$. Correct (an ordinary derivative suffices; no Hasse derivative is needed). The "consequence" ($R = \sum_q z_q \otimes R_q$, $\dim R_q = w_q$) is immediate: the rows of one subtree are independent because a subtree is a bijection. The remarks on the four-subspace problem are not statements and were not refereed.

**T1.** There $t_{q'} = t_q + 1$ for the two children of a root, and the same computation gives only $(0, 1) \otimes (\rho_q + \rho_{q'}) \in R$. The lemma as stated is a T2 statement.

**Exact check (`80_lem23 deriv`, output `80_lem23_output.txt`).** Random and block-constant patterns with dependent rows and rank below $Lk$ (otherwise there is nothing to test), a random relation $\nu$ from the left kernel, its four block parts: $(1, 0) \otimes \rho_q$ and $(0, 1) \otimes \rho_q$ lie in the row space in every case: T2, 2957 and 4852 patterns at $(8, 16)$ with 3 and 4 members and 743 at $(16, 32)$ with 4 members (30900 nonzero block parts), no failure. With T1 twiddles the single block parts were in the row space as well in all 30889 cases, so I have no counterexample to the lemma for T1, only no proof.

## 8. Conjecture 4.4 (three members: generic rank = split-and-merge recursion $r_v$): FALSE

**Definition used.** $r_v$ exactly as in `76_scheme.py`: at every level each leaf of each sibling pair goes to side 0 or 1 (four options a pair, two leaves on one side are joined), and $r_0(x, y) = \dim(x + y)$. For three members the spaces are $0$, the three lines `01`, `02`, `12` and $K^2$ (`012`). My program `80_conj44.c` tabulates $r_1$ and $r_2$ (all $5^8$ patterns), computes $r_3$ exactly ($4^8$ options, two table lookups each) and $r_4$ exactly by branch and bound (bounds: weights, and the exact ranks of the two upper patterns with every undecided pair joined on both sides, valid because $r_3 \le \mathrm{rank}$ by Theorem 4.1 and the rank is monotone). $r_v \le \mathrm{rank}$ held in every case below, as Theorem 4.1 requires.

**$(4, 8)$: true, exhaustively.** For all 390625 three-member patterns (190818 with dependent rows) $r_2$ equals the exact rank (`80_conj44 all48`); the T1 rank is the same in every case.

**$(8, 16)$: false.** Among $4 \cdot 10^6$ patterns (five generators: independent leaves at random densities, block-constant with a block of common zeros and a few flips, two scales mixed, dense, sibling-correlated; 663768 of the first million have dependent rows) **55 have $r_3 < \mathrm{rank}$**, all with $w = \mathrm{rank} = 16 = Lk$ and $r_3 = 15$. First instance, as classes per leaf: `02 02 012 012 | . . . 012 || . 012 012 . | 12 12 01 01`. Its 16 incidence rows are independent (no nonzero solution), and no split-and-merge scheme certifies more than 15. This is certain, not statistical: rank 16 is attained at a draw of the twiddles and is the number of columns, so it is the generic rank; and $r_3$ is a finite maximum. **Confirmed with note 76's own code** (`80_conj44_check.py` imports `r` from `76_scheme.py` and the rank from `76_match.py`, `76_dual.py`): rank 16 for three draws, $r_v = 15$, for this pattern and two others. The matching bound of Corollary 4.2 is also 15 here ($l_0 - f_1 = 3 - 2$ in the formula of Section 2). Since $r_2$ is the rank for every pattern at $(4, 8)$, the same example refutes the one-level identity $\dim R = \max_{\rm sides}(\dim R^0 + \dim R^1)$ that the note says the conjecture contains: for every choice of sides the two upper ranks add up to at most 15.

**$(16, 32)$: false, also for patterns that carry lists, and the gap grows.** Among $2 \cdot 10^6$ patterns (same generators) a labelling reaching the rank exists for all but **41**, for which the branch and bound completes and gives $r_4 = \mathrm{rank} - 1$ (no undecided case): 24 with $(w, \mathrm{rank}) = (32, 32)$, 7 with $(34, 32)$, 2 with $(33, 32)$, 1 with $(32, 31)$, 2 with $(31, 31)$, 5 with $(30, 30)$. So the failure is not confined to full column rank: `02 02 02 02 | . . . . | 012 . 012 . | 012 . . . || 12 12 12 12 | 01 01 01 01 | . 012 012 012 | 012 012 012 012` has weight 32, rank 31 and carries three distinct codewords (a list above the generalized Singleton bound, `80_t1`), and $r_4 = 30$. Lifting the $(8, 16)$ example by one layer gives weight 32, rank 32 and $r_4 = 30$ exactly (branch and bound, and again by full enumeration of all options with no pruning): the gap doubles.

**What remains true.** $r_v$ is a valid lower bound (Theorem 4.1), exact at $(4, 8)$ and wrong on about 14 per million of my samples at $(8, 16)$ and 20 per million at $(16, 32)$. Note 76's tables (336, 945 and 1740 deficient orbits, 3300 random patterns) are not contradicted: its random sample at $(8, 16)$ had 300 patterns. I did not re-run those tables. At $(8, 16)$ every counterexample I found has independent rows, so "exact on patterns with dependent rows at $(8, 16)$" is not refuted there; at $(16, 32)$ it is. The sentence of the note "it would settle suggestion 1 of the brief for three members" has to go: there is no combinatorial rank formula of this kind.

**By-product (referee; T1 against T2).** For three members the generic ranks of T1 and T2 differ: 19 of the $4 \cdot 10^6$ patterns at $(8, 16)$, all with weight 16, T2 rank 16 and T1 rank 15, for instance `12 12 01 01 | 02 02 . 012 || 12 12 012 . | 01 01 012 .`, which carries three distinct codewords in T1 and none in T2 (`80_t1`). No pattern has T1 rank above T2 rank (T1 is a specialization) and none at $(4, 8)$ differs.
