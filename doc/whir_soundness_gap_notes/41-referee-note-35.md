## 0. Verdict

**The conditional theorem of note 35 is correct with changes.** No lemma of the note is false. Two changes are needed before anything is built on it, and I made both.

1. **The hypothesis of the conditional statement is false for generic words as it is written (E1).** Note 35, Section 6: "if no explainer of $y$ has an irreducible first-integral factor with $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha > 2n$, then ...". For any two explainers $Q, Q'$ of multiplicity $m$ the Wronskian $Q'\partial Q - Q\partial Q' = Q_1Q_0' - Q_0Q_1'$ is again an explainer (multiplicity $2m$), is a first integral, and lies in $I_\alpha^m$ for every $\alpha$: contact order $2m$ on every line **[proved, Proposition W]**. For random words at $n = 16$ and $32$, rates 1/4 and 1/2, subspaces and arbitrary evaluation sets, it is absolutely irreducible over $F(\tau)$, has $b \ge 1$ and a leading $Y_1$-coefficient involving $Y_0$ **[checked, 9 instances]**. So for such words "no explainer has such a factor" does not hold, and the sentence of Section 6 "I know no example of an irreducible first integral singular along a positive fraction of the lines of a word" has counterexamples: squares for every word (Section 3.1), and the Wronskian in every random instance tested. The proof of the note never uses the hypothesis in that form: it uses one explainer. The right statement is about the **fixed components** of the explainer space (Proposition F), and only the **absolutely irreducible** ones matter (Lemma R). With these two changes the statement is Theorem 8' below.
2. **The final inequality is now evaluated uniformly (E2).** Note 35 checks inequality (2) of Lemma C at leading order and on 150 contact profiles, with the side condition of Lemma C tested at the lower bound of $W$ instead of the actual $W$. Lemma U below reduces all contact profiles to finitely many by convexity, and `41_counts.py D, H, I` do the exact integer evaluation at $n = 2^{25}$ with the side condition at the largest possible $W$. Result **[proved, with an exact finite computation]**: at rate 1/2, $a = 0.70$, certificate $(18, 5)$, every first-integral factor with $b \ge 1$ is cut by Lemma C with multiplicity at most 79, except the factors of the residual class; the list bound becomes $2750 < 2^{12}$ instead of $2^{16}$, for $n = 2^{25}, 2^{26}, 2^{27}, 2^{28}$ (`41_counts.py K`), and the cap $M = 600$ is not needed. The same holds at $n = 2^{25}$ and $a = 0.6949$ (certificate $(37, 11)$, bound $12240 < 2^{14}$, 206 queries) and $a = 0.6919$ (certificate $(100, 28)$, bound $93702 < 2^{17}$, 204 queries).

**What remains open is exactly what note 35 says, but it is a quantitative statement, not a qualitative one.** Singular first integrals of efficiency $W/\sum o_\alpha$ just below $a$ exist for every generic word (the Wronskians). A component escapes Lemma C only if its efficiency is below $e^*(a)$, and $e^*(a_1) = a_1$ exactly, $e^*(a) < a_1$ for $a > a_1$ **[proved]** ($0.6717$ at $a = 0.70$; `41_counts.py J` finds $0.6721$ with exact integers). So the claim to prove is: no absolutely irreducible fixed first-integral component has efficiency in the window below $e^*(a)$, a margin of $0.028$ at $a = 0.70$ that shrinks to zero at $a_1$. Any proof that "singular first-integral factors do not exist" without using the efficiency is wrong, and any proof must fail when $e^*(a)$ is replaced by $a$. Section 7 gives the exact residual statement for researcher 40.

## 2. Answers to the six questions of the brief

**(a) Step 1 in characteristic 2.** Correct. Only the first derivative is used, and the first Hasse derivative is the formal derivative in every characteristic; identity (1) is $P(X) - P(\alpha) - (X - \alpha)P'(X) \equiv 0 \bmod (X - \alpha)^2$, true over $\mathbb{Z}$. In characteristic 2 the coefficient of $(X - \alpha)^2$ is $+c_2$ instead of $-c_2$, which changes nothing. The rank bound is a statement about the kernel "multiples of $(U - Y_1)^{m - s}$ in a rectangle" and inverts no integer; I checked it over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{16})$ on the actual systems (table above). "$Q(X, P, P') = 0$ for every close $P$" is forced: $\deg_X Q(X, P, P') \le \max(x + \Delta a + (\Delta - 1)c) < mA$ and each of the $\ge A$ agreements is a zero of order $\ge m$. **[checked]** random elements of all 28 computed explainer spaces kill every list member (lists of 1 to 126 members), and never a random polynomial. Nothing is lost at this step because $P \mapsto (P, P')$ is injective on polynomials: $(P, P')$ is an invertible image of $(g, h)$. What is lost is Step 2, as the note says.

**(b) From $Q(X, P, P') = 0$ to $Q_0 = Q_1 = 0$, and Bezout.** Correct. $F[X, Y_0, Y_1] = F[\tau, u, v] \oplus X F[\tau, u, v]$, and on a jet $u = g(X^2)$, $v = h(X^2)$, so the two parts lie in $F[X^2]$ and $X F[X^2]$. Distinct list members are distinct points of $F(\tau)^2$ with polynomial coordinates, and two coprime polynomials of $F(\tau)[u, v]$ of total degrees $d_0, d_1$ have at most $d_0 d_1$ common zeros in the affine plane over any extension; so the number of sections is bounded by the intersection number. Coprimality passes from $F[\tau][u, v]$ to $F(\tau)[u, v]$ by Gauss. **[checked]** on every instance: the members off the common component never exceed $(d_0 - t)(d_1 - t)$, and every member is either on the common component at all three random $\tau$ or off it at all three.

**(c) Lemmas A, A+, C.** All three proofs are right (Section 3.5 for the one word to add in Lemma A). The cap $M$ only makes the bound explicit. Irreducibility is used over $F(\tau)$ only (to get a coprime pair for Bezout), and primitivity to pass divisibility through Gauss; absolute irreducibility is not needed by the lemmas, but Lemma R shows that a component that is not absolutely irreducible carries at most $\delta^2/4$ sections anyway. Multiplicities of components play no role: $L_G$ is a set and Theorem 4 sums over distinct factors.

**(d) Lemma L, Propositions 6 and 7.** Correct. $W \ge (b + 2)\Delta$ needs only that $\hat G_b \in F[X, Y_0^2]$ contains a monomial with $Y_0^2$. "Smooth along its lines implies total contact at most $2n$" is the definition ($o_\alpha \ge 4$ iff $G \in I_\alpha^2$ iff $G$ and its gradient vanish along $\ell_\alpha$). Proposition 6 is sharp: Section 4.2.

**(e) Weighted degrees and contact order.** Consistent throughout: $o_\alpha$ is the largest $o$ with $\hat G \in \mathfrak{c}_o(\alpha, y_\alpha)$, equal to $2 j_\alpha$ with $G \in I_\alpha^{j_\alpha} \setminus I_\alpha^{j_\alpha + 1}$ by Proposition 2, and in the coordinates $(T, w, Y_1)$ Proposition 2 is the tautology "$i + 2l \ge m$ iff $i + 2l \ge 2\lceil m/2 \rceil$ for $i$ even, $\ge 2\lfloor m/2 \rfloor + 1$ for $i$ odd". My program measures $j_\alpha$ as the order of $G$ along a random transversal line through a random point of $\ell_\alpha$ (minimum of three trials).

**(f) The flat cubic.** Identity $\tau G^\circ = u\mathcal{L}^2 + e_0\mathcal{L} + uL_D$ and $\hat G^\circ = \partial E$ verified by hand. **[checked]** at $n = 16, 32, 64$ on the leanVM shape and on a random subspace of $\mathrm{GF}(2^{16})$: the cubic contains exactly the $n - 1$ lines $\alpha \ne 0$, with order exactly 1 (contact 2), and order 0 at $\alpha = 0$; all $n - 1$ flats are sections; every explainer computed is divisible by it exactly once and all list members lie on that component. ## 3. The changes

### 3.1 Lemma R: only absolutely irreducible components matter

**Lemma R [proved].** Let $G \in F(\tau)[u, v]$ be irreducible of degree $\delta$ and not absolutely irreducible. Then $G$ has at most $\delta^2/4$ zeros in $F(\tau)^2$; in particular $\Sigma_G$ has at most $\delta^2/4$ sections of any degree.

*Proof.* Over an algebraic closure, $G = c \prod_{i \le r} G_i^e$ with the $G_i$ pairwise non-associate, conjugate under the automorphisms of $\overline{F(\tau)}$ over $F(\tau)$, and $e$ a power of 2. If $r \ge 2$, a rational zero of $G$ is a zero of some $G_i$, hence of all its conjugates, hence a common zero of the coprime $G_1, G_2$, of degrees $\delta/(re) \le \delta/2$. If $r = 1$ then $e \ge 2$ and $G_1$, normalized, has coefficients in a finite purely inseparable extension $L$ of $F(\tau)$ and not in $F(\tau)$. Write $G_1 = \sum_k \theta_k H_k$ on a basis $(\theta_k)$ of $L$ over $F(\tau)$, $H_k \in F(\tau)[u, v]$. A rational zero of $G_1$ is a common zero of all $H_k$. Their gcd divides $G_1$, so it is 1 (if it were associate to $G_1$ then $G$ would be an $e$-th power over $F(\tau)$). As $F(\tau)$ is infinite, two $F(\tau)$-combinations of the $H_k$ are coprime, of degree $\le \delta/e \le \delta/2$. $\square$

*The typical case.* For $S = S_0 + XS_1 \in F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$ irreducible and not a first integral, $S^2 = S_0^2 + \tau S_1^2$ is a first integral, irreducible over $F(\tau)$, with $o_\alpha(S^2) = 2\,\mathrm{ord}_\alpha(S)$, $b(S^2) = 2b(S)$, $W(S^2) = 2W(S)$, and its sections are the common zeros of the coprime $S_0, S_1$. So irreducible first integrals singular along all the lines of a word exist trivially (take $S$ any irreducible polynomial of order $\ge 2$ at every received point), and they are harmless. **[checked]** `41_theory plant2`: for a word that is two polynomials $P_1, P_2$ on two halves of the positions ($n = 32$, $\Delta = 7$, $A = 15$, certificate $(6, 2)$, arbitrary points and subspace) the common component of a random explainer has degree 4 and is exactly $(Y_0 - P_1)^2 (Y_0 - P_2)^2$.

### 3.2 Proposition F: only fixed components matter

**Proposition F [proved].** Let $\mathbb{E}$ be the space of explainers of $y$ for a certificate $(m, B_\partial)$, and $H_{\mathbb{E}}$ the gcd in $F[\tau, u, v]$ of all $Q_0, Q_1$, $Q \in \mathbb{E}$. Then
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le (B_{\mathrm{jet}} - \deg_{u,v} H_{\mathbb{E}})^2 + \sum_{G \mid H_{\mathbb{E}}} |L_G|,$$
the sum over the irreducible factors of positive $(u, v)$-degree of $H_{\mathbb{E}}$, that is, over the irreducible first integrals that divide every explainer.

*Proof.* The section of a list member is a common zero of all $Q_0, Q_1$. Off $\{H_{\mathbb{E}} = 0\}$ it is a common zero of the family $\{Q_0/H_{\mathbb{E}}, Q_1/H_{\mathbb{E}}\}$, whose gcd is 1; over the infinite field $F(\tau)$ one member of the family and one $F(\tau)$-combination of it are coprime (avoid, for each irreducible factor of the first, the proper subspace of combinations divisible by it), and Bezout applies. $\square$

A Wronskian of two explainers is never a fixed component of $\mathbb{E}$ (it has twice the multiplicity). In the experiments the gcd of a random explainer was: trivial for random words and codewords with errors, the flat cubic for the flat word, $(Y_0 - P_1)^2(Y_0 - P_2)^2$ for planted pairs at $(6, 2)$.

### 3.3 Proposition W: singular first integrals always exist, so the hypothesis must be about fixed components

**Proposition W [proved].** Let $Q, Q'$ be explainers of multiplicity $m$ for the same word and agreement, with $Y_1$-degrees $\le B_\partial$. Then $\mathcal{W} = Q'\partial Q - Q\partial Q' = Q_1 Q_0' - Q_0 Q_1'$ (as an element of $F[\tau, u, v]$) is a first integral, lies in $I_\alpha^m$ for every $\alpha \in D$ (contact order $\ge 2m$ on every line), has weighted degree $< 2mA$ and $Y_1$-degree $\le 2B_\partial + 1$, and vanishes on the section of every list member. If $\mathcal{W} \ne 0$ it is an explainer of multiplicity $2m$, and some irreducible factor $G$ of it has efficiency $W(G)/\sum_\alpha o_\alpha(G) < a$.

*Proof.* $\partial Q = Q_1$ gives the determinant formula (the terms in $X$ cancel). Write $\mathcal{W} = Q_1(Q_0' + \alpha Q_1') - (Q_0 + \alpha Q_1)Q_1'$ and use Proposition 2: the two products lie in $I_\alpha^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor + \lceil m/2 \rceil}$. The operator $\partial$ does not raise the weighted degree. Weighted degrees and contact orders add over the factors of $\mathcal{W}$, so the smallest efficiency among the factors is at most that of $\mathcal{W}$, which is $< 2mA/(2mn)$. $\square$

### 3.4 Lemma U and Theorem 8': the uniform evaluation

Fix $n, \Delta, A$, an integer $b \ge 1$ and a pair $(m, B)$ with $B \ge b$. Put $\psi(j) = R(m, B) - R(m - 2j, B - b)$ (the contribution of a line of contact $2j$ to the right side of (2)), $\varphi(j) = \max(j, 2j - b)$, and $\Phi(W) = N(B, mA) - N(B - b, mA - W)$ (the left side).

**Lemma U [proved].** (i) $\Phi$ is nondecreasing and concave in $W$ ($N(B', \cdot)$ is a positive combination of the convex functions $(Z - \Delta t)_+$). (ii) Suppose $j \mapsto R(m - 2j, B - b)$ is convex on the integers $j \ge 0$ (**[checked]** for all $m \le 520$, $B' \le m$, which covers every pair used below: `41_counts.py C`). Then $\psi$ is concave and nondecreasing, and: (a) for every profile with $\sum_\alpha j_\alpha \le n$, $\sum_\alpha \psi(j_\alpha) \le n\psi(1)$, so (2) holds for every first integral with $W \ge W_0$ and total contact $\le 2n$ as soon as $\Phi(W_0) > n\psi(1)$; (b) (2) holds for every first integral with $W \ge b\Delta + \sum_\alpha \varphi(j_\alpha)$ and $W \le W_{\max}$, whatever its profile, as soon as $\Phi(b\Delta + \lfloor ns \rfloor) > nt$ at the vertices $(s, t)$ of the polygon $\mathrm{conv}\{(\varphi(j), \psi(j)) : j \ge 0\} \cap \{s \le (W_{\max} - b\Delta)/n\}$.

*Proof.* (a) is Jensen for the concave $\psi$. (b): the point $(\frac1n\sum\varphi(j_\alpha), \frac1n\sum\psi(j_\alpha))$ lies in the polygon, and the set $\{(s, t) : nt < \tilde\Phi(b\Delta + ns)\}$, $\tilde\Phi$ the piecewise linear interpolation of $\Phi$, is convex because $\tilde\Phi$ is concave; a polygon lies in a convex set iff its vertices do. Only the upper boundary matters, and it meets the line $s = s_{\max}$ on a chord between an inner and an outer point; the script tests all such chords. $\square$

The side condition of Lemma C, $\Delta(m + B) \le mA - W$, depends on the actual $W$, not on its lower bound; I impose it at $W_{\max} = m_0 A - 1$, the largest weighted degree of a factor of an explainer with certificate $(m_0, B_0)$. Factors of such an explainer have $1 \le b \le B_0$ (a point not used in note 35: $b \le B_\partial = 5$, not $25$).

## 4. Facts found while refereeing

### 4.1 $e^*(a_1) = a_1$ **[proved]**

At $a = a_1$ the bracket of the margin vanishes at its maximum $\beta^* = 3(1 - a)/(2(2 - \rho))$, so $J = \partial_\beta \mathrm{Margin}$ vanishes there and is negative beyond: the largest $\beta$ with $J \ge 0$ is $\beta^*$. And $e^*(\beta^*) = a$ is equivalent to $a^2/\rho - 1 + (1 - a)\beta^*/2 = 0$, which is the vanishing of the bracket at $\beta^*$. So a component escapes Lemma C at the threshold exactly when it is a better "explainer" than the count provides. For $a > a_1$, $e^*$ decreases: $0.6858$ at $0.692$, $0.6717$ at $0.70$, $0.660$ at $\sqrt{1/2}$ (`41_counts.py G`). The Wronskians of Section 3.3 have efficiency just below the agreement fraction of the explainers they are built from, and for a random word no explainer is expected below $a_1 \ge e^*(a)$ (**[checked]** at toy size: the explainer space of a random word has exactly the dimension $N - nR$): they are cut, as they must be, with a margin that vanishes at $a_1$.

### 4.2 The plane through all the lines; Proposition 6 is sharp **[proved]**

Let $c_D \in F[\tau]$ interpolate $\alpha$ at $\tau = \alpha^2$ and $\psi_y \in F[\tau]$ interpolate $y_\alpha$ at $\tau = \alpha^2$ (degrees $< n$), and $g_y = u + c_D(\tau)v - \psi_y(\tau)$, $V_2 = \prod_\alpha (\tau - \alpha^2)$. Then $F[\tau, u, v] = F[\tau, g_y, v]$, $I_\alpha = (\tau - \alpha^2, g_y)$, and for every profile
$$\bigcap_\alpha I_\alpha^{j_\alpha} = \bigoplus_{l \ge 0} g_y^{\,l} \prod_\alpha (\tau - \alpha^2)^{(j_\alpha - l)_+} F[\tau, v] .$$
In particular the first integrals of contact $\ge 2j$ on all lines are the elements of $(g_y, V_2)^j$. (Proof: modulo $\tau - \alpha^2$, $g_y$ is the generator $u + \alpha v - y_\alpha$; in the coordinates $(\tau, g_y, v)$ the ideal $I_\alpha$ is generated by two coordinates.) In jet coordinates $\hat g_y = Y_0 - \Lambda_D(X) Y_1 - \psi_y(X^2)$ with $\Lambda_D = X + c_D(X^2)$, which vanishes on $D$ with derivative 1; on a subspace $\Lambda_D = L_D/e_0$. For the zero word on a subspace this is $Y_0 - (L_D/e_0)Y_1$: a first integral with $b = 1$, leading coefficient in $F[X]$, containing all $n$ lines, with $W = n + \Delta$ and efficiency exactly $(1 + \rho)/2$. So the bound of Proposition 6 is attained. The description above turns the open question into an explicit one: how small can the jet weighted degree of an element of $\bigoplus_l g_y^{\,l}\prod_\alpha(\tau - \alpha^2)^{(j_\alpha - l)_+}F[\tau, v]$ be.

### 4.3 The sketch of Section 6 is right **[proved]**

Let $\hat G = c(X, Y_0) + a(X, Y_0)Y_1$ be a first integral with $b = 1$ ($a = E_{Y_0} \in F[X, Y_0^2]$, $c = E_X \in F[X^2, Y_0]$, $a_X = c_{Y_0}$) and contact order $\ge 4$ at $\alpha$. Expand $a = \sum a_{rq}T^rS^q$, $c = \sum c_{rq}T^rS^q$ at $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ and substitute $S = w + TY_1$. The terms of weight $\le 3$ give $a_{00} = a_{10} = a_{20} = a_{02} = 0$ and $c_{00} = c_{01} = c_{20} = c_{02} = 0$ (the others of total degree $\le 2$ vanish by parity), $a_{30} = c_{21}$, $a_{12} = c_{03}$. So both $E_{Y_0}$ and $E_X$ have multiplicity $\ge 3$ at the received point (Lemma L part 2 alone gives only 2 for $a$). Hence for a list member $P$ with $s$ agreements on lines of contact $\ge 4$ and $s'$ on lines of contact 2: either $E_{Y_0}(X, P) = 0$ or $W - \Delta \ge 3s + s'$. With contact 4 on all lines: $W \ge 3A + \Delta = 2.6\,n$ at $a = 0.70$, against the cut at $W \ge 2.688\,n$ (`41_counts.py J`): the window $[2.600, 2.688)\,n$ of the note. The same argument with Lemma L part 2 for general $b$ gives $W \ge b\Delta + 2A$ on a list member that is not a root of $\hat G_b$ when the contact is 4 on all lines, which closes $b = 3$ ($2.9\,n$ against a cut at $2.521\,n$) and leaves $[2.4, 2.613)\,n$ for $b = 2$.

### 4.4 The slack-1 version of the flat cubic **[proved; checked at $n = 16, 32, 64$]**

Not in note 35. For $D$ a subspace, the word $y_\alpha = \alpha^{n/2}$ and the degree bound $\Delta = n/4$ (one more than the genuine degree), the $2(n - 1)$ polynomials $X^{n/2} - Z_H(X)$, $H$ an affine hyperplane, agree with $y$ on $n/2$ points, which is exactly the Johnson boundary $A^2 = n\Delta$. All of them are sections of the cubic
$$G' = \tau v^3 + u^2 v + e_0 u + \tau\gamma_D(\tau)v + e_0\tau^{n/4}, \qquad \hat G' = \partial E', \quad E' = Y_0^3 + (L_D(X) - X^n)Y_0 + e_0 X^{n/2 + 1},$$
which contains all $n$ lines with contact 2 and has $W = 3\Delta$. (Proof: $X^{n/2} - Z_H = XP_V + c$ with $c \in \{0, \theta_V\}$, so its section is $(c + \tau h_V, c_0)$ where $(c_0, h_V)$ is the section of $P_V$; substitute in $\tau G^\circ(\tau, c_0, h_V) = 0$ and use $c(c_0 c + e_0) = 0$.) **[checked]** certificate $(7, 2)$ exists at $(n, \Delta, A) = (16, 4, 8), (32, 8, 16), (64, 16, 32)$; the common component of a random explainer is exactly this cubic; the exhaustive lists at $n = 16$ (agreement $\ge 6$) and $n = 32$ (agreement $\ge 12$) are exactly the 30 and 62 affine flats. So the obstruction of Section 5 persists with the analysis degree $k - 1 + \ell$, and at the Johnson boundary itself.

### 4.5 Proposition 5 part 4, sharp form **[proved; checked]**

$\deg_X \hat G^\circ(X, P, P') \le 3k - 3$ (it is the derivative of $E(X, P)$, of degree $\le 3k - 2$), so every $P$ of degree $< k$ with at least $3n/8 - 1$ agreements on $D \setminus 0$ is a section. This is sharp and was a real test: on the domain of `35_flat_list.c` ($\mathrm{span}(1..z^4)$ in $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$, embedded in $\mathrm{GF}(2^{16})$) the flat word at $n = 32$ has 31 polynomials at agreement 15, 20 at agreement 11 and 627 at agreement 10; 18 of the 20 have their 11 agreements off 0 and are sections of the cubic, the 2 others and all 627 are not. On the leanVM shape in $\mathrm{GF}(2^{16})$ and on a random subspace the list down to agreement 11 is the 31 flats only, and at $n = 16$ the "3 at agreement 5" exist on the two polynomial-basis domains and not on a random subspace. So $|L_{G^\circ}|$ can exceed $n - 1$ below $3n/8$, and the statement "the list is exactly the $n - 1$ flats above $3n/8$" is confirmed on three domains.

## 7. The residual statement, for whoever tries to close it

At $n = 2^{25}$, rate 1/2, $a = 0.70$, certificate $(18, 5)$, an unconditional bound $|\mathrm{List}| \le 2750$ follows from: **(S) the explainer space $\mathbb{E}$ of every word has no fixed component $G$ that is an absolutely irreducible first integral with $1 \le b \le 5$, $3 \le \delta \le 25$, leading $Y_1$-coefficient in $F[X, Y_0^2] \setminus F[X]$, $(b + 2)\Delta \le W < 18A$, and a contact profile that is not cut**; or from a bound on the sections of such $G$ close to the word. What is known about "not cut" **[checked with exact integers, `41_counts.py J`, $m \le 600$]**:

- total contact $> 2n$ is necessary; for contact orders in $\{2, 4\}$, with contact 4 on $xn$ lines, a factor at the smallest weighted degree $(b + 2)\Delta$ is cut for $x < 0.139, 0.56, 0.985$ when $b = 1, 2, 3$, and always when $b = 4, 5$: so an uncut factor needs total contact at least $2.28\,n$, $3.12\,n$, $3.97\,n$ for $b = 1, 2, 3$ and contact order 6 somewhere for $b \ge 4$;
- with contact 4 on all lines a factor is cut as soon as $W \ge 2.688, 2.613, 2.521\,n$ for $b = 1, 2, 3$ (efficiencies $0.672, 0.653, 0.630$: for $b \ge 2$ the term $bJ(\beta)$ of (3) helps), and Section 4.3 gives $W \ge 2.6\,n$ for $b = 1$, $2.4\,n$ for $b = 2$ and closes $b = 3$ on a list member that is not a $Y_0$-root of $\hat G_b$: the open windows are $[2.600, 2.688)\,n$ and $[2.400, 2.613)\,n$.

Three warnings. First, (S) cannot be proved by showing that singular first integrals of small weighted degree do not exist: Proposition W produces, for every word, first integrals with contact $2m$ on all lines and efficiency below $a$, absolutely irreducible in every random instance I tried, including rate 1/2. A proof has to separate efficiency $e^*(a) = 0.672$ from efficiency $0.70$, or use that $G$ is a fixed component (it divides every explainer, so $\mathbb{E} = \hat G \cdot \mathbb{E}'$ and $\dim \mathbb{E}' \ge N - nR$), or count sections. Second, the statement is false at rate 1/4 (flat cubic, and Section 4.4 with the analysis degree), so a proof must use $\rho = 1/2$ through $W \ge (b + 2)\Delta$ or an analogue. Third, a referee of such a proof should test it on the Wronskians and on the squares $S^2$, on the plane $g_y$ of Section 4.2, and on the two cubics.
