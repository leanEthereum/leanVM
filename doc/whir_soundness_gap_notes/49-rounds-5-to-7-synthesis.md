# Rounds 5 to 7 synthesis: the list bound in the DEEP-fold framework (lead)

State after the night of 2026-11-20 to 21. The goal was a list-size bound below the Johnson radius, to close the gap between proof and attack for the DEEP-fold protocol (paper in `doc/deep_fold/`, committed on branch `deep-fold`). **The gap is not closed.** The proven threshold on leanVM's real domain is still the Johnson radius. What changed: the attack side of the new framework is mapped, the gap is reduced to one algebraic hypothesis by a refereed and (in progress) machine-checked reduction, a first refereed list bound below Johnson exists for random evaluation points, and a characteristic-2 version of the large-characteristic method gives a conditional bound on every evaluation set. Statuses below are: **refereed** (a second researcher re-derived it with independent code), **proved** (one author, unrefereed), **checked** (numerics only), **conjectured**.

## 3. The reduction to one hypothesis (refereed and machine-checked)

**Theorem R** (`37` Section 3, `39` Section 1, signed by the referee in `46`). On every $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace domain, in every window: if every list with more than $f$ members, at the levels $j < j_0$ of the descent (lists of the periodic subcodes $\{Q(L_U(X))\}$ against words that may be blank at some points), contains three members whose triple function $(c - c_0)/(c_1 - c_0)$ is constant or invariant under a nonzero translation (hypothesis PL$(f, j_0)$), then every list has at most $\binom{f+1}{3}^{j_0} 2^{N j_0 - j_0(j_0 - 1)/2}(B_{j_0} + 1)$ members. At $N = 25$, rate 1/2, $\tau = 0.64$: $2^{445}$ ($f = 7$) to $2^{908}$ ($f_0 = 134$ for ordinary words), which costs 23 to 38 DEEP points per level.

**Machine-checked** (`47-lean-period-descent.md`; standalone Lean 4 project `lean_descent/`, about 3900 lines, no `sorry`, built on the same Lean and Mathlib versions as the paper's development): Theorem R in the referee's form for any field of characteristic two and any subspace domain (`PeriodDescent.theoremR`), the base case, the algebraic core of one descent step, the three-wise Johnson bound of note 44, and the arithmetic at $N = 25$ (`theoremR_N25_f7`: at most $2^{467}$ codewords under PL$(7, 24)$). Formalizing gave a sharper constant, $\binom{f}{2}^{j_0}$ in place of $\binom{f+1}{3}^{j_0}$ ($2^{433}$ instead of $2^{467}$ for $f = 7$). Not formalized: the passage from multi-valued to ordinary words (note 39 Lemma 1.4), so the machine-checked hypothesis is on words with blanks at the levels of the descent.

**From triples to PL** (`44`, proved, unrefereed). A three-wise Johnson bound: if the period conjecture holds (in the weak form wP: a triple with excess above $\epsilon n$ over the generic-points count has a nonzero period), primitive lists are bounded for $\tau > \tau_3(\rho)$, the root of $\tau^2(3 - \tau) = 2\rho$:

So: **proven at Johnson; 11 to 19% fewer queries under one algebraic conjecture (wP on the domain and its quotients), verified exhaustively for $N \le 5$ on generic bases and proved in special cases; the attack frontier is another 35% beyond that.**

## 4. The characteristic-2 hidden-derivative theory (`35`, `41`, `40`, `43`, and `45` in progress)

The 2026 proofs of list bounds beyond Johnson in large characteristic are one argument in two steps: an interpolation step, which is characteristic-free and uses nothing about the evaluation points, and a solution-counting step, which needs characteristic above the degree. In characteristic 2 the second step fails exactly: writing $P = g(X^2) + Xh(X^2)$, a word is $n$ lines in 3-space, a codeword is a section, and list members can live on common components of the two explainer surfaces ("first integrals"). Those components are cut by a second interpolation unless they are fixed, absolutely irreducible, and economical.

- **Rate 1/2:** the residual is narrow (a quintic surface singular along at least about 15% of the word's lines, weighted degree in $[2.611, 2.688)n$ in the hard core; two more families with no tool). An explicit singular first integral of efficiency $11/16$ exists on subspace domains (checked), cut at the certified agreements. Closing the residual would give 204 to 210 queries unconditionally on every evaluation set. Not closed after two attempts (`40`, `45`); counting the sections is a Mordell-type problem. The second attempt proved a determinant bound $W \ge (8n + 41)/3$ for non-degenerate surfaces, which empties the main window at $\tau = 0.703$; what remains at rate 1/2 is a degenerate sub-case of the family with one hidden-derivative degree ($b = 1$) and the families with $b \ge 2$, untreated. Round 8 (`48`, `50`) tries certificates of small hidden-derivative degree, which would remove the $b \ge 2$ families.
- **Rate 1/4:** economical components exist (the flat cubic, carrying all $n - 1$ flats, and with the degree slack $2(n-1)$ affine flats on the Johnson boundary). A polynomial bound is false on dense domains (the Gold family, $2^{O(N^2)}$); the count is a minimum-weight count in a binary BCH-type code (`43`).
- **Rate 1/16:** a constant bound is false on $\mathbb{F}_4$-structured domains (new family); open on ours. **Rate 1/8:** open.
- Reach of the first-order method: at most 7 to 9% of the queries.

## 5. Dead ends established this night

- A dimension induction on agreement sets (random perturbation of the word destroys set invariance; `37`, refereed).
- Extending the unique-factorization proof of the period conjecture to roots outside the domain through divisors (explicit counterexample to the key step; `44`).
- Difference operators, subspace-polynomial towers and subfield points as substitutes for the derivative (no contact identity; `35`).
- The generic-points certificates with points in $K$ (fail by about $0.3n$ bits; `36`), Rudra-Wootters and Guruswami-Mosheiff at our sizes, coset unions with random representatives at levels 0 and 1, random subsets of a larger subspace.
- The affine-rank route (rank is polynomial in $N$ wherever measured, but the resulting bound is far lossier than the period route; `39`).
- A multi-parent strengthening of the random-points theorem as originally stated (its regularization lemma is false; `42`).

## 7. Rounds 8 and 9 (added 2026-11-21, early morning; reports 51, 52, 53 pending when this was written)

**Round 8, the rate-1/2 residual of the hidden-derivative theory.**

- `48-small-B-certificates.md` (proved by one author, not yet refereed): closed form of the local rank of the contact conditions for explainers of $Y_1$-degree at most $B$; no certificate with $B = 1$ exists at or below the Johnson radius, so the plan "only $b = 1$ remains by a $B = 1$ certificate" fails; certificates with $B = 2$ exist from $A/n \ge 0.704434$. The fibre lemma (Lemma F) and its corollary give lower bounds on the weighted degree $W$ of a component from its contact profile. On finite grids of contact profiles (not all profiles) the $b = 1$ class is reduced to degenerate quintic potentials with contact 4 on at least $0.925\,n$ lines, in a window of width at most $0.017\,n$.
- `50-residual-b-at-least-2.md`, **Theorem 1.3 (parity)** (proved by one author; the lead re-derived Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2 and the count by hand and found no error; referee report `52` pending). Explainers that are polynomials in $Y_1^q$, $q$ a power of 2, have only $m^2 - (q-1)m + q(3q-1)/2$ contact conditions per point in characteristic 2, and an absolutely irreducible first integral with $b \ge 1$ divides such a polynomial with multiplicity divisible by $q$. Consequence, on every evaluation set, for every word, with no hypothesis on contact profiles: for every $A \ge 0.700839\,n$ (rate 1/2, $n = 2^{25}$ to $2^{28}$), $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le d^2 + |L_{G^\star}|$ with $d \le 1509$ ($d = 24$ at $a = 0.7065$), where $G^\star = \partial E^\star$ is at most ONE absolutely irreducible first integral with $b = 1$; if it carries many members it has $\delta \le 6$ and $W < 3.094\,n$ at $a = 0.7065$, so $E^\star$ is a cubic or a quintic in $Y_0$. So the class $b \ge 2$ is gone, and the whole obstacle to an unconditional list bound below the Johnson radius at rate 1/2 is the number of list members on one $b = 1$ component.

**Lead's observation for the degenerate $b = 1$ component (to be verified by `53`).** Write $\varepsilon_P = E_Y(X, P) = E_1(X, P^2)$. Note 48's working form says a polynomial $P$ is a section iff $(P + \psi)\varepsilon_P$ is a square, for a fixed rational function $\psi$. Then: (1) the $F(\tau)$-lines $P_i + F(\tau)\varepsilon_i$ in the plane $F(X) = F(\tau)^2$ all pass through $\psi$; two sections determine $\psi = [(P_1\varepsilon_1)'\varepsilon_2 + (P_2\varepsilon_2)'\varepsilon_1]/(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)'$ and pairs are unconstrained, so all the structure is in triples, which satisfy $(P_1\varepsilon_1)'(\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3)' + (P_2\varepsilon_2)'(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_3)' + (P_3\varepsilon_3)'(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)' = 0$. (2) With $\psi = N/M$, $M = b_0m^2$: the logarithmic derivatives $\varepsilon_i'/\varepsilon_i$ of all sections are congruent to $N'/N$ modulo $m^2$, with the analogous statement at infinity, on a divisor $\mathfrak{M}$ of degree at least about $|S_i| - \Delta$, that is $0.19\,n$ to $0.2\,n$ in the window. (3) When $\varepsilon_i$ is $\Lambda_{S_i}$ up to squares, the symmetric differences of the agreement sets of the sections lie in the binary Goppa code $\{T \subseteq D : \mathrm{dlog}\,\Lambda_T \text{ vanishes on } \mathfrak{M}\}$, so the number of list members on the component is at most the size of a binary Goppa code of degree about $0.2\,n$ on $D$: a property of the domain, not of the word. On a generic subspace with the divisor at infinity that code is $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ ($2^{326}$ words at $N = 25$, an affordable budget) or $\mathrm{RM}(1, N)$; for the flat cubic at rate 1/4 the same computation gives $2n$ against the true $n - 1$, and on field domains it is the BCH code behind the Gold family of note 43, so no bound of this kind can hold on every evaluation set with a polynomial constant. Status: derived by the lead by hand, not checked numerically, not refereed; the small factor $r_P$ and divisors at finite places are not handled.

**Report `53` (arrived about 05:10 EST; one author, unrefereed): the pencil and Goppa structure is real and exact, but gives no unconditional bound.**

- *Structure (proved, checked with equality on planted examples, the flat cubic, Gold codewords and the $5n/8$ family).* A trichotomy for a $b = 1$ potential: (I) a fixed centre $\psi$ exists and the lines $P_i + F(\tau)\varepsilon_i$ are concurrent; (II) all lines are parallel (the dependent sub-case), and then the component carries at most one list member in the window, on every evaluation set; (III) no point lies on more than a bounded number of the lines. With the factors $r_P$ the support must be extended by a fixed finite set of points outside $D$. **Report `56` (arrived about 06:55 EST; one author, unrefereed): the exact list condition on the real field, in Witt vectors. No attack, no transfer theorem.** The lead's Witt-vector formulation is correct (and was already Proposition 3.3 of note 13): $e_j(A) = e_j(B)$ for all $j \le g$ iff $\sum_A[\alpha]^k \equiv \sum_B[\beta]^k \pmod{2^{1+v}}$ for all odd $k$ and $v \ge 0$ with $k2^v \le g$. The lead's worry that the higher 2-adic digits lose strata on a 64-bit field the way the linear part does is false: with all digits counted, sets of Boolean degree 2 to 6 face 555 to 29913 times more bits of conditions than they have bits of freedom at $N = 25$, so the deficit of the linear code (47938 against 326) says nothing about lists. New (Theorem 4 of note 56): on every subspace of every field of characteristic 2, a difference sharing $e_1..e_g$ with $g \ge n/8$ has Walsh level at least $2 + \lfloor\log_2((N-1)/3)\rfloor$ (all hyperplane counts agree modulo 16 at $N = 25$ to 28; the formal model gives modulo $n/4$). Open: Walsh levels 5 to $N - 3$, which is the same "a count is not a proof" obstruction as before, now with exact exponents (expected number of non-formal pairs below $2^{-359}$ under the random model). Explicit non-formal configurations on the real domain exist only at the top level and form families of constant size (352 verified pairs at $N = 25$; star families of 4 sets at $11n/16$). No family growing with $n$ or with the field deficiency was found. So the attack frontier of note 38 stands, and plain Reed-Solomon on the real domain remains unproved below Johnson.

**Report `61` (arrived about 07:15 EST; one author, unrefereed): the protocol and the cost of the window-mixed code, assuming its list bound.** Everything is conditional on Theorem M or the $t$-wise inequalities of note 57, which are not proved.

- *Obstacles note 57 missed.* (1) The affine fold $G + (\alpha + \beta X)F$ does not commute with the mixing (a code with one symbol a position that is closed under such a twist is a generalized Reed-Solomon code), so the fold must be the plain $G + \alpha F$; with a loss of $n/2^{13}$ columns a level and a 12-bit fold grind this costs no query. (2) The factor-2 reductions from lanes to one $K$-valued word need lists below $2^{63}$; with lists $q^{L-1}$ a new reduction (Proposition I$'$) roughly doubles the list exponent (61 points at $L = 15$, not 36). With the constant lists of the ladder this obstacle disappears and the points stay at 11, 8, 8. (3) The efficient extractor only decodes one error; knowledge soundness of the fold messages drops to $2^{-113}$ to $2^{-116}$ unless the fold grinds grow to about $\log_2 n - 2$ bits (3 to 9% of proving time). (4) Any spanning set of XOR offsets makes the verifier's weight for a query cost $\kappa(\kappa+1)/2 + R\kappa$ field products instead of $\kappa$ (324 against 24); in the recursive verifier that is more than the queries saved.
- *Modified theorem.* Protocol D$'$ (plain folds, no degree slack, one window-weighted claim a query) is round-by-round sound with the paper's error rows except the fold row; its only code hypothesis for soundness is the list bound for interleaved words; the fold lemma holds for every linear code. About 5,000 to 6,000 lines of the Lean development would be touched, mostly retyping.
