Notation. $G$ is the index group of the positions (order $n = 2^N$): $\mathbb{F}_2^N$ for XOR offsets, $\mathbb{Z}/2^N$ for integer (index) strides, a product for mixed windows. $C \ni 0$ is the offset set, $r = |C|$, the window of $x$ is $x + C$. For a set $J$ of positions: the closure $\bar J = J + C$, the boundary $\partial J = \bar J \setminus J$, the interior $\mathrm{int}\,F = \{x : x + C \subseteq F\}$. A block is a coset of the subgroup $\langle C \rangle$ generated by $C$ (a connected component of the window graph).

## 1. Local windows: what the rungs need

After the lead's erratum (note 57 Section 10) local windows protect nothing against free agreements: a union of blocks is its own interior, so its interior loss is 0. This section stays as the combinatorics of Cases A and B (random agreements), for any window shape; the free side is Sections 2 and 3.

### 1.1 One lemma for every window shape

**Kneser's theorem** (M. Kneser, Math. Z. 58 (1953) 459 to 484; Tao and Vu, *Additive Combinatorics*, Theorem 5.5): for nonempty finite subsets $A, B$ of an abelian group, with $H = \{g : A + B + g = A + B\}$ the stabilizer of the sumset, $|A + B| \ge |A + H| + |B + H| - |H|$.

**Lemma 1.1 [proved].** Let $J \ne \emptyset$, $H$ the stabilizer of $\bar J = J + C$, and $\nu_H(C)$ the number of cosets of $H$ that meet $C$. Put $\kappa(C) = \min\{(\nu_H(C) - 1)\,|H| : H \le G,\ C \not\subseteq H\}$.

1. (closed sets) $\bar J = J$ if and only if $J$ is a union of blocks.
2. (periodic type) If $C \subseteq H$ then $\bar J$ is a union of blocks and $J$ is $\bar J$ minus the $|\partial J|$ points of $\partial J$.
3. (generic type) If $C \not\subseteq H$ then $|\partial J| \ge (\nu_H(C) - 1)|H| \ge \kappa(C)$.

So every set of positions whose boundary is smaller than $\kappa(C)$ is a union of blocks minus $|\partial J|$ points, and conversely a union of blocks minus $b$ points $S$ has boundary exactly $b$ whenever every point of $S$ has a point of $S - C$ outside $S$ (always true if $b < r$).

*Proof.* (1) If $J + C = J$ then $J + c \subseteq J$ and $|J + c| = |J|$, so $J + c = J$ for every $c \in C$, and $J$ is invariant under $\langle C \rangle$. The converse is clear. (2) If $C \subseteq H$ then $J + C \subseteq J + H \subseteq J + C + H = J + C$, so $\bar J = J + H$ is a union of cosets of $H \supseteq \langle C \rangle$. (3) Kneser with $A = J$, $B = C$: $|\bar J| - |J| \ge (|J + H| - |J|) + |C + H| - |H| \ge (\nu_H(C) - 1)|H|$, and $\nu_H(C) \ge 2$ because $0 \in C \not\subseteq H$. For the converse statement: $s \in S$ lies in the closure iff $s - c \notin S$ for some $c \in C$, and $|s - C| = r > b$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.2 [proved; checked by exhaustion on groups of order 16, `60_local.py`, and on the cubes up to $Q_5$, `60_small.c`].** $\kappa(C) = r - 1$ for each of the following shapes.

- *Block cube:* $G = \mathbb{F}_2^N$, $C = \{0, b_0, \dots, b_{t'-1}\}$ ($r = t' + 1$, blocks of $2^{t'}$ points).
- *Block cube plus XOR long offsets* $b_{s_1}, \dots, b_{s_j}$: the same statement with $t' + j$ directions (blocks of $2^{t'+j}$ points, not contiguous).
- *Consecutive indices:* $G = \mathbb{Z}/2^N$, $C = \{0, 1, \dots, r - 1\}$, $r \le n/2$ (one block: the window graph is connected).
- *Index strides:* $G = \mathbb{Z}/2^N$, $C = \{0, 2^{s_1}, \dots, 2^{s_d}\}$, $s_1 < \dots < s_d$ ($r = d + 1$; blocks are the residue classes modulo $2^{s_1}$, one block if $s_1 = 0$).
- *Block cube plus an index stride:* $G = \mathbb{F}_2^{t'} \times \mathbb{Z}/2^{N - t'}$, $C = \{0, b_0, \dots, b_{t'-1}, g\}$, $g$ the stride "next block" with carry ($r = t' + 2$, connected).

*Proof.* $H = 0$ gives $r - 1$, so $\kappa \le r - 1$. Cube with $d$ directions: for $H \not\supseteq C$ let $j = \dim(H \cap \langle C\rangle) < d$; the images of the directions span $\langle C \rangle/(H \cap \langle C \rangle) \cong \mathbb{F}_2^{d-j}$, so $\nu_H \ge d - j + 1$ and $(\nu_H - 1)|H| \ge (d - j)2^j \ge (d - j)(1 + j) \ge d$. Consecutive indices: the subgroups are $H_j = 2^{N-j}\mathbb{Z}$ of order $2^j$, $\nu_{H_j} = \min(r, 2^{N-j})$, and $(\nu - 1)2^j \ge r - 1$ in the first case, $= n - 2^j \ge n/2$ in the second. Index strides: $\nu_{H_j} - 1 = d_j := \#\{i : s_i < N - j\}$, at most $j$ strides are lost so $d_j \ge d - j$, and $d_j 2^j \ge \max(d - j, 1)2^j \ge d$. Mixed (outline only; the statement is checked on $\mathbb{F}_2^2 \times \mathbb{Z}/4$ and $\mathbb{F}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}/8$): a subgroup $H$ meets $C$ in a subset; the classes of the remaining offsets are distinct modulo $H$ unless two of them differ by an element of $H$, which puts an element of order 2 in $H$ and doubles $|H|$ for each identification; the same computation gives $\ge t' + 1$. $\square$

### 1.3 What a rung needs from the window, and what each shape gives

Rung $t$ of note 57 Section 8 is the first moment for tuples of rank $u = t - 1$. In the transversal case (Case A of Section 9) the lead's count for pencils is "loss minus cost $\le \sum_a (|J_a| - |\bar J_a| + 1)$" over the clusters $J_a$ of deficient positions. The same count for rank $u$ **[derived here under the same transversality assumption as Case A; the rungs themselves are not proved here]**: a cluster on whose closure the vectors $(d_1(y), \dots, d_u(y))$ lie in one subspace of codimension $j$ loses $j|J_a|$ conditions and costs $j|\bar J_a|$ linear conditions minus the $j(u - j)$ parameters of the subspace, so

$$\text{net gain of a cluster} \ =\ j\,\bigl(u - j - |\partial J_a|\bigr) \ \text{field elements}, \qquad 1 \le j \le u - 1 .$$

For pencils ($u = 2$, $j = 1$) this is the lead's $1 - |\partial J_a|$. So the combinatorial input of rung $t$ is exactly: **which clusters have $|\partial J| \le u - 2 = t - 3$, and how many of them fit in the window graph.** By Lemma 1.1 and Proposition 1.2 the answer is the same for every shape: if $r \ge u$ (that is $\kappa = r - 1 \ge u - 1$), the only clusters with positive net gain are blocks minus at most $u - 2$ points.

**Corollary 1.4 [proved, given the count above].** Let the window have $r \ge u = t - 1$ points and be of one of the shapes of Proposition 1.2.

1. A block carries at most one cluster of positive net gain (two clusters with disjoint closures in one block are both generic), and its gain is at most $\max_j j(u - j) = \lfloor u^2/4 \rfloor$, attained by the whole block. So the total excess is at most $E_t \cdot (\text{number of blocks})$ field elements, with $E_3 = 1$, $E_4 = 2$, $E_5 = 4$, in general $E_t = \lfloor (t-1)^2/4 \rfloor$.
2. Block cube of dimension $t'$: the excess is $E_t\, n / 2^{t'}$ field elements, that is the term $2^{-t'}$ in the threshold inequality of note 57 Section 9 becomes $E_t\,2^{-t'}$. It needs $t' \ge t - 2$.
3. Connected windows (consecutive indices, index strides with a stride 1, block cube plus the stride "next block"): there is one block, a cluster whose closure is everything is impossible in Case A ($|Y| \le 2m + 1 < n$), so **the excess is zero** as soon as $r \ge t - 1$.
4. Zero-gain clusters (which cost entropy only): blocks minus exactly $u - j$ points, at most $\binom{2^{t'}}{u - j} \le 2^{(u-j)t'}$ per block and level $j$; and, when $r = u$, the generic clusters of minimal boundary $r - 1$ at level $j = 1$. The latter are few for cube and spread-stride windows (for the block cube: single positions and blocks minus a window; `60_local.py` finds 32 such sets in $\mathbb{Z}/16$ for $C = \{0,1,4\}$ against 208 intervals for $C = \{0,1,2\}$), and numerous for consecutive indices (every interval of any length has boundary exactly $r - 1$). Taking $r \ge u + 1$ removes them.

**The number of clusters needed to cover more than $2m$ points (Case B) [proved].** Closures of distinct clusters are disjoint (clusters whose closures meet carry the same value and are merged), and $r \le |\bar J| \le$ block size, with $|\bar J| = r$ exactly for single positions and $|\bar J| \ge |J| + h(|J|)$ in general (Table 1). So the number $p_0$ of clusters needed to cover more than $2m + 1$ points satisfies $\lceil (2m+2)/2^{t'} \rceil \le p_0 \le \lceil (2m+2)/(t'+1) \rceil$ for the block cube and $1 \le p_0 \le \lceil (2m+2)/r \rceil$ for connected windows. Case B of Section 9 needs only $p_0 \le m + O(1)$, that is $r \ge 2$; a rank-$u$ analogue that fixes a subspace per cluster needs $(u-1)p_0 \le (u-1)m$, again $r \ge 3$ suffices. No further combinatorics enters Case B as the lead wrote it.

**The within-block separator inequality.**

**Proposition 1.5 [proved for the cube shapes; checked exhaustively for all shapes of Proposition 1.2 on groups of order 16, and on $Q_5$].** If a set $F$ of positions is not a union of blocks, then at least $r$ positions of some block lie in $(F \setminus \mathrm{int}\,F) \cup \partial F$; this is attained by a single position. Hence, for any partition of the positions into fibres (and zeros of $g$): every block that is not contained in one fibre contains at least $r$ positions whose window is not contained in one fibre, and
$$\#\{\text{deficient windows}\} \ \le\ n - r \cdot \#\{\text{blocks not contained in one fibre}\} .$$
*Proof for a cube block $Q_{t'}$.* Let $A = F \cap$ block, a proper nonempty subset. If $N[A] \ne$ block then $|\partial A| \ge t'$ ($Q_{t'}$ is $t'$-connected) and at least one point of $A$ has a neighbour outside. Symmetrically if $N[A^c] \ne$ block. If both closures are the whole block, every point of the block has a neighbour of the other side: $2^{t'} \ge t' + 1$ points. $\square$

Nothing stronger holds for local windows: a fibre may consist of whole blocks, then all its windows are deficient, and an inequality of the form "$m + |B'| \ge (1/2 + \sigma)n$" is false for every local window (take $\xi$ block-periodic: $B' = \emptyset$, $m$ any multiple of the block size). The size cap enters only when $m <$ block size: then every block is split and the number of deficient windows is at most $n \cdot \max_a a/|N[I_a]|$ over clusters with closure smaller than the block, that is at most $0.571n$, $0.733n$, $0.839n$, $0.905n$, $0.945n$, $0.969n$, $0.982n$, $0.990n$ for $t' = 3, \dots, 10$ **[checked, `60_harper.py blocks`]**.

## 2. Index-stride windows

Here $G = \mathbb{Z}/2^N$, positions are indexed by integers through their binary digits, and $C = \{0, 2^{s_1}, \dots, 2^{s_d}\}$ with integer addition: the partner $x \boxplus 2^s$ is $x \oplus \delta$ with $\delta = 2^s(2^{l+1} - 1)$, $l$ the number of consecutive one bits of $x$ starting at bit $s$ (the carry run; $l = h$ with probability $2^{-(h+1)}$, and a carry out of bit $N - 1$ is dropped). The mean cost of a partner for the verifier, in the scale of its offset, is about $s + 2$ (worst case $N$).

**What Section 1 gives [proved].** $\kappa(C) = d$: every cluster other than "everything minus a few points" has boundary at least $d$; no proper closed set if $s_1 = 0$; minimal clusters are few (only single positions and their complements when the strides are spread). So the rank-one count of Step 5 and the Case A count go through with $d$ in place of $N$.

**Subspace-periodic sets.** Let $U$ be a coordinate subspace, $E$ the set of excluded coordinates ($U = \{x : x_e = 0,\ e \in E\}$, codimension $c = |E|$). A stride leaves the coset of $x$ exactly when its carry run reaches an excluded coordinate.

**Proposition 2.1 [proved; checked by enumeration at $N = 12$ for 40 random pairs (strides, $E$), `60_strides.py`].** For each stride $s$ let $e(s)$ be the least excluded coordinate $\ge s$ (none if there is none above $s$), and for each excluded $e$ that occurs let $s(e)$ be the largest stride with $e(s) = e$. The fraction of positions whose whole window lies in their own coset of $U$ is the same in every coset and equals
$$\mathrm{stay}(C, E) = \prod_{e}\bigl(1 - 2^{-(e - s(e))}\bigr).$$
*Proof.* Stride $s$ leaves iff bits $s, \dots, e(s) - 1$ of $x$ are all one (if $e(s) = s$: always). For the strides sharing the same $e$ these events are nested and the largest stride gives the largest event; for different $e$ they depend on disjoint sets of bits, none of them in $E$. $\square$

So the lead's product $\prod_j (1 - 2^{-(h_j + 1)})$ is right when every run is closed by its own excluded coordinate, that is for codimension $c = d$ ($(7/8)^8 = 0.34$ for 8 strides at gaps of 3). **It is not the worst case.** Runs that touch merge (a carry leaving one run enters the next and stays in $U$), and with fewer excluded coordinates much more survives: one excluded coordinate just below a stride, or at the top, gives $1 - 2^{-(g - 1)}$ resp. $1 - 2^{-g}$ for strides at gaps $g$. Writing $I^*(c)$ for the largest value of stay over coordinate subspaces of codimension $c$ **[checked, exhaustive over $E$, `60_strides.py`]**:

A subspace containing $e_j + e_{j+1}$ instead of excluding both gains at most a factor 2 in one escape probability **[proved for one stride, conjectured in general]**.

**The vertex-isoperimetric constant of the circulant [proved upper bounds, no lower bound].** The set $\{x : x \bmod 2^{s'+1} < 2^{s'}\}$ (bit $s'$ equal to 0) has density 1/2 and is invariant under every stride above $s'$; if $s$ is the largest stride at or below $s'$ its interior is $\{x \bmod 2^{s'+1} < 2^{s'} - 2^{s}\}$, so it loses the fraction $2^{-(s' - s)}$ of its points. With gaps $g$ this gives sets of density 1/2 with $\beta \le 2^{1-g}$ (last column; such sets are unions of cosets of a coordinate subspace of codimension 1). More generally intervals of block-periodic type of density $\varphi$ lose $2^{1-g}/(2\varphi)$ at most. So a stride window can only match the cube's $\beta(1/2) = 0.31$ with gaps at most 2 ($d \ge N/2$, cost about half the cube's), and with all $N$ strides the Hamming balls still lose only one layer ($x \boxplus 2^s$ changes the weight by $1 - l \le 1$), so the constant is at most the cube's and I see no reason for it to be equal. **No lower bound of Harper type is known to me for any stride set**; spectral bounds are void below about $1.7\log n$ generators (for $d$ random strides the largest nontrivial eigenvalue of the window operator is within a constant factor of $d$ unless $d \gtrsim 30$ at $n = 2^{25}$ [heuristic, large deviations of $d$ unit vectors]).

**Can the partial protection be turned into a theorem? [assessment]** What is proved is only the transfer: a list of plain Reed-Solomon on the quotient $D/U$ at agreement $\alpha'$ lifts to members whose free agreement with the lifted word is $|\mathrm{int}\,F|$ for $F$ the union of the agreeing cosets, and $\mathrm{stay}\cdot|F| \le |\mathrm{int}\,F| \le |F|$. The lower bound is the generic case; the upper bound is approached by agreement sets that are themselves "fat" for the induced strides on the quotient (for instance aligned intervals of cosets, that is cosets of a larger coordinate subspace: the flat families of note 00 contain such members). So a statement of the form "lifts are pushed above the Johnson radius of the quotient" ($\alpha' \ge a/I^*$) is true for generic agreement sets and false in general, and the structure repeats on the quotient with fewer strides (the strides above the cut act on the quotient, the ones below act as an occasional $+1$). A theorem would need the list problem of plain Reed-Solomon on every quotient at agreement $a/\mathrm{stay}$ with agreement sets constrained to have small interior loss, which is not easier than the original problem. My conclusion: stride windows with $d < N/2$ give no provable protection beyond the Kneser constant; with gaps 2 they give interior losses comparable to the cube on the sets I can test, at about half the verifier cost, but with no isoperimetric theorem behind them.

## 3. The full cube

From here $G = \mathbb{F}_2^N$, $C = \{0, b_0, \dots, b_{N-1}\}$, the window graph is the cube $Q_N$, $N[T] = T + C$ is the closed neighbourhood, $\partial T = N[T] \setminus T$, $q = 2^{64}$.

### 3.1 Sets by boundary, and the rank-one term of Step 5

**Lemma 3.1 [proved; checked exhaustively for $N \le 5$, `60_small.c`].** (a) Every nonempty $T$ with $N[T] \ne V$ has $|\partial T| \ge N$. (b) For $N \ge 4$, every $T$ with $|T| \ge 2$ and $|N[T]| \le n/2$ has $|\partial T| \ge 2N - 2$. (c) For every set $S$ of $b$ vertices there are at most $2^b - 1$ nonempty $T$ with $\partial T = S$; so the number of $T$ with $|\partial T| = b$ is at most $\binom nb(2^b - 1)$.

*Proof.* (a) $\partial T$ separates $T$ from $V \setminus N[T]$ and $Q_N$ is $N$-connected (also Lemma 1.1 with $\kappa = N$). (b) By Harper (Theorem 3.2) it is enough to look at the initial segments. For $2 \le a \le N + 1$ the segment is $\{0, e_1, \dots, e_{a-1}\}$ with boundary $a(N - a + 1) + \binom{a-1}{2} \ge 2N - 2$. For $a = |B(r)| + j$ with $r \ge 1$, $0 \le j \le \binom N{r+1}$: $|\partial I_a| = \binom N{r+1} - j + |\partial^+ J| \ge \binom N{r+1} + j\bigl(\tfrac{N-r-1}{r+2} - 1\bigr)$ (local LYM), which is linear in $j$ with end values $\binom N{r+1}$ and $\binom N{r+2}$; $|N[I_a]| \le n/2$ forces $r + 2 \le (N+1)/2$, so both are at least $\binom N2 \ge 2N - 2$. (c) $T$ is a union of connected components of $Q_N - S$. If there are $c \ge 2$ components, each has a boundary of at least $N$ points inside $S$ by (a), and a point of $S$ is adjacent to at most $N$ components, so $cN \le bN$. $\square$

**Proposition 3.1b [proved].** For $4 \le N \le 28$ and $q = 2^{64}$:
$$n\,q^{-N} \ \le \sum_{\emptyset \ne T,\ |N[T]| \le n/2} q^{-|\partial T|} \ \le\ n\,q^{-N}\,(1 + 2^{-363}).$$
The sets with $|\partial T| = N$ are exactly the $n$ single vertices; everything else has $|\partial T| \ge 2N - 2$ and contributes at most $\sum_{b \ge 2N-2} (2n/q)^b/b!$ (`60_boundary_sum.py`: relative size $2^{-376}$ at $N = 10$, $2^{-452}$ at 25, $2^{-363}$ at 28; the argument works while $2N^2 - 65N + 126 < 0$, that is $N \le 30$). So the rank-one term of Step 5 is $q^{L-1} \cdot n q^{-N}(1 + 2^{-363}) = 2^{N + 64(L - 1 - N)}$: at most $2^{-128}$ for $L \le N - 2$ (it is $2^{N - 64}$ at $L = N$). With $u$ independent direction classes the term is at most $(n q^{L - N}(1 + 2^{-363}))^u$.

**What is wrong in note 57 here.** The count "$\binom nb 2^{bN}$ sets with boundary $b$" is true but far too weak for the conclusion drawn from it: it gives $\sum_b \binom nb 2^{bN} q^{-b} \approx 2^{N(2N - 64)}/N!$, that is $2^{-434}$ at $N = 25$ and $2^{-322}$ at $N = 28$ instead of $2^{-1575}$ and $2^{-1764}$, and would only allow $L \le 5$ (resp. $L \le 4$). The conclusion ($n q^{L-1-N}$) is nevertheless correct, by Lemma 3.1.

### 3.2 Harper's theorem and the interior function

**Theorem 3.2 (Harper 1966).** Order the vertices of $Q_N$ (subsets of $[N]$) by the simplicial order: $x < y$ if $|x| < |y|$, or $|x| = |y|$ and $\min(x \triangle y) \in x$. Let $I_a$ be the set of the first $a$ vertices. Then every $A \subseteq V$ has $|N[A]| \ge |N[I_{|A|}]|$, and $N[I_a]$ is again an initial segment. (L. H. Harper, *Optimal numberings and isoperimetric problems on graphs*, J. Combin. Theory 1 (1966) 385 to 393; proofs also in Katona, *The Hamming-sphere has minimum boundary*, Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 10 (1975) 131 to 140, Frankl and Füredi, Discrete Math. 34 (1981) 311 to 313, and Bollobás, *Combinatorics*, Section 16.)

**Formula [proved from Theorem 3.2 and Kruskal-Katona; checked against exhaustive search for $N \le 5$].** Write $B(r) = \sum_{i \le r}\binom Ni$. For $a = B(r) + j$, $0 \le j \le \binom N{r+1}$:
$$\mathrm{NI}_N(a) := \min_{|A| = a}|N[A]| = B(r+1) + \mathrm{KK}_{N-r-1}(j),$$
where $\mathrm{KK}_s(j) = \sum_i \binom{a_i}{i-1}$ for the cascade $j = \binom{a_s}{s} + \binom{a_{s-1}}{s-1} + \dots$ ($a_s > a_{s-1} > \dots$) is the minimal shadow of $j$ sets of size $s$. The **interior function** is $\iota_N(f) = \max\{|\mathrm{int}\,F| : |F| = f\} = \max\{a : \mathrm{NI}_N(a) \le f\}$, and the **interior loss** is
$$\beta(N, f) = 1 - \iota_N(f)/f ,$$
the least fraction of a set of $f$ points that lies in its inner vertex boundary. At ball sizes $\beta(N, B(r)) = \binom Nr / B(r)$ exactly.

**Lemma 3.3 [proved].** For $B(r) \le a \le B(r+1)$: $a/\mathrm{NI}_N(a) \le \max\bigl(B(r)/B(r+1),\ B(r+1)/B(r+2)\bigr) = B(r+1)/B(r+2)$. So over sets whose interior has at most $B(r)$ points the interior ratio $\iota(f)/f$ is at most $B(r)/B(r+1)$, and $\beta(N, f) \ge \binom N{r+1}/B(r+1)$ whenever $\iota_N(f) \le B(r)$.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{KK}_{N-r-1}(j) \ge j\,\lambda$ with $\lambda = \binom N{r+2}/\binom N{r+1}$ (local LYM), so $a/\mathrm{NI}(a) \le (B(r) + j)/(B(r+1) + \lambda j)$, a ratio of affine functions of $j$, monotone, with end values $B(r)/B(r+1)$ and $B(r+1)/B(r+2)$; and $B(r)/B(r+1)$ increases with $r$ by log-concavity of the binomial coefficients. $\square$

The scripts do not rely on Lemma 3.3: `60_beta.py` bounds $\max_{f \le c}\iota(f)/f$ rigorously by an adaptive subdivision with exact values of $\mathrm{NI}$ (on $[a_1, a_2]$ the ratio is at most $a_2/\mathrm{NI}(a_1)$), to seven digits; lower and upper bounds coincide in every entry below.

**Gaussian form [heuristic, for orientation only].** With $z = \Phi^{-1}(\varphi)$: $\beta(N, \varphi n) \approx \frac{2}{\sqrt N}\cdot\frac{\phi(z - 1/\sqrt N)}{\varphi}$ (one layer of width $2/\sqrt N$ in standard units, centred half a step inside). The loss decreases like $N^{-1/2}$: every positive statement below has an $N$ beyond which it fails.

### 3.5 The Isoperimetric Lemma of note 57: false as stated, the correct form, what is proved

**The statement of note 57 is false [proved].** "If $B'$ separates the rest into parts of at most $m$ points each then $m + |B'| \ge (1/2 + \sigma)2^N$": take $B'$ = the odd vertices. The parts are single even vertices, $m = 1$, $m + |B'| = n/2 + 1$. So $\sigma \le 2^{-N}$, and for $N \le 5$ the minimum is exactly $n/2 + 1$ (3.4). For large $N$ it is even below $n/2$ (remove every $s$-th layer: about $n/s + 0.8sn/\sqrt N$; this needs $N$ of several hundred).

**What Step 6(iii) actually has.** The parts are not arbitrary: they are the interiors of the fibres $F_i$ of $\xi$, which are disjoint sets of at most $m$ points, so each part $P_i = \mathrm{int}\,F_i$ satisfies $|N[P_i]| \le m$ (not only $|P_i| \le m$), and the closed neighbourhoods $N[P_i]$ are pairwise disjoint. The parity example dies: a single vertex needs a fibre of $N + 1$ points. Define
$$\Xi(N, m) = \max\Bigl\{\sum_i |\mathrm{int}\,F_i| : F_i \subseteq V \text{ pairwise disjoint},\ |F_i| \le m\Bigr\},$$
the largest number of windows on which a map with fibres of at most $m$ points can be constant ($= n - \min|B'|$).

**What Step 6(iii) needs, and its dependence on the rate.** Recounting the non-transversal term (zero set of $g$ empty): at most $q^{k-m}$ choices of $g$, at most $q^{p-3}$ pencils for a pattern with $p$ clusters (the values $t_i$ are free; note 57 counts one, see Section 4), probability $q^{-2(k-L)}$, and $q^{I}$ families, $I$ the number of deficient windows. The exponent is $I + p - k - m + 2L - 3$, so the claim needed is $\Xi(N, m) \le k + m - (\text{entropy and } p)$ for every $m < k$, that is
$$m + |B'| \ \ge\ (1 - \rho + \sigma)\,n \qquad\text{for all } m < \rho n,$$
which is the lead's $(1/2 + \sigma)n$ only at rate 1/2. At rate $\rho$ the margin is $\sigma_\rho(N) := \rho - \max_{m < \rho n}(\Xi(N, m) - m)/n$.

**Theorem 3.5 (fractional separator inequality) [proved].** For disjoint $F_1, \dots, F_p$ with $|F_i| \le m_i$: $\sum_i |\mathrm{int}\,F_i| \le \sum_i \iota_N(|F_i|)$. In particular, with all $m_i = m$: $\Xi(N, m) \le n\,(1 - \beta_{\min}(N, m))$, and
$$\max_{m \le \rho n - 1}\frac{\Xi(N, m) - m}{n} \ \le\ \Gamma_N(\rho) := \max_{1 \le f \le \rho n - 1}\Bigl(\frac{\iota_N(f)}{f} - \frac fn\Bigr).$$
*Proof.* $N[\mathrm{int}\,F_i] \subseteq F_i$, so $|\mathrm{int}\,F_i| \le \iota_N(|F_i|)$ by Harper; $\sum |F_i| \le n$ and $\iota(f) \le f \max_{f' \le m}\iota(f')/f'$. $\square$

This is also the weighted version (a) (parts with different caps: maximize $\sum \iota_N(f_i)$ over $f_i \le m_i$, $\sum f_i \le n$) and version (c) (as a function of $\varphi = m/n$: $\Xi/n \le 1 - \beta_{\min}(N, \varphi)$, Table 2). With one more field element per cluster ($\sum_i(|\mathrm{int}\,F_i| + 1)$) the same proof gives $\max_f (\iota(f) + 1)/f$, which changes nothing beyond $1/(N+1)$ for $m \le 2N$ and less than $10^{-5}$ in $\Gamma$.

### 3.6 The three-set statement (first rung, note 57 Section 10)

The free agreements of a triple are the interiors of the zero sets $Z_{12}, Z_{13}, Z_{23}$ of the three differences, each of fewer than $k$ points; any two of them meet exactly in the common zero set $B$ of $d_1, d_2$. A position in $\mathrm{int}\,B$ has $c_x = 3$, a position in exactly one interior has $c_x = 2$, so the free sum is
$$\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ \ =\ \sum_{i}|\mathrm{int}\,Z_i| - |\mathrm{int}\,B| \ =\ \Bigl|\bigcup_i \mathrm{int}\,Z_i\Bigr| + |\mathrm{int}\,B| .$$

### 3.7 Free agreements of $t$ members

For $t$ distinct polynomials let $Z_{ij}$ be the zero set of $P_i - P_j$ ($|Z_{ij}| \le k - 1$), and at a position $x$ let $c^\circ_x$ be the size of the largest set of members that coincide pairwise on the whole window of $x$ (the largest class that can agree freely with a word at $x$).

**Proposition 3.7 [proved].** $\sum_x (c^\circ_x - 1) \le n\,\Phi\bigl((1 - \beta_{\min}(N, \rho))\,\rho\,\binom t2\bigr)$, where $\Phi$ is the piecewise linear concave function with $\Phi(\binom s2) = s - 1$ for $s = 1, 2, \dots$.

*Proof.* Let $P^\circ(x)$ be the number of pairs $\{i, j\}$ with $x \in \mathrm{int}\,Z_{ij}$. Then $\binom{c^\circ_x}{2} \le P^\circ(x)$, so $c^\circ_x - 1 \le \Phi(P^\circ(x))$, and $\sum_x P^\circ(x) = \sum_{i<j}|\mathrm{int}\,Z_{ij}| \le \binom t2 (1 - \beta_{\min})(k - 1)$ by Harper. Jensen for the concave $\Phi$. $\square$

## 4. What is wrong or missing in the combinatorics of note 57

1. **Step 5, the count of sets by boundary.** "$\binom nb 2^{bN}$" is true and useless for the stated conclusion (it gives $2^{-434}$ at $N = 25$, allowing only $L \le 5$). The conclusion $n q^{L-1-N}$ is correct by Lemma 3.1: the only sets with boundary $N$ are the $n$ single vertices, the next boundary is $2N - 2$, and a boundary set $S$ has at most $2^{|S|} - 1$ sets behind it.
2. **Step 6, the Isoperimetric Lemma is false as stated** (odd vertices: $m + |B'| = n/2 + 1$). The correct hypothesis is that the parts are interiors of disjoint sets of at most $m$ points. In that form it is **proved at rate 1/2** ($\sigma \ge 0.145, 0.137, 0.131, 0.121$ at $N = 25$ to 28, Table 3).
3. **Step 6 is rate dependent.** The inequality needed is $m + |B'| \ge (1 - \rho + \sigma)n$ for $m < \rho n$, not $(1/2 + \sigma)n$. It is proved by Harper alone at rate 1/4 only for $N \le 15$ (with $\sigma \ge 0.031$ only for $N \le 13$), is probably true at rate 1/4 for $N = 25$ to 28 (best configuration found leaves 0.10) but needs a multi-part isoperimetric inequality that I do not have, and is **false at rates 1/8 and 1/16 for $N \ge 20$** and at every rate below $1/32$ (explicit configurations, 3.5). So Step 6(iii) cannot be closed by counting windows at the later levels: there the non-transversal patterns have to be excluded algebraically.
4. **Step 6(i), "at most one primitive $\xi$".** True for prescribed values $t_i$ on the clusters; the values are free, so a pattern with $p$ clusters has up to $q^{p-3}$ pencils and the exponent gains $p$ (harmless: $p \le n/(N+1)$, and it is absorbed by counting $|\mathrm{int}\,F_i| + 1$ per fibre in Theorem 3.5).
5. **Zeros of $g$ (the set $B$).** With a common factor the three zero sets of a triple share $B$, the free sum is $\sum|\mathrm{int}\,Z_i| - |\mathrm{int}\,B|$, and "trivial with room at rate 1/2" (Section 10, item 2) holds only for $B = \emptyset$. For $B \ne \emptyset$ Harper gives $3\iota(k-1)$, which exceeds $n$ from $N = 23$ on (3.6). Open, probably true (best configuration $0.585n$ at $N = 25$).
6. **Section 10, item 3, "enough for $t \le 5$ at rate 1/2".** Not confirmed: with $\beta_{\min}(25, 1/2) = 0.310 < 1/3$ the pairwise-plus-Harper bound on the free sum is below $(t-1)k$ for no $t$ at rate 1/2 when $N \ge 23$ (3.7). As thresholds it gives constant lists of free type above 0.659, 0.629, 0.629, 0.620, 0.616, 0.614 for $t = 3, \dots, 8$ and never below 0.587.
7. **Section 10, item 4, the product formula for strides** is the value at codimension equal to the number of strides. Lower codimensions keep more (0.875, 0.75, 0.56 for 8 strides at codimension 1, 2, 3), because touching runs merge (Proposition 2.1).
8. **Section 9** (retracted by Section 10 for another reason) is correct as combinatorics: "a cluster costs nothing unless it equals its closure" is Lemma 1.1, for every window shape, and the excess for rank $u$ is $\lfloor u^2/4 \rfloor$ field elements per block, not one.

Correct as stated: "every nonempty $T$ with $|T + C| < n/2$ has $|\partial T| \ge N$" (simpler by connectivity than by Harper); Section 4 ("a hyperplane coset has empty interior", "$\{x_1x_2 = 0\}$ of density 3/4 has interior density 1/4"); Section 7 ($c + 1$ values on every window for group-sum subspaces).

## 5. Summary

- **Sum over sets by boundary [proved]:** $\sum_{T \ne \emptyset,\ |N[T]| \le n/2} q^{-|\partial T|} = n q^{-N}(1 + \epsilon)$, $0 \le \epsilon \le 2^{-363}$, for $4 \le N \le 28$, $q = 2^{64}$. Number of sets with boundary $b$: 0 for $b < N$, exactly $n$ for $b = N$, 0 for $N < b < 2N - 2$, at most $\binom nb(2^b - 1)$ in general.
- **Interior loss [exact]:** $\beta(N, f) = 1 - \iota_N(f)/f$ from Harper and Kruskal-Katona, Table 2. $\beta_{\min}(N, 1/2) = 0.3100, 0.3100, 0.2989, 0.2989$ and $\beta_{\min}(N, 1/4) = 0.4494, 0.4413, 0.4331, 0.4248$ at $N = 25, 26, 27, 28$.
- **Separator inequality [proved at rate 1/2]:** for disjoint sets of at most $m < n/2$ points, $n - \sum|\mathrm{int}\,F_i| + m \ge (1/2 + \sigma)n$ with $\sigma(25..28) \ge 0.1453, 0.1365, 0.1312, 0.1212$; true value at most 0.310, 0.310, 0.299, 0.299. Table 3 for $N = 10$ to 28. Rate dependent form: proved at rate 1/4 only for $N \le 15$, false at rates $\le 1/8$ for $N \ge 20$.
- **Three sets [proved at rates $\le 1/4$ for all $N \le 28$; at rate 1/2 for coprime differences; open at rate 1/2 with a common factor for $N \ge 23$]:** Table 4.
- **$t$ members [proved]:** free sum $\le n\Phi((1 - \beta)\rho\binom t2)$; below $(t-1)k$ iff $\beta$ exceeds the thresholds of 3.7: at $N = 25$ to 28 for no $t$ at rate 1/2, $t = 3$ at 1/4, $t \le 4$ at 1/8 and 1/16.
- **Local and stride windows [proved]:** for every shape $\kappa(C) = r - 1$ (Kneser): clusters with boundary below $r - 1$ are unions of blocks minus that many points. Per-block excess of rung $t$: $\lfloor (t-1)^2/4 \rfloor$ field elements; zero for connected windows with $r \ge t - 1$. Within a block: a block not inside one fibre has at least $r$ non-deficient positions, and nothing more.
- **Cheapest window that I can justify:** for any statement that needs interior losses (free agreements, Step 6) it is the full cube, $r = N + 1$. In $\mathbb{F}_2^N$ fewer than $N$ directions leave closed blocks; index strides have $\kappa = d$ and no closed set but no isoperimetric theorem, sets of density 1/2 with loss $2^{1-g}$ for gaps $g$, and coordinate-periodic sets keeping $1 - 2^{-(g-1)}$ of their windows. Matching the cube's loss on the sets I can test needs gaps at most 2, about half the cube's verifier cost.
- **From which $N$ things fail:** every positive statement decays like $N^{-1/2}$. Separator at rate 1/2 by Harper: $\Gamma_N(1/2) = 0.392, 0.448, 0.465, 0.472, 0.494, 0.507$ at $N = 30, 40, 44, 46, 52, 56$, so $\sigma > 0.031$ until $N = 44$ and $\sigma > 0$ until about $N = 53$ **[checked]**. Three sets: until $N = 48$, 74, 104 at rates 1/4, 1/8, 1/16. Free $t$-sum at rate 1/2: $t \le 4$ only for $N \le 22$. Smaller $N$ helps everywhere; lower rates help the three-set statement and hurt the separator inequality of Step 6.
