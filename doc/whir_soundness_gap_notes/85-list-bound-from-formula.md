# 85. Is the rank formula the right target? A counterexample to (C\*), and what does give a list bound

**No bound is proved, the rank formula is not the right target, and exactly one inequality is missing.** (1) **(C\*) and (C') are false** at rate 1/2 for three members at $(k, n) = (16, 32)$ (Counterexample 1.1: weight 30, rank 29, every binary upper pattern gives 30; proved, computer-assisted, confirmed by note 82's own program; found independently of researcher 86 and referee 87, on another pattern), and Lemma 1.2 gives the solution space of a whole family in closed form: a lifted pattern with three or more zero blocks has kernel $\bigoplus_r (\bigcap_{s \ne r} K_s) \otimes B_r$, sums of products with different lower factors, which the level-by-level binary chain undercounts (ten members: six relations that no binary pattern explains). The failure goes the wrong way for lists (more solutions, hence more deficiency, than the formula predicts); note 74's one-level identity survives on the examples with a minimizer that depends on the upper twiddles; what stays true of the chain is the two-sided inequality $2 d_{j+1} - B_j \le d_j \le 2 d_{j+1} - E_j$ (Lemma 2.1). (2) **Even read as an inequality the formula gives no list bound**: with the ranks free it is the one-functional count, and a random profile with exponentially many members at agreement $5n/8$ satisfies every refereed inequality (4.7); it is not realized at $n = 8, 16$ and nothing refereed excludes it for large $n$. (3) **Proved, unconditionally (Theorem 4.1):** for every list at agreement $k + e$ and every scale $N = n/b$, $\sum_l (e + b - \beta_l(N)) \le b\,(N/2 + 1)\binom{N}{N/2}$, where $\beta_l(N)$ is the number of leaves at which the member $l$ agrees with the word together with a member from which it differs somewhere in the block of scale $N$. So a list whose members have on average at most $e$ such leaves at some scale $N$ has at most $F(N)$ members (350 at $N = 8$, $2^{17}$ at 16, $2^{259}$ at 256), and fewer than $e + b$ gives $b F(N)/(e + b - \bar\beta)$: **at $5n/8$ every list whose binary full part at scale 8 has kernel dimension at most 2 has at most $44n$ members** (Corollary 4.2). Also proved: the members whose agreement set contains at most ten full blocks covering $15n/32$ leaves number fewer than $n^{10}$ (Proposition 4.3, shortening and Johnson), and the members with $5/8$ of their agreement in full blocks of scale $N$ number at most $\lambda_{5/8}(N)$ (Proposition 4.4), so the recursion closes with constant 1 on the members that do not use half leaves. (4) **The missing inequality is (M)** (4.6): at some scale $N \le 256$ the members have on average fewer than $e + n/N$ such leaves, with a margin $n^{-c}$; a sufficient one-level form is (M1): a member lies in the splitting classes of at most $m_0$ blocks of each height, which gives the constant $F(8 m_0)$ at $5n/8$ and $F(16 m_0)$ at $9n/16$, below $2^{300}$ for $m_0 \le 32$ and $16$, and below $n^{11}$ for $m_0 \le (11/16) \log_2 n - 1$. Every known list satisfies (M) at scale 8 or 16, with equality or one leaf to spare: the staircases have $\beta_l(8) = n/8 - 1$, which is $e - 1$ at agreement $5n/8$ (15 members) and $e$ at $5n/8 - 1$ (35 members). (C\*) holds at every level of the chain of every maximal list at $n = 8, 16$ and of the lists of 7, 11, 15 and 35 members, so its failure is not what limits the known lists. No refereed statement bounds the number of leaves of a member whose class splits at a given height; a proof of (M) needs a statement in which a leaf carrying several members costs more than one condition, and the general statement of that kind, (C\*), is false.

## 1. The rank formula (C\*) is false for class patterns at rate 1/2

Notation of note 82: a pattern $\mathcal P$ gives each leaf $x$ a class $C_x$ of members, $\Lambda_x$ is spanned by the differences $e_a + e_b$, $a, b \in C_x$ ($e_0 = 0$), $\kappa_x = (|C_x| - 1)_+$, $w = \sum_x \kappa_x$; a solution is $M \in K^{L \times k}$ with $\Lambda_x M g_x = 0$; $r(\mathcal P)$ is the rank of the rows $\Lambda_x \otimes g_x$; $\mathcal Q^*$ is the binary full part one level up, $Y^* = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q^*)$, and $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) = 2 r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) + \sum_x (r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q \vee \Lambda_x@p(x)) - r^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)) \ge r(\mathcal P)$ for every binary $\mathcal Q$ (Lemma 1.1 of note 82). (C\*) says $r(\mathcal P) = B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*)$, (C') says $r(\mathcal P) = \min_{\mathcal Q} B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q)$.

**Counterexample 1.1 (three members, $(k, n) = (16, 32)$; proved, computer-assisted).** Blocks of four leaves (scale 8):

`012 012 012 . | 012 012 012 . | 012 012 012 . | 01 01 01 01 | 02 02 02 02 | 12 12 12 12 | . . . . | . . . .`

Weight $w = 30 \le Lk = 32$. **The rank is 29**, the kernel has dimension 3 and its generic point is three distinct codewords with agreement 17 each. $\mathcal Q^*$ = `01+02 . | 01+02 . | 01+02 . | 01 01 | 02 02 | 12 12 | . . | . .` (pairs; weight 12, rank 12, kernel 4), and $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 2 \cdot 12 + 3 \cdot 2 = 30$: **(C\*) fails**. The row system has exactly one relation, and its components at the leaves 2, 6, 10 (the common leaf of the mixed pair of each of the first three blocks) are not in $Y^* \otimes F$; an exact binary $\mathcal Q$ lies below $\mathcal Q^*$ and forces every relation component into $R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) \otimes F \subseteq Y^* \otimes F$, so **no binary $\mathcal Q$ is exact: (C') fails** too. This is the "smallest open case" of note 82 (three members, three leaves carrying all three, in three different bottom pairs): it is true at $(4, 8)$ by note 82's exhaustive computation and false at $(16, 32)$.

*Checks.* `scripts/85_cex.py` (three draws, from first principles): rank 29, kernel 3, $\mathcal Q^*$ of weight 12 and rank 12, $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 30$, one relation with components outside $Y^*$ at leaves 2, 6, 10. Note 82's own program, `VERBOSE=1 82_bin/82_big file 4 1 3 85_tmp/cex3_82.txt`, prints `w 30, L*k 32, rank 29, B(P;Q*) 30` and `OPEN ... best B found 30 ... not certified 1` (its coordinate descent from $\mathcal Q^*$, from the sibling join and from 0 finds nothing below 30).

*Why one draw suffices.* The generic kernel has dimension at least 3 by Lemma 1.2 below (an explicit family of solutions, for any twiddles), and at most the dimension at a draw, which is 3; so the generic rank is 29, the draw is generic for the kernel, and the generic $\mathcal Q^*$ is the one computed (a binary functional that kills the generic solutions kills their specializations, which span the kernel at the draw; conversely the listed functionals are forced by the classes). Its rank 12 equals its weight and the three ranks $\mathrm{rank}(\Lambda_x|_{\Delta_p}) = 2$ are maximal, so $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 30$ generically. The generic relation specializes to the relation at the draw, so its components are generically outside $Y^*$. That an exact $\mathcal Q$ puts the relation components in $R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$: if $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q) = r$ the proof of Lemma 1.1 of note 82 is tight, $R = R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q) \otimes F^2 \oplus \bigoplus_x Q_x \otimes z_x$ with $Q_x$ a complement of $U_x \cap R^{\rm up}(\mathcal Q)$ in $U_x$, and $\mathcal Q \subseteq \mathcal Q^*$ by the definition of $\mathcal Q^*$.

**Lemma 1.2 (a lifted pattern with common leaves in several blocks; proved).** Let $n = Nb$, $b = 2^i$, let $\mathcal Q$ be a binary pattern on the top code $C_N$ with solution space $S'$, let $q_1, \dots, q_m$ be blocks (coordinates of $C_N$), $K_r = \{N \in S' : N g_{q_r} = 0\}$ the top solutions in which all members coincide at $q_r$, and $\pi_r : S' \to S'/K_r$. Let $Z_r$ be a set of $b - 1$ leaves of the block $q_r$ and $\mathcal P$ the pattern of length $n$ that is the lift of $\mathcal Q$ (the space $\mathcal Q_q$ at every leaf of the block $q$) with, in addition, all members in the class of every leaf of $Z_1 \cup \dots \cup Z_m$. Let $B_r \in K^b$ be the vector, unique up to a scalar, killed by the rows $\beta_x$ of the block transform of $q_r$ at the leaves $x \in Z_r$. Then (a) $\ker \mathcal P = \{\xi \in S' \otimes K^b : (\pi_r \otimes 1)\xi \in (S'/K_r) \otimes B_r \text{ for all } r\}$; (b) $\ker \mathcal P \supseteq \sum_r \big(\bigcap_{s \ne r} K_s\big) \otimes B_r$; (c) if $\bigcap_r K_r = 0$ and $B_1, \dots, B_m$ are linearly independent, which holds for generic bottom twiddles as soon as $m \le b$, then $\ker \mathcal P = \bigoplus_r \big(\bigcap_{s \ne r} K_s\big) \otimes B_r$ and $\dim \ker \mathcal P = \sum_r \dim \bigcap_{s \ne r} K_s$. *Proof.* (a) The lift of $\mathcal Q$ has kernel $S' \otimes K^b$ (the rows $\theta \otimes g_q \otimes \beta_x$, $x$ in the block $q$, span $\theta \otimes g_q \otimes (K^b)^*$). The evaluation of $\xi$ at $q_r$ is $(\pi_r \otimes 1)\xi$, and it is killed by the $\beta_x$, $x \in Z_r$, iff it lies in $(S'/K_r) \otimes B_r$. (b) For $N \in \bigcap_{s \ne r} K_s$ the tensor $N \otimes B_r$ has zero evaluation at the $q_s$, $s \ne r$, and evaluation $\pi_r(N) \otimes B_r$ at $q_r$. (c) $\iota = (\pi_r)_r : S' \to \bigoplus_r S'/K_r$ is injective. For $\xi \in \ker \mathcal P$ write $(\pi_r \otimes 1)\xi = v_r \otimes B_r$ and expand $(\iota \otimes 1)\xi$ in a basis of $K^b$ that contains $B_1, \dots, B_m$: the coefficient of $B_r$ is $(0, \dots, v_r, \dots, 0)$ and it must lie in $\iota(S')$, so $v_r = \pi_r(N_r)$ with $N_r \in \bigcap_{s \ne r} K_s$, and the other coefficients vanish; hence $\xi = \sum_r N_r \otimes B_r$. Independence of the $B_r$: $B_r$ is the pure tensor $\bigotimes_j (t_j, 1)$ over the layers of the block, $t_j$ the twiddle of the sibling of the path to the leaf left out of $Z_r$ (a vector vanishing on a whole half block has $B^0 = t B^1$, then recurse); the monomials in independent twiddles are linearly independent functions, so these tensors span $K^b$ as the twiddles of one block vary, and the blocks have disjoint twiddles. $\square$

**Reading of Counterexample 1.1.** $S'$ is the solution space of `. . . 01 02 12 . .` on $C_8$: $\dim S' = 8 - 3 = 5$, $\dim K_r = 5 - 2 = 3$, $\dim (K_s \cap K_t) \ge 5 - 4 = 1$ and $K_1 \cap K_2 \cap K_3 = 0$, $b = 4 \ge 3$. So the kernel is $\bigoplus_r (K_s \cap K_t) \otimes B_r$, of dimension 3: **three products with three different lower factors, each top factor vanishing at the zero blocks of the two others** (`85_cex.py`: $\dim S' = 5$, $\dim K_r = 3, 3, 3$, $\dim K_s \cap K_t = 1, 1, 1$, triple intersection 0, at three draws). The level-by-level chain goes through scale 16, where each block is two half blocks and the three lower factors live in $K^2$: three dependent lines, Lemma 1.2 (c) does not apply, the kernel is $2 \cdot 5 - 3 \cdot 2 = 4$ and $\mathcal Q^*$ is exact there; one level down the three common leaves of the mixed pairs are counted as $3 \cdot 2$ independent conditions on $\ker(\mathcal Q^*) \otimes K^2$, which gives $8 - 6 = 2 < 3$. The product structure with three independent lower factors only exists two levels below the top pattern, and the binary full part of the intermediate level does not remember it.

**Counterexample 1.3 (ten members, $(16, 32)$; same status).** Members are the ten pairs of the five blocks 3 to 7, the class of a leaf of block $q \ge 3$ is the four members containing $q$, and blocks 0, 1, 2 have three common leaves each (`85_cex.py 10`; `85_chain.py stairm 8 4 3 0`). $w = 141 \le Lk = 144$, rank 135, kernel 9, $B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q^*) = 2 \cdot 57 + 27 = 141$: six relations that no binary $\mathcal Q$ explains. Here $\dim S' = 21$, $\dim K_r = 12$, $\dim K_s \cap K_t = 3$, kernel $3 + 3 + 3$ against the chain's $4 \cdot 21 - 9 \cdot 9 = 3$. Its generic point is ten distinct codewords at agreement 17: the member of the family "$m$ zero blocks at scale 8" with $m = 3$ (Section 5).

**Proposition 1.4 (what survives on these examples; exact at the stated size).** Note 74's one-level identity, $r = \min_{\Lambda'} [2 \dim \sum_x \Lambda'_x + \sum_x \dim U_x/\Lambda'_x]$ over all tuples of subspaces $\Lambda'_x \subseteq U_x = \Lambda_x \otimes g_{p(x)}$, holds on both examples, with a minimizer that is not binary: let $\nu$ run over the functionals of $K_1^L$ that kill the difference vectors of all solutions at the mixed pair $p_r$ of the zero block $r$ (for three members a single $\nu_r$, the annihilator of the line $\pi_r(K_s \cap K_t) \subset K_1^2$; it depends on the twiddles of the top layers). Then $Y = Y^* + \langle \nu \otimes g_{p_r} \rangle$ has dimension $12 + 1 = 13$ for three members (the three vectors $\nu_r \otimes g_{p_r}$ are congruent modulo $Y^*$) and $57 + 6 = 63$ for ten (18 functionals spanning 6 new dimensions), and $2 \dim Y + \sum_x \dim U_x / (U_x \cap Y) = 29$ and $135$, the ranks. This is exactly the configuration that note 82 singled out as the missing case ("$\nu_1 \otimes g_{p_1} \equiv \nu_2 \otimes g_{p_2} \equiv \nu_3 \otimes g_{p_3} \not\equiv 0$ modulo $Y^*$ with $\nu_i$ not binary; (C\*) says this never happens for class patterns"): it happens. It is consistent with Theorem 4.4 of note 82 (the identity with the rational full part $Y^K$ when at most four leaves have a full part not spanned by rational vectors): three leaves here.

**Consequences (each tagged).** (1) **False:** (C\*) and (C') for class patterns of three members at rate 1/2; the conjectures of note 82 that the closed recursion over binary patterns holds for at most five members at every rate, or for any number of members at rate 1/2; Theorem 5.1 (ii) to (iv) and Corollaries 5.2, 5.3 of note 82 as statements about every class pattern (they are proved there only where (C\*) is, and stay true there). (2) **Proved, unchanged:** Lemma 1.1 of note 82 ($r \le B(\mathcal P; \mathcal Q)$, that is $\dim \ker \mathcal P \ge 2 d^{\rm up} - \sum_x \rho_x$) and Theorem 5.1 (i) (every solution has its slices in $\ker \mathcal Q^*$). (4) **An exact recursion cannot be closed over a finite alphabet of spaces a node:** the minimizer needs functionals $\nu$ that are evaluations of sub-kernels of top patterns (solutions vanishing at other blocks), rational functions of the upper twiddles. ## 2. What is true of the chain: a two-sided inequality (proved)

**The chain (note 82, Corollary 5.2).** $\mathcal Q^{(0)} = \mathcal P$, $\mathcal Q^{(j+1)} = \mathcal Q^*(\mathcal Q^{(j)})$, a binary pattern on the $N_j = n/2^j$ nodes of height $j$ (the coordinates of the top code $C_{N_j}$, of dimension $N_j/2$); $d_j = \dim \ker \mathcal Q^{(j)}$; $\Delta_u \subseteq K^L$ the span of the difference vectors $N g_u$ of the solutions $N$ of $\mathcal Q^{(j)}$ at the node $u$; for a node $u$ of height $j$ with parent $p$, $\rho_u = \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u|_{\Delta_p})$. Call $u$ **essential** if $\rho_u \ge 1$, equivalently $\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u \not\subseteq \mathcal Q^{(j+1)}_p$ (a binary functional kills $\Delta_p$ iff it lies in $\mathcal Q^{(j+1)}_p$; note 82, Section 4). $E_j$ is the number of essential nodes of height $j$ and $B_j = \sum_{u \text{ of height } j} \rho_u$.

**Lemma 2.1 (two-sided chain inequality; proved, for every binary pattern and every level).** $2 d_{j+1} - B_j \le d_j \le 2 d_{j+1} - E_j$. (C\*) is the statement that the left inequality is an equality; in Counterexample 1.1 at $j = 0$ it reads $2 \le 3 \le 5$. *Proof.* Left: Lemma 1.1 of note 82. Right: the argument of Lemma 2.2 of note 83, run on the binary chain. Let $K_1$ be the field of the twiddles above the bottom layer of the code at scale $N_j$ and $t_u$ the bottom twiddles, independent over $K_1$. $S = \ker \mathcal Q^{(j)} \subseteq (S^* \otimes K)^2$ with $S^* = \ker \mathcal Q^{(j+1)}$ defined over $K_1$. Let $[N]$ be the generic point of $\mathbb P(S)$: $\mathrm{trdeg}_{K_1} K([N]) = N_j + \dim S - 1$. For an essential node $u$ choose a binary $\lambda \in \mathcal Q^{(j)}_u \setminus \mathcal Q^{(j+1)}_p$: the generic point has $(\lambda^T N_0 g_p, \lambda^T N_1 g_p) \ne 0$ and $\lambda^T N_0 g_p + t_u \lambda^T N_1 g_p = 0$, so $t_u \in K_1([N])$, the field generated by the coordinates of $[N]$ in a $K_1$-basis of $S^{*2}$, of transcendence degree at most $2 \dim S^* - 1$. So $N_j + \dim S - 1 \le 2 \dim S^* - 1 + N_j - E_j$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.2 (fine cost; proved).** Put $c_j = 2^j d_j - d_0$. Then $\sum_{i<j} 2^i E_i \le c_j \le \sum_{i<j} 2^i B_i$; in particular **at most $c_j$ leaves have an essential node on their path below height $j$**. Call the other leaves *good at level $j$*.

**Lemma 2.3 (good leaves vote for a block; proved).** If $x$ is good at level $j$ and $u$ is its ancestor of height $j$, then $\Lambda_x \subseteq \mathcal Q^{(j)}_u$, and in every solution of $\mathcal P$ any two members of $C_x$ coincide on all $2^j$ leaves of the block of $u$. *Proof.* An inessential node has $\mathcal Q^{(i)}_{u_i} \subseteq \mathcal Q^{(i+1)}_{u_{i+1}}$; and $\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u$ kills the $2^j$ slices of every solution at $u$ (Theorem 5.1 (i) of note 82, iterated), which are the columns of the block of $u$. $\square$

## 3. Task 1: the list problem as an optimization problem

**3.1 Lists in rank terms (proved, trivial).** A class pattern $\mathcal P$ with members $0..L$ carries a list (a solution with pairwise distinct members) iff no difference $e_a + e_b$ kills every solution, iff $r(\mathcal P) \le r(\mathcal P/ab) + k - 1$ for every pair, $\mathcal P/ab$ the class pattern with $a$ and $b$ identified (a linear space over an infinite field is not a finite union of proper subspaces). So an exact rank formula for class patterns would decide the existence of every list, and the list problem at agreement $a$ is: maximize $L + 1$ over the class patterns in which every member lies in at least $a$ classes and these $\binom{L+1}{2}$ rank inequalities hold.

**3.2 Deficiency form (proved, trivial).** A list of $s + 1$ members at agreement $a$ has $w \ge (s+1) a - n$ and $r \le sk - 1$, so its deficiency $w - r$ is at least $(s+1) a - n - sk + 1$, and this holds for every sub-list. At $a = 5n/8$: **every $s + 1$ members of a list form a pattern of deficiency at least $(s - 3) k/4 + 1$**, and $L + 1 \le 4 + 4(\delta - 1)/k$ with $\delta$ the deficiency of the whole pattern. A list bound is an upper bound on deficiencies of list patterns that is linear in $k$ with a constant independent of $L$. By Section 1 the formula gives no upper bound on deficiencies.

**3.3 The program the formula was meant to give (valid for the patterns where (C\*) holds at every level; false in general).** Unknowns: a binary space $\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u$ and a subspace $\Delta_u \subseteq K^L$ at every node, with $\mathcal Q^{(0)}_x = \Lambda(C_x)$. Constraints: $\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u$ is the set of binary vectors of $\Delta_u^\perp$; $\Delta_u \subseteq \Delta_p \cap (\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u)^\perp$; $\rho_u = \dim \Delta_p - \dim (\Delta_p \cap (\mathcal Q^{(j)}_u)^\perp)$; $d_v = \dim \Delta_{\rm root}$, $d_j = 2 d_{j+1} - \sum_u \rho_u$, $d_0 \ge 1$; no $e_a + e_b$ in $\Delta_{\rm root}^\perp$; for every set $T$ of $N_j/2$ nodes of height $j$, $\sum_{u \in T} \dim \Delta_u \ge d_j$ (any $N_j/2$ coordinates determine a top codeword); every member in at least $a$ classes. Objective: $L + 1$. In this program the deficiency is $k \dim \Theta + \sum_j 2^j G_j$, $G_j = \sum_u (\dim \mathcal Q^{(j)}_u - \rho_u) - 2 \sum_p \dim \mathcal Q^{(j+1)}_p$, and a node contributes only if it is essential, at most $e_p = \mathrm{codim}\, \Delta_p - \dim \mathcal Q^{(j+1)}_p$ (the number of independent functionals depending on the upper twiddles that kill the upper solutions at its parent). **It is not a linear program in the fractions of leaves of each type**, as the one-functional bounds of referee 75 were: the cost $\rho_u$ of a node is a function of the space $\Delta_p$, which is solution data of the pattern one level up, so the program is recursive; and by Section 1 the equality $d_j = 2 d_{j+1} - \sum \rho_u$ is false for some class patterns (the true $d_j$ is larger), so its optimum is not an upper bound on list sizes.

**3.4 The sound part (proved): the one-functional program.** Replace the equality by Lemma 2.1: $d_j \le 2 d_{j+1} - E_j$. This is linear in the profile (which leaves have an essential node at which height), it is unconditional, and it is what Section 4 uses.

**Note added after the lead's message (round 21).** Researcher 86 and referee 87 found, independently, that (C\*) is false at $(16, 32)$ with three members (another pattern, mechanism described as two conditions in a two-dimensional quotient at scale 8 pointing to the same line) and for five and six members at $(8, 16)$. Counterexample 1.1 is a different pattern at the same size; Lemma 1.2 gives its solution space in closed form and a family (any top pattern, three or more zero blocks). The question was then changed to: prove any bound $\lambda(n) \le n^c$, $c \le 11$, at agreement $5n/8$, without (C\*). Sections 4 to 6 answer that question: the bound is **not proved**; Theorem 4.1 reduces a bound linear in $n$ to one inequality, (M), stated in 4.6.

## 4. Task 2: what can be proved without (C\*), and the inequality that is missing

Throughout: a list is $L + 1$ distinct codewords $c_l$ and a word $W$, $A_l$ the set of leaves where $c_l$ agrees with $W$, $|A_l| \ge a = k + e$ ($e = n/8$ at agreement $5n/8$, $n/16$ at $9n/16$), $C_x$ the set of all members agreeing with $W$ at $x$. A scale is $N = n/b$, $b = 2^j$; its blocks are the $N$ nodes of height $j$. $F(N) = (N/2 + 1)\binom{N}{N/2}$.

**Definition (incoherent leaves).** A leaf $x \in A_l$ is *incoherent for $l$ at scale $N$* if some member of $C_x$ differs from $c_l$ somewhere in the block of $x$; $\beta_l(N)$ is their number. $\beta_l(n) = 0$, $\beta_l$ is non-increasing in $N$, and $\beta_l(2) = |\{x \in A_l : |C_x| \ge 2\}|$.

**Theorem 4.1 (incoherence count; proved, unconditional; it uses only that any $N/2$ blocks determine a codeword).** For every list and every scale $N = n/b$: $\sum_l \big(|A_l| - k + b - \beta_l(N)\big) \le b\, F(N)$. Hence if the members have on average $\bar\beta < e + b$ incoherent leaves at scale $N$, then $L + 1 \le b\, F(N) / (e + b - \bar\beta)$; and if $\bar\beta \le e$, then $L + 1 \le F(N)$. *Proof.* A coherent leaf $x \in A_l$ votes for a symbol of the block $q$ of $x$: the common restriction to $q$ of all members of $C_x$. Give each such leaf mass $1/b$ on that symbol; a block carries mass at most 1, and the credit of $l$ (the mass of its own symbols) is $(|A_l| - \beta_l)/b$. The sum of the credits is at most $\sum_q |H_q|$, $H_q$ a largest set of members with one symbol at $q$, and $\sum_q |H_q| = \sum_l |T(l)|$, $T(l) = \{q : l \in H_q\}$. Members with $|T(l)| \ge N/2$ are determined by any $N/2$ blocks of $T(l)$, so there are at most $\binom{N}{N/2}$ of them and $\sum_l |T(l)| \le (N/2 - 1)(L + 1) + (N/2 + 1)\binom{N}{N/2}$ (Lemma A of note 83). Multiply by $b$: $\sum_l (|A_l| - \beta_l) \le (k - b)(L + 1) + b F(N)$. $\square$ At $N = 8$ and $\bar\beta \le e$ this is $L + 1 \le 350$; note 83's Proposition 6.1 (30 members) is the sharper statement for the sub-class with one lower factor.

**Corollary 4.2 (kernel form; proved).** With the chain of Section 2 for the class pattern of the list, $\beta_l(N_j) \le c_j = 2^j d_j - d_0$ for every member (an incoherent leaf is not good at level $j$, Lemma 2.3 and Corollary 2.2). So $d_j \le N_j e/n$ gives $L + 1 \le F(N_j)$, and $d_j \le N_j e/n + 1$ gives $L + 1 \le (n/N_j) F(N_j)/d_0$. At agreement $5n/8$: **a list whose binary full part at scale 8 has kernel dimension at most 2 has at most $44 n$ members, and at most 350 if that dimension is 1.** This improves Proposition 2.4 of note 83 (one level instead of bounded kernels at every depth, the exact count $2^j d_j - d_0$ instead of a geometric series), but the incoherence form is stronger: the family with two zero blocks of Section 5 has $d_8 = 11$ or 22 and still $\beta_l(8) = n/4 - 2 < e + b$.

**Proposition 4.3 (clusters and certificates; proved, classical: shortening and the Johnson count).** In an MDS code $[n, n/2]$, let distinct codewords at agreement at least $\tau n$ with one word coincide pairwise on a common set of $\zeta n$ leaves, $\zeta > \zeta^*(\tau) = (1/2 - \tau^2)/(3/2 - 2\tau)$. Then there are at most $J(\tau, \zeta) = (\tau - 1/2)(1 - \zeta) / \big(\zeta (3/2 - 2\tau) - (1/2 - \tau^2)\big)$ of them. At $\tau = 5/8$: $\zeta^* = 7/16$ and $J = 8(1 - \zeta)/(16\zeta - 7)$, at most 8 for $\zeta \ge 15/32$; at $\tau = 9/16$: $\zeta^* = 47/96$, $J = 16(1 - \zeta)/(96\zeta - 47)$. *Proof.* Subtract one of them: the differences lie in the code shortened at the common set, an MDS code $[n - \zeta n, n/2 - \zeta n]$, and each agrees with the translated word on at least $\tau n - \zeta n$ of the remaining leaves (whether or not it agrees with the word on the common set). Two of them agree on fewer than $\rho' n'$ leaves, so $M (\tau'^2 - \rho') \le \tau' - \rho'$ with $\tau' = (\tau - \zeta)/(1 - \zeta)$, $\rho' = (1/2 - \zeta)/(1 - \zeta)$. $\square$ *Consequence (certificates).* Call the *block complexity* of a member the least number of disjoint blocks of the tree (nodes, any heights) on which it is full (agrees with the word on the whole block) that cover at least $15n/32$ leaves. **The members of block complexity at most $c$ number at most $8 \sum_{c' \le c} \binom{2n}{c'}$, which is below $n^{10}$ for $c = 10$**, for every list at agreement $5n/8$ of any MDS code with a tree of blocks. Every member of the lists at agreement $5n/8$ in the table of Section 5 has block complexity at most 5 (four full blocks of scale 8 in the staircases and in the lists of 7, 11 and 15 members; at $n = 8, 16$ a few pairs and leaves); it was not computed for the voted lists of note 83's class over $C_{16}$.

**Proposition 4.4 (members that are heavy at a coarse scale; proved).** For every scale $N$ and every $\tau$, the members that are full on at least $\tau N$ blocks of scale $N$ number at most $\lambda_\tau(N)$, the largest list of the generic tree code $[N, N/2]$ at agreement $\tau N$. *Proof.* Their restrictions to blocks are distinct codewords of $C_N \otimes K^b$ read in private frames; a generic linear functional of $K^b$ (coefficients in an extension field) applied in every block, after undoing the frames, sends them to distinct codewords of $C_N$ that agree with the image of the word on their full blocks. $\square$ So $\lambda_{5/8}(n) \le \lambda_{5/8}(n/2) + \#\{\text{members with fewer than } 5n/16 \text{ full pairs}\}$: the recursion closes on $\lambda$ with constant 1 for the members that keep their agreement in full pairs, and the whole difficulty is the members that use half leaves.

**4.5 The quantities of a one-level recursion (definitions; the identities are proved, trivial).** For a leaf $x$ with $|C_x| \ge 2$ let $h(x)$ be the largest height $h$ such that the members of $C_x$ coincide on the whole block of height $h$ containing $x$ (the class of $x$ is then exactly the set of members with that symbol on that block, and it splits on the parent block). For a member $l$ put $\pi_l = |\{x \in A_l : C_x = \{l\}\}|$ (private leaves) and $\nu_l(i) = |\{x \in A_l : |C_x| \ge 2,\ h(x) = i\}|$. Then (1) $|A_l| = \pi_l + \sum_{i < v} \nu_l(i)$ and $\sum_l \pi_l \le n$; (2) $\beta_l(n/2^j) = \sum_{i < j} \nu_l(i)$; (3) a leaf with $h(x) = i$ has an essential node of height at most $i$ on its path in the chain of the list's pattern (Lemma 2.3), so the leaves with $h(x) < j$ number at most $\sum_{i<j} 2^i E_i \le 2^j d_j - d_0$ (Lemma 2.1; for a generic point of the kernel the node of height $i$ itself is essential); (4) Theorem 4.1 reads $\sum_l \big(e + 2^j - \sum_{i<j} \nu_l(i)\big) \le 2^j F(n/2^j)$ for every $j$; (5) the members with at least $\tau N$ full blocks at scale $N$ number at most $\lambda_\tau(N)$ (Proposition 4.4), and those of block complexity at most $c$ at most $8 \binom{2n}{\le c}$ (Proposition 4.3). These five lines are everything a one-level recursion can use at present. The one quantity for which no inequality is proved is $\nu_l(i)$: how many leaves of a member sit in classes that split exactly at height $i$.

**4.6 The missing inequality (not proved; three forms, each sufficient).**
- **(M1), one level:** there is $m_0$ such that in every list at agreement $k + e$, every member has $\nu_l(i) \le m_0 2^i$ for every height $i$ below the scale $N_0$, the smallest power of two that is at least $m_0 n/e$: a member belongs to the splitting classes of at most $m_0$ blocks of each height. Then $\beta_l(N_0) < m_0 n/N_0 \le e$ and Theorem 4.1 gives $L + 1 \le F(N_0)$: at $5n/8$, $F(8) = 350$ for $m_0 = 1$, $F(16) \approx 2^{17}$ for $m_0 = 2$, $F(256) \approx 2^{259}$ for $m_0 \le 32$; at $9n/16$, $F(16 m_0)$. A bound $n^c$ follows as soon as $F(N_0) \le n^c$, for instance from $m_0 \le (11/16) \log_2 n - 1$ for $n^{11}$ at $5n/8$ ($N_0 < 16 m_0$ and $F(N) < 2^{N+4}$ in the range of interest); and exceptions are allowed: $M$ members violating it add $M$ (remove them; the incoherent leaves of a sub-list are among those of the list).
- **(M), averaged at one scale:** for some scale $N \le 256$, $\sum_l (e + n/N - \beta_l(N)) \ge (L + 1) n^{-c'}$. Then $L + 1 \le n^{1 + c'} F(N)/N$.
- **Covering form:** the list is a union of $M$ sub-lists, and inside each sub-list every member has fewer than $e + n/N$ leaves at which it agrees with the word together with a member of the same sub-list from which it differs somewhere in the block of scale $N$. Then $L + 1 \le M (n/N) F(N)$ (Theorem 4.1 applied to each sub-list). This is the form with a multiplicative budget: $M = C^{\log_2 n}$ is allowed.

On the lists of Section 5: (M) holds at scale 8 for every maximal list at $n = 8, 16$ and for the lists of 7, 11 and 15 members, with $\beta_l(8) \le e - 1$ except for the 34 two-family orbits at $n = 16$ ($\beta_l(8) = 3 < e + b = 4$); (M1) holds with $m_0 = 1$ for the lists of 7, 11, 15 members and the staircases, and with $m_0 = m$ for the families with $m$ zero blocks, whose number of members decreases with $m$. Small lists can need a finer scale: a six-member list at $n = 32$ of note 83's class "one product family at level 1 over $C_{16}$" has $\beta_l(8) = 11 > e + b = 8$ and $\nu_l(1) = 10$, and $\beta_l(16) = 1$. So (M1) can only be true with exceptions for lists of few members, or with $m_0$ of the order of the scale where the list stops being a lift; (M) is the robust form.

**4.7 Why the refereed statements cannot give (M1) (expectations proved; concentration sketched).** Take a random profile: every member agrees with the word at every leaf independently with probability $5/8$, and no two members coincide on any pair of sibling leaves. Then $\nu_l(0) = |A_l|$ for every member: no inequality on $\nu$ at all. This profile satisfies, for any number of members up to $\exp(cn)$, every refereed inequality that holds for arbitrary lists: two members agree on about $25n/64 < k$ leaves (MDS, Johnson); for a sub-list of $s + 1$ members the leaves with at least two of them number about $n \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(s+1, 5/8) \ge 2] \le sk - 1$ (the one-functional theorem $Q_t$ at every size, and its multi-scale form, Lemma 2.1, since all essential nodes are leaves); three members have weight about $1.86 k \le 5k/2 - 2$; and for every bijection $\mu$ between the leaves of the two trees $\sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\mu(x)}) \le Lk - 1$, because two classes of density $5/8$ miss a common member as soon as $L \gg \log n$ (the matching inequality of note 76). This is the profile asked for in Task 2: unboundedly many members, every constraint that the formula gives when it is read as an inequality (the ranks $\rho_u \ge 1$ free) is satisfied. **It is not realized by lists at $n = 8, 16$ (exhaustive: 4 and 5 members), and whether it is realized for large $n$ is the question itself**: nothing refereed excludes it. So a proof of (M1) must use a statement in which a leaf carrying several members costs more than one condition. The refereed statements of that kind are narrow: Theorems 4.2 and 4.4 of note 82 (at most two, or four, leaves with large classes), the three-member rank up to $k = 8$, and note 83's Proposition 6.1 (one lower factor at scale 8). (C\*) was the general statement of that kind, and it is false.

**4.8 A route to (M1) at height 0 that I did not complete (sketched).** $\nu_l(0)$ counts the leaves $x \in A_l$ at which some member of $C_x$ differs from $l$ at the sibling leaf. In the pencil form of note 83 these are the values $\theta = t_x$ at which the line $c_l(\theta)$ of top codewords meets, at the coordinate $p(x)$, a line $c_{l'}(\theta)$ from which it differs there for generic $\theta$. Lemma 2.1 bounds the number of such leaves for the whole list by $2 d_1 - d_0$, which is not bounded. What is needed is the same count for one member against all others with the top kernel replaced by a quantity attached to $l$; Lemma 3.4 of note 83 is of this type ($h^{\rm ess}_l \le 2 s'_R + 2(n'/2 - f^{\rm sh}_l)_+ - 1$) and shows that a member with at least $n/4$ shared full pairs has at most $2 s'_R - 1$ essential half leaves, $s'_R$ the top kernel of the other members. So (M1) at height 0 would follow from a bound on the top kernel of the list without one member. I have no such bound; the data of Section 5 have $d_1 \le 3$ at $5n/8$ for all maximal lists.

**Which instances of (C\*) are needed: none.** Theorem 4.1 and Propositions 4.3, 4.4 use the MDS property at a block scale only, and Corollary 4.2 uses the right inequality of Lemma 2.1 (the one-functional count). The whole remaining content is (M), a statement about lists and not about ranks of patterns: "in a list with many members, the leaves where a member agrees with the word together with members from which it differs nearby are fewer than its excess over the dimension plus one block". (M) is empty for $n \le N$ (at scale $n$ every leaf is coherent and the theorem is the trivial bound $F(n)$), so its smallest instances are at $n = 2N$: at $n = 16$, scale 8, it says that the members have on average fewer than 4 leaves where they agree with the word together with a member from which they differ at the sibling leaf, and it holds on all 162 orbits (at most 3); at $n = 32$ scale 8 is not enough (the six-member list of 4.6 has 11 incoherent leaves against $e + b = 8$) and the instance is scale 16: on average fewer than 6 such leaves per member (the lists of note 83 have 1). These are statements about all lists of a length where no exhaustive search exists beyond $n = 16$.
