# 39. Period descent: the period-free part is a base-index effect, primitive lists, and no phantom roots below the slack

Researcher 39, round 5. Starting point: note 37 (the descent on periods of codeword differences and its hypothesis DP), which is not repeated. Every statement is tagged **[proved]** (proof in this note), **[quoted]** (from a cited note, not re-proved), **[checked]** (numerically, sizes stated) or **[conjectured]**. Scripts are `scripts/39_*`, section 7 says how to run them.

Notation as in note 37. $F$ is a field of characteristic two, $D \subset F$ an $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of dimension $N$, $n = 2^N$. $D$ is **generic** if it has a basis $b_1, \dots, b_N$ algebraically independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (the formal model), and then $K = \mathbb{F}_2(b)$. Codewords are polynomials of degree $< k$, $T$ is an agreement count, $\Delta = T - k + 1$ the slack, $\sigma = \Delta/n$. A list is a word with distinct codewords $c_i$ and agreement sets $S_i$, $|S_i| \ge T$. For a multiset $\mu$ on $D$, $Z_\mu = \prod_s (X - s)^{\mu(s)}$, and $\hat\mu(\varphi) = \sum_x \mu(x)(-1)^{\varphi(x)}$. For three codewords the *triple function* is $\xi = (c_l - c_i)/(c_j - c_i)$, and $\Pi(\xi) = \{a \in D : \xi(X + a) = \xi(X)\}$. The six functions obtained by permuting the three members are Möbius images of each other, so $\Pi$ and "constant" depend on the triple only. A triple is **bad** if its function is constant or has a nonzero period, a list is **primitive** if it has no bad triple. $\binom{m}{r}_2$ is the Gaussian binomial coefficient. The *level-$j$ problem* is the $U$-problem of note 37 Definition 3.1 with $\dim U = j$: members are polynomials $P(L_U(X))$, $\deg P \le \kappa$, agreeing with an arbitrary word on at least $A$ points of $D$, with slack $A - |U|\kappa \ge \Delta$, and their triple functions live in $F(Y)$ with periods in $(D/U) \setminus 0$.

## 0. Summary

**The honest headline.** Three things were settled, one was reduced, nothing unconditional was obtained for all words.

1. **The decisive experiment has an exact answer, with a proof.** On a generic subspace the complete list of the word $X^{n/2}L_W$ ($|W| = n/8$, codewords of degree $\le n/2$) at agreement $19n/32$ has exactly $56n - 392 + 14336\binom{N-3}{2}_2$ members for every $n \ge 32$ (Proposition 4.1): $15736$, $103544$, $508536$ at $n = 32, 64, 128$ and $2^{55.2}$ at $N = 25$. Every member is a multiset top and is invariant under a subspace of $W$ of size at least $n/32$. Consequently the period-free part, for the best pair, is $13568$ of $15734$ thirds at $n = 32$ (the exact minimum over all $1.2 \cdot 10^8$ pairs, so note 37's sampled value was the truth) and **exactly $0$ for every $n \ge 64$**. The $14336 = 56 \cdot 4^4$ members without any period at $n = 32$ are the base of a family of index 32, as note 37 guessed.
2. **DP in the form "all but $B$" has a constant that is set in small dimension, and the right hypothesis is about primitive lists.** The levels of the descent contain every smaller dimension, so $B^{\mathrm{diff}}_{N, N-5} \ge 13568$ at $(\rho, \tau) = (1/2, 19/32)$ for every $N$ (Remark 1.7): DP$(B)$ with $B$ small is false there, DP$(B)$ with a constant $B(\rho, \tau)$ is what can be true. The clean form: if every list with more than $f$ members contains a bad triple (primitive lists have at most $f$ members, at every level), then $\Lambda^\ast_N(k, T) \le \binom{f+1}{3}^J\, 2^{NJ - J(J-1)/2}\,(n/\Delta + 1)$ on every subspace domain (Theorem 1.3), and the multi-valued words of the levels reduce to ordinary words at half the slack (Lemma 1.4). The bounded-index form of DP and this hypothesis are equivalent up to the constant (Proposition 1.6).
3. **Primitive lists do not obey the generalized Singleton bound.** In the list of item 1 the largest primitive sublists have at least $133$ members at $n = 32$ (Singleton bound 7), exactly $14$ at $n = 64$ (bound 6), exactly $10$ at $n = 128$ (bound 5), at most $8$ at $n = 256, 512$ and at most $2$ from $n = 1024$ on. So "primitive implies generic-points bound" is refuted, by a factor 19 at the base index, and "primitive lists are bounded in terms of $(\rho, \tau)$" survives and is the conjecture (PL, section 6).
4. **New theorem: no phantom roots outside $D$ below the slack (generic model).** If a word is a multiset top modulo codewords, every list member whose phantom degree $r$ satisfies $2r \le \deg w - k + 1$ is a multiset top: all roots of $w - c$ lie in $D$ (Theorem S). For an arbitrary word of degree $M \le T + (T - k + 1)/3$ all members have the same roots outside $D$, so the list is a twist of a multiset top list (Theorem S2). With note 12 Theorem A this gives the constant list bound $\max(1/(16\epsilon^2), 1 + (2\tau - \frac12)/\epsilon)$ at rate 1/2, $\tau = 5/8 + \epsilon$, for all words of degree at most $T + (T - k + 1)/3$ (phantom degree up to about $n/24$, against $n/96$ and a weaker bound in note 12 Theorem B). The thresholds are sharp or nearly so, and both statements are **false on the real-shape domain at small depth** ($n = 32$).
5. **Affine rank.** The list at $5n/8$ has affine rank exactly $4N - 7$ (bound proved, equality measured for $5 \le N \le 12$) and the list at $19n/32$ has rank $N^2 - 3N + 5$ (measured for $5 \le N \le 12$, exact on the whole list for $N \le 7$, bound $4(N-3)^2 + 1$ proved). Flats have rank $N - 1$ and the padded hyperplanes in the window at most $N + 1$. So the rank is polynomial in $N$ on every family that can be enumerated, as the lead suggested, but the rank route loses much more than the period route: $2^{186}$ against a true $2^{30.8}$ at $5n/8$, $2^{1340}$ against $2^{55.2}$ at $19n/32$ ($N = 25$).

## 1. The reduction to primitive lists

Everything in this section holds on every subspace domain of every field of characteristic two.

**Definition 1.1.** For a list of a level-$j$ problem, a triple of members is *bad* if its function $(P_l - P_i)/(P_j - P_i) \in F(Y)$ is constant or invariant under $Y \mapsto Y + a'$ for some $a' \in (D/U) \setminus 0$. A list is *primitive* if it has no bad triple (lists of at most two members are primitive). $f_j$ is the largest size of a primitive list of a level-$j$ problem with degree at most $\lfloor(k-1)/2^j\rfloor$ and slack at least $\Delta$, over all fields, domains, $U$ and words.

**Lemma 1.2 (supersaturation). [proved]** Let a list have $L$ members and no primitive sublist of $f + 1$ members, $L > f \ge 2$. Then there is a pair $(i, j)$ for which at least $(L - 2)/\binom{f+1}{3}$ of the other members $l$ make the triple $(i, j, l)$ bad.

*Proof.* Every $(f+1)$-subset contains a bad triple and a triple lies in $\binom{L-3}{f-2}$ of them, so there are at least $\binom{L}{f+1}/\binom{L-3}{f-2} = \binom L3/\binom{f+1}{3}$ bad triples. A triple contains three pairs and there are $\binom L2$ pairs, so some pair lies in at least $3\binom L3/(\binom L2\binom{f+1}{3})$ bad triples. $\square$

(De Caen's bound on Turán numbers of 3-graphs improves $\binom{f+1}{3}$ to about $\binom f2$. I do not use it.)

**Theorem 1.3 (reduction). [proved]** Let $0 \le j_0 \le J = \lceil\log_2 k\rceil$, $j_0 \le N - 1$, and suppose $f_j \le f$ for all $j < j_0$, with $f \ge 2$ and $G = \binom{f+1}{3}$. Then, with $\Lambda_{N,j}$ as in note 37,
$$\Lambda^\ast_N(k, T) \ \le\ G^{j_0}\, 2^{Nj_0 - j_0(j_0-1)/2}\,\bigl(\Lambda_{N,j_0}(\lfloor(k-1)/2^{j_0}\rfloor, \Delta) + 1\bigr), \qquad\text{and for } j_0 = J:\quad \Lambda^\ast_N(k, T) \le G^{J}\, 2^{NJ - J(J-1)/2}\Bigl(\frac n\Delta + 1\Bigr) .$$

*Proof.* Let $a_j = \Lambda_{N,j}(\lfloor(k-1)/2^j\rfloor, \Delta)$ and take a list of $L > f$ members at a level $j < j_0$. By Lemma 1.2 some pair $(P_0, P_1)$ has at least $(L - 2)/G$ bad thirds. A bad third lies in the set $M_{a'}$ of note 37 Proposition 3.3 for at least one $a' \ne 0$ (for all of them if its function is constant), and $|M_{a'}| \le a_{j+1}$ by that proposition, the slack being preserved. So $(L - 2)/G \le \sum_{a' \ne 0}(|M_{a'}| - 2) \le (2^{N-j} - 1)(a_{j+1} - 2)$, hence $a_j \le f + G\,2^{N-j}a_{j+1}$. Unrolling, $a_0 \le \sum_{i < j_0} f\,G^i 2^{Ni - i(i-1)/2} + G^{j_0}2^{Nj_0 - j_0(j_0-1)/2}a_{j_0}$. Consecutive terms of the sum grow by the factor $G\,2^{N-i} \ge 2$, so the sum is at most twice its last term, that is at most $2f/(G\,2^{N - j_0 + 1})$ times the coefficient of $a_{j_0}$, and $2f \le G\,2^{N-j_0+1}$ for $f \ge 2$, $j_0 \le N - 1$. (If $a_{j+1} \le 2$ the inequality says $L \le 2$, so $a_j \le f$.) At level $J$ the degree is $0$ and note 37 Lemma 3.2 gives $a_J \le n/\Delta$. $\square$

This is note 37 Theorem 3.5 with the hypothesis moved from "all but $B$ members are periodic for some pair" to "no large list is primitive". The second is weaker in what it asks of a list (one bad triple in every large sublist, instead of a pair that works for almost everything) and it is the form in which the data of sections 4 are stable.

**Lemma 1.4 (multi-valued words reduce to ordinary words). [proved]** Let $\mathcal P$ be a set of members of a level-$j$ problem ($|U| = m$, $D' = L_U(D)$, $n' = n/m$, degree $\le \kappa$, agreement $\ge A$, $A - m\kappa \ge \Delta = \sigma n$). Then there is an ordinary word $w$ on the subspace $D'$ such that at least $(\sigma/2)|\mathcal P|$ of the members of $\mathcal P$ agree with $w$ on at least $\kappa + \lceil\sigma n'/2\rceil$ points of $D'$. In particular, with $f^{\mathrm{ord}}(n', \kappa + 1, T')$ the largest primitive list of the ordinary problem on an $n'$-point subspace,
$$f_j \ \le\ \frac2\sigma\ f^{\mathrm{ord}}\bigl(n',\ \kappa + 1,\ \kappa + \lceil\sigma n'/2\rceil\bigr), \qquad \Lambda_{N,j}(\kappa, \Delta) \ \le\ \frac2\sigma\ \Lambda^\ast_{N-j}\bigl(\kappa + 1,\ \kappa + \lceil\sigma n'/2\rceil\bigr) .$$

*Proof.* For each $y \in D'$ choose $x_y$ uniformly in the coset $L_U^{-1}(y) \cap D$ and put $w(y) = v(x_y)$. For a member $P$, $\mathrm{agr}_w(P) = \#\{y : v(x_y) = P(y)\}$ has expectation $\mathrm{agr}_v(P)/m \ge \kappa + \sigma n'$ and is at most $n'$. With $a = \kappa + \lceil\sigma n'/2\rceil$, Markov's inequality for $n' - \mathrm{agr}_w$ gives $\Pr[\mathrm{agr}_w(P) \ge a] \ge (\kappa + \sigma n' - a + 1)/n' \ge \sigma/2$. So the expected number of members of $\mathcal P$ with $\mathrm{agr}_w \ge a$ is at least $(\sigma/2)|\mathcal P|$. A subset of a primitive list is primitive, and periods in $D/U$ are periods in $D'$. $\square$

So the levels are not harder than ordinary list problems in lower dimension, at half the slack and at every rate up to the original one. This is a reduction of the hypothesis only: it cannot be iterated to bound lists, because the slack halves each time.

**Corollary 1.5. [proved]** Suppose that for every $N' \le N$ with $2^{N'} \ge 4/\sigma$, every subspace domain of dimension $N'$, every $k' \le \rho 2^{N'} + 1$ and every $T' \ge k' - 1 + \sigma 2^{N'}/2$, primitive lists have at most $f$ members (hypothesis PL$_0(f)$; the restriction on $N'$ keeps the slack $T' - k' + 1$ at least 2). Then Theorem 1.3 applies with $f' = \lfloor 2f/\sigma\rfloor$ in place of $f$ and $j_0 = N + 1 - \lceil\log_2(4/\sigma)\rceil$, the first level whose quotient has fewer than $4/\sigma$ points, where $\Lambda_{N,j_0} \le \sigma^{-(\kappa + 1)}$ with $\kappa < 4\rho/\sigma$ (note 37 Lemma 3.2).

**Proposition 1.6 (equivalence with the bounded-index form). [proved]** For a class of lists closed under taking sublists, the following are equivalent up to the constants shown. (a) Every primitive list has at most $f$ members. (b) Every list with $L > f$ members has a pair $(i, j)$ such that at least $(L - 2)/\binom{f+1}{3}$ thirds are bad. (c) Every list with more than $f$ members has a pair with at least one bad third.

*Proof.* (a) to (b) is Lemma 1.2, (b) to (c) is trivial, and (c) says that a list with more than $f$ members is not primitive. $\square$

So question (iii) of the brief ("for $N > N_0(\tau)$ every large list has a pair with an inverse polynomial fraction of periodic thirds") is the statement $f \le \mathrm{poly}(n)$ for primitive lists, no more and no less.

**Remark 1.7 (the levels contain the small dimensions). [proved; the numbers checked]** A level-$j$ problem whose word is constant on the cosets of $U$ is the ordinary problem on the $(N - j)$-dimensional subspace $L_U(D)$, which is generic if $D$ is. Hence $B^{\mathrm{diff}}_{N,j}(\kappa, \Delta) \ge B^{\mathrm{diff}}_{N-j,0}$ and $f_j \ge f^{\mathrm{ord}}$ at the corresponding parameters, for every $N$. With section 4: for $(k, T) = (n/2 + 1, 19n/32)$, $B^{\mathrm{diff}}_{N,N-5}(16, 3n/32) \ge 13568$ and $f_{N-5} \ge 133$ for every $N \ge 5$. So DP$(B)$ of note 37 is false for $B < 13568$ at that point, in every dimension, and the constants of both hypotheses are at least the largest values reached in small dimension. Since $B$ enters note 37 Theorem 3.5 additively and $f$ enters Theorem 1.3 through $\binom{f+1}{3}^J$, this costs $14$ bits in the first and about $18J$ bits in the second.

**Numbers. [proved; evaluated]** $N = 25$, $k = 2^{24}$, $\tau = 0.64$ ($\sigma = 0.14$, $J = 24$): $2^{466}$ for $f = 7$, $2^{553}$ for $f = 16$, $2^{773}$ for $f = 133$. Through Corollary 1.5 with $f = 133$ ($f' = 1899$, $j_0 = 21$): $2^{970}$. In the accounting of the brief these are between 14 and 28 extra points.

## 2. No phantom roots outside $D$ below the slack

In this section $D$ is generic. For $f : D \to \mathbb{Z}$ put $\Psi_f(Z) = \prod_{s \in D}(1 + \ell_s Z)^{f(s)} \in \mathbb{F}_2[b][[Z]]$, where $\ell_s = \sum_i s_i b_i$ is the element $s$ written in the basis. The coefficient of $Z^j$ is homogeneous of degree $j$ in $b$. $\Psi_{f+g} = \Psi_f\Psi_g$, the point $0$ does not contribute, and for a multiset $\mu$, $\Psi_\mu$ is the reversed polynomial of $Z_\mu$. $\partial_i = \partial/\partial b_i$ acts on $K$ and coefficientwise on $K((Z))$, and extends uniquely to every separable algebraic extension of $K$. $E = \sum_i b_i\partial_i$ is the Euler derivation. An element of $K$ is *graded of degree $j$* if it is a quotient of homogeneous polynomials whose degrees differ by $j$ (then $E c = jc$), and $A = \sum_j a_j Z^j \in K[Z]$ is graded if $a_0 = 1$ and each $a_j$ is graded of degree $j$.

**Theorem W. [quoted, note 02, with its converse]** If $f : D \to \mathbb{Z}$ has $\sum f = 0$ and $\hat f(\varphi) \in 2^m\mathbb{Z}$ for all $\varphi$, then $\Psi_f \equiv 1 \pmod{Z^{2^{m-1}}}$, and conversely on a generic domain.

**Lemma 2.1 (the square property). [proved]** For $f : D \to \mathbb{Z}$ and every $i$, the series $v_{f,i} := Z^{-1}\partial_i\Psi_f/\Psi_f = \sum_s f(s)s_i/(1 + \ell_sZ)$ satisfies: its even part (in $Z$) equals its square.

*Proof.* $1/(1 + \ell Z) = (1 + \ell Z)/(1 + \ell^2Z^2)$ has even part $1/(1 + \ell^2Z^2) = (1/(1 + \ell Z))^2$. The coefficients $f(s)s_i$ are integers read modulo 2, the square of a sum is the sum of the squares, and $t^2 \equiv t \pmod 2$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (squares are doubles). [proved]** If $\Psi_f \equiv C^2 \pmod{Z^{m'}}$ for some $C \in K[[Z]]$, then there is $g : D \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $\Psi_f \equiv \Psi_g^2 \pmod{Z^{m'}}$.

*Proof.* $\partial_i(C^2) = 0$, so $v_{f,i} \equiv 0 \pmod{Z^{m'-1}}$: $\sum_s f(s)s_i\ell_s^d = 0$ for $0 \le d \le m' - 2$ and all $i$. Expand $\ell_s^d = \prod_{a \in \mathrm{bits}(d)}\sum_k s_kb_k^{2^a}$. For a map $\kappa$ from the binary digits of $d$ to $\{1, \dots, N\}$ the coefficient of $\prod_a b_{\kappa(a)}^{2^a}$ is $N_A(f) := \sum_{s \supseteq A}f(s) \bmod 2$ with $A = \{i\} \cup \mathrm{Im}\,\kappa$, and distinct $\kappa$ give distinct monomials. So $N_A(f) = 0$ for every $A$ with $1 \le |A| \le w + 1$, where $w$ is the largest binary weight of a $d \le m' - 2$. If $2^\mu \le m' - 1 < 2^{\mu+1}$ then $d = 2^\mu - 1$ is allowed, so $w \ge \mu$ and $2^{w+1} \ge m'$. Changing $f(0)$, which does not change $\Psi_f$, we may assume $N_\emptyset(f) = 0$. Then $f \bmod 2$ is orthogonal to all monomials of degree at most $w + 1$, so it lies in $\mathrm{RM}(N - w - 2, N)$ and is a sum modulo 2 of indicators of flats $\{x : x_i = 1,\ i \in A\}$ of dimension at least $w + 2$. Let $F$ be the same sum over $\mathbb{Z}$: $\hat F \in 2^{w+2}\mathbb{Z}$, and after changing $F(0)$ by a multiple of $2^{w+2}$, $\sum F = 0$. By Theorem W, $\Psi_F \equiv 1 \pmod{Z^{2^{w+1}}}$. Finally $f - F = 2g$ and $\Psi_f = \Psi_F\Psi_g^2$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.3 (rational truncations of $\Psi_f$ split). [proved]** Let $D \subset F$ be generic, $f : D \to \mathbb{Z}$, and $R \in F(Z)$ a rational function with $R(0) = 1$ whose numerator and denominator in lowest terms have degrees $\alpha$ and $\beta$. If
$$R \equiv \Psi_f \pmod{Z^{m'}} \qquad\text{and}\qquad m' \ge 2(\alpha + \beta),$$
then $R = \Psi_e$ for a function $e : D \setminus 0 \to \mathbb{Z}$: all zeros and poles of $R$ are of the form $1/s$ with $s \in D \setminus 0$.

*Proof.* Induction on $\alpha + \beta$, the case $0$ being trivial. Let $\alpha + \beta \ge 1$, so $m' \ge \alpha + \beta + 1$.

*Step 1, $R$ is defined over $K$ and graded.* The conditions "$A' - \Psi_fB' \equiv 0 \pmod{Z^{m'}}$, $\deg A' \le \alpha$, $\deg B' \le \beta$" are linear with coefficients in $K$ and have a nonzero solution over $F$, hence over $K$, and any two solutions with $B' \ne 0$ give the same fraction (the cross difference has degree $\le \alpha + \beta < m'$ and vanishes modulo $Z^{m'}$). So $R = A/B$ with $A, B \in K[Z]$ coprime, $A(0) = B(0) = 1$, of degrees $\alpha, \beta$. Let $\lambda$ be an indeterminate and $\sigma_\lambda$ the automorphism $b_i \mapsto \lambda b_i$ of $K(\lambda)$ over $\mathbb{F}_2(\lambda)$. It maps $\Psi_f(Z)$ to $\Psi_f(\lambda Z)$, so $\sigma_\lambda(A)(Z)/\sigma_\lambda(B)(Z)$ and $A(\lambda Z)/B(\lambda Z)$ both solve the conditions for $\Psi_f(\lambda Z)$, and by uniqueness and normalization $\sigma_\lambda(A)(Z) = A(\lambda Z)$: $A$ and $B$ are graded. The same argument applied to an irreducible factor $p$ of $A$ or $B$ over $K$, normalized by $p(0) = 1$ ($\sigma_\lambda(p)(Z)$ is one of the factors $p'(\lambda Z)$, and $\lambda = 1$ gives $p' = p$), shows that every irreducible factor is graded.

*Step 2, partial fractions.* Write $A/B = \prod_p p^{e_p}$ with $p$ irreducible, $e_p \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus 0$, and $p(Z) = p_s(Z^{q})$ with $q = q_p$ a power of two and $p_s$ separable, $p_s(Y) = \prod_\omega(1 + \omega Y)$ over the separable closure. Then
$$v := Z^{-1}\frac{\partial_iR}{R} = \sum_p e_p\sum_\omega\frac{Z^{q-1}\,\partial_i\omega}{1 + \omega Z^{q}} \ \equiv\ v_{f,i} \pmod{Z^{m'-1}} .$$
A term with $q \ge 2$ is odd in $Z$, and for $q = 1$ the even part of $\partial_i\omega/(1 + \omega Z)$ is $\partial_i\omega/(1 + \omega Z)^2$. So, the $e_p$ counting modulo 2,
$$v_{\mathrm{even}} - v^2 = \sum_{q_p = 1,\ e_p\ \mathrm{odd}}\ \sum_\omega\frac{\partial_i\omega + (\partial_i\omega)^2}{(1 + \omega Z)^2} \ +\ \sum_{q_p \ge 2,\ e_p\ \mathrm{odd}}\ \sum_\omega\frac{Z^{2q-2}(\partial_i\omega)^2}{(1 + \omega Z^{q})^2} .$$
Every term is a proper fraction in $Z^2$ and the common denominator $\prod_{e_p\ \mathrm{odd}}p^2$ has degree at most $2(\alpha + \beta)$, so the numerator has degree at most $2(\alpha + \beta) - 2 < m' - 1$. By Lemma 2.1 the left side vanishes modulo $Z^{m'-1}$, so the numerator is zero. The terms have pairwise disjoint sets of poles, so each vanishes: for all $i$, $\partial_i\omega \in \mathbb{F}_2$ if $q_p = 1$, and $\partial_i\omega = 0$ if $q_p \ge 2$ (always for $e_p$ odd).

*Step 3, separable factors of odd exponent are points of $D$.* Let $q_p = 1$, $e_p$ odd, $d = \deg p$ and $\tilde p(X) = X^dp(1/X) = \sum_j c_jX^{d-j}$, with roots $\omega$. $p$ is graded, so $Ec_j = jc_j$, and applying $E$ to $\tilde p(\omega) = 0$ gives $\sum_j jc_j\omega^{d-j} + \tilde p'(\omega)E\omega = 0$, while $\omega\tilde p'(\omega) = \sum_j(d - j)c_j\omega^{d-j} = \sum_j jc_j\omega^{d-j}$ in characteristic two. As $\tilde p'(\omega) \ne 0$: $E\omega = \omega$. Now $x = \omega - \sum_i(\partial_i\omega)b_i$ has $\partial_jx = 0$ for all $j$ because the $\partial_i\omega$ are constants, so $Ex = 0$, and also $Ex = E\omega - \sum_i(\partial_i\omega)b_i = x$. Hence $x = 0$ and $\omega = \sum_i(\partial_i\omega)b_i \in D$, and $p = 1 + \ell_sZ$.

*Step 4, no inseparable factor of odd exponent.* Let $q_p \ge 2$ and $e_p$ odd. $b_1, \dots, b_N$ is a 2-basis of the separable extension $K(\omega)$, so $\partial_i\omega = 0$ for all $i$ gives $\omega = \theta^2$ with $\theta \in K(\omega)$, compatibly with conjugation. Then $p(Z) = \prod_\omega(1 + \theta Z^{q/2})^2$ is a square in $K[Z]$, which contradicts irreducibility.

*Step 5, descent.* So $A = A_1A_2^2$ and $B = B_1B_2^2$ with $A_1 = \Psi_{1_{O_A}}$, $B_1 = \Psi_{1_{O_B}}$ for disjoint sets $O_A, O_B \subseteq D \setminus 0$, and $A_2, B_2 \in K[Z]$ coprime with $\deg A_2 + \deg B_2 \le (\alpha + \beta)/2$. For $f' = f - 1_{O_A} + 1_{O_B}$, $\Psi_{f'} \equiv (A_2/B_2)^2 \pmod{Z^{m'}}$. By Lemma 2.2, $\Psi_{f'} \equiv \Psi_g^2$, and square roots with constant term 1 are unique, so $\Psi_g \equiv A_2/B_2 \pmod{Z^{\lceil m'/2\rceil}}$ with $\lceil m'/2\rceil \ge \alpha + \beta \ge 2(\deg A_2 + \deg B_2)$. By induction $A_2/B_2 = \Psi_{e'}$, and $R = \Psi_e$ with $e = 1_{O_A} - 1_{O_B} + 2e'$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.4 (Theorem S, one side). [proved]** Let $\mu_0$ be a multiset on a generic $D$ of mass $M$ and $P \in F[X]$ monic of degree $M$ with $\deg(P - Z_{\mu_0}) < k$. Let $S$ be the set of roots of $P$ in $D$ and $r = M - |S|$. If
$$M - k + 1 \ \ge\ 2r, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad |S| \ \ge\ \frac{M + k - 1}{2}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad r \le |S| - k + 1,$$
then $P = Z_\mu$ for a multiset $\mu$ on $D$. In words: if the word is a multiset top modulo codewords, every list member whose phantom degree is at most its own slack is a multiset top, and the list at agreement $T \ge (M + k - 1)/2$ is a class of Theorem W.

*Proof.* With $h = P/Z_S$, the reversed polynomials satisfy $\mathrm{rev}(h) = \mathrm{rev}(P)/\Psi_{1_S} \equiv \Psi_{\mu_0 - 1_S} \pmod{Z^{M-k+1}}$, and $\mathrm{rev}(h)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $r$. Theorem 2.3 with $\beta = 0$: its roots are inverses of elements of $D$, and the roots of $h$ at $0$ are in $D$. $\square$

## 3. Multiset tops are periodic

**Proposition 3.1 (index of a multiset top triple). [proved]** Let $\mu_0, \mu_1, \mu_2$ be members of a multiset top list on a generic domain with $2^a \le M - k$, and $f_i = \mu_i - \mu_0$. Then $f_i$ is invariant under a subspace $K_i \le D$ of codimension at most $\nu_i = \lfloor n\lVert f_i\rVert^2/4^{a+2}\rfloor$, $\lVert f\rVert^2 = \sum_x f(x)^2$, and the triple function is invariant under $K_1 \cap K_2$: $|\Pi(\xi)| \ge n/2^{\nu_1 + \nu_2}$. For strict tops $\nu_i \le \nu := \lfloor 2n\min(T, n - T)/4^{a+2}\rfloor < \min(\tau, 1 - \tau)/(2(\tau - \rho)^2)$, so for $N > 2\nu$ a strict top list has no primitive triple.

*Proof.* By the converse of Theorem W, $\hat f_i \in 2^{a+2}\mathbb{Z}$, and Parseval gives at most $\nu_i$ nonzero coefficients. $f_i$ is invariant under their common kernel $K_i$, and so are its positive and negative parts $f_i^\pm$, so $Z_{f_i^\pm}$ are $K_i$-periodic polynomials. Finally $\xi = (Z_{\mu_2} - Z_{\mu_0})/(Z_{\mu_1} - Z_{\mu_0}) = (Z_{f_2^+}/Z_{f_2^-} - 1)/(Z_{f_1^+}/Z_{f_1^-} - 1)$. $\square$

With Corollary 2.5: for every word of degree at most $T + (T - k + 1)/3$ on a generic domain, the triple functions are those of a multiset top list, and they are periodic with bounded index whenever the multiplicities are bounded ($\lVert f\rVert^2 \le c\,n$). For multiset tops with multiplicities of order $n$ the proposition gives nothing, and in the windows it is not needed (Corollary 2.6 bounds the list). Below the windows, where lists grow, the family of section 4 shows the mechanism at full strength.

The exhaustive oracle finds exactly $16$ members at agreement $\ge 21$ ($15$ at $21$, one at $22$, as in note 18 section 4.1), all multiset tops $1_B + 1_{H_i}$. All $\binom{16}{3} = 560$ triples have a function with $|\Pi| \in \{4, 8\}$ (none is collinear), so $B(i, j) = 0$ for every pair, no primitive triple exists, and the affine rank is $5$. On the real shape the result is the same. For the lift of this word to $n = 64$ ($M = 46$, $k = 32$, $T = 42$) the list is again exactly these $16$ members **[proved, generic]**: by Corollary 2.4 all members are multisets $\mu = 1_{B'} + \nu$ with $\hat\nu \in 32\mathbb{Z}$, $\sum\nu = 32$, so $\nu = \frac12(1 + \sum_{\varphi \ne 0}c_\varphi\chi_\varphi)$ with integers $c_\varphi$, $\sum c_\varphi$ odd, $u = \sum c_\varphi^2$ odd and $\sum_x\nu^2 = 16(1 + u)$. The support condition says that $\nu = -1$ only on the $14$ points of $B'$ and that $\sum_{\nu \ge 2}(\nu - 1) \le 4$; with $\sum\nu = 32$ this gives $\sum\nu^2 = 32 + 2\,\#\{\nu = -1\} + \sum_{\nu \ge 2}\nu(\nu - 1) \le 32 + 28 + 20$, so $u \le 3$. For $u = 3$, $\nu = \frac12(1 \pm \chi_1 \pm \chi_2 \pm \chi_3)$ equals $2$ on at least $8$ points, or $-1$ on $16$ points, both excluded. So $u = 1$ and $\nu$ is the indicator of an affine hyperplane, which meets $B'$ in at most $4$ points only if it is a lift (the others contain exactly one point of each of the $7$ fibres). In general for $\mu_i = 1_B + 1_{H_i}$: $Z_{\mu_l} - Z_{\mu_i} = Z_B\cdot(\Lambda_{li} + \text{constant})$ with $\Lambda_{li}$ a linearized polynomial that vanishes on the common directions of $H_l$ and $H_i$, so every triple function is invariant under the common directions of its three hyperplanes and has $|\Pi| \ge n/8$ **[proved, every subspace domain]**.

(b) $11/16$. The lift to $n = 32$ of the three parallel planes of $(16, 8, 11)$ (`xb 0 fcffff00`, $k = 16$): exactly $3$ members at agreement $22$ and no other down to $21$, the triple function has $|\Pi| = 8 = n/4$.

So at the two window points where lists are known at two sizes, the period-free part is $0$ and no primitive triple exists. This is what Corollary 2.6 and Proposition 3.1 predict for words of low degree, and it says nothing about words of high phantom degree.

## 5. Affine rank

The affine rank of a list is the dimension of the span of the differences of its members over $F$. By note 04 Theorem 1.2 a list of rank $r$ has at most $\prod_{i \le r}(n - k + i)/(T - k + i)$ members.

**Proposition 5.1 (the two families). [bounds proved; values checked]** (a) The $56n - 392$ members at $5n/8$ of Proposition 4.1 have affine rank at most $4N - 7$, with equality for $5 \le N \le 12$. (b) The whole list at $19n/32$ has affine rank at most $4(N - 3)^2 + 1$, and exactly $N^2 - 3N + 5$ for $5 \le N \le 12$.

*Proof of the bounds.* (a) A member is $w - V(L_{W'})$ for a hyperplane $W'$ of $W$ (or $W$ itself) and a polynomial $V$ of degree $10$ with $V - (Y^2 + \eta Y)^5$ of degree $\le 8$ (note 02 Theorem 5), where $L_W = L_{W'}^2 + \eta L_{W'}$. A polynomial of degree $\le 8$ in $Y = L_{W'}$ is $A(L_W) + B(L_W)Y$ with $\deg A \le 4$, $\deg B \le 3$, and $L_{W'}$ lies in the span $\mathcal{L}$ of $X^{2^i}$, $i \le N - 4$. So all differences lie in $\mathrm{span}\{L_W^i\}_{i \le 4} + \sum_{i \le 3}L_W^i\mathcal{L}$, of dimension at most $5 + 4(N - 3)$. (b) A member of type (b) is $w - V(L_\Pi)$ with $\Pi \le W$ of index 4 and $L_W = L_\Pi^4 + \alpha L_\Pi^2 + \beta L_\Pi$. Its difference with a $W$-invariant member has degree $\le 16$ in $Y = L_\Pi$, so it is $\sum_{a \le 3}A_a(L_W)Y^a$ with $\deg A_0 \le 4$ and $\deg A_a \le 3$, and $Y, Y^2$ lie in spaces of linearized polynomials of dimension $N - 4$, $Y^3$ in the span of the $X^{2^i + 2^j}$, of dimension at most $(N - 4)^2$: at most $5 + 4(2(N - 4) + (N - 4)^2) = 4(N - 3)^2 + 1$. $\square$

**[checked]** `39_family N 1 rank` computes the rank of the whole list: $13$ and $15$ at $n = 32$, $17$ and $23$ at $n = 64$, $21$ and $33$ at $n = 128$ (simple members, all members), out of $17$, $33$, $65$ possible. `39_rank N 1` builds random members as lifts $V(L_\Pi)$ of the $15736$ members of the 32-point problem until $400$ consecutive samples add nothing:

**Lemma 5.2 (rank of a bounded-index family). [proved, every subspace domain]** Let $c_0$ be a codeword, $\varrho$ a polynomial, and suppose that every member of a list is $c_l = c_0 + \varrho\,Q_l(L_{\Pi_l})$ with $\deg Q_l \le q$ and $\Pi_l \le D$ a subspace. Then the affine rank is at most the number of exponents $\sum_{a \in \mathrm{bits}(j)}2^{a + i_a}$, $j \le q$, $0 \le i_a \le N$, which is at most $(q + 1)(N + 1)^{\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor + 1}$.

*Proof.* $L_\Pi$ is a combination of $X^{2^i}$, and $L_\Pi^j = \prod_{a \in \mathrm{bits}(j)}(L_\Pi)^{2^a}$. $\square$

This is the structural reason behind the lead's observation: the members of a difference descent with one pair, a common $\varrho$ and periods of index at most $M_0$ have rank polynomial in $N$, with exponent the number of binary digits of $\rho M_0$. The two families show that the truth is smaller (relations such as $L_W = L_{W'}^2 + \eta L_{W'}$ cut the exponent to the codimension of $\Pi_l$ in a fixed $W$), and that the exponent grows when the agreement goes down: $1$ at $5n/8$ and for the flats (rank $N - 1$: `39_oracle 5 8 13 1 rand pow 15 analyse 1 1` gives $4$ at $N = 5$), $2$ at $19n/32$.

**Assessment of the rank route. [proved arithmetic; the hypothesis conjectured]** For the families, rank $r$ gives through note 04 Theorem 1.2 the bounds $4^{4N-7} = 2^{186}$ at $5n/8$ (true size $2^{30.8}$) and $(16/3)^{N^2-3N+5} = 2^{1340}$ at $19n/32$ (true size $2^{55.2}$), at $N = 25$. Inside the windows the only lists known have rank at most $N + 1$ (padded hyperplanes: $Z_B\cdot(L_H + c)$ lies in $Z_B\cdot\mathrm{span}(1, X^{2^i})$) or at most $2$, and at most $278$ members at $\tau = 0.64$ if the degree is low (Corollary 2.6), so a rank bound $r \le N^2/2$ there is consistent with everything and proved for nothing beyond the words of Corollary 2.6. I found no list of large rank: every list I could enumerate at $n \le 128$ has rank far below both $k$ and its size. I also found no proof that $r + 1$ affinely independent members force a lower agreement in a way that goes beyond the generic-points count: on a subspace the flats have $N - 1$ independent members at the Johnson radius of rate 1/4, and the family at $5n/8$ has $4N - 6$ at the generic threshold for four members, so any such statement has to start below these. The rank route and the period route use the same structure (members are polynomials of bounded degree in linearized polynomials), and the period route counts it more economically.

## 6. Status of DP, the sharpened conjecture, and answers to the brief

**DP, in both directions.**

- *Refuted in the naive form.* "At every level all but $B$ members have a periodic triple function for some pair, with $B$ small or polynomial in $N$" is false at $(\rho, \tau) = (1/2, 19/32)$: $B \ge 13568$ at level $N - 5$ for every $N$ (Remark 1.7, the exact minimum of section 4.2). The constant is set by the base index. This does not hurt the bound of note 37 Theorem 3.5, where $B$ is additive.
- *Proved in a class.* For every word of degree at most $T + (T - k + 1)/3$ on a generic domain the list is a twisted multiset top list (Corollary 2.5); in the windows it has at most $\max(1/(16\epsilon^2), \dots)$ members (Corollary 2.6), and for the family of section 4, which grows like $n^2$ below the window, $B = 0$ for all $n \ge 64$ and every primitive sublist has at most $14$ members for $n \ge 64$ and $2$ for $n \ge 1024$ (Corollaries 4.2, 4.3).
- *Open for all words.* Nothing here constrains a word of phantom degree above $(T - k + 1)/3$ that is not a twist of a top.

**Conjecture PL (primitive lists). [conjectured]** For every rate $\rho$ and slack fraction $\sigma > 0$ there is $f(\rho, \sigma)$ such that on every subspace domain, in every dimension, at every rate up to $\rho$, every list at slack fraction at least $\sigma$ with more than $f$ members contains three members whose triple function is constant or has a nonzero period. By Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.5, PL implies H$_K(\tau, b)$ with $2^b = \binom{f'+1}{3}^{j_0}2^{Nj_0 - j_0(j_0-1)/2}(\sigma^{-4\rho/\sigma - 1} + 1)$, $f' = 2f(\rho, \sigma/2)/\sigma$: of the order $2^{N^2/2 + O(N\log f)}$.

Evidence for: Corollary 2.5 with Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 2.6 (all words of low degree, generic); the family of section 4 at five sizes; the window points of section 4.4; no counterexample. Evidence that it is delicate: $f(1/2, 3/32) \ge 133$, nineteen times the generic-points bound, so $f$ is not the generalized Singleton bound, and at half the slack (which Corollary 1.5 needs) the base indices are larger and $f$ is certainly larger; the value $133$ comes from a greedy search and is only a lower bound. I do not consider PL better supported than Conjecture G of note 12; it is a weaker statement in the windows (bounded lists imply it with $f$ the list bound) and it has the advantage of being the exact hypothesis that the dimension induction needs.

**Answers to the brief.**

1. *The decisive experiment.* Section 4.2: list $15736$, $103544$, $508536$; period-free part for the best pair $13568$ (exact, $424$ per point), $0$, $0$, and $0$ for every $n \ge 64$ by proof. The period-free part does not grow: it disappears at the second size. Inside the window ($21/32$ and $11/16$): lists $16$ and $3$ at both sizes, period-free part $0$. This supports DP in the bounded-index form and refutes nothing; it is a statement about top-type words, which is all that a structured enumeration can reach, and for those it is now a theorem.
2. *Primitive lists.* (i) Not bounded by the generic-points bound (Corollary 4.3: $133$ against $7$, $14$ against $6$, $10$ against $5$). Bounded by a function of $(\rho, \tau)$: proved for the words of Corollary 2.6 (where the whole list is bounded) and for the family of section 4; conjectured in general (PL). (ii) On every subspace domain: Theorem 1.3, Lemma 1.4 and Proposition 1.6 hold verbatim; Theorem 2.3 does not, with explicit failures on the real shape at $n = 32$ (section 2.8). (iii) The bounded-index form is equivalent to PL (Proposition 1.6).
3. *Affine rank.* Section 5: $4N - 7$, $N^2 - 3N + 5$, $N - 1$, at most $N + 1$. Polynomial in $N$ wherever measured, with a proved structural bound (Lemma 5.2), but weaker than the period route by hundreds of bits, and with no theorem in the windows.
4. *The list bound.* Unconditional, generic model: Corollary 2.6, at most $278$ codewords at $\tau = 0.64$, rate 1/2, any number of lanes, for words of degree at most $T + (T - k + 1)/3$. Conditional on PL: the numbers of section 1. For all words: no theorem and no refutation.

**What remains.** (1) PL for words of high phantom degree. The first test that is not covered by section 2 is a word of degree above $T + (T - k + 1)/3$ that is not a twist, at $n = 64$, where no enumeration exists. (2) The gap between $3r + 1$ and $4r$ in Theorem S2, and whether the one-sided threshold $2r \le M - k + 1$ extends to pairs after removing the common part (the twisted example of section 2.7 suggests it does). (3) A proof of the rank formula $N^2 - 3N + 5$, and of the conjecture that the list of $X^{n/2}L_U$, $|U| = n/2^j$, at its first non-simple agreement has rank of order $N^{j-2}$. (4) Any substitute for Theorem 2.3 on the real domain at depth of order $n$.
