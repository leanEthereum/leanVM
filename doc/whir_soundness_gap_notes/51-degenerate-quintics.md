(iv) The order of $\mathrm{Res}_Y(a, c)$ at $X = \alpha$ is at least the sum of the intersection numbers above $\alpha$. Hence $\sum_\alpha (o_\alpha^2 - o_\alpha - 1)_{o_\alpha \ge 2} \le D_c(W - \Delta) + D_aW - \Delta D_aD_c$. The right side is nondecreasing in $D_c$ for $W \ge (D_a + 1)\Delta$. All steps are correct **[proved]**.

**Theorem 4 of note 45, as I checked it, and its extension to every contact order [proved].** $a = (\mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B})^2 + T\mathcal{B}^2$ and $c = (\mathcal{C} + \zeta_\alpha\mathcal{B})^2 + S\mathcal{B}^2$; in each, the two terms have orders of different parity, so $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j-1}$ (contact at least $2j$) iff $\mathcal{B} \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{j-1}$ and $\mathcal{A}' := \mathcal{A} + \xi_\alpha\mathcal{B}$, $\mathcal{C}' := \mathcal{C} + \zeta_\alpha\mathcal{B}$ lie in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{j}$ (Proposition 1 of note 45). Adding constant multiples of the row of $\mathcal{B}$ to the two other rows of $\mathbf{E}$, and passing from the coefficients of $1, Y, Y^2$ to those of $1, S, S^2$ (a constant unimodular change of columns), does not change $\det\mathbf{E}$. A conic $q_0(T) + q_1(T)S + q_2(T)S^2$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}_\alpha^k$ iff $\mathrm{ord}_T\,q_i \ge k - i$. So the entries of the new matrix have orders at least $(j - i)_+$ in the rows of $\mathcal{A}', \mathcal{C}'$ and $(j - 1 - i)_+$ in the row of $\mathcal{B}$, column $i \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, and every term of the determinant has order at least $3j - 1 - (0 + 1 + 2) = 3j - 4$ (for $j = 2$ the entry of $\mathcal{B}$ in column 2 has order 0 instead of $-1$, which only helps). Each term of the determinant has degree at most $(3W - 8\Delta - 1)/2$ (the three parity classes of $\partial E$ do not cancel). So $\det\mathbf{E} = 0$ or $3W \ge 8\Delta + 1 + 2\sum_{j \ge 2} n_j(3j - 4)$.

### 2.2 Convexity

For a pair $(m, B)$ put $\Phi(W) = N(B, mA) - N(B - 1, mA - W)$ and $\psi(j) = R(m, B) - R(m - 2j, B - 1)$. Lemma C of note 35 ($b = 1$): if $\Delta(m + B) \le mA - W$ and $\Phi(W) > \sum_j n_j\psi(j)$ then $|L_G| \le \delta\lfloor (mA - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$.

**Lemma 2.0 [proved].** $\Phi$, defined for real $W$ by the same formula, is concave and nondecreasing. Hence for every linear function $\ell(x) = \mathrm{const} + \sum_j x_jw_j$ the function $g_{m,B,\ell}(x) = \sum_j x_j\psi(j) - \Phi(n\,\ell(x))/n$ is convex on the simplex, and if $g_{m,B,\ell} < 0$ at the vertices of a polytope $\mathcal{P}$ of profiles then every first integral whose profile lies in $\mathcal{P}$, which satisfies $W \ge n\ell(x)$ and $W \le mA - \Delta(m + B)$, is cut by Lemma C with $(m, B)$.

*Proof.* $N(B - 1, Z') = \sum_t (\min(t, B - 1) + 1)(Z' - \Delta t)_+$ is convex and nondecreasing in $Z'$. A convex function on a polytope is maximal at a vertex. For an integer profile in $\mathcal{P}$ and an integer $W \ge n\ell(x)$: $\Phi(W) \ge \Phi(n\ell(x)) > \sum_j n_j\psi(j)$. $\square$

This is Lemma U of note 41 for an arbitrary linear lower bound, and it needs neither the convexity of $j \mapsto R(m - 2j, B - 1)$ nor a reduction to two contact orders: the profile enters linearly.

## 3. The dependent sub-case

**Lemma 3.1 [proved].** Let $G = \partial E$ with $K = 2$ and $\mathcal{A} = \rho_1\mathcal{Q}$, $\mathcal{B} = \rho_2\mathcal{Q}$, $\rho_1, \rho_2 \in F[X]$ coprime, $\mathcal{Q} \in F[X][Y]$ of $Y$-degree 2. Then for every polynomial $P$ of degree at most $\Delta$ with agreement set $S_P$, either $\mathcal{Q}(X, P) = 0$ (at most 2 such $P$) or
$$W \ \ge\ 3\Delta + \sum_{\alpha \in S_P} j_\alpha .$$

*Proof.* $a = \rho\,\mathcal{Q}^2$ with $\rho = \rho_1^2 + X\rho_2^2 \ne 0$. Weighted degrees add, so $\deg\rho + 2\,\mathrm{wdeg}\,\mathcal{Q} \le W - \Delta$ and $\mathrm{wdeg}\,\mathcal{Q} \ge 2\Delta$: $\deg\rho \le W - 5\Delta$ and $\deg\mathcal{Q}(X, P) \le (W - \Delta - \deg\rho)/2$. At a line of contact $2j \ge 2$, $a \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^{2j - 1}$ (Lemma N) and orders add: with $k_\alpha = \mathrm{ord}_\alpha\rho$, $\mathrm{ord}_{p_\alpha}\mathcal{Q} \ge \lceil (2j_\alpha - 1 - k_\alpha)/2 \rceil = j_\alpha - \lceil k_\alpha/2 \rceil$. So $\sum_{S_P}(j_\alpha - \lceil k_\alpha/2 \rceil) \le (W - \Delta - \deg\rho)/2$, and $\sum_\alpha \lceil k_\alpha/2 \rceil \le \deg\rho$ gives $\sum_{S_P} j_\alpha \le (W - \Delta + \deg\rho)/2 \le W - 3\Delta$. $\square$

With contact 4 on all lines this is $W \ge 3\Delta + 2A = 2.913\,n$ (note 45 obtained "at most 2 members" in that case by a different route). The bound (D$_\lambda$) is the dual form, as for (L2).

## 4. The class $\mathcal{D}$: what can be proved about its sections

Throughout, $G = \partial E \in \mathcal{D}$ (only (D1), (D2) are used), $(M_1, M_2, M_0)$ is the primitive left kernel of $\mathbf{E}$, $\mu = \deg M_0$, $N_\psi = M_2^2 + XM_1^2$, $f_P = M_0^2P + N_\psi$, and for a polynomial $P$ of degree at most $\Delta$ with agreement set $S$: $S^c$ the agreements on contained lines, and $r_P = E_Y(X, P)/\prod_{\alpha \in S^c}(X - \alpha)^{2j_\alpha - 1}$, a polynomial of degree at most $W - \Delta - \sum_{S^c}(2j_\alpha - 1)$. By note 48, $P$ is a section iff $f_P\,E_Y(X, P)$ is a square; the orders $2j_\alpha - 1$ are odd, so this says $f_P = \Pi_P\,g_P^2$ with $\Pi_P$ the squarefree part of $\Lambda_{S^c}r_P$. I use $x = \sqrt X$, $L = F(x)$, $K = F(X)$, and $\mathsf{D} = d/dx$ on $L$, whose kernel is $K$; in characteristic 2, $\mathsf{D}(uv) = u\mathsf{D}v + v\mathsf{D}u$ is the Wronskian.

**Proposition 4.1 (the square-root form) [proved].** Put $\Omega = \mathcal{A} + x\mathcal{B} = \Omega_0 + \Omega_1Y + \Omega_2Y^2 \in F[x][Y]$ and $s = (M_2 + xM_1)/M_0 \in L$. Then $E_Y = \Omega^2$, $\tilde\psi = s^2$, $\mathcal{B}_k = \mathsf{D}\Omega_k$, $\mathcal{C}_k = \mathsf{D}(s\Omega_k)$, and a polynomial $P = p(x)^2$ is a section iff $(p - s)\,\Omega(x, P) \in F(x^2)$. Moreover $E_Y$ has multiplicity at least 3 at every received point iff $\lambda_D \mid \Omega_1$ and $\lambda_D^3 \mid \Omega_0 + \Omega_1\psi_y + \Omega_2\psi_y^2$ in $F[x]$, where $\lambda_D = \prod_{\alpha \in D}(x - \sqrt\alpha)$ and $\psi_y$ interpolates the word; equivalently the syndrome series $\sigma = \psi_y/\Lambda_D \in F((1/X))$ of the word satisfies $\Omega_2\lambda_D\,\sigma^2 + (\Omega_1/\lambda_D)\,\sigma \in F[x] + O(x^{-(3n - W + \Delta)})$.

*Proof.* $M_0\mathcal{C}_k = M_1\mathcal{A}_k + M_2\mathcal{B}_k$ is the $x$-odd part of $(M_2 + xM_1)\Omega_k$. $(P - \tilde\psi)E_Y(X, P) = ((p - s)\Omega(x, P))^2$ and $L^2 = K$. The last statement is Lemma 6.0 of note 45 for $a = \Omega^2$ (every element of $F[X]$ is a square in $F[x]$), divided by $\lambda_D^3$, using $\deg_x\Omega_0 \le W - \Delta$. $\square$

So the existence of the factor $E_Y$ alone is a Hermite and Padé condition on the syndromes: about $3n - W + \Delta \approx 0.865\,n$ conditions on the $2W - 8\Delta - n \approx 0.27\,n$ coefficients of $(\Omega_2, \Omega_1/\lambda_D)$ in the window.

**Proposition 4.2 (injectivity and distance) [proved].** (i) $\gcd(g_P, M_0) = 1$ for every $P$. (ii) If $P \ne P'$ and $\Pi_P = \Pi_{P'}$ then $\deg\Pi_P \le \Delta$; so on the sections with $|S^c| - \deg r_P > \Delta$ (all list members in the hard core, where this number is at least $4A + \Delta - W$) the map $P \mapsto \Pi_P$ is injective. (iii) Let $M_0^\star$ be the largest divisor of $M_0$ prime to $N_\psi$, $\mu^\star$ its degree, and $p_P = \deg f_P - 2\mu$. For two sections $P \ne P'$ with $\Pi_P \ne \Pi_{P'}$:
$$|S^c \,\triangle\, S'^c| \ \ge\ 2\mu^\star + 1 + \big(\max(p_P, p_{P'}) - \Delta\big)_+ - \deg r_P - \deg r_{P'} .$$

*Proof.* Write $P = \gamma^2 + X\eta^2$, $U = M_0\gamma + M_2$, $V = M_0\eta + M_1$, so $f_P = U^2 + XV^2$ and, with $\Pi_P = \lambda_0^2 + X\lambda_1^2$ (coprime $\lambda_0, \lambda_1$ since $\Pi_P$ is squarefree), $(U, V) = g_P(\lambda_0, \lambda_1)$. (i) A common prime of $g_P$ and $M_0$ divides $U$ and $V$, hence $M_2$ and $M_1$. (ii) If $\Pi_P = \Pi_{P'}$ then $(g_P + g_{P'})(\lambda_0, \lambda_1) = M_0(\gamma + \gamma', \eta + \eta')$, so $M_0 \mid g_P + g_{P'}$ and $P + P' = t^2\Pi_P$. (iii) Let $d = P + P'$. Then $f_{P'} = f_P + M_0^2d$ and, derivatives of squares being zero, $(g_Pg_{P'})^2(\Pi_P\Pi_{P'})' = (f_Pf_{P'})' = M_0^2(f_Pd)'$. Let $\kappa = \gcd(\Pi_P, \Pi_{P'})$ and $\Theta = \Pi_P\Pi_{P'}/\kappa^2$, squarefree and not constant, so $\Theta' \ne 0$ and $(g_Pg_{P'}\kappa)^2\Theta' = M_0^2(f_Pd)'$. By (i), $M_0^2 \mid \kappa^2\Theta'$; a prime of $M_0^\star$ does not divide $f_P \equiv N_\psi \pmod{M_0^2}$, hence not $\kappa$; so $(M_0^\star)^2 \mid \Theta'$. Degrees: $\deg\Theta' \le \deg f_P + \Delta - 1 + 2\mu - 2\deg(g_Pg_{P'}\kappa) = \deg\Theta - 1 - (p_{P'} - \Delta)$, and symmetrically. So $\deg\Theta \ge 2\mu^\star + 1 + (\max p - \Delta)_+$. Finally $\Theta$ is the squarefree part of $\Lambda_{S^c \triangle S'^c}\,r_Pr_{P'}$. $\square$

When $\deg N_\psi > 2\mu + \Delta$, $p_P = \deg N_\psi - 2\mu =: p$ is the pole order of $\tilde\psi$ at infinity, the same for all $P$. In all cases $p_P \ge |S^c| - \deg r_P - 2\mu$, so the right side of (iii) is at least $|S^c| + 1 - \Delta - 2(\mu - \mu^\star) - 2\deg r_P - \deg r_{P'}$: in the hard core, with $M_0$ prime to $N_\psi$, at least $A - \Delta - 3(W - \Delta - 3A) \ge 0.159\,n$. This is the statement that the symmetric differences of agreement sets lie in a binary Goppa code with a divisor of degree about $0.19\,n$ supported on the poles of $\tilde\psi$ (the lead's formulation), with the factors $r_P$ accounted for.

**Proposition 4.3 (large divisor implies a bounded list) [proved].** Let $S_1, \dots, S_L \subseteq D$ with $|S_i| \ge A > n/2$ and $|S_i \triangle S_j| \ge d_0$ for $i \ne j$. If $d_0n > 2A(n - A)$ then $L \le d_0n/(d_0n - 2A(n - A))$. Consequently, for $G \in \mathcal{D}$ with contact 4 on all lines and $W < 2.6352\,n$ at $a = 0.7065$: if $2\mu^\star + (p - \Delta)_+ \ge n/2$ then $|L_G| \le 9$, and more generally $|L_G|$ is bounded by a constant as soon as $2\mu^\star + (p - \Delta)_+ \ge 0.447\,n$.

*Proof.* With $c_\alpha$ the number of $i$ with $\alpha \in S_i$: $\binom L2 d_0 \le \sum_\alpha c_\alpha(L - c_\alpha) \le n\,\bar c(L - \bar c)$, and $\bar c \ge LA/n > L/2$, so the right side is at most $L^2A(n - A)/n$. In the hard core all list members are sections (Proposition 11 of note 40), $S^c = S$, $\deg r \le W - \Delta - 3A < 0.0158\,n$, $2A(n - A)/n = 0.41474\,n$, and $d_0 \ge 0.5\,n - 0.0315\,n$ gives $L \le 0.4685/0.0538 < 9$. $\square$

**Where the known families sit.** 11/16 family: $\tilde\psi = X^{3n/4 - 1}$, $\mu = 0$, $p - \Delta = n/4$. 27/40 family: $\tilde\psi = X^{3n/4 - j}$, $\mu = 0$, $p - \Delta \approx n/5$. Both are below the range of Proposition 4.3, and both carry no list at all (note 45). I have no upper or lower bound on $2\mu^\star + (p - \Delta)_+$ for a member of $\mathcal{D}$ beyond $2\mu + (p - \Delta)_+ \ge 4A - W \approx 0.19\,n$ (needed for one section to exist) and $2\mu + (p - \Delta)_+ \le 2W - 8\Delta \approx 1.27\,n$.

**Proposition 4.4 (pairs are free, triples are not) [proved].** For any two polynomials $p_1, p_2 \in F[x]$ and any $\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \in F[x]$ that are not $K$-proportional there is exactly one $s \in L$ with $(p_i - s)\lambda_i \in K$, namely $s = p_1 + \lambda_1\,\mathsf{D}((p_1 + p_2)\lambda_2)/\mathsf{D}(\lambda_1\lambda_2)$. So the condition "$f_P\Lambda_Sr_P$ is a square" alone never excludes two list members: for every pair of codewords with agreement sets $S_1, S_2$ there is a $\tilde\psi = s^2$ for which both satisfy it with $r = 1$, with $\mu \le (|S_1 \triangle S_2| - 1)/2$. A third member imposes $\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}} \mathsf{D}(p_i\lambda_i)\,\mathsf{D}(\lambda_j\lambda_k) = 0$.

*Proof.* $L$ is a plane over $K$ and $s \mapsto \mathsf{D}(s\lambda_i)$ are two independent $K$-linear forms; the condition is $\mathsf{D}(s\lambda_i) = \mathsf{D}(p_i\lambda_i)$. $\square$

So any proof for $\mathcal{D}$ has to use the existence of the conics $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ of small weighted degree (Proposition 4.1), not only the working form; or accept a bound that depends on the domain through the Goppa code.

## 6. The exact remaining case

At $n = 2^{25}$ to $2^{28}$, rate 1/2, $a = 0.7065$ (216 queries against 217 at the Johnson radius), an unconditional bound $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le 19578$ on every evaluation set in characteristic 2 follows from Theorem 2.1 and one of:

- **(R1$'$)** a bound $|L_G| \le 19002$ (any bound within the budget would do, with the constant of the theorem changed accordingly) for $G \in \mathcal{D}$: a polynomial count of the $P$ of degree at most $\Delta$ with at least $A$ agreements and $(p - s)\,\Omega(x, P) \in F(x^2)$, where $\Omega$ is a conic over $F[x]$ of weighted degree below $2.14\,n$ (at most $2.27\,n$ in the outer window of (D3)) vanishing to order 3 at the received points on more than $0.91\,n$ of them; or
- the non-existence of a member of $\mathcal{D}$ satisfying (D4).

Known about (R1$'$): Proposition 4.2 (distance at least about $0.16\,n$ between agreement sets, injectivity), Proposition 4.3 (closed when $2\mu^\star + (p - \Delta)_+ \ge 0.447\,n$), Proposition 4.4 (two members cannot be excluded by the square condition alone), Proposition 4.1 (the Hermite and Padé condition on the syndromes). Not known: any member of $\mathcal{D}$ at all; the two families of note 45 miss the window by $0.065\,n$ and $0.115\,n$ and carry no list.

Everything else is closed for every contact profile: $b \ge 2$ (note 50), and for $b = 1$: $K = 0$, the cubic, $D_c \in \{4, 5, 6\}$ with $K \le 2$, non-degenerate quintics, dependent quintics (this note). Dependencies that are not refereed: Lemma B of note 40 and Theorem 4 of note 45 (re-read here), Corollary F of note 48, Lemma 3.1 and the covering of this note.

**Routes I tried on (R1) that gave nothing.** The Frobenius descent $E \equiv \Phi^2$, $\Phi = (y - s)\Omega(x, y^2)$: the problem for $\Phi$ on $\sqrt D$ is the Frobenius twist of the same problem, no gain. Mason and Stothers on $\lambda_{T_1}^3\varrho_1 + \lambda_{T_2}^3\varrho_2 = \delta^2\kappa$ (the difference of $\Omega(x, P_1)$ and $\Omega(x, P_2)$): margin about $0.2\,n$. The identity $\mathfrak{B}(P_1, P_2)(P_1 + P_2) = \mathsf{D}(\Omega(x, P_1)\Omega(x, P_2))$ for the involution of the pencil $\langle\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}\rangle$: it holds for all pairs of polynomials and adds only $M_0 \mid \mathsf{D}(\lambda_1\lambda_2)$, which is Proposition 4.2. Counting the cofactors $\lambda$ of (D4): they are Guruswami and Sudan polynomials of multiplicity $m - 4q$ and weighted degree below $mA - qW$, which exist for generic points up to $W \approx 2.65\,n$ at $(q, m) = (2, 17)$, just above the window; no contradiction. A lower bound on the Goppa divisor from the weighted degree: the degree bounds on $\mathcal{C}$ force the pencil to degenerate at infinity when $p > \Delta$, which gives an upper bound $2\mu + (p - \Delta) \le 2W - 8\Delta$, the wrong direction.
