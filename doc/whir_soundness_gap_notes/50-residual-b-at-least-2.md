## 0. Verdict

**The class $b \ge 2$ is empty for every agreement $A \ge 0.700839\,n$, on every evaluation set, for every word and every contact profile, at $n = 2^{25}, 2^{26}, 2^{27}, 2^{28}$ [proved; the finite inequalities are exact integer computations].** In particular it is empty on the whole range $[0.7045, 0.7071)\,n$ where certificates with $B = 2$ exist, which is the lead's question. The tool is new and is not a refinement of Lemma C: an explainer that is a polynomial in $Y_1^q$, $q$ a power of 2.

**Theorem 1.3 (parity), short form.** In characteristic 2 the polynomials $R = R_0(X, Y_0) + R_q(X, Y_0)Y_1^q$ have only $m^2 - (q - 1)m + q(3q - 1)/2$ contact conditions of order $m$ per point (in characteristic 3 the support $\{1, Y_1^2\}$ has $m^2 + 2$), because $w^q = S^q + T^qY_1^q$ is itself of this shape. So such an $R \ne 0$ with contact $m$ at all received points and weighted degree below $mA$ exists as soon as $N_0(mA) + N_0(mA - q\Delta) > n\,(m^2 - (q - 1)m + q(3q - 1)/2)$, and it vanishes on the jet of every list member. An absolutely irreducible first integral with $b \ge 1$ divides a polynomial in $Y_1^q$ with a multiplicity divisible by $q$ (Lemma 1.1), so it divides $R$ only if $qb \le q$. Hence, with $d = \lfloor (mA - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$: every absolutely irreducible first integral with $b \ge 2$ carries at most $\delta d$ list members, and
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \ \le\ d^2 + |L_{G^\star}|,$$
where $G^\star = \partial E^\star$ is **one** absolutely irreducible first integral with $b = 1$, present only if $R = \lambda\,(\hat G^\star)^q$ with $\lambda \in F[X, Y_0]$.

## 1. The parity theorem

### 1.1 Multiplicities in a polynomial in $Y_1^q$

**Lemma 1.1 [proved].** Let $F$ have characteristic 2, $q = 2^e \ge 2$, $R \in F[X, Y_0][Y_1^q]$ nonzero, and $\hat G \in F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$ irreducible with $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G \ne 0$. If $\hat G^\mu$ is the exact power of $\hat G$ dividing $R$, then $q \mid \mu$.

*Proof.* Induction on $e$, the case $q = 1$ being empty. Put $q' = q/2$; by induction $\mu = q'\nu$. Write $R = \hat G^\mu R'$ with $\hat G \nmid R'$, and $U = Y_1^{q'}$. Then $\hat G^{q'} = \Phi(U)$ with $\Phi = \sum_c \hat G_c^{\,q'}U^c$, and $R = \varrho(U)$ with $\varrho \in F[X, Y_0][U^2]$. $R' = \varrho/\Phi^\nu$ lies in the fraction field of $F[X, Y_0][U]$ and is integral over this integrally closed ring, so $R' \in F[X, Y_0][U]$. Since $\partial_U\varrho = 0$: $\nu\,\Phi_U R' = \Phi\,\partial_U R'$, so $\hat G^{q'}$ divides $\nu\,\Phi_U(Y_1^{q'})\,R'$, and $\Phi_U(Y_1^{q'}) = \sum_c c\,\hat G_c^{\,q'}Y_1^{(c-1)q'} = (\partial_{Y_1}\hat G)^{q'}$. If $\nu$ were odd, $\hat G^{q'}$ would divide $(\partial_{Y_1}\hat G)^{q'}R'$; $\hat G$ is irreducible and does not divide the nonzero polynomial $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G$ of smaller $Y_1$-degree, so $\hat G \mid R'$, a contradiction. $\square$

The lemma applies to every absolutely irreducible first integral with $b \ge 1$: $\hat G$ is irreducible in $F[X, Y_0, Y_1] = F[X][u, v]$ (absolute irreducibility over $F(\tau)$ passes to $F(X)$, and a factor $X - \beta$ of $\hat G$ would give the factor $\tau - \beta^2$ of $G$), and $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G = X\,\widehat{G_u} + \widehat{G_v}$ vanishes only if $G \in F[\tau][u^2, v^2]$, which is a square over $\overline{F(\tau)}$. It does not apply to the squares $S^2$ of note 41 (there $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G = 0$), as it must not: $S^2$ divides $S^q$ with multiplicity $q/2$.

### 1.2 The local rank on even supports

Let $\mathcal{V}_{q,k} = \bigoplus_{i \le k} F[X, Y_0]\,Y_1^{iq}$.

**Lemma 1.2 [proved; checked by exact linear algebra over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$, `50_parity.py A`: $k = 1$, $q = 2$ ($4 \le m \le 12$), $q = 4$ ($8 \le m \le 14$), $q = 8$ ($m = 16, 17, 19$); $k = 2$, $q = 2$ ($8 \le m \le 12$), $q = 4$ ($m = 16, 17$)].** In characteristic 2, for $q = 2^e \ge 2$, the image of $\mathcal{V}_{q,k}$ in $F[X, Y_0, Y_1]/\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ has dimension
$$\rho_1(q, m) = m^2 - (q - 1)m + \frac{q(3q - 1)}2 \quad (k = 1,\ m \ge 2q), \qquad \rho_2(q, m) = \frac{3m^2 - (6q - 3)m + 13q^2 - 3q}2 \quad (k = 2,\ m \ge 4q).$$

*Proof.* Grade $F[T, S, Y_1]$ by the degree in $(T, S)$. The ideal $\mathfrak{c}_m$ is spanned by the homogeneous elements $T^iw^lY_1^e$, $i + 2l \ge m$, and its part of degree $N < m$ is $w^{m - N}F[T, S, Y_1]_{2N - m}$ (the conditions $i + l = N$, $i + 2l \ge m$ say $l \ge m - N$); for $N \ge m$ it is everything of degree $N$. So the dimension is $\sum_{N < m}\big[(k + 1)(N + 1) - \dim\{\Phi \in \mathcal{V}_{q,k} \text{ of degree } N,\ w^{m - N} \mid \Phi\}\big]$. Let $V = Y_1^q$ and $\varpi = w^q = S^q + T^qV$, which is linear in $V$. A form $\Phi(V)$ of $V$-degree $\le k$ over $F[T, S]$, viewed as a polynomial in $Y_1$ over $F(T, S)$, has at $Y_1 = S/T$ a root of multiplicity $q\,i$, where $\varpi^i$ is the exact power of $\varpi$ dividing $\Phi$ in $F(T, S)[V]$ (if $\Phi = \varpi^i\Phi'$ and $\Phi'(S^q/T^q) \ne 0$ then $w \nmid \Phi'(Y_1^q)$). So $w^{m - N}$ divides $\Phi$ iff $\varpi^{\lceil (m - N)/q \rceil}$ does, and by Gauss the quotient has coefficients in $F[T, S]$, forms of degree $N - q\lceil (m - N)/q\rceil$. For $k = 1$: dimension $(N - q + 1)_+$ for $m - q \le N < m$ and 0 below, so $\rho_1 = m(m + 1) - \sum_{t = m - 2q + 1}^{m - q} t$. For $k = 2$: $2(N - q + 1)$ for $m - q \le N < m$, $N - 2q + 1$ for $m - 2q \le N < m - q$, 0 below. $\square$

In characteristic $p \ne 2$ the same computation for the support $\{1, Y_1^2\}$ gives $m^2 + 2$ (**[checked]** over $\mathrm{GF}(3)$, $4 \le m \le 10$): $w^2$ is not a polynomial in $Y_1^2$. The saving $m - 3$ per point (and $(q - 1)m$ for larger $q$) is what makes the certificates below exist under the Johnson radius; it is the only place where characteristic 2 helps in this whole theory.

### 1.3 The theorem

For $q = 2^e \ge 2$, $k \in \{1, 2\}$ and $m \ge 2kq$ let $\mathcal{S}(q, k; m)$ be the span of the monomials $X^xY_0^aY_1^{iq}$, $i \le k$, $x + \Delta(a + iq) < mA$; it has $\sum_{i \le k} N_0(mA - iq\Delta)$ elements. Call $(q, k; m)$ an *even certificate* at agreement $A$ if
$$\sum_{i=0}^{k} N_0(mA - iq\Delta) \ >\ n\,\rho_k(q, m). \tag{1}$$
The left side is nondecreasing in $A$, so a certificate at $A$ is one at every larger $A$.

**Theorem 1.3 [proved].** Let $F$ have characteristic 2, $D \subset F$ any $n$ points, $y$ any word, and $(q, k; m)$ an even certificate at $A$. Put $d = \lfloor (mA - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$. Then there is a nonzero $R \in \mathcal{S}(q, k; m)$ with $R(X, P, P') = 0$ for every $P \in \mathrm{List}(y, A)$, and for every such $R$:

1. every absolutely irreducible first integral $G$ with $\deg_{Y_1}\hat G = b \ge 1$ and $\hat G \nmid R$ has $|L_G| \le \delta d$; if $\hat G \mid R$ then $\hat G^q \mid R$, so $b \le k$;
2. in particular every absolutely irreducible first integral with $b > k$ has $|L_G| \le \delta d$;
3. $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le d^2 + \sum_i |L_{G_i}|$, the sum over the absolutely irreducible first integrals $G_i$ with $b_i \ge 1$ such that $\hat G_i^{\,q\nu_i}$ exactly divides $R$; they satisfy $\sum_i \nu_ib_i \le k$. For $k = 1$ there is at most one, it has $b = 1$, and $R = \lambda\,\hat G^q$ with $\lambda \in F[X, Y_0]$.

*Proof.* The conditions $R \in \mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$, $\alpha \in D$, have rank at most $n\rho_k(q, m)$ on $\mathcal{V}_{q,k}$ (Lemma 1.2; translation by $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ preserves $\mathcal{V}_{q,k}$), so by (1) a nonzero solution $R$ exists in $\mathcal{S}(q, k; m)$. For $P \in \mathrm{List}(y, A)$ the polynomial $R(X, P, P')$ has degree below $mA$ and a zero of order $m$ at each of its at least $A$ agreements (Lemma 0 of note 35), so it is zero. Write $R = R_{(0)} + XR_{(1)}$ with $R_{(i)} \in F[\tau, u, v]$, of $(u, v)$-degree at most $d$; the section of a list member is a common zero of $R_{(0)}$ and $R_{(1)}$ (Proposition 3 of note 35). (1): if $\hat G \nmid R$ then $G$ does not divide one of the $R_{(i)}$ and Bezout over $F(\tau)$ gives $|L_G| \le \delta d$; if $\hat G \mid R$, Lemma 1.1 gives $q \mid \mu$, and $\mu b \le \deg_{Y_1}R \le kq$. (2) follows. (3): let $H = \gcd(R_{(0)}, R_{(1)})$ (the nonzero one if the other vanishes), $h = \deg_{u,v}H$. Off $H$ there are at most $(d - h)^2$ sections; an irreducible factor of $H$ with $b = 0$ has at most $\delta$ sections, one that is not absolutely irreducible at most $\delta^2/4$ (Lemma R of note 41), and $(d - h)^2 + \sum \delta_i^2 \le d^2$; the remaining factors are the $G_i$, with $q \mid \mu_i$ and $\sum \mu_ib_i \le kq$. $\square$

No hypothesis on the contact profile, on the degree, on fixedness or on non-degeneracy is used, and no second interpolation: the statement is about every first integral, and the constant is $d^2$.

### 1.5 Remarks

**Why $b = 1$ survives.** For $G = \partial E = c + aY_1$, $\hat G^q = c^q + a^qY_1^q \in \mathcal{V}_{q,1}$. So a potential whose first integral is cheap enough ($qW + \mathrm{wdeg}\,\lambda < mA$ with $\lambda$ a Guruswami and Sudan polynomial of multiplicity $m - 2qj_\alpha$, which exists for every word as soon as its efficiency exceeds $\sqrt{\Delta/n}$) produces even explainers, and Theorem 1.3 cannot see through it. This is as it must be: at rate 1/4 the flat cubic is such a component (Section 1.6), and note 48's class (R1) is not excluded by any count I could make (the cofactor spaces are far larger than the margins of (1)).

**Slack supports [proved; thresholds exact, `50_parity.py C`].** The first form in which I found the theorem: if the monomials with odd $c$ of an explainer $Q$ of $Y_1$-degree 3 satisfy $x + \Delta(a + c) < (m - 1)A + \Delta$, then $\partial_{Y_1}Q = Q_1 + Q_3Y_1^2$ has degree below $(m - 1)A$ on jets and contact order $m - 1$ ($\partial_{Y_1}\mathfrak{c}_m \subseteq \mathfrak{c}_{m-1}$), so it vanishes on all list jets and is a polynomial in $Y_1^2$; if it is zero, $Q$ itself is one. This support has a certificate ($> nR(m, 3)$ monomials) from $A = 0.705688\,n$ ($m = 38$); the analogous support of $Y_1$-degree 4 with slack 2 on the coefficient of $Y_1^2$ from $0.705432\,n$; of $Y_1$-degree 2 from $0.706802\,n$, and there the alternative is sharper: either some explainer has $Q_1 \ne 0$ and then the whole list has at most $\deg_{Y_0}Q_1$ members, or every explainer of that space is a polynomial in $Y_1^2$. Full slack (all coefficients, as in the large-characteristic treatment of singular solutions) exists only above the Johnson radius (for $Y_1$-degree 3 and $m \le 1500$: from $A = 23726599$ at $n = 2^{25}$, where the Johnson boundary is $23726569$). The even supports of Section 1.3 are simpler and reach further, so I use them.

**The classes $b > k$ for larger $k$.** Polynomials in $Y_1^q$ of degree $kq$ kill $b > k$; I computed only $k \le 2$.

**Other rates.** Theorem 1.3 and Lemmas 1.1, 1.2 do not depend on the rate. I did not compute the thresholds at rates 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 **[not done]**. Where a certificate with $k = 1$ exists, every large list must lie on one $b = 1$ component; a referee should test this against the Gold family and the $\mathbb{F}_4$ family of note 43, which I did not do.

## 2. Local structure of a first integral along a line

This is the structure theory asked for in the brief. In jet coordinates the lines of a word are the fibres of $(X, Y_0, Y_1) \mapsto (X, Y_0)$ over the received points, $\Sigma_G$ is a $b$-sheeted cover of the $(X, Y_0)$-plane, and in the plane $\tau = \tau_0$ of the $(\tau, u, v)$-space $b$ is the degree of $G$ on the lines of direction $(\sqrt{\tau_0}, 1)$, that is $\delta$ minus the multiplicity of the plane curve at the point at infinity $[\sqrt{\tau_0} : 1 : 0]$ through which all lines of all words in that plane pass. The first integrals with $\deg_{Y_1}\hat G \le b$ are $G = \sum_{c \le b} v^cG_{0,c}(\tau, q) + u\sum_{c < b} v^cG_{1,c}(\tau, q)$, $q = u^2 + \tau v^2 = Y_0^2$, a free $F[\tau, q]$-module of rank $2b + 1$, with $\hat G_c = G_{0,c} + Y_0G_{1,c} + XG_{1,c-1}$. Equivalently $\partial_X\hat G_c = \partial_{Y_0}\hat G_{c-1}$: the forms $\hat G_{c-1}dX + \hat G_c\,dY_0$ are closed; for $b = 1$ the single form is exact ($dE$), for $b = 2$ the two forms are $dE_1 + \mathcal{W}^2\,Y_0\,dY_0$ and $dE_2 + \mathcal{T}^2\,X\,dX$ with $E_1 = X\mathcal{P}^2 + Y_0\mathcal{R}^2 + XY_0\mathcal{T}^2$, $E_2 = X\mathcal{R}^2 + Y_0\mathcal{S}^2 + XY_0\mathcal{W}^2$, where $\mathcal{P}^2, \mathcal{R}^2, \mathcal{S}^2, \mathcal{T}^2, \mathcal{W}^2$ are the five coefficients called $p, r, s, t, w$ in note 45, Section 5 (perfect $F$; here $w$ is kept for the contact form). For $b = 2$ and $\hat G_1 \ne 0$ the cover is the Artin and Schreier cover $z^2 + z = \hat G_0\hat G_2/\hat G_1^2$, $Y_1 = z\hat G_1/\hat G_2$, branched along $\hat G_1 = 0$. Over a perfect field every first integral is the Frobenius twist of an arbitrary polynomial $\Gamma(X, \gamma, \eta)$, $u = \gamma^2$, $v = \eta^2$, and a section is a zero $(\gamma(X), \eta(X))$ of $\Gamma$ with $P = \gamma^2 + X\eta^2$; this is the square root normal form of note 45 for every $b$, and it carries no information beyond the normal form above.

**Lemma 2.1 (supports; any characteristic) [proved; checked `50_local.py B`: $p \in \{2, 3, 10007\}$, $m \le 9$, $b \le 5$, 378 spaces each, no violation, and (ii), (iii) attained for all 54 pairs $(m, b)$].** Let $Q = \sum_{c \le b} Q_cY_1^c \in \mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$. Every monomial $T^rS^q$ of $Q_c(\alpha + T, y_\alpha + S)$ satisfies (i) $r + q \ge m - b$; (ii) $2r + q \ge m - (b - c)$; (iii) $r + 2q \ge m - c$.

*Proof.* Grade by the $(T, S)$-degree; $w$ has degree 1 and the part of degree $N < m$ of $Q$ is $w^{m - N}R_N$ with $\deg_{Y_1}R_N \le b - (m - N)$ (degrees in $Y_1$ add). This gives (i). The coefficient of $Y_1^c$ in $w^kR_N$, $k = m - N$, is $\sum_i \binom ki(-T)^iS^{k - i}[R_N]_{c - i}$ with $c - b + k \le i \le c$; so $r \ge k - (b - c)$ and $q \ge k - c$, with $r + q = m - k$. $\square$

(i) is Lemma N of note 40; (ii) for $c = b$ is Lemma F of note 48, which I had not seen when I proved it; (iii) is its mirror, and for $q = 0$ it says that $Q$ restricted to $Y_0 = y_\alpha$ lies in $(T, Y_1)^m$, a special case of Lemma 2.5 below.

**Proposition 2.2 (graded structure, characteristic 2) [proved; checked `50_local.py A`: dimensions for all $b \le 5$, $j \le 6$, $N \le 2j + 1$ over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$].** A first integral $G$ with $\deg_{Y_1}\hat G \le b$ lies in $I_\alpha^j$ iff for every $N$ its part of $(T, S)$-degree $N$ is
$$\hat G^{(N)} = (S^2 + T^2Y_1^2)^{e}K_N \ \ (N \text{ even},\ e = (j - N/2)_+), \qquad \hat G^{(N)} = (S + TY_1)(S^2 + T^2Y_1^2)^{e}K_N \ \ (N \text{ odd},\ e = (j - (N+1)/2)_+),$$
with $K_N$ a form in $(T^2, S^2)$ with coefficients in $F[Y_1]$, of $Y_1$-degree at most $b - 2e$ (even $N$), $b - 1 - 2e$ (odd $N$).

*Proof.* First integrals are $F[T^2, w, Y_1] = A \oplus wA$, $A = F[T^2, S^2, Y_1]$ (because $w^2 = S^2 + T^2Y_1^2$), so parts of even degree lie in $A$ and parts of odd degree in $wA$; $G \in I_\alpha^j$ iff $w^{(2j - N)_+}$ divides the part of degree $N$; degrees in $Y_1$ add. $\square$

**Corollary 2.3 (orders of the coefficients) [proved; checked on every basis solution of every lattice of Section 5, 3640 solutions in 40 runs].** At a received point of contact $2j \ge 2b$ the coefficient $\hat G_c$ has multiplicity at least $2j - b + \epsilon_b(c)$ with $\epsilon_1 = (0, 0)$, $\epsilon_2 = (0, 1, 0)$, $\epsilon_3 = (0, 0, 0, 0)$, $\epsilon_4 = (0, 1, 1, 1, 0)$, $\epsilon_5 = (0, 0, 2, 2, 0, 0)$. For $b = 2$, $j \ge 2$: $\hat G_2 = T^2K + Tk_1 + \dots$, $\hat G_1 = Tk_0 + Sk_1 + \dots$, $\hat G_0 = S^2K + Sk_0 + \dots$ with $K$ a form of degree $j - 2$ and $k_0, k_1$ forms of degree $j - 1$ in $(T^2, S^2)$. In terms of the square roots: contact 4 with $b = 2$ is $\mathcal{P} = \mathcal{R} = \mathcal{S} = \mathcal{T} = \mathcal{W} = 0$, $\nabla\mathcal{R} = \xi_\alpha\nabla\mathcal{T} + \zeta_\alpha\nabla\mathcal{W}$, $\nabla\mathcal{P} = \zeta_\alpha\nabla\mathcal{T} + (0, \kappa)$, $\nabla\mathcal{S} = \xi_\alpha\nabla\mathcal{W} + (\kappa, 0)$ at $p_\alpha$: ten conditions.

**Corollary 2.4 (number of conditions) [proved; the rank of the conditions equals this number in every lattice computed, except for words with dependent conditions].** Contact $\ge 2j$ on one line imposes on the first integrals of $Y_1$-degree $\le b$ exactly $\kappa(b, j) = \sum_{N < 2j}[\dim\{\text{all}\} - \dim\{\text{in } I^j\}]$ conditions: $(3j^2 + j)/2$ for $b = 1$; $3, 10, 22, 39, 61, 88$ for $b = 2$, $j = 1, \dots, 6$; $4, 13, 28, 50, 79, 115$ for $b = 3$; $5, 16, 34, 60$ for $b = 4$; $6, 19, 40, 70$ for $b = 5$. The lattice of solutions of weighted degree at most $W$ has rank equal to the number of blocks $\tau^iq^kv^c$, $\tau^iq^kuv^c$ of base weight at most $W$, and its successive minima satisfy $\sum W_i = \sum w_i + 2\sum_\alpha \kappa(b, j_\alpha)$ (the conservation law of note 45; rank 13 and $\sum = 36\Delta + 5 + 20n$ for $b \le 2$, $(u, v)$-degree $\le 5$, contact 4: confirmed).

**Lemma 2.5 (translation) [proved; checked `50_local.py C`].** If $Q \in \mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ and $P(\alpha) = y_\alpha$, then $Q(X, P, P' + Y_1) \in (T, Y_1)^m$. Hence $\partial^{[c]}_{Y_1}Q(X, P, P')$ (Hasse derivative) vanishes to order $m - c$ at $\alpha$, and its degree is at most $W(Q) - c\Delta$.

*Proof.* $w(X, P, P' + Y_1) = [P - y_\alpha - TP'] - TY_1 \in (T^2, TY_1)$ by identity (1) of note 35. $\square$

## 3. Lower bounds on $W$

**Theorem 3.1 ((L1), Lemma L for singular lines) [proved; coincides with Corollary F part 1 of note 48; checked on every lattice solution].** Let $G$ be a first integral with $b \ge 0$ and $d = \deg_{Y_0}\hat G_b$ (even). Then $\prod_\alpha (X - \alpha)^{\epsilon_d(j_\alpha)}$ divides $\hat G_b$, $\epsilon_d(j) = \max(j - d/2,\ 2j - b - d,\ 0)$, and
$$W \ \ge\ (b + d)\Delta + \sum_\alpha \epsilon_d(j_\alpha). \tag{L1}$$
If $d \ge 2$ and $2\Delta \ge \#\{\alpha : j_\alpha \ge 2\}$, then $W \ge (b + 2)\Delta + \sum_\alpha (j_\alpha - 1)_+$.

*Proof.* Lemma 2.1 (i), (ii) with $c = b$ and $q \le d$ give $r \ge \epsilon_d(j_\alpha)$; the coefficient of $Y_0^d$ has degree at most $W - (b + d)\Delta$. The last sentence: $(j - d/2)_+ \ge (j - 1)_+ - (d/2 - 1)$ for $j \ge 2$. $\square$

For $d = 0$ this is Lemma L part 3 of note 35. It is the statement asked for in note 35, Section 8 item 1, and it is sharp (Section 5).

**Remark 3.2.** When $d \ge 2\max_\alpha j_\alpha$ there is no content and (L1) is $W \ge (b + d)\Delta$, which also follows from $\delta \ge b + d$. This full-fibre case ($K = j$ in note 48) is where the bounds below are needed.

**Theorem 3.3 ((L2), content and Sudan) [proved; Corollary F part 2 of note 48].** With $C = \prod_\alpha (X - \alpha)^{\epsilon_d(j_\alpha)}$: for $P$ of degree $\le \Delta$ with agreement set $S_P$, either $(\hat G_b/C)(X, P) = 0$ (at most $d$ polynomials) or $W \ge b\Delta + \sum_{\alpha \in S_P}\max(j_\alpha, 2j_\alpha - b) + \sum_{\alpha \notin S_P}\epsilon_d(j_\alpha)$.

*Proof.* $(\hat G_b/C)(X, P)$ has degree at most $W - b\Delta - \sum_\alpha \epsilon_d(j_\alpha)$ and vanishes at an agreement $\alpha$ to order $\max(j_\alpha, 2j_\alpha - b) - \epsilon_d(j_\alpha)$ (Lemma N of note 40 for $\hat G_b$, minus the order of $C$). $\square$

**Theorem 3.4 ((J), regular sections) [proved; checked on planted words, Section 5].** Let $G$ be an absolutely irreducible first integral with $b \ge 1$. All sections $P$ of degree $\le \Delta$, except at most $\delta(\delta - 1)$, satisfy
$$W \ \ge\ \Delta + \sum_{\alpha \in S_P}(2j_\alpha - 1)_+ . \tag{J}$$

*Proof.* Lemma 2.5 with $c = 1$: $q_1 = \partial_{Y_1}\hat G(X, P, P')$ has degree at most $W - \Delta$ and a zero of order $2j_\alpha - 1$ at every agreement. If $q_1 = 0$ the section is a common zero of $G$, $G_u$, $G_v$ ($\partial_{Y_1}\hat G = X\widehat{G_u} + \widehat{G_v}$); these are not both zero and have degree below $\delta$. $\square$

Corollary N of note 40 is the case $c = b$ of Lemma 2.5, $W \ge b\Delta + \sum_{S_P}(2j_\alpha - b)$; (J) is larger by $(b - 1)(|S_P| - \Delta)$ when all agreement lines have $2j_\alpha \ge b$. For contact $2j$ on all lines: $W \ge \Delta + (2j - 1)A$ instead of $b\Delta + (2j - b)A$. For $b = 2$ it is also the statement that the middle coefficient $\hat G_1$ has multiplicity $2j - 1$ (Corollary 2.3). It is attained by the flat cubic (agreement $n/2 - 1$) and nearly by the Wronskians of note 41 at rate 1/2 ($W = 86$ against $84$, $350$ against $345$).

**Corollary 3.5 [proved].** Let $G$ be an absolutely irreducible first integral with $b \ge 1$ and $|L_G| \ge \delta^2$. Then some list member on $G$ satisfies both (L2) and (J) (they fail for at most $d \le \delta - b$ and $\delta(\delta - 1)$ members). Both right sides only decrease when $S_P$ is replaced by a subset, and both weights are nondecreasing in $j$, so (L2) and (J) hold with $S_P$ replaced by the $A$ lines of smallest contact.

**Theorem 3.6 ((B2), Bezout for $b = 2$) [proved].** Let $G$ be absolutely irreducible with $b = 2$, $d_i = \deg_{Y_0}\hat G_i$, and suppose the primitive parts of $\hat G_1$ and $\hat G_2$ are coprime (call $G$ *non-degenerate*). With $\mu_1 = 2j_\alpha - 1$, $\mu_2 = \max(j_\alpha, 2j_\alpha - 2)$ on the contained lines:
$$\sum_\alpha \big[\min(\mu_1, d_1)\min(\mu_2, d_2) + d_1(\mu_2 - d_2)_+ + d_2(\mu_1 - d_1)_+\big] \ \le\ (d_1 + d_2)W - \Delta(2d_1 + d_2 + d_1d_2), \tag{B2}$$
and $W \ge (1 + d_1)\Delta + \sum_\alpha (\mu_1 - d_1)_+$. When neither coefficient has content at the received points: $\sum_\alpha (2j_\alpha - 1)(2j_\alpha - 2) \le (W - \Delta)(W - 2\Delta)/\Delta$.

*Proof.* Let $e_\alpha, f_\alpha$ be the orders at $\alpha$ of the contents of $\hat G_2, \hat G_1$. The primitive parts have multiplicities $\tilde\mu_2 \ge \mu_2 - e_\alpha$, $\tilde\mu_1 \ge \mu_1 - f_\alpha$ at $p_\alpha$ (Corollary 2.3), at most $d_2, d_1$ (a primitive polynomial is nonzero on the fibre), and weighted degrees at most $W - 2\Delta - \sum e_\alpha$, $W - \Delta - \sum f_\alpha$. The resultant in $Y_0$ vanishes at $\alpha$ to order at least $\tilde\mu_1\tilde\mu_2$ and has degree at most $d_1(W - 2\Delta - \sum e) + d_2(W - \Delta - \sum f) - \Delta d_1d_2$ (isobaric, as in Lemma B of note 40). So $\sum_\alpha h(e_\alpha, f_\alpha) \le (d_1 + d_2)W - \Delta(2d_1 + d_2 + d_1d_2)$ with $h(e, f) = (\mu_1 - f)_+(\mu_2 - e)_+ + d_1e + d_2f$, and on the admissible box $e \ge (\mu_2 - d_2)_+$, $f \ge (\mu_1 - d_1)_+$ the function $h$ is nondecreasing in each variable. $\square$

For contact $2j$ on all lines and no content (B2) gives $W \ge (0.75 + \sqrt{(2j - 1)(j - 1) + 1/16}\,)\,n$ at $\Delta = n/2$, of slope $\sqrt 2$ in $j$, above the slope $1.385$ of the cut of Lemma C; so it wins for large contact, and by convexity for mixed profiles.
