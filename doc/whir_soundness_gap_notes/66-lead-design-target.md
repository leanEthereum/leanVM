# 66. Design target after the user's ruling: DEEP fold over a sparsely mixed code with oracle-random windows (lead, 2026-11-21)

The user's goal: make the DEEP fold work with **no conjecture**, **as efficiently as possible**; a result counts only if it is a practical win (smaller proof, recursive verifier not slower, at equal or lower prover time, by a clear margin over the rate trade and over the proven re-tuning of note 08). Status of everything below: lead's plan and sketches, unrefereed. It replaces the cube window of note 57 as the design to prove.

## 1. Why not plain Reed-Solomon, why not the cube, why not local windows

- Plain Reed-Solomon on the real domain: no list bound below Johnson is provable with anything we have (notes 34 to 56).
- Mixed code with the cube window (note 57): provable in outline for the first rungs, but 25 partners a symbol and about 324 field products a query in the verifier; note 61's verdict is that it is a wash against halving the rate.
- Local or non-spanning XOR windows: broken (note 57, Section 10): plain Reed-Solomon lists on the quotient domain lift with free agreements.

## 2. The design to prove: windows from a sparse random graph

Each position $x$ has $d$ partners $\pi_1(x), \dots, \pi_d(x)$ and coefficients $\mu_1(x), \dots, \mu_d(x)$, all derived from the random oracle with a public seed; the committed symbol is $c(P)(x) = P(x) + \sum_{l \le d} \mu_l(x)\,P(\pi_l(x))$, the same for all lanes. Target $d$ about 6 to 8 at rate 1/2. Two things change against the cube:

1. **Protection comes from expansion, and a random graph has much more of it per partner.** A free agreement set is $\mathrm{int}(Z) = \{x \in Z : \pi_l(x) \in Z\ \forall l\}$ for a zero set $Z$ of fewer than $k$ points. For a random $d$-out graph, a first moment over the graph (there are only $2^n$ sets $Z$, and for a fixed $Z$ of density $\varphi$ each point is interior with probability $\varphi^d$ independently) gives, with probability $1 - 2^{-\Omega(n)}$: every $Z$ of density at most $1/2$ has $|\mathrm{int}\,Z| \le \gamma |Z|$ with $\gamma$ the root of $D(\gamma \,\|\, 2^{-d}) = 2$ bits, about $0.5$ at $d = 6$, $0.35$ at $d = 8$, $0.22$ at $d = 12$ (lead's estimate; to be computed exactly, with the density dependence). The cube has $\gamma = 0.69$ at $N = 25$ with 25 partners.
2. **Windows are almost disjoint**, so deficiency clusters are single windows and the counting of note 57 simplifies: a pencil-deficient window costs $d$ conditions for a loss of one.

**Axioms** the proofs should be written against, so that they apply to the cube, to random graphs and to cheaper structured graphs alike: (W1) every window $W_x = \{x, \pi_1(x), \dots, \pi_d(x)\}$ has $d + 1$ distinct points; (W2, expansion) for every $Z \subseteq D$ with $|Z| \le \varphi_0 n$: $|\{x : W_x \subseteq Z\}| \le \gamma(\varphi_0)\,|Z|$; (W3, bounded overlap) every point lies in at most $\Delta$ windows.

## 3. What has to be proved

1. **Expansion lemma** for the oracle-random graph, with failure probability below $2^{-128}$ at $n = 2^{10}$ to $2^{28}$: exact $\gamma(d, \varphi)$. Combinatorics only.
2. **The ladder** $H_t$ (note 57, Section 8) under (W1) to (W3): with probability $1 - 2^{-128}$ over $\mu$, every $t$ members of every list satisfy $\sum_x (c_x - 1)_+ \le (t - 1)k + \varepsilon_t n$; constant lists above $g_t(\alpha) = (t - 1)\rho + \varepsilon_t$. Ingredients: (a) exact probability $q^{-\sum_x \mathrm{rank}_x}$; (b) free configurations: with $\gamma \le 1/2$ a crude bound already works for every $t$ at rate 1/2: ordering the members, $\sum_x (c^{\mathrm{free}}_x - 1) \le \sum_{j \ge 1} |\bigcup_{i < j} \mathrm{int}\,Z_{ij}| \le \gamma k\,(1 + 2 + 2(t - 3)) = \gamma (2t - 3) k \le (t - 1.5)k$; at lower rates $\gamma$ must be about $\rho$, so $d$ grows; (c) deficient windows: in the transversal range the count is exact by MDS and the lattice argument and each deficient window costs at least $d + 2 - u$ conditions at rank $u$; outside it the solution lattice is unbalanced, the tuples are polynomial combinations of $s < u$ short vectors, the cost is at least $(u - s)k$, and for $s = u - 1$ the cross product of the short vectors is a map of bounded degree that is constant on every deficient window, so the deficient windows lie in the interiors of its fibres, at most $\gamma n \le k$ of them by (W2): loss at most cost. The general $s$ is the open algebra (induction on the rank).
3. **Protocol D$'$** of note 61 (plain fold, window-weighted claims) with this code, and the list bound for lanes from the constant single-lane bound (the paper's Proposition 6.2 applies since the lists are below $2^{63}$).

## 4. Cost questions (for note 65)

- Prover: $d$ multiply-adds a symbol; the access pattern decides the real cost. Options: fully random partners (a cache miss a partner row); partners $x \oplus c$ with $c$ drawn per position from a small public pool of offsets; a product structure (far partners at block level shared by a block, streamed, plus local partners inside the block).
- Verifier and guest: a partner $x \oplus c$ with $c$ from a public pool costs about $\kappa$ additions and $2\kappa$ products (the constants $\widehat W_k(c)$ are public); an unrelated partner costs a full basis evaluation. So the pool design is the candidate: about 600 cycles a query at $d = 8$ against 735 for today's whole query, to be weighed against 30 to 40% fewer queries.
- The pool and product structures must still satisfy (W2); a pool that is too small or too structured recreates periodic sets with large interiors.

## 5. What note 60 (isoperimetry, arrived the same morning) settles

For the cube window the combinatorics is now exact (Harper), and it is **not enough**: the interior loss of sets of fewer than $k$ points is only $\beta = 0.31$ at rate 1/2 for $N = 25$ to 28 (0.45, 0.53, 0.60 at rates 1/4, 1/8, 1/16), while the bound on free configurations of $t$ members from pairwise coincidence plus interior loss needs $\beta > 1/3$: with the cube it is below $(t - 1)k$ for no $t$ at rate 1/2 (the three-set statement with a common factor is open for $N \ge 23$), for $t = 3$ only at rate 1/4, for $t \le 4$ at rates 1/8 and 1/16. Note 60 also proves that the free sum of $t$ members is at most $n\,\Phi\bigl((1 - \beta)\rho\binom t2\bigr)$ ($\Phi$ piecewise linear concave with $\Phi(\binom s2) = s - 1$), the optimum of the linear program with pairwise constraints and interior losses, so the threshold below which free configurations cannot be excluded by counting is $\sqrt{\rho(1 - \beta)}$. For stride windows no theorem is available.

Consequence: the window graph must lose **more** than the cube, and a random $d$-out graph does. With interior fraction $\gamma = 1 - \beta$ of about 0.37 at $d = 8$ for sets of density 1/2 (smaller for smaller sets), $\sqrt{\rho\gamma} \approx 0.43$ at rate 1/2, below capacity, so free configurations are excluded at every rung by note 60's bound; at rate 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 the same needs $\gamma \lesssim (\alpha_t^2/\rho)$, about 0.4 to 0.5 for $t = 8$, which $d = 8$ to 10 gives. So the cube is abandoned and Section 2's design is the one to prove.

## 6. Adversary's report (note 64, same morning): nothing breaks at toy size, and the first moment is confirmed exhaustively

Checked numerically, one researcher. (i) Local windows are broken as predicted: lifted quotient triples violate $H_3$ for every sampled $\mu$ with all agreements free (36 against $2k = 32$; 68 and 72 against 64). (ii) For the cube and for random windows with $d \ge 4$ no triple, quadruple or quintuple found violates $H_3$, $H_4$, $H_5$: random triples score the predicted $3n/q$, structured families score their free count plus $3n/q$, an adaptive adversary reaches $2k$ plus an excess that falls from 4 to 1 when $q$ is squared (the $1/q$ term, not a structure). (iii) **Exhaustive check of the first moment**: all $2.1 \cdot 10^9$ triples at $q = 16$, $N = 4$, $k = 4$, for 256 sampled $\mu$: the mean number of triples at each score matches the exact expectation within the standard error, and the probability of a violation is below the first-moment bound (0.098 against 0.279 at score 15). (iv) Free configurations for $t = 3, 4, 5$ are always $(t - 1)$ times the best interior of one set plus at most 3; nothing scales. (v) Random windows, largest interior fraction of a set of $k - 1$ points found at $N = 5$ to 8: about 0.88 at $d = 2$, 0.6 at $d = 4$, 0.4 at $d = 6$, 0.29 at $d = 8$ (lower bounds beyond $n = 32$; the first-moment ceilings are 1.0, 0.71, 0.50, 0.37): so $d = 6$ is borderline and $d = 8$ has room. Lifted flats triples keep only $|Z|(|Z|/n)^d$ free agreements. (vi) **Pooled offsets are a risk**: a hyperplane containing a fraction $f$ of the pool has interior at least $f^d$, so a pool equal to a basis gives 0.72 at $N = 25$, $d = 8$; measured at $N = 8$, $d = 8$: basis 0.43, pool of $2N$ offsets 0.48, of $4N$ offsets 0.34, all offsets 0.30. The pool must be large and unstructured.

## 7. First adversary's report (note 59, resumed and delivered late)

One researcher, exact toy computations and proofs for the cube window. (i) **The mixed code is statistically a random linear code at toy size**: all codewords and all candidate triples enumerated at $N = 4$, $q = 16$: the number of triples at each agreement matches the first moment and matches random linear codes (mean 259 against 233 predicted and 215 for random codes at agreement 11, $k = 6$; 1039 against 1010 and 1004 at agreement 12, $k = 8$). (ii) **The naive Key Lemma of note 57 is false much earlier than the erratum said**: for $L \ge 6$ at rate 1/2 and $L \ge 4$ at lower rates, by "grid" spaces $g \cdot \mathrm{span}(1, M_1, \dots, M_j, M_iM_{i'})$ built from subspace polynomials each changed by one group of cube directions, and the family is thick ($hV$ has the same deficiencies). So no version of the counting that charges the deficiency of the whole span $V$ at every position can work; the true rank of the span $V_x$ of the differences of the members covering $x$ must be used. These grid spaces are specific to the cube: their deficiency comes from a window step changing a single $M_i$, which random partners do not do. (iii) **Free configurations** are exactly the lists of the "window-folded" Reed-Solomon code (symbol at $x$ = restriction to $W_x$); pair counting plus convexity gives the free $t$-wise inequality for every $t$ as soon as the largest interior of a set of $k - 1$ points is at most $\rho^2 n$, that is $\gamma \le \rho$. For the cube at $N = 25$ this fails, and the open deterministic problem is narrowed to Reed-Solomon lists with ball-shaped agreement sets; for a random $d$-out graph $\gamma \le \rho$ holds at rate 1/2 from $d = 6$, at rate 1/4 from about $d = 10$, at rate 1/8 from about $d = 16$ (lead's numbers from Section 2's first moment), so the free part is closed at every rung by counting alone. (iv) One fixed public seed is right; the list statement for lanes must go through the generic reduction, not through a first moment for interleaved words. (v) Singleton plus pigeonhole show inequality (1) of note 57 cannot be improved for any linear code, so a full proof leaves 124 queries against 114 at rate 1/2.

**State of the proof under the design of Section 2** (random $d$-out windows, $d \ge 8$ at rate 1/2): exact probability [proved, checked exhaustively]; free configurations at every rung [note 60's linear program with $\gamma \le \rho$; to be written out under the axioms]; deficient windows at rank 2 [lattice argument, being written by researcher 62]; deficient windows at rank 3 and above [open: researcher 63; the gap is the rigid case, note to 63].

## 8. The practical verdict (note 65) and the first rung (note 62), both arrived the same morning

**Note 65 (one researcher; bytes exact in note 61's model, prover and recursion estimated from measured parts): no version of the window-mixed code beats the proven knobs by a useful margin, even with everything proved.** Best case (oracle-random graph, ladder to rung 8, 7 partners): opening $-26\%$ at rate 1/2 for $+13$ to $+23\%$ of proving time, recursion about $+2\%$ with the window weights delegated to a small GKR (designed and counted in note 65, Section 1.3) and $+59\%$ without. At the same prover time the proven knobs (note 08's configuration C, and the rate) give about $-23\%$; at $+32$ to $+52\%$ the rate gives $-34\%$ and a recursive verifier a third faster. Rungs 4 and 5 sit on or below the proven curve. Reasons: a partner the expansion proof can treat as uniform needs about $N$ oracle bits, whose decoding costs the guest about 38 cycles a partner; public pools of offsets, explicit expanders (proved interior fraction 0.877 for Gabber-Galil), product structures and local windows each fail on the proof side. Measured: uniformly random partner rows cost the prover the same as strided ones (0.07 to 0.13 of the encode's field work a partner, standalone kernel, Apple machine, four threads). The mixed code would become a modest win only with a window graph that has at once fewer than about 8 partners, a proved interior fraction below about 0.55, and partners that are a fixed cheap function of the index; none is known. Recommendation of the note: deploy configuration C, spend further prover time on the rate.

**Note 62 (one researcher): the first rung is not proved.** Proved under the axioms: the expansion lemma for the random $d$-out graph (interior fractions 0.50, 0.37, 0.29 at $d = 6, 8, 10$ for density 1/2, failure below $2^{-129}$; an additive term is needed at small $N$); the pair bound for any window containing its centre; the negative result for every non-spanning XOR window, with explicit triples that push the three-wise threshold above Johnson; for triples: the collinear case, the free part (at most $3\gamma k$, so $\gamma < 2/3$ is needed and sharp), the exact lattice count of pencils satisfying a deficiency configuration (Lemma 5.4, which corrects the lead's "Case A"), and the sum over transversal configurations when the closure has density at most $2\rho \cdot$(expansion range), which is complete at rates 1/4 and below and cannot be complete at rate 1/2 for any window system. Not proved: **Hypothesis L**, covering the non-transversal configurations (and at rate 1/2 the transversal ones with large closure). Under it: constant lists (10 to 37 members) above 0.6664 / 0.4564 / 0.3173 / 0.2225, that is 185 / 96 / 66 / 50 queries at the four rates, with $d \ge 7$. Three things block L: windows with exactly one point outside a common zero set are deficient automatically and need a second expansion axiom; the entropy of the support enters doubled (absorbed only for $\gamma \lesssim 0.3$, about $d \ge 10$); the crude count of directions fails for large closures at rate 1/2. The note also lists seven errors in the lead's Section 9 of note 57.

**Assessment.** The modified-code route is sound as far as anyone could test, incomplete as a proof even at the first rung, and at best a marginal practical gain when complete. It does not meet the user's bar. The only route that would (plain Reed-Solomon with a proven list bound below Johnson, at about 2% of prover time) is where every approach has stopped.

## 9. Reports 63 and 67 (2026-11-21)

**Report 63 (higher rungs, one author, not refereed).** $H_4$ and $H_5$ are not proved. Proved strata: a weighted first moment (Proposition 1.2), free and paid agreements together for $\gamma \le 2/t$, a revealed-set lemma for windows of rank at most $u - 1$ on at most $m + 1$ points, low primitive degree, rank-one windows, the cross product for separated classes, and rigid triples with at most $(2k + m)/3$ windows. Open: the rigid overlapping stratum for every $u \ge 3$, the overlapping cross product, ranks 2 to $u - 2$ for $u \ge 4$, the assembly. The lemma "few deficient windows" is false under the axioms (a planted four-part graph), so the target is a count of tuples. The graph requirement of the proved parts is $\gamma \le 1/(t-1)$, that is $d = 9, 12, 14, 19, 36$ at rate 1/2 for $t = 4, 5, 6, 8, 16$. Verdict: the window-mixed code stays conditional beyond pairs.

**Report 67 (best proven DEEP fold, exact in the model of note 08, guest cycles estimated).** With proven list bounds only, the DEEP fold is 1.6 to 6.0% smaller than configuration C, its recursive verifier 2 to 9% slower, the prover about 2% slower, and it is a new protocol everywhere. Reason: any bound that uses only the distance leaves every level of dimension above a few hundred on the Johnson radius, where today's analysis is within one query; the counting bound helps only the last one or two levels. Priced hypothetical: lists of $2^{40}$ at agreement $0.66 n$ at rate 1/2 at level 0 would put the opening 10 to 12.5% below C and the guest 4 to 7% below. So the DEEP fold pays only with a list bound below Johnson at level 0, and the size of the payoff is set by how far below.

**Consequence.** The only level that matters is level 0, and note 68 Section 1 observes that level 0's code is free. The effort moves to note 68 Section 3 (random tree codes) and to the wide search of researcher 72.
