## 2. Note 50, Section 1, line by line

### 2.1 Lemma 1.2: the local rank on $\{1, Y_1^q\}$

**The statement and its direction [proved].** $\rho_1(q, m)$ is the dimension of the image of $\mathcal{V}_{q,1} = F[X, Y_0] \oplus F[X, Y_0]Y_1^q$ in $F[X, Y_0, Y_1]/\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$, that is, the exact rank of the conditions "$R \in \mathfrak{c}_m$" on the whole of $\mathcal{V}_{q,1}$. On a subspace (the truncated support $\mathcal{S}(q, 1; m)$) the rank can only be smaller, and the conditions at $n$ points have rank at most $n\rho_1$. So a nonzero $R$ exists as soon as $\dim\mathcal{S} > n\rho_1$: the lemma is used as an upper bound on the number of independent conditions, which is what an existence statement needs. Equality on a support holds when it contains the box $\{x + a < m\}$ in both layers; globally the rank is often smaller than $n\rho_1$ even for random words (toy example: $n = 16$, $(q, m) = (2, 4)$: rank 252 against $16 \cdot 17 = 272$), which only helps.

**The proof [proved].** The ideal $\mathfrak{c}_m$ is homogeneous for the $(T, S)$-degree; its part of degree $N < m$ is $w^{m - N}F[T, S, Y_1]_{2N - m}$. A form $\Phi = \Phi_0 + \Phi_1Y_1^q$ of degree $N$ is, as a polynomial in $Y_1$ over $F(T, S)$, equal to $\Phi_1(Y_1 + (\Phi_0/\Phi_1)^{1/q})^q$: its root has multiplicity $q$ or does not exist. So $w^{m - N} \mid \Phi$ with $m - N \ge 1$ iff $m - N \le q$ and $\Phi_0T^q = \Phi_1S^q$, that is $\Phi = \theta\,(S^q + T^qY_1^q)$ with $\theta$ a form of degree $N - q$. The intersection has dimension $(N - q + 1)_+$ for $m - q \le N < m$ and 0 below, and $\rho_1 = m(m + 1) - \sum_{t = m - 2q + 1}^{m - q} t = m^2 - (q - 1)m + q(3q - 1)/2$ as soon as $m - 2q + 1 \ge 0$. This is the proof of the note. It uses characteristic 2 exactly once ($w^q = S^q + T^qY_1^q$), and it is uniform in the point: the support $\mathcal{V}_{q,1}$ and its truncation by weighted degree are invariant under $(X, Y_0) \mapsto (X + \alpha, Y_0 + y_\alpha)$, so $\alpha = 0$, $y_\alpha = 0$ or any other special position plays no role.

**Recomputation [checked, `52_ranks.py A`].** The matrix of the map $\mathcal{V}_N \to F[T, S, Y_1]_N/(\mathfrak{c}_m)_N$ is written in the basis $T^{i}w^{l}Y_1^{e}$ (substitute $S = w + TY_1$; a form lies in $\mathfrak{c}_m$ iff its coefficients with $l < m - N$ vanish); its entries are binomial coefficients, so its rank is the rank over the prime field and does not depend on $F$. Over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$: rank $= \rho_1(q, m)$ for $q = 2, 4, 8, 16$ and every $m$ from $2q - 1$ to 64, for $q = 32$ and $63 \le m \le 72$; below $m = 2q - 1$ the rank is smaller than the formula. $k = 2$: rank $= \rho_2(q, m)$ for $q = 2$ ($7 \le m \le 30$), $q = 4$ ($15 \le m \le 30$), $q = 8$ ($31 \le m \le 40$). Over $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ at three random points $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ each (two for $q = 16$), with untranslated monomials (`52_toy.py rank`, through `52_even.c`): the same numbers for $(q, m) \in \{2\} \times \{3, 4, 5, 8, 13, 20\}$, $\{4\} \times \{7, 8, 9, 14, 20\}$, $\{8\} \times \{15, 16, 17, 20\}$, $(16, 31)$. Other characteristics: on $\{1, Y_1^2\}$ the rank is $m^2 + 2$ for $p = 3, 5$ ($4 \le m \le 14$), as note 50 says; on $\{1, Y_1^3\}$ in characteristic 3 it is $m^2 - 2m + 12$, the same formula with $q = 3$, and on $\{1, Y_1^3\}$ in characteristic 2 it is $m^2 + 3$: the saving is exactly the Frobenius.

### 2.2 Lemma 0 on the even support

**[proved].** Lemma 0 of note 35 is a statement about one polynomial in $\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$: if $P(\alpha) = y_\alpha$ then $(X - \alpha)^m$ divides $R(X, P, P')$. The support plays no role. A monomial $X^xY_0^aY_1^{c}$ of $\mathcal{S}(q, 1; m)$ has $x + \Delta(a + c) < mA$, so $\deg R(X, P, P') \le x + \Delta a + (\Delta - 1)c < mA$ for $\deg P \le \Delta$, and $R(X, P, P')$ has at least $mA$ zeros with multiplicity: it is zero. **[checked]** in all 33 toy instances with explainers a random element of the computed space vanishes on the jets of all known list members (up to 3683 of them) and not on a random polynomial of degree $\Delta$.

### 2.3 Lemma 1.1

**[proved].** The proof of the note is right; I rechecked each step. With $q' = q/2$, $U = Y_1^{q'}$: $\hat G^{q'} = \Phi(U)$ by Frobenius, $R = \varrho(U)$ with $\partial_U\varrho = 0$, and $R' = R/\hat G^{q'\nu}$ lies in $F[X, Y_0][U]$ because it is a polynomial in $Y_1$ (integral over $F[X, Y_0][U]$) that lies in the fraction field of this factorial ring. Differentiating $\varrho = \Phi^\nu R'$ in $U$ gives $\nu\,\Phi_UR' = \Phi\,\partial_UR'$, and $\Phi_U(Y_1^{q'}) = (\partial_{Y_1}\hat G)^{q'}$ because the exponents $c$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_2$. If $\nu$ is odd, $\hat G^{q'}$ divides $(\partial_{Y_1}\hat G)^{q'}R'$, and $\hat G$ is prime to the nonzero polynomial $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G$ of smaller $Y_1$-degree, so $\hat G \mid R'$: contradiction. The hypotheses are only: $\hat G$ irreducible in $F[X, Y_0, Y_1]$ and $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G \ne 0$. It is not needed that $\hat G$ be a first integral.

**To which components it applies [proved].** Let $G \in F[\tau, u, v]$ be primitive and absolutely irreducible over $F(\tau)$. (a) $\hat G$ is irreducible in $F[X, Y_0, Y_1] = F[X][u, v]$: $G$ stays irreducible over $F(X) \subset \overline{F(\tau)}$, and its content over $F[X]$ is 1 because a Bezout relation between its coefficients in $F[\tau]$ is one in $F[X]$. (b) $\partial_{Y_1}\hat G = X\widehat{G_u} + \widehat{G_v}$ vanishes iff $G \in F[\tau][u^2, v^2]$, and such a polynomial is a square over $\overline{F(\tau)}$. So Lemma 1.1 applies to every absolutely irreducible first integral, and in particular **every absolutely irreducible first integral has $b \ge 1$** (if $b = 0$ then $\hat G \in F[X^2, Y_0^2]$, which is case (b)). The case "$b = 0$" of the proof of Theorem 1.3 is therefore a sub-case of "not absolutely irreducible". For a component that is not absolutely irreducible the lemma can fail, and does: $(Y_0 - P)^\mu$ divides even explainers of planted words with $\mu = 1$ and $\mu = 2$ for $q = 2$ (**[checked]**, Section 3, rate 1/2 rows); these components are the squares of note 41 and Lemma R bounds their sections by $\delta^2/4$. **[checked]** `52_ranks.py D`: for 31 random irreducible $G \in \mathrm{GF}(2)[Y_0, Y_1]$ with $b \le 3$ and an odd power of $Y_1$, and $q \in \{2, 4\}$, the polynomials $R'$ in a box with $GR' \in F[Y_0][Y_1^q]$ are exactly $G^{q - 1}F[Y_0][Y_1^q]$.

### 2.4 The count, re-derived

Let $R \ne 0$ in $\mathcal{S}(q, 1; m)$ vanish on the jets of all list members (for instance any solution of the contact conditions). Write $R = R_{(0)} + XR_{(1)}$ with $R_{(i)} \in F[\tau, u, v]$; the substitution $Y_0 = u + Xv$, $Y_1 = v$ is linear, so $\deg_{u,v}R_{(i)} \le d = \lfloor (mA - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$. By Proposition 3 of note 35 the section $(g, h) \in F[\tau]^2$ of a list member $P = g(X^2) + Xh(X^2)$ is a common zero of $R_{(0)}$ and $R_{(1)}$, and distinct members are distinct points of $F(\tau)^2$. Let $H = \gcd(R_{(0)}, R_{(1)})$, $h = \deg_{u,v}H$ (if one part is zero, $H$ is the other).

1. Sections off $H$: common zeros of the coprime $R_{(0)}/H$, $R_{(1)}/H$, at most $(d - h)^2$ (Bezout over $F(\tau)$).
2. Sections on an irreducible factor $G$ of $H$ that is not absolutely irreducible: at most $\delta^2/4$ (Lemma R of note 41, whose proof I rechecked: a rational zero of $G$ is a common zero of two coprime polynomials of degree at most $\delta/2$, either two conjugate absolutely irreducible factors, or two $F(\tau)$-combinations of the components of a factor defined over a purely inseparable extension).
3. An absolutely irreducible factor $G$ of $H$: $\hat G$ divides $R$, has $b \ge 1$ (Section 2.3), and its exact multiplicity $\mu$ in $R$ is divisible by $q$ (Lemma 1.1). Since $\deg_{Y_1}R \le q$ and $Y_1$-degrees add, $\mu b \le q$: so $\mu = q$, $b = 1$, there is one such factor at most, and $R = \lambda\,\hat G^q$ with $\deg_{Y_1}\lambda = 0$.

With $\sum\delta_i \le h$ over the distinct factors of item 2, $(d - h)^2 + \sum\delta_i^2/4 \le (d - h)^2 + h^2 \le d^2$. Hence $\lvert\mathrm{List}(y, A)\rvert \le d^2 + \lvert L_{G^\star}\rvert$ **[proved]**. What is shown about the common component, exactly: it is at most one absolutely irreducible surface, of the form $\partial E^\star = E^\star_X + E^\star_{Y_0}Y_1$ (Proposition 7 of note 35), and it occurs in $R$ with multiplicity exactly $q$ and a bivariate cofactor. Nothing is shown about the number of its sections. Two additions. (i) For any absolutely irreducible first integral $G$ with $\hat G \nmid R$, $G$ does not divide one of the nonzero $R_{(i)}$ and $\lvert L_G\rvert \le \delta d$; so for a fixed certificate and a fixed word there is at most one absolutely irreducible first integral with $\lvert L_G\rvert > \delta d$, and it is the $G^\star$ of every admissible $R$ (N50-4). (ii) A usable criterion **[proved]**: if some admissible $R$ has $R_q = 0$, or $R_0/R_q$ not a $q$-th power in $F(X, Y_0)$, then $\lvert\mathrm{List}(y, A)\rvert \le d^2$ (in item 3, $R_0/R_q = (E_X/E_{Y_0})^q$; the case $R_0 = 0$ is not excluded: then $\hat G^\star = Y_1$, $E^\star = Y_0$, and $L_{G^\star}$ is the set of list members in $F[X^2]$).

## 5. The statement I would sign

**Theorem S [proved; the finite inequalities are the exact integer computations of Section 2.5].** Let $F$ be a field of characteristic 2, $D \subset F$ a set of $n$ points, $y : D \to F$, $\Delta \ge 1$, $A \le n$, $q = 2^e \ge 2$ and $m \ge 2q - 1$ such that
$$N_0(mA) + N_0(mA - q\Delta) \ >\ n\Big(m^2 - (q - 1)m + \frac{q(3q - 1)}2\Big), \tag{$\ast$}$$
and put $d = \lfloor (mA - 1)/\Delta \rfloor$. Then:

1. there is a nonzero $R = R_0(X, Y_0) + R_q(X, Y_0)Y_1^q$, all of whose monomials $X^xY_0^aY_1^c$ have $x + \Delta(a + c) < mA$, with $R(X, P, P') = 0$ for every $P \in \mathrm{List}(y, A)$;
