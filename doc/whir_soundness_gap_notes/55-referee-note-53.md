## 5. Section 6: the weight gap

### 5.1 Theorem 6.2

**Lemma 6.1 [proved].** $\binom M2w_1 \le \sum_\alpha c_\alpha(M - c_\alpha) \le n\,\bar c(M - \bar c)$ by concavity, and $x(M - x)$ decreases for $x \ge M/2$, which is where $\bar c \ge MA'/n$ lies because $A' > n/2$. Larger sets only help, for the same reason. (This monotonicity is what fails for $A < n/2$, C9 (ii).)

**Theorem 6.2 [proved, in the formal model, with C3 and C4].** The chain, and where each hypothesis enters:

1. Contact 4 on all lines and $W < 4A$: every list member is a section (Lemma 0), $S_P^c = S_P$, $\varepsilon_P = r_P\prod_{S_P}(X - \alpha)^3$ with $\deg r_P \le s := W_{\max} - \Delta - 3A$, so $O_P \cap D = S_P \triangle (\mathrm{Odd}(r_P) \cap D)$.
2. Case (I): $P + \psi \equiv \varepsilon_P$, so $\mathrm{dlog}\Lambda_{O_P \triangle O_{P'}} = \mathrm{dlog}\,u$, with $O_P \subset \overline F$.
3. **(H1)** $p_\infty \ge \Delta + n/8$: no list member equals $\psi$, Proposition 2.3 (2) applies to every pair, and the power sums of $O_P \triangle O_{P'}$ vanish up to $2\lfloor (p_\infty - \Delta)/2 \rfloor \ge n/8$. The hypothesis is sharp for this route: $p_\infty = \Delta + n/8 - 1$ gives only $g = n/8 - 2$ and $\mathrm{RM}(3, N)$.
4. **(H2)** $O_P \setminus D$ is the same for all members: $O_P \triangle O_{P'} = (O_P \cap D) \triangle (O_{P'} \cap D)$ is a subset of $D$; this is the only place where (H2) is used, and it is what turns step 3 into membership in $\Gamma_\infty(g)$. It also makes $P \mapsto O_P \cap D$ injective (Theorem 3.2 (3), $\lvert O_P\rvert \ge A - s > \Delta$).
5. The formal model (Proposition 5.1, $g \ge n/8 - 1$): the differences are in $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$.
6. Replacing $S_P$ by $O_P \cap D$ costs at most $s$ points per member: $\lvert O_P \cap D\rvert \ge A - s$ and $\lvert (O_P \cap D) \triangle (O_{P'} \cap D)\rvert \ge 2(A - \Delta) - 2s > 3n/8$; the next weight of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ is $7n/16$.
7. Lemma 6.1 with $A' = A - s$, $w_1 = 7n/16$.

Each step is right. The gain of working with $O_P \cap D$ rather than $S_P$ is real: with the $S_P$ the distances are only $7n/16 - 2s$ and the Johnson step needs $s < 0.0114\,n$, as the note says. **Exact integers (`55_window.py`), $n = 2^{25}, 2^{26}, 2^{27}, 2^{28}$:** with the note's cut ($W \le \lfloor 2.6352\,n \rfloor$): $s \le 0.01570\,n$, $A' = 0.69080\,n$, distances at least $0.38160\,n$, $2A'(n - A')/n = 0.42719\,n$, **42**; with the cut of note 54: $0.01575\,n$, $0.69075\,n$, $0.38151\,n$, $0.42723\,n$, **42**; $r_P$ constant: distances at least $0.41300\,n$, $2A(n - A)/n = 0.41472\,n$, **19**; with $w_1 = n/2$: **6** and **5**. Remark (ii) (24, 21, 20, 19 at $a = 0.70084, 0.703, 0.705, 0.7065$) and remark (iii) (width $0.00172\,n$) are right.

**What the theorem is.** A statement about any family of polynomials of degree at most $\Delta$ with one common centre having a pole of order at least $\Delta + n/8$ at infinity, whose sets $\mathrm{Odd}(P + \psi)$ have a common part outside $D$ and at least $A'$ points in $D$, pairwise at distance above $3n/8$, on a generic subspace. The potential enters only through steps 1 and 6 (the sizes). No potential satisfying the hypotheses is known (the 11/16 family has a polynomial centre but $W = 2.75\,n$ and no list member), so the theorem may be vacuous; (H1) holds automatically when $\psi$ is a polynomial, by (2.1).

### 5.3 Proposition 6.3, and what its construction really gives

**The proof is right [proved].** For $3n/16 - 1 \le g \le 7n/32 - 2$ the odd $k \le g$ of weight at least $N - 3$ are $k_1 = n/8 - 1$ and $k_2 = 3n/16 - 1$ (C6), and on $\mathrm{RM}(3, N)$ the functionals $p_{k_1}, p_{k_2}$ see only the cubic part, so every function of degree 3 whose cubic part is $x_0\bar Q$ with $\bar Q \in \mathcal{X}$ lies in $\Gamma_\infty(g)$, and $\dim\mathcal{X} \ge \binom{N-1}2 - 2m$. A fixed 4-dimensional radical costs $4(N - 1) - 10$ conditions. A linear space of alternating forms of rank at most 2 on a space of dimension $v$ has dimension at most $\max(v - 1, 3)$: the sum of two decomposable bivectors $a, b$ is decomposable iff $a \wedge b = 0$ (the divided square, valid in characteristic 2), so the planes meet pairwise, and a family of pairwise meeting planes has a common line or lies in a 3-space **[proved; exhaustive check for $v = 4, 5$: 3 and 4]**. At $N = 25$, $m = 64$: $276 - 128 - 86 = 62 > 19$. The two halves are quadratic with forms $q_0$ and $\bar Q + q_0$ on disjoint coordinates, of ranks 4 and $2h' + 4$, and the weights follow.

**The construction is more flexible than the proof uses [proved here; checked].** Let $\bar Q \in \mathcal{X}$ be nonzero of rank $2h'$ and let $q_A$ be any quadratic function on $x_1, \dots, x_{N-1}$ of rank 4 with $\bar Q + q_A$ of rank $2b$. Such a $q_A$ exists with $b = \max(3, h' - 2)$ for every $h' \ge 1$ when $N \ge 8$: in a symplectic basis $\bar Q = \sum_{i \le h'}u_i \wedge v_i$ take $q_A = u_1 \wedge v_1 + u_2 \wedge v_2$ for $h' \ge 5$ (rank of the sum $2h' - 4$); $u_1 \wedge v_1 + u_2 \wedge (v_2 + u_1)$ for $h' = 4$; $u_1 \wedge u_3 + u_2 \wedge v_3$ for $h' = 3$; $u_1 \wedge z + w \wedge t$ for $h' = 2$ and $z_1 \wedge w_1 + z_2 \wedge w_2$ for $h' = 1$ with new independent forms (sum of rank 6 in the last four cases). **What it does not give.** No bound on $\lvert L_{G^\star}\rvert$ for a member of class $\mathcal{D}$ on any explicit domain: (R1$'$) of note 54 is open. Item (f) is conditional on (H1), (H2), holds only in the formal model, and has no known instance. The gaps (G1) to (G4) of note 53 are stated correctly; by Proposition R, for $N \ge 26$ the sufficient condition stated in (G3) (no word of $\Gamma_\infty(g)$ of weight in $[0.3816\,n, 0.4272\,n]$) is false on every subspace of $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$ for every $g \le 7n/32 - 2$, so (G3) needs the list-decoding form or the even conditions (or a pole at infinity of order at least $\Delta + 7n/32$).

## 9. Limits of this report

- The algebraic tests are at $n \le 128$ and test statements that hold for all $n$; they say nothing about whether class $\mathcal{D}$ is empty in the window.
- The formal model is emulated by random bases in $\mathrm{GF}(2^{607})$ (profiles) and $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$ (the search at $N = 5$, confirmed on a second basis); the profiles are exact in those fields and equal the formal values, which is what the proof predicts, but the emulation is not a proof.
- The search of Section 5.2 covers clean pencils on one 5-dimensional generic subspace. It is exhaustive there and nowhere else.
- I did not recompute the first cut of note 54, and I did not re-referee Theorem S or Theorem 54.
- Proposition R is my own and has had no referee.
