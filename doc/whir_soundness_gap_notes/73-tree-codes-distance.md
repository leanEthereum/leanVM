# 73. The distance of the random tree code

**Theorem (proved, Section 5; same constants for T1 and T2).** Let the top code $T_J$ be MDS and the twiddles of levels $J+1, \dots, v$ be random ($L = v - J$ random levels, $n = 2^{v+r} \le 2^{26}$, $n_{J+1} = 2^{J+1+r}$). For every $\lambda \in (0, 64)$ and $p > 0$, except with probability $p$, every nonzero codeword has fewer than $(\rho + \varepsilon_0) n$ zeros, every set of leaves has rank above $\min(|Z|, k) - \varepsilon_0 n$, and the same holds at every level, with
$$\varepsilon_0 = \frac{1}{64 - \lambda}\Big(\lambda\rho + \tfrac12 C_J + \tfrac12\log_2(1 + 2^{-2\lambda - C_J}) + \frac{\log_2(L/p) + c_\kappa + 2^{-32}}{n_{J+1}}\Big), \qquad C_v = 0,\ \ C_i = C_{i+1} + \log_2\big(1 + 2^{1 - \lambda - C_{i+1}/2}\big),$$
where $c_\kappa \le 10.2$ bits is an explicit sum of logarithms of binomials (computed for $n = 2^{19}$ to $2^{25}$, $J = 4$ to 10; exact definition in Section 5). At $p = 2^{-130}$ and the best $\lambda$ (3.5 at rate 1/2, 5 at rate 1/4 are within 0.03 of it): **rate 1/2: $64\varepsilon_0 = 5.3$ to $5.7$ for $J = 4$, $4.1$ to $4.5$ for $J = 5$, $3.5$ to $3.9$ for $J = 6$, $2.9$ to $3.3$ for $J = 8$; rate 1/4: $3.1$ to $3.2$ for $J = 4$, $2.4$ to $2.6$ for $J = 5$, $2.1$ to $2.3$ for $J = 6$, $1.8$ to $2.0$ for $J = 8$** (ranges over $n = 2^{19}$ to $2^{25}$; full table in Section 6). The slack is a term $2.35/n_{J+1}$ from the first random level, which is within 16 percent of what a random code of that length forces at $p = 2^{-130}$ (sketched: $J = 4$ at rate 1/2 should not be expected to give less than $2/64$, two coincidences among the first random twiddles having first moment about $2^{-67}$), plus a main term of $3.0/64$ to $3.3/64$ at rate 1/2 and $1.85/64$ to $2.0/64$ at rate 1/4 that grows like $\rho\log_2(L/\rho)/64$. "$T_J$ is MDS" is checkable by enumeration only for $J = 4$ at rate 1/2 ($6 \cdot 10^8$ determinants) and $J = 3$ or, heavily, $4$ at rate 1/4 ($J = 3$: $4.3$ to $4.5$); larger $J$ means a top that is MDS by construction (the Reed-Solomon specialization of the top $J$ levels). Below $2/64$ is **proved** only at rate 1/4 with such a top of $J \ge 8$ levels and $n \le 2^{24}$ ($J = 10$: $1.63$ to $1.90$); at rate 1/2 it is proved only with at most 3 random levels. The proof is: (i) the lead's splitting lemma, re-proved with exact hypotheses and found correct (Lemma 2.1; the count is over labels $(Y, D, c)$, at most $4^{n_j}$ a level, and the factor 2 is really $1/(1 - |D|/q)$); (ii) every flat has a chain of flats with no rounding, along which excess in leaf units only increases, by $u_{j+1} G_j$ at level $j$ (Lemmas 3.1, 3.2); (iii) defect events indexed by *prefixes* of chains with a tilted prefix code that satisfies Kraft with equality, conditioned top-down level by level, so that each level's events are counted once (Lemmas 4.1, 4.2); (iv) Abel summation and a Lagrange multiplier for the nullity budget (Theorem 5.1). **Only sketched or conjectured:** the main term is an artifact of the lemma's bound $q^{-g}$ being blind to the nullity left; the conjectured truth is $\varepsilon_0 \approx h(\rho)/64 + 2.03/n_{J+1}$ (about $2.0/64$ at $J = 5$ and $1.5/64$ at $J = 6$, rate 1/2), which would follow from an additive law $q^{-(g + \nu' - 1)}$ for a fresh excess $g$ leaving nullity $\nu'$ (Conjecture 9.3; attained by Reed-Solomon parents, so the product law of general position is **false** for structured parents; exact counts over GF($2^4$) in Section 8 agree with the additive law, and show T1 parents at the Reed-Solomon rate and T2 parents at the general-position rate for nullity 2). **The dual statement (small sets are independent) is not proved** beyond the first random level, where Proposition 7.5 gives the right order $q^{-(\sigma+1)}$ for a set $\sigma$ below the dimension; what comes for free is the rank statement above. A deterministic by-product (Corollary 7.3, proved): a fresh excess on a label $(Y, D, c)$ is at most $e_j(Y \cup D) - e_j(Y)$, so the maximal excess can only grow through labels that exhaust the nullity.

## 1. Notation and object

$K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$, $q = |K|$. Rate $\rho = 2^{-r}$, depth $v$, $k = 2^v$, $n = 2^{v+r}$. $P_j$ is the set of level-$j$ nodes, $n_j = |P_j| = 2^{j+r}$, $k_j = 2^j$, $u_j = n/n_j = 2^{v-j}$ (leaves below a level-$j$ node). $T_j \subseteq K^{P_j}$ is the code of the top $j$ levels, with generator rows $g_p \in K^{k_j}$, $p \in P_j$. A node $p \in P_j$ has children $(p,0), (p,1) \in P_{j+1}$ with twiddles $t_{p,0} \ne t_{p,1}$ and $g_{(p,b)} = (g_p, t_{p,b}\, g_p) \in K^{2k_j}$: a message of $T_{j+1}$ is a pair $x = (a_0, a_1) \in (K^{k_j})^2$ and its symbol at $(p,b)$ is $\alpha_p(x) + t_{p,b}\,\beta_p(x)$ with $\alpha_p(x) = \langle g_p, a_0\rangle$, $\beta_p(x) = \langle g_p, a_1\rangle$.

**Ensemble.** $T_J$ is fixed. For $j \ge J$ the twiddle pairs $(t_{p,0}, t_{p,1})$, $p \in P_j$ (the twiddles "of level $j+1$") are independent of each other and of all coarser levels; T1: $t_{p,0}$ uniform in $K$ and $t_{p,1} = t_{p,0} + 1$; T2: $(t_{p,0}, t_{p,1})$ uniform among ordered pairs of distinct elements. The only properties used below are (P1) $t_{p,0} \ne t_{p,1}$ always, (P2) for each $p$ and $b$ the marginal of $t_{p,b}$ is uniform on $K$, (P3) pairs of distinct nodes are independent. So every statement below holds verbatim for T1 and T2, with the same constants. (Checked: P2 holds for T2 with distinct pairs because the uniform distribution on ordered distinct pairs has uniform marginals.)

For $Y \subseteq P_j$: $N_j(Y) = \{a \in K^{k_j} : \langle g_p, a\rangle = 0\ \forall p \in Y\}$, $\mathrm{null}_j(Y) = \dim N_j(Y)$, $\mathrm{rk}_j(Y) = k_j - \mathrm{null}_j(Y)$, and the row excess $e_j(Y) = |Y| - \mathrm{rk}_j(Y) = |Y| - k_j + \mathrm{null}_j(Y) \ge 0$. In leaf units: $\mathcal N_j(Y) = u_j\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y)$ and $\mathcal E_j(Y) = u_j\, e_j(Y)$. A flat of $T_j$ is a set $Y$ with $g_p \not\equiv 0$ on $N_j(Y)$ for every $p \notin Y$ (a closed set of the row matroid). $T_j$ is MDS iff $e_j(Y) = \max(|Y| - k_j, 0)$ for all $Y$.

**Goal.** "Every nonzero codeword of $T_v$ has at most $k + s$ zeros" is the statement: every $Z \subseteq P_v$ with $\mathrm{null}_v(Z) \ge 1$ has $|Z| \le k + s$. Since $|Z| = k - \mathrm{null}_v(Z) + e_v(Z) \le k - 1 + e_v(Z)$, it is implied by: **every $Z \subseteq P_v$ with $\mathrm{null}_v(Z) \ge 1$ has $e_v(Z) \le s + 1$**, and it is enough to prove this for flats (the closure of $Z$ has the same null space and at least as many elements, so at least as large an excess).

## 2. One level: the splitting lemma, re-proved

Fix $j \ge J$ and condition on $T_j$ (all twiddles of levels up to $j$). An *event label* at level $j$ is a triple $(Y, D, c)$ with $Y \subseteq P_j$, $D \subseteq P_j \setminus Y$ and $c: D \to \{0,1\}$. Its set of positions of $T_{j+1}$ is $Z(Y,D,c) = \{(p,0),(p,1) : p \in Y\} \cup \{(p, c(p)) : p \in D\}$; every $Z \subseteq P_{j+1}$ is $Z(F, H, c)$ for its set $F$ of full pairs, its set $H$ of half pairs and the sides $c$ of the present children (notation of note 68). Put $t_p = t_{p,c(p)}$ for $p \in D$ and
$$S(Y,D,c) = \{x \in N_j(Y)^2 : \alpha_p(x) + t_p\,\beta_p(x) = 0\ \ \forall p \in D\}.$$
Call the label *tight* (a random property) if for every $p \in D$ the functional $\beta_p$ is not identically zero on $S(Y,D,c)$. The *fresh excess* is $G(Y,D,c) = \dim S(Y,D,c) - (2\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y) - |D|)$.

**Lemma 2.1 (proved).** (a) (deterministic) $N_{j+1}(Z(Y,D,c)) = S(Y,D,c)$, so $\mathrm{null}_{j+1}(Z) = 2\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y) - |D| + G$ and $e_{j+1}(Z) = 2 e_j(Y) + G$. (b) (deterministic) $0 \le G \le |D|$, and if the label is tight and $D \ne \emptyset$ then $G \le |D| - 1$ and $\dim S \ge 1$. (c) For every label with $|D| < q$ and every integer $g \ge 1$: $\Pr[\text{tight and } G(Y,D,c) \ge g \mid T_j] \le q^{-g}/(1 - |D|/q)$.

*Proof.* (a) For $p \in Y$ both children are in $Z$: $\alpha_p + t_{p,0}\beta_p = \alpha_p + t_{p,1}\beta_p = 0$, and $t_{p,0} \ne t_{p,1}$ (P1) gives $\alpha_p = \beta_p = 0$, that is $a_0, a_1 \in N_j(Y)$; the remaining conditions are those of $D$. Then $|Z| = 2|Y| + |D|$, $k_{j+1} = 2k_j$ and $e_{j+1}(Z) = |Z| - k_{j+1} + \mathrm{null}_{j+1}(Z) = 2(|Y| - k_j + \mathrm{null}_j(Y)) + G$. (b) $S$ is cut out of $N_j(Y)^2$, of dimension $2\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y)$, by $|D|$ linear conditions, so $0 \le G \le |D|$. If the label is tight and $p \in D$, then $g_p \not\equiv 0$ on $N_j(Y)$ (otherwise $\beta_p \equiv 0$ on $N_j(Y)^2 \supseteq S$), so the functional $(a_0,a_1) \mapsto \langle g_p, a_0 + t_p a_1\rangle$ is nonzero on $N_j(Y)^2$ and the rank of the $|D|$ conditions is at least 1: $G \le |D| - 1$. And $S \ne 0$ because $\beta_p \not\equiv 0$ on it. (c) Let $m = 2\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y)$, $d = |D|$. On the event $\mathcal A$ = "tight and $\dim S = \delta$", the sets $S_p = \{x \in S : \beta_p(x) = 0\}$, $p \in D$, are proper subspaces of $S$, so $S^\circ = S \setminus \bigcup_{p \in D} S_p$ has at least $q^\delta - d\,q^{\delta - 1} = q^\delta(1 - d/q)$ elements. For $x \in S^\circ$ and $p \in D$: $\beta_p(x) \ne 0$ and $t_p = \alpha_p(x)/\beta_p(x)$ (characteristic 2). For $\sigma \in K^D$ define the deterministic (given $T_j$) set $W_\sigma = \{x \in N_j(Y)^2 : \beta_p(x) \ne 0,\ \alpha_p(x) = \sigma_p \beta_p(x)\ \forall p \in D\}$. The $W_\sigma$ are pairwise disjoint subsets of $N_j(Y)^2$, and on $\mathcal A$ we have $S^\circ \subseteq W_{(t_p)_{p \in D}}$. Hence $\mathcal A \subseteq \{(t_p)_{p\in D} \in \Sigma_\delta\}$ with $\Sigma_\delta = \{\sigma : |W_\sigma| \ge q^\delta(1 - d/q)\}$, and by disjointness $|\Sigma_\delta| \le q^{m - \delta}/(1 - d/q)$; the sets $\Sigma_\delta$ decrease in $\delta$. By (P2), (P3) the vector $(t_p)_{p \in D}$ is uniform on $K^D$ given $T_j$ (one used twiddle per node of $D$, distinct nodes independent; in T1 and T2 alike). So $\Pr[\text{tight}, \dim S \ge \delta \mid T_j] \le |\Sigma_\delta| q^{-d} \le q^{m - \delta - d}/(1 - d/q)$; put $\delta = m - d + g$. $\square$

**Remarks (proved).** (i) Note 68's Lemma 5.1 follows: for $Z \subseteq P_{j+1}$ and its realized rounding $R$ (the half pairs where every solution has $\alpha_p = \beta_p = 0$), the label $(F \cup R, H \setminus R, c)$ is tight and $S(F \cup R, H \setminus R, c) = N_{j+1}(Z)$ (every solution vanishes on both children of $R$, so adding the absent children of $R$ changes nothing); sum (c) over $R \subseteq H$. The factor 2 there is $1/(1 - |H|/q) \le 1 + 2^{-37}$ here ($|H| \le n_j \le 2^{26}$); the hypothesis needed is $|D| < q$ (a vector space over $K$ is not a union of fewer than $q$ proper subspaces; the count used is the cruder $q^\delta - d q^{\delta-1} > 0$). The lead's lemma is correct. (ii) The label does not record how $Y$ splits into full pairs and rounded half pairs: all $Z$ with the same $(F \cup R, H \setminus R, c)$ give the same event. This is why the count below is over labels (at most $4^{n_j}$) and not over sets with roundings ($6^{n_j}$). (iii) No independence between different labels is used anywhere; only a union bound.

## 3. Flat chains: the deterministic skeleton

**Lemma 3.1 (proved).** Let $Z$ be a flat of $T_{j+1}$ with $\mathrm{null}_{j+1}(Z) \ge 1$, $F$ the nodes of $P_j$ with both children in $Z$, $H$ those with exactly one, $c(p)$ the child present. Then (a) $Z = Z(F, H, c)$ and the label $(F, H, c)$ is tight; (b) $F$ is a flat of $T_j$ and $\mathrm{null}_j(F) \ge 1$.

*Proof.* (a) $N_{j+1}(Z) = S(F,H,c)$ by Lemma 2.1(a). For $p \in H$ the absent child $(p, 1 - c(p))$ is outside the flat $Z$, so $\alpha_p + t_{p,1-c(p)}\beta_p \not\equiv 0$ on $S$, while $\alpha_p + t_{p,c(p)}\beta_p \equiv 0$ on $S$. If $\beta_p \equiv 0$ on $S$ then $\alpha_p \equiv 0$ on $S$ and the absent child's functional would vanish on $S$. So $\beta_p \not\equiv 0$ on $S$. (b) If $p \notin F$ and $g_p \equiv 0$ on $N_j(F)$, then $\alpha_p \equiv \beta_p \equiv 0$ on $N_j(F)^2 \supseteq S$, both children of $p$ vanish on $N_{j+1}(Z)$ and so lie in the flat $Z$: $p \in F$, a contradiction. And $0 \ne S \subseteq N_j(F)^2$. $\square$

**The chain of a flat.** For a flat $Z_v$ of $T_v$ with $\mathrm{null}_v(Z_v) \ge 1$ define, for $j = v-1$ down to $J$: $Z_j = F(Z_{j+1})$, $D_j = H(Z_{j+1})$, $c_j$ the present children, $d_j = |D_j|$, $G_j = G(Z_j, D_j, c_j)$. By Lemma 3.1 every $Z_j$ is a flat with nullity at least 1, every label $(Z_j, D_j, c_j)$ is tight, and $Z_{j+1} = Z(Z_j, D_j, c_j)$: the chain is determined by the *prefix label* $\pi = (Z_J; (D_i, c_i)_{J \le i \le v-1})$, a purely combinatorial object (a subset of $P_J$, then for each level a set of half pairs disjoint from the current set, with sides), and $\pi_j$ denotes its truncation to levels $J, \dots, j$. No rounding occurs along the chain of a flat ($R = \emptyset$): the "leaves added by rounding up" of note 68 are absorbed by passing to the closure.

**Lemma 3.2 (proved; bookkeeping in leaf units).** Along the chain, with $\mathcal N_j = \mathcal N_j(Z_j)$, $\mathcal E_j = \mathcal E_j(Z_j)$, $\Gamma_j = \sum_{i=J}^{j} G_i$:
1. $\mathcal E_{j+1} = \mathcal E_j + u_{j+1} G_j$ and $\mathcal N_{j+1} = \mathcal N_j - u_{j+1}(d_j - G_j)$, with $G_j = 0$ if $d_j \le 1$ and $1 \le d_j - G_j$ if $d_j \ge 1$. So the excess never decreases, the nullity never increases, and a level with no half pairs changes neither.
2. $u_j |Z_j| = u_J |Z_J| + \sum_{i=J}^{j-1} u_{i+1} d_i$.
3. If $T_J$ is MDS: $e_J(Z_J) = 0$, $\mathrm{null}_J(Z_J) = k_J - |Z_J|$, hence $\mathcal E_v = \sum_{j=J}^{v-1} u_{j+1} G_j = \sum_{j=J}^{v-1} \omega_j \Gamma_j$ with $\omega_j = u_{j+1} - u_{j+2}$ (and $u_{v+1} := 0$), $\sum_{j \ge i}\omega_j = u_{i+1}$; and $1 \le \mathcal N_v = k - u_J|Z_J| - \sum_{i} u_{i+1} d_i + \mathcal E_v$, that is
$$\sum_{i=J}^{v-1} u_{i+1} d_i \;\le\; k - u_J |Z_J| + \mathcal E_v - 1.$$
4. $\Gamma_j \le \mathcal E_{j+1}/u_{j+1} \le \mathcal E_v / u_{j+1}$.

*Proof.* 1 is Lemma 2.1(a),(b) multiplied by $u_{j+1} = u_j/2$. 2: $|Z_{j+1}| = 2|Z_j| + d_j$. 3: MDS and $\mathrm{null}_J(Z_J) \ge 1$ give $|Z_J| < k_J$ and $e_J = 0$; sum 1 over the levels and use Abel summation. 4: $\mathcal E_{j+1} = \sum_{i \le j} u_{i+1} G_i \ge u_{j+1}\Gamma_j$. $\square$

So a codeword with more than $k + s$ zeros needs a flat chain with $\sum_j u_{j+1} G_j \ge s + 2$: coincidences $G_j$ at the levels, each worth $u_{j+1}$ leaves, as the lead says (his index is shifted by one: a coincidence among the twiddles that split level $j$ into level $j+1$ is worth $u_{j+1}$ leaves, half a level-$j$ block). What remains is probabilistic: which profiles $(G_j)$ can occur.

## 4. The defect events and the good event

Fix a real $\lambda > 0$ and a target probability $p$. Put $L = v - J$ (the number of random levels) and $\chi = (1 - n/q)^{-L}$ ($\log_2 \chi < 2^{-32}$ for $n \le 2^{26}$).

**Multipliers.** $C_v = 0$ and, for $i = v-1$ down to $J$: $c_i = \log_2\big(1 + 2^{\,1 - \lambda - C_{i+1}/2}\big)$, $C_i = C_{i+1} + c_i$, $\lambda_i = \lambda + C_{i+1}/2$; and $\lambda'_J = 2\lambda + C_J$.

**Code length of a prefix label.** For $\pi_j = (Z_J; (D_i, c_i)_{J \le i \le j})$, with $Z_{i+1} = Z(Z_i, D_i, c_i)$, $d_i = |D_i|$ and $m_i = n_i - |Z_i|$ (the number of nodes available for $D_i$):
$$w(\pi_j) = \lambda'_J |Z_J| + n_J \log_2(1 + 2^{-\lambda'_J}) + \sum_{i=J}^{j} \big(\lambda_i d_i + m_i c_i\big).$$
**Kraft equality (proved):** $\sum_{\pi_j} 2^{-w(\pi_j)} = 1$ for each $j$, the sum over all prefix labels of length $j$. Indeed $\sum_{D \subseteq M,\, c} 2^{-\lambda_i |D|} = (1 + 2 \cdot 2^{-\lambda_i})^{|M|} = 2^{|M| c_i}$ for the last level, then induction down the levels, and $\sum_{Z_J \subseteq P_J} 2^{-\lambda'_J|Z_J|} = (1 + 2^{-\lambda'_J})^{n_J}$.

**Defect event.** For a prefix label $\pi_j$ and an integer $\Gamma \ge 1$: $A(\pi_j, \Gamma)$ = "every label $(Z_i, D_i, c_i)$, $J \le i \le j$, is tight and $\sum_{i=J}^{j} G(Z_i, D_i, c_i) \ge \Gamma$". This is an event about the twiddles of levels $J+1, \dots, j+1$ only; the sets $Z_i$ in it are deterministic.

**Lemma 4.1 (proved; top-down conditioning).** $\Pr[A(\pi_j, \Gamma)] \le \binom{\Gamma + j - J}{j - J}\, \chi\, q^{-\Gamma}$.

*Proof.* If $\sum_i G_i \ge \Gamma$ there are integers $0 \le g_i \le G_i$ with $\sum_i g_i = \Gamma$; there are $\binom{\Gamma + j - J}{j - J}$ vectors $(g_i)_{J \le i \le j}$ of nonnegative integers with sum $\Gamma$. Fix one and let $B_i$ = "label $i$ tight and $G_i \ge g_i$". $B_i$ is measurable with respect to the twiddles of levels up to $i+1$, and by Lemma 2.1(c), $\Pr[B_i \mid T_i] \le b_i$ with $b_i = q^{-g_i}/(1 - n/q)$ if $g_i \ge 1$ and $b_i = 1$ if $g_i = 0$, for every value of $T_i$. Since $B_J, \dots, B_{i-1}$ are functions of $T_i$: $\Pr[\bigcap_{i' \le i} B_{i'}] = \mathbb E\big[\mathbf 1_{\bigcap_{i' < i} B_{i'}} \Pr[B_i \mid T_i]\big] \le b_i \Pr[\bigcap_{i' < i} B_{i'}]$. Induction gives $\prod_i b_i \le \chi q^{-\Gamma}$. $\square$

No independence between levels is claimed or used: the levels are peeled from the top, each conditional on everything coarser. Sets with no half pairs at a level contribute $g_i = 0$ there (no fresh randomness, no fresh excess).

**The good event.** Let $\kappa_j(\Gamma) = \log_2 \binom{\Gamma + j - J}{j - J}$. The function $\Gamma \mapsto 64\Gamma - \kappa_j(\Gamma)$ is increasing ($\kappa_j(\Gamma + 1) - \kappa_j(\Gamma) \le \log_2(1 + L) < 64$). For each $\pi_j$ let $\Gamma^*(\pi_j)$ be the least integer $\Gamma \ge 1$ with
$$64\,\Gamma - \kappa_j(\Gamma) \;\ge\; w(\pi_j) + \log_2(L\chi/p).$$
$\mathcal G(\lambda, p)$ is the event that $A(\pi_j, \Gamma^*(\pi_j))$ fails for every $j \in [J, v-1]$ and every prefix label $\pi_j$.

**Lemma 4.2 (proved).** $\Pr[\lnot \mathcal G(\lambda, p)] \le p$, in T1 and in T2, for any fixed $T_J$. On $\mathcal G(\lambda, p)$, every prefix label $\pi_j$ all of whose labels are tight satisfies
$$64\,\Gamma_j - \kappa_j(\Gamma_j) \;<\; w(\pi_j) + \log_2(L\chi/p). \qquad (\star)$$

*Proof.* By Lemma 4.1 and the definition of $\Gamma^*$: $\Pr[A(\pi_j, \Gamma^*)] \le \chi\, 2^{\kappa_j(\Gamma^*) - 64\Gamma^*} \le 2^{-w(\pi_j)}\, p/L$. Sum over $\pi_j$ (Kraft) and over the $L$ values of $j$. On $\mathcal G$, a tight $\pi_j$ has $\Gamma_j \le \Gamma^* - 1$; if $\Gamma_j \ge 1$ it is below the least solution so $(\star)$ holds, and if $\Gamma_j = 0$ the left side is 0 and the right side is positive. $\square$

**Why prefixes.** $(\star)$ for the full chain only would be useless: a coarse defect of probability $q^{-g}$ followed by fine levels with many half pairs and no coincidence has a large first moment (the fine levels contribute entropy and no probability), although its true probability is at most $q^{-g}$ times the number of coarse labels. Requiring $(\star)$ for every truncation says that the chain was never improbable when cut at any level: this is the union bound over defect events at each level, with the code length of a level-$j$ event (at most about $2 n_j$ bits, the lead's $4^{n_j}$) charged once and not once per finer level. Because $(\star)$ bounds the cumulated count $\Gamma_j$ and the weights $\omega_j$ of Lemma 3.2 are those of an Abel summation, the bits of level $i$ end up multiplied by $u_{i+1}$, the leaf value of a coincidence at that level, and no more.

## 5. The theorem

**Definition of the slack.** For $\lambda \in (0, 64)$, with $C_J = C_J(\lambda)$ of Section 4, let
$$M(\lambda) = \lambda\rho + \tfrac12 C_J + \tfrac12 \log_2\!\big(1 + 2^{-2\lambda - C_J}\big), \qquad c_\kappa(\varepsilon) = \sum_{j=J}^{v-1} \frac{\omega_j}{u_{J+1}} \log_2 \binom{\lfloor \varepsilon n_{j+1} \rfloor + j - J}{j - J},$$
and let $\varepsilon_0 = \varepsilon_0(\lambda; r, v, J, p)$ be the eventual value of the sequence $\varepsilon^{(0)} = 1$, $\varepsilon^{(t+1)} = \Psi(\varepsilon^{(t)})$ (monotone because $\Psi$ is nondecreasing, and eventually constant because $\Psi$ is a bounded step function; in every case computed it decreases),
$$\Psi(\varepsilon) = \frac{1}{64 - \lambda}\Big( M(\lambda) + \frac{\log_2(L/p) + 2^{-32} + c_\kappa(\varepsilon)}{n_{J+1}} \Big).$$
Here $\omega_j/u_{J+1} = 2^{-(j-J+1)}$ for $j \le v-2$ and $2^{-(L-1)}$ for $j = v-1$; $c_\kappa$ is a few bits (Section 6).

**Theorem 5.1 (proved).** Let $r \ge 1$, $J \ge 1$, $v > J$, $n = 2^{v+r} \le 2^{26}$, and let $T_J$ be any fixed code of the top $J$ levels that is MDS (every $k_J = 2^J$ of its $n_J = 2^{J+r}$ rows are independent). Let the twiddles of levels $J+1, \dots, v$ be random as in variant T1 or as in variant T2. Then for every $\lambda \in (0,64)$ and $p > 0$, with probability at least $1 - p$ (on the event $\mathcal G(\lambda, p)$): every set $Z$ of leaves with $\mathrm{null}_v(Z) \ge 1$ has row excess $e_v(Z) < \varepsilon_0 n$. Consequently, on that event:
1. **(distance)** every nonzero codeword of $T_v$ has fewer than $(\rho + \varepsilon_0) n$ zeros, so the relative distance is above $1 - \rho - \varepsilon_0$;
2. **(rank of every set)** every set $Z$ of leaves has $\mathrm{rk}_v(Z) > \min(|Z|, k) - \varepsilon_0 n$;
3. **(every level)** every $Y \subseteq P_j$, $J \le j \le v$, with $\mathrm{null}_j(Y) \ge 1$ has $e_j(Y) < \varepsilon_0 n_j$.

The constants are the same for T1 and T2.

*Proof.* Let $Z$ have $\mathrm{null}_v(Z) \ge 1$; its closure $Z_v$ is a flat with the same null space and $e_v(Z_v) \ge e_v(Z)$. Take the chain of $Z_v$ (Section 3) with prefix label $\pi$; all its labels are tight, so on $\mathcal G$ the inequality $(\star)$ holds for every $j$. Multiply $(\star)$ by $\omega_j \ge 0$ and sum over $j$, using $64\,\mathcal E_v = \sum_j \omega_j\, 64\,\Gamma_j$ (Lemma 3.2(3)) and $\sum_{j \ge i} \omega_j = u_{i+1}$:
$$64\,\mathcal E_v < u_{J+1}\big(\lambda'_J |Z_J| + n_J \log_2(1 + 2^{-\lambda'_J}) + \log_2(L\chi/p)\big) + \sum_{i=J}^{v-1} u_{i+1}\big(\lambda_i d_i + m_i c_i\big) + \sum_j \omega_j \kappa_j(\Gamma_j).$$
- Step 1 (available nodes). By Lemma 3.2(2), $u_{i+1} m_i = \tfrac12 (n - u_i |Z_i|) = \tfrac12\big(n - u_J|Z_J| - \sum_{i' < i} u_{i'+1} d_{i'}\big)$, so $\sum_i u_{i+1} m_i c_i = \tfrac12 C_J (n - u_J |Z_J|) - \tfrac12 \sum_{i'} u_{i'+1} d_{i'} C_{i'+1}$.
- Step 2 (the multipliers were chosen for this). With $\lambda_i = \lambda + C_{i+1}/2$: $\sum_i u_{i+1}(\lambda_i d_i + m_i c_i) = \lambda \sum_i u_{i+1} d_i + \tfrac12 C_J(n - u_J|Z_J|)$.
- Step 3 (nullity budget, Lemma 3.2(3)). $\lambda \sum_i u_{i+1} d_i \le \lambda(k - u_J|Z_J| + \mathcal E_v - 1)$.
- Step 4. The terms in $|Z_J|$ are $u_{J+1}\lambda'_J |Z_J| - (\lambda + C_J/2)\, u_J |Z_J| = 0$ by the choice $\lambda'_J = 2\lambda + C_J$.
- Step 5. By Lemma 3.2(4), $\kappa_j(\Gamma_j) \le \log_2\binom{\lfloor \mathcal E_v/u_{j+1} \rfloor + j - J}{j - J}$, so with $\varepsilon = \mathcal E_v/n$: $\sum_j \omega_j \kappa_j(\Gamma_j) \le u_{J+1}\, c_\kappa(\varepsilon)$.

Collecting, dividing by $n$ and using $u_{J+1} n_J = n/2$, $u_{J+1}/n = 1/n_{J+1}$, $\log_2\chi < 2^{-32}$: $(64 - \lambda)\varepsilon < M(\lambda) + (\log_2(L/p) + 2^{-32} + c_\kappa(\varepsilon))/n_{J+1}$, that is $\varepsilon < \Psi(\varepsilon)$. Since $\varepsilon \le 1 - \rho < \varepsilon^{(0)}$ and $\Psi$ is nondecreasing, $\varepsilon \le \varepsilon^{(t)}$ implies $\varepsilon < \Psi(\varepsilon) \le \Psi(\varepsilon^{(t)}) = \varepsilon^{(t+1)}$; by induction $\varepsilon < \varepsilon^{(t)}$ for every $t \ge 1$, hence $\varepsilon < \varepsilon_0$. Item 1: a nonzero codeword vanishing on $Z$ gives $\mathrm{null}_v(Z) \ge 1$ and $|Z| = k - \mathrm{null}_v(Z) + e_v(Z) \le k - 1 + e_v(Z)$. Item 2: if $\mathrm{null}_v(Z) = 0$ the rank is $k$; otherwise $\mathrm{rk}_v(Z) = |Z| - e_v(Z)$. Item 3: the leaf set below $Y$ has $\mathcal N_v = \mathcal N_j(Y)$ and $\mathcal E_v = \mathcal E_j(Y)$ (Lemma 2.1(a) with $D = \emptyset$ at every finer level). $\square$

**Referee checks on my own proof.** (i) Dependence between levels: only Lemma 4.1's top-down conditioning is used. (ii) Count of events: the Kraft sum is over all prefix labels, tight or not, of flats or not; nothing is assumed about how many flats exist. (iii) Sets with no half pairs at a level: $D_i = \emptyset$, $G_i = 0$; the level contributes $m_i c_i$ bits to the code length (the price of saying "no half pair here") and nothing else. (iv) Top levels: the only property of $T_J$ used is $e_J(Y) = 0$ whenever $\mathrm{null}_J(Y) \ge 1$. If instead $e_J(Y) \le a$ for all such $Y$, the same computation (now $64\,\mathcal E_v = 64\,\mathcal E_J + \sum_j \omega_j 64 \Gamma_j$ with $\mathcal E_J \le u_J a$, and the nullity budget of Step 3 unchanged because $\mathcal N_J = k - u_J|Z_J| + \mathcal E_J$) gives $\varepsilon < \Psi(\varepsilon) + \frac{64}{64 - \lambda}\frac{a}{n_J}$ (**proved**): a non-MDS top costs slightly more than $a/n_J$. Nothing is assumed about the levels above $J$ beyond this. (v) The realized chain is random, but $(\star)$ is asserted for all prefix labels simultaneously, so there is no selection issue. (vi) In T2 a pair of equal twiddles is excluded by definition (P1); if the two twiddles of a node were drawn independently, add $n/q$ to $p$.

## 7. The dual side (task C): what is proved and what is not

**7.1 What comes for free (proved).** Theorem 5.1(2): on $\mathcal G(\lambda,p)$ every set of $z \le k$ leaves has rank above $z - \varepsilon_0 n$. It does not say that small sets are independent.

**7.2 Relation form of the fresh excess (proved).** For a label $(Y, D, c)$ at level $j$ let $\varphi_p = g_p|_{N_j(Y)}$, $p \in D$, $V = \{(\varphi_p(a))_{p \in D} : a \in N_j(Y)\} \subseteq K^D$ (the code $N_j(Y)$ read on $D$; $\dim V = \mathrm{rk}(\varphi_D) = \mathrm{null}_j(Y) - \mathrm{null}_j(Y \cup D)$), $\Lambda = V^\perp = \{\mu \in K^D : \sum_p \mu_p \varphi_p = 0\}$ and $t = (t_p)_{p \in D}$. Then
$$G(Y,D,c) = \dim\{\mu \in \Lambda : t * \mu \in \Lambda\}, \qquad \dim S(Y,D,c) = 2\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y \cup D) + \dim\{v \in V : t * v \in V\},$$
where $*$ is the coordinatewise product. *Proof.* The $|D|$ conditions are the functionals $(\varphi_p, t_p \varphi_p)$ on $N_j(Y)^2$; $G$ is the dimension of their relations $\{\mu : \sum \mu_p \varphi_p = 0,\ \sum \mu_p t_p \varphi_p = 0\}$. For the second formula, $N_j(Y \cup D)^2 \subseteq S$ and $S / N_j(Y\cup D)^2 \cong \{(v_0, v_1) \in V^2 : v_0 + t * v_1 = 0\}$. $\square$

**Corollary 7.3 (proved; deterministic).** $G(Y,D,c) \le \dim \Lambda = |D| - \mathrm{rk}(\varphi_D) = e_j(Y \cup D) - e_j(Y)$. So a fresh excess at level $j$ on the label $(Y, D, c)$ needs the larger set $Y \cup D$ to carry more excess than $Y$ at level $j$ already, or to have nullity zero. Consequently, with $\mathcal M_j = \max\{\mathcal E_j(Y) : \mathrm{null}_j(Y) \ge 1\}$: if $\mathrm{null}_j(Y \cup D) \ge 1$ then $\mathcal E_{j+1}(Z(Y,D,c)) = \mathcal E_j(Y) + u_{j+1} G \le \tfrac12(\mathcal E_j(Y) + \mathcal E_j(Y \cup D)) \le \mathcal M_j$. **The maximal excess can only grow, from one level to the next, through labels that exhaust the nullity: $\mathrm{null}_j(Y \cup D) = 0$, hence $|Y| + |D| \ge k_j$.** (This is not used in Theorem 5.1; Section 9 says why it should be.)

**Lemma 7.4 (proved; first moment for one coincidence, no tightness needed).** For every label, $\Pr[G(Y,D,c) \ge 1 \mid T_j] \le \sum_{[\mu] \in \mathbb P(\Lambda)} q^{-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu})}$. *Proof.* $G \ge 1$ iff some nonzero $\mu \in \Lambda$ has $t * \mu \in \Lambda$, a property of the projective point. For fixed $\mu$ with support $A$, the map $t \mapsto \sum_p t_p \mu_p \varphi_p$ is linear with image $\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_p : p \in A\}$, and $t$ is uniform on $K^D$ (P2, P3), so $\Pr[t * \mu \in \Lambda] = q^{-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi_A)}$. $\square$

**Proposition 7.5 (proved; the dual side at the first random level, at the right order).** Let $T_J$ be MDS and $Z \subseteq P_{J+1}$ with $|Z| = k_{J+1} - \sigma$, $\sigma \ge 0$. Then $\Pr[Z \text{ is dependent in } T_{J+1}] \le \frac{q}{q-1}\, q^{-(\sigma + 1)}$. Hence, except with probability $\frac{q}{q-1}\sum_{z \le k_{J+1} - \sigma} \binom{n_{J+1}}{z} q^{-(k_{J+1} - z + 1)}$, every set of at most $k_{J+1} - \sigma$ positions of $T_{J+1}$ is independent, and so is every set of leaves that is a union of at most $k_{J+1} - \sigma$ blocks of level $J+1$. At $p = 2^{-130}$: $\sigma = 2$ for $n_{J+1} = 64$ and $\sigma = 3$ for $n_{J+1} = 128$ (the sum is about $2^{-131}$ and $2^{-132}$; with one unit less it is about $2^{-67}$ and $2^{-68}$).

*Proof.* Use the label $(F, H, c)$ of $Z$ (tight or not). If $|F| \ge k_J$ then $N_J(F) = 0$ and $Z$ has rank $k_{J+1} \ge |Z|$, impossible unless $\sigma = 0$, where $Z$ is then independent. Otherwise $\nu = k_J - |F| \ge 1$, $e_J(F) = 0$ and $e_{J+1}(Z) = G$, so $Z$ is dependent iff $G \ge 1$. $T_J$ is MDS, so any $\min(|A|, \nu)$ of the $\varphi_p$, $p \in A \subseteq H$, are independent. If $d = |H| \le \nu$ then $\Lambda = 0$ and $G = 0$. If $d > \nu$: $\dim\Lambda = d - \nu$, every nonzero $\mu \in \Lambda$ has more than $\nu$ nonzero entries, so $\mathrm{rk}(\varphi_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}) = \nu$, and Lemma 7.4 gives $\frac{q^{d-\nu} - 1}{q - 1} q^{-\nu} < \frac{q}{q-1} q^{d - 2\nu - 1}$, with $2\nu - d = k_{J+1} - |Z| = \sigma$. $\square$

**7.6 What is not proved (open).** The statement "every set of at most $(\rho - \varepsilon)n$ leaves is independent" for the full tree code. Lemma 2.1 gives $q^{-1}$ for one coincidence whatever the deficit $\sigma$ (the lead's remark (3) on his Lemma 5.1), and there are far more than $q$ small leaf sets, so Theorem 5.1's method gives nothing here. Lemma 7.4 gives the right order $q^{-(\sigma+1)}$ when the parent is MDS, and $q^{-(\sigma + 1 - 3b)}$ when the parent's sets have excess at most $b$ (**sketched**: $\dim \Lambda \le d - \nu + b$ and the supports have rank at least $\nu - 2b$); with $b$ of the order of $\varepsilon_0 n_j$ from Theorem 5.1(3) this is useless for $\sigma < 3\varepsilon_0 n_{j+1}/2$, and iterating "$\sigma_{j+1} = 2\sigma_j + $ union bound" loses $1/64$ a level, the naive invariant again. A proof needs the chain accounting of Section 4 with a probability that depends on the nullity left, that is the conjecture of Section 9.

## 9. Where the constant is lost, and what would give $h(\rho)/64$

**9.1 The two terms against the truth (the comparison is sketched, not proved).** A uniformly random $[n', \rho n']$ code over $K$ has, by its first moment, zero sets of size up to $k' - 1 + e$ with $e \approx (n' h(\rho) + \log_2(1/p))/64$ at probability $p$. Applied to each level's code $T_{j+1}$ and converted to leaves, every level gives the same $h(\rho)\,n/64$ plus $\log_2(1/p)\,n/(64\,n_{j+1})$, largest at the first random level. So the conjectured truth is $\varepsilon_0 \approx h(\rho)/64 + 2.03/n_{J+1}$ at $p = 2^{-130}$ (this is the lead's "$1/64 + 130/(64 \cdot 2^{J+r+1})$" at rate 1/2): $3.0/64$, $2.0/64$, $1.5/64$, $1.13/64$ for $J = 4, 5, 6, 8$ at rate 1/2 and $1.8/64$, $1.3/64$, $1.07/64$, $0.87/64$ at rate 1/4. The term in $1/n_{J+1}$ of Theorem 5.1 ($2.35/n_{J+1}$) is within 16 percent of this; in particular **a theorem at $p = 2^{-130}$ with $J = 4$ at rate 1/2 should not be expected to have $\varepsilon_0$ below $2/64$**: a set of $k_{J+1} + 1 = 33$ positions of $T_{J+1}$ with nullity 1 needs two coincidences among the first random twiddles, has excess $e = 2$ (codewords with $k + 2n/64 - 1$ zeros), and the expected number of such sets is about $\binom{64}{33} q^{-2} \approx 2^{-67}$ (**sketched**: Lemma 2.1 is sharp for nullity 1 in the toy checks of Section 8, but a lower bound on the probability that one exists is not proved here). The main term of Theorem 5.1 is $3.0$ to $3.3$ times $1/64$ at rate 1/2 against $h(\rho) = 1$, and $1.85$ to $2.0$ against $0.81$ at rate 1/4.

**9.2 The extremal profile of the proof (proved to be what the optimization allows; conjectured not to occur).** The bound $M(\lambda)$ is attained, in the deterministic optimization, by a chain that starts from $Z_J = \emptyset$ (nullity $k$), drops a fraction $\delta \approx 2\rho/L$ of the nodes at every one of the $L$ levels, and collects $G_i \approx n_i\,\delta \log_2(2e/\delta)/64$ fresh coincidences at each, keeping a large nullity all the way. Lemma 2.1 allows it because its bound $q^{-g}$ does not depend on the nullity that is left. In a code in general position a fresh excess $g$ that leaves nullity $\nu'$ has probability about $q^{-g\nu'}$ (count of matrices of given corank), so this profile is far too expensive. For a parent that is a Reed-Solomon code it has probability about $q^{-(g + \nu' - 1)}$, **proved as a lower bound**: if $V$ is the Reed-Solomon code of dimension $\nu$ on $D$ and $t = P/Q$ on $D$ with $P, Q$ of degree at most $e$, then $\{Qb : \deg b < \nu - e\} \subseteq \{v \in V : t * v \in V\}$, a family of $q^{2e+1}$ vectors $t$ (probability $q^{2e + 1 - d}$) with $\nu' = \nu - e$ and $g = d - 2\nu + \nu' = d - \nu - e$. So the product law $q^{-g\nu'}$ is false for structured parents and the right conjecture is additive. Measured over GF($2^4$) (Section 8): two-level T1 tree codes sit at the Reed-Solomon rate, two-level T2 tree codes and random MDS codes at the general-position rate.

**Conjecture 9.3 (additive law; conjectured).** There is an absolute constant $a$ such that for every level $j \ge J$ of the ensemble of Theorem 5.1, on an event of $T_j$ of probability $1 - 2^{-140}$, for every label $(Y, D, c)$ with $\mathrm{null}_j(Y \cup D) = 0$ and all $g, \nu' \ge 1$: $\Pr[\text{tight},\ G \ge g,\ \dim S \ge \nu' \mid T_j] \le q^{-(g + \nu' - 1) + a\, b_j}$, where $b_j$ is the largest excess $e_j$ of a set of nullity at least 1 at level $j$. For an MDS parent ($b_j = 0$) it says: an MDS code $V$ of length $d$ and dimension $\nu$ has, for every $\delta \ge \max(1, 2\nu - d + 1)$, at most $q^{2\nu - 2\delta + 1}$ vectors $t \in K^d$ for which $\{v \in V : t*v \in V\}$ has dimension at least $\delta$ and full support, with equality for Reed-Solomon codes (for $\delta \le 2\nu - d$ every $t$ qualifies by a dimension count, so the restriction is needed); it is true for $\delta = 1$ (Lemma 2.1) and for $\delta = \nu$ (then $t * V \subseteq V$, and an MDS code of dimension $0 < \nu < d$ is not a direct sum over a partition of its coordinates, so $t$ is constant: $q$ vectors), and in general position the count is $q^{d - \delta(d - 2\nu + \delta)}$, which is smaller. I did not find it in the literature on Schur products of codes (the nearest statements I know are the product Singleton bound and its critical pairs); it should be checked there before anyone tries to prove it.

**9.4 What the conjecture would give (sketched).** With Corollary 7.3 (only exhausting labels raise the maximum) and an additive law, keeping nullity $\nu'$ after a coincidence costs $64(\nu' - 1)$ bits at level $j+1$, while the half pairs it pays for at any finer level bring at most $\nu' \log_2(2e\,n_{j+1}/\nu')$ bits per unit of leaf value: less than it costs when $\log_2(2 e\, n_{j+1}) < 64$. So spreading is unprofitable, the optimal chain has one large defect and corrections of relative size about $(j + r + 3)/64$, and $\varepsilon_0$ would be about $h(\rho)/64 + 2.1/n_{J+1}$ times $1 + O(\log_2 n/64)$. The same law would give the dual statement of 7.6 with a slack of the same size. Neither is proved here.

**9.5 A proved half step that is not exploited (proved facts, sketched use).** Corollary 7.3 gives a *dominating chain* for every flat $Z_v$: going up, replace $Z_i$ by the flat $\mathrm{cl}(Z_i \cup D_i)$ whenever the step at level $i$ has $G_i \ge 1$ and is not exhausting; then $\mathcal E_v(Z_v) \le \sum u_{i+1} G_i$ over the exhausting steps only, and every exhausting step has $u_{i+1} d_i \ge \mathcal N_i/2$ (it uses more than half of the nullity). In the optimization of Theorem 5.1 restricted to chains whose fresh steps are all exhausting, the nullity halves at each fresh level and the entropies form a convergent series: I estimate a main term of about $1.9/64$ at rate 1/2 and $1.3/64$ at rate 1/4 (**estimate**, by hand, not a theorem). What is missing is the price of a replacement: the new set is a subset of the doubling of the old one, about $2\rho n_i$ bits to describe, and nothing proved here prevents a replacement at every level from resetting the nullity, which gives back the loss of $1/64$ a level. Excluding it needs the dual side (a small subset of a set with nullity cannot carry its excess), so the two sides have to be proved together.
