## 0. Findings in one page

1. **The residual components of note 35 at rate 1/4 are completely described [proved, checked].** A cubic first integral $\partial E$, $E = A_3Y^3 + A_2Y^2 + A_1Y + A_0$, is a plane cubic over $F(\tau)$ with a cusp at a point that is not rational: a form of the additive group. Its sections, with the chord law $P_1 \oplus P_2 = (A_3P_1P_2 + A_1)/(A_3(P_1 + P_2))$, are an elementary abelian 2-group, isomorphic to $\{(\lambda, \zeta) \in F(\tau) \times F(X) : \zeta^2 + \zeta = \omega\lambda^2\}$ for one explicit $\omega \in F(X)$ (Theorem 1). A polynomial section $P$ is *tight*: $A_3P^2 + A_1$ divides one polynomial $\mathrm{Disc}_E$ whose roots are the contained lines, and $P$ agrees with the word on a contained line exactly at the roots of $A_3P^2 + A_1$ (Theorem 2). So a list member on such a component is a subset of the roots of $\mathrm{Disc}_E$, the subsets form (part of) an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear code, and the count asked in the brief is a count of minimum-weight words in a binary alternant code.
2. **Rate 1/4: the polynomial bound asked for is false on dense domains, and the suggested list-recovery bound fails by exactly one unit [proved for the identification, checked for the counts].** On $D = \mathbb{F}_{2^N}$ the polynomial sections of the flat cubic are exactly the Gold codewords of [DKTZ26] quoted in note 01 Section 3.5 (same numbers at $N = 5, 6, 7$: 186, 196 and 1890, 3556), and they exist inside the window $(a_1, 1/2)$: at $n = 2048$ the word $x^{1023}$ has about $1.0 \cdot 10^6$ codewords at agreement $991 = 0.4839\,n$ against 2047 flats (a random sample of 2000 sections contains 257 of them, the predicted density). By [DKTZ26, Cor. 5.2] **[quoted]** the count is $n^{\Omega(\log n)}$ for every fixed $\tau < 1/2$. The statement of note 35 Section 8 item 2 ("at most $n^c$") is therefore false as a statement about every evaluation set; the right target is $2^{O(N^2)}$. The list-recovery count of the brief applies to $N_P = \mathrm{Disc}/M_P$, which takes two prescribed values at every root of $\mathrm{Disc}$; it gives a Plotkin bound when $\deg M_P > \frac12\deg\mathrm{Disc}$, and the flat cubic has $\deg\mathrm{Disc} = 2\deg M_P + 1$ at its top agreement.
3. **Rate 1/4: what is proved.** (a) Exact classification at $n = 2^{25}$, analysis degree $k + 5$ (Proposition 5.1, exact integers with Lemma U of note 41): a fixed component with total contact $\le 2n$ that Lemma C does not cut has $b = 1$, $E$ cubic and $W < 3.68\Delta$ ($a = 0.4738$) or $3.60\Delta$ ($a = 0.4788$); everything else contributes at most 39528, respectively 10584, list members. (c) On the leanVM shape in $K$, $N = 5..14$: $\dim\Lambda_j = N$ for every $j$ in the window, so the list of the flat word above $a_1 n$ is exactly the flats **[checked, exact linear algebra]**. ## 2. Cubic first integrals

### 2.1 Normal form and invariants

Let $E = A_3Y^3 + A_2Y^2 + A_1Y + A_0$ with $A_i \in F[X]$, $A_3 \ne 0$ (here $Y = Y_0$). Since $\partial$ kills $F[X^2, Y^2]$, only $A_3$, $A_1$ and the derivatives $c_2 = A_2'$, $c_0 = A_0'$ matter. Put $b_2 = A_3'$, $b_0 = A_1'$ (all four in $F[\tau]$), and
$$M(Y) = E_Y = A_3Y^2 + A_1, \qquad E_X = b_2Y^3 + c_2Y^2 + b_0Y + c_0, \qquad \delta = (A_1A_3)' = A_1b_2 + A_3b_0 \in F[\tau], \qquad U = c_2A_1 + c_0A_3, \qquad \mathrm{Disc} = A_1\delta^2 + A_3U^2 .$$
Two identities in $F[X][Y]$ **[proved by expansion; checked]**:
$$\text{(I1)}\quad A_3E_X = (b_2Y + c_2)\,M + (\delta Y + U), \qquad\qquad \text{(I2)}\quad A_3(\delta Y + U)^2 = \delta^2 M + \mathrm{Disc} .$$
With $A_3 = a_2 + Xb_2$, $A_1 = a_0 + Xb_0$ ($a_i, b_i \in F[\tau]$) the surface of $\hat G = \partial E$ is $G = (u^2 + \tau v^2)(a_2v + b_2u + c_2) + a_0v + b_0u + c_0$ **[checked]**: a cubic whose only term of degree 3 is the norm form $u^2 + \tau v^2 = Y_0^2$ times a linear form. $G$ is absolutely irreducible iff $\mathrm{Disc} \ne 0$ **[proved]**: over $F(X)$, $G = M(Y_0)\,v + E_X(Y_0)$ is of degree 1 in $v$, so it is irreducible over an extension of $F(X)$ iff $M$ and $E_X$ are coprime there, and by (I1), (I2) a common factor divides $\delta Y + U$ and then the constant $\mathrm{Disc}$; conversely if $\mathrm{Disc} = 0$ then $\delta Y + U$ (or $M$ itself when $\delta = U = 0$) is a common factor. By Lemma R of note 41 only $\mathrm{Disc} \ne 0$ matters. I assume $\mathrm{Disc} \ne 0$ and, until Section 2.5, $\delta \ne 0$.

A *section* is a $P \in F(X)$ with $E(X, P) \in F(\tau)$, equivalently $M(P)\,P' + E_X(X, P) = 0$ (for a polynomial $P$ this is the section of $\Sigma_G$ of note 35). For $P \in F(X)$ put
$$M_P = A_3P^2 + A_1, \qquad \lambda_P = (A_3P)' + c_2 \in F(\tau), \qquad \zeta_P = \frac{\mathrm{Disc}}{\delta^2 M_P}, \qquad \omega = \frac{A_3\,\mathrm{Disc}}{\delta^4} .$$
$M_P \ne 0$, because $(A_1/A_3)' = \delta/A_3^2 \ne 0$ and squares of $F(X)$ have derivative 0.

### 2.2 The group of sections

**Theorem 1 [proved; checked on 12 random cubics over $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ with two planted polynomial sections, 7 sections each, `43_structure.py A`].** Assume $\mathrm{Disc} \ne 0$ and $\delta \ne 0$.

1. $P$ is a section iff $\lambda_P M_P = \delta P + U$.
2. $Q = U/\delta$ is a section, with $\lambda_Q = 0$ and $\zeta_Q = 1$, and it is the only section with $\lambda = 0$.
3. If $P_1 \ne P_2$ are sections, so is $P_1 \oplus P_2 = (A_3P_1P_2 + A_1)/(A_3(P_1 + P_2))$. With a neutral element $O$ and $P \oplus P = O$, the sections form an elementary abelian 2-group $\mathcal{S}$.
4. $P \mapsto \lambda_P$, $P \mapsto 1/M_P$ and $P \mapsto \zeta_P$ (with $O \mapsto 0$) are homomorphisms to $(F(\tau), +)$ and $(F(X), +)$; the last two are injective and $\ker\lambda = \{O, Q\}$.
5. $\zeta_P^2 + \zeta_P = \omega\lambda_P^2$, and $P \mapsto (\lambda_P, \zeta_P)$ is an isomorphism of $\mathcal{S}$ onto $\mathcal{G} = \{(\lambda, \zeta) \in F(\tau) \times F(X) : \zeta^2 + \zeta = \omega\lambda^2\}$, with inverse $P = (\lambda M + U)/\delta$, $M = \mathrm{Disc}/(\delta^2\zeta)$.

*Proof.* (1) By (I1) at $Y = P$, $A_3E_X(X, P) = (b_2P + c_2)M_P + \delta P + U$, so $A_3(M_PP' + E_X(X, P)) = M_P(A_3P' + b_2P + c_2) + \delta P + U = \lambda_PM_P + \delta P + U$. (2) $(A_3U)' = (c_2A_1A_3 + c_0A_3^2)' = c_2\delta$, so $\lambda_Q = c_2 + c_2 = 0$, and $\delta Q + U = 0$; $M_Q = \mathrm{Disc}/\delta^2$. If $\lambda_P = 0$ then $\delta P + U = 0$. (3) and (4) Let $P_3 = P_1 \oplus P_2$. Expanding, $M_3 = M_1M_2/(A_3(P_1 + P_2)^2)$ and $M_1 + M_2 = A_3(P_1 + P_2)^2$, so $1/M_3 = 1/M_1 + 1/M_2$. Expanding again, $(\delta P_3 + U)/M_3 = (\delta P_1 + U)/M_1 + (\delta P_2 + U)/M_2 = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$ by (1). Let $\mu_i = E(X, P_i) \in F(\tau)$. Subtracting and dividing by $P_1 + P_2$: $A_3(P_1^2 + P_1P_2 + P_2^2) + A_2(P_1 + P_2) + A_1 = (\mu_1 + \mu_2)/(P_1 + P_2)$, and the left side is $A_3(P_1 + P_2 + P_3)(P_1 + P_2) + A_2(P_1 + P_2)$. Hence $A_3(P_1 + P_2 + P_3) + A_2 = (\mu_1 + \mu_2)/(P_1 + P_2)^2 \in F(\tau)$, that is $\lambda_{P_3} = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$. So $\lambda_{P_3}M_3 = \delta P_3 + U$ and $P_3$ is a section by (1). In $F(X)(s)$, $s^2 = A_1/A_3$, one has $1/(P_3 + s) = 1/(P_1 + s) + 1/(P_2 + s)$, so $\oplus$ is transported from addition by the injective map $P \mapsto 1/(P + s)$: associativity and commutativity follow. $M_P$ determines $P^2$, hence $P$. (5) By (I2) at $Y = P$ and (1), $A_3\lambda_P^2M_P^2 = \delta^2M_P + \mathrm{Disc}$, which is $\zeta^2 + \zeta = \omega\lambda^2$. Conversely, given $(\lambda, \zeta)$ with $\zeta \ne 0$, put $M = \mathrm{Disc}/(\delta^2\zeta)$ and $P = (\lambda M + U)/\delta$; then $A_3P^2 + A_1 = (\delta^2M + \mathrm{Disc} + A_3U^2 + A_1\delta^2)/\delta^2 = M$, and $(A_3P)' = (\lambda(A_3M)' + (A_3U)')/\delta = \lambda + c_2$ because $(A_3M)' = (A_3^2P^2 + A_1A_3)' = \delta$; so $\lambda_P = \lambda$ and (1) holds. $\square$

Remarks. (i) Geometrically **[not used]**: the cubic over $k = F(\tau)$ has one singular point, at infinity in the direction $(\sqrt\tau : 1)$, with tangent cone $A_3U^2 + A_1z^2$, a double line only over $k^{1/4}$; it is a quasi-elliptic curve, the surface a quasi-elliptic fibration over the $\tau$-line, $\mathcal{S}$ its Mordell-Weil group, and $1/(P + s)$ the additive coordinate. The reducible fibres are over the roots of $\mathrm{Disc}$ (a line, which is $\ell_\alpha$ when the root is a received point, and a conic), and Theorem 2 below is the statement that a section is determined by the fibre components it meets. (ii) $\omega' = 1/\delta$, so the second component of $\zeta^2 + \zeta = \omega\lambda^2$ reads $\zeta' = \lambda^2/\delta$. (iii) The group is in general not finite-dimensional in any useful sense: see Section 4.

### 2.3 Polynomial sections are tight

**Theorem 2 [proved; checked, `43_structure.py A, B`].** Assume $\mathrm{Disc} \ne 0$, $\delta \ne 0$, and let $P \in F[X]$ be a section.

1. $\lambda_P \in F[\tau]$ and $\mathrm{Disc} = M_P\,(A_3\lambda_P^2M_P + \delta^2)$. In particular $M_P$ divides $\mathrm{Disc}$, and $\deg_X\lambda_P = \deg(\delta P + U) - \deg M_P$.
2. Let $\beta \in \overline F$ with $\mathrm{Disc}(\beta) = 0$ and $A_3(\beta)\delta(\beta) \ne 0$. Then $E$ has exactly one critical point above $\beta$, namely $(\beta, Q(\beta))$, and $Q(\beta)^2 = A_1(\beta)/A_3(\beta)$. Conversely every critical point $(\alpha, y)$ of $E$ with $A_3\delta(\alpha) \ne 0$ has $\mathrm{Disc}(\alpha) = 0$ and $y = Q(\alpha)$.
3. For such $\beta$: $P(\beta) = Q(\beta)$ iff $M_P(\beta) = 0$. If $\beta$ is a simple root of $\mathrm{Disc}$ then $\zeta_P(\beta) \in \{0, 1\}$, with value 1 iff $P(\beta) = Q(\beta)$.

*Proof.* (1) $\lambda_P = (A_3P)' + c_2$ is a polynomial with zero derivative, and (I2) with Theorem 1 (1). (2) A critical point has $M = E_X = 0$; by (I1) $\delta y + U = 0$, and then (I2) gives $\mathrm{Disc} = 0$. Conversely if $\mathrm{Disc}(\beta) = 0$ and $y = U/\delta$ at $\beta$, (I2) gives $M(y) = 0$ and (I1) gives $E_X = 0$. (3) $M_P(\beta) = A_3(\beta)(P(\beta)^2 - Q(\beta)^2) = A_3(\beta)(P(\beta) - Q(\beta))^2$. $\mathrm{Disc}' = b_0\delta^2 + b_2U^2 = \delta^2(b_0 + b_2Q^2)$ at $\beta$ and $M_P' = b_2P^2 + b_0$, so if $M_P(\beta) = 0$ and $\mathrm{Disc}'(\beta) \ne 0$ the quotient $\mathrm{Disc}/(\delta^2M_P)$ is 1 at $\beta$. $\square$

Consequences for a word $y$ and a component $\partial E$ of its explainers. Let $\mathrm{Crit}$ be the set of $\alpha \in D$ whose line is contained in $\Sigma_G$ (Proposition 7 of note 35: $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ is a critical point of $E$) and at which $A_3\delta \ne 0$; at most $\deg A_3 + \deg_\tau\delta$ contained lines are lost. **The word is forced on $\mathrm{Crit}$: $y_\alpha = Q(\alpha) = \sqrt{A_1(\alpha)/A_3(\alpha)}$**, the square of the word is a rational function of low degree there. A list member $P \in L_G$ agrees with $y$ on $\mathrm{Crit}$ exactly at the roots of $M_P$ in $\mathrm{Crit}$, $M_P$ divides $\mathrm{Disc}$, and $P \mapsto \lambda_P$ is at most two-to-one ($P$ and $P \oplus Q$) into
$$\Lambda(d) = \{\lambda \in F[\tau] : \deg\lambda \le d,\ \omega\lambda^2 \in \{z^2 + z : z \in F(X)\}\}, \qquad d = \lfloor (W - 2\Delta - 1)/2 \rfloor,$$
an $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space (the bound on $d$ is $\deg_X\lambda_P \le \deg A_3 + \Delta - 1 \le W - 2\Delta - 1$; by (1) also $2\deg_\tau\lambda_P = \deg(\delta P + U) - \deg M_P$, so a list member with many agreements has a $\lambda$ of small degree). Hence
$$|L_G| \le 2\,|\Lambda(d)| . \tag{5}$$

**Lemma D [proved; checked on 44850 pairs of polynomial sections of the flat cubic on $\mathbb{F}_{64}$, `43_structure.py C`].** Let $P_1 \ne P_2$ be polynomial sections. Then every root of $P_1 - P_2$ of odd multiplicity is a root of $A_3$ or a common root of $M_{P_1}$ and $M_{P_2}$. So $P_1 - P_2 = \xi^2\nu$ with $\nu$ squarefree dividing $\mathrm{rad}(A_3)\cdot\gcd(M_{P_1}, M_{P_2})$: up to a square, two list members of one component differ by the locator of common agreement points.

*Proof.* Subtract the two relations of Theorem 1 (1) and use $M_1 = M_2 + A_3(P_1 + P_2)^2$, $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 + (A_3(P_1 + P_2))'$: with $T = P_1 + P_2$, $\delta T = (A_3T)'M_2 + \lambda_1A_3T^2$. So $T$ divides $(A_3'T + A_3T')M_2$, hence $A_3T'M_2$. At a root of $T$ of odd multiplicity $\mu$, $T'$ vanishes to order exactly $\mu - 1$. $\square$

### 2.4 The split case and higher degree

**$\delta = 0$ [proved].** Then $A_1A_3$ is a square, $s = \sqrt{A_1/A_3} \in F(X)$, $M(Y) = c\,(DY + N)^2$ with $s = N/D$, the word is the rational function $s$ on the contained lines, and $DP + N$ vanishes at every agreement of a list member on a contained line. Since $\deg(DP + N) \le (W - \Delta)/2$, such a component carries at most one list member ($P = s$) as soon as $A - (n - n') > (W - \Delta)/2$, (up to the at most $\deg c$ lines where $c$ vanishes), which at rate 1/4, $a = 0.4738$, $W < 3.68\Delta$ holds when more than $0.86\,n$ lines are contained. At lower rates the chord law and the additive coordinate $t = D/(DP + N) \in F(X)$ still exist and the sections are the solutions of an additive equation $(ct)' = \kappa_2t^2 + \kappa_4t^4$; I did not pursue it.

**Lemma T ($b = 1$, any degree) [proved].** Let $\hat G = \partial E$ with $E \in F[X, Y]$ of any degree in $Y$ and $R_E = \mathrm{Res}_Y(E_Y, E_X) \ne 0$. For every polynomial section $P$, $E_Y(X, P)\,P' = E_X(X, P)$ and $E_Y(X, P)$ divides $R_E$. (Write $aE_Y + bE_X = R_E$ with $a, b \in F[X][Y]$ and substitute.) An agreement of $P$ with the word on a contained line is a root of $E_Y(X, P)$. This is all that survives of Theorem 2 when $E$ is not cubic: for $\deg_Y E \ge 4$ the curve is still rational over $F(X)$ but it is no longer a form of the additive group (for $E = A_4Y^4 + \text{cubic}$ the function $\lambda$ acquires a term $c_4t^{-2}$ in the additive coordinate), and a root of $E_Y(X, P)$ need not be an agreement (Section 6.2, $Q8$).

## 3. Counting on a cubic component

**What the structure gives.** (i) Inequality (5): the list on a component injects two-to-one into the $\mathbb{F}_2$-space $\Lambda(d)$, which is the kernel of an explicit $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear map (reduce $\omega\lambda^2$ modulo $\{z^2 + z\}$ at each pole: $c\,T^{-2^v o} \sim c^{2^{-v}}T^{-o}$ for odd $o$). (ii) A Plotkin bound at the top agreement **[proved]**: assume $\mathrm{Disc}$ squarefree and coprime to $A_3\delta$, $R = \deg\mathrm{Disc}$, and for a polynomial section let $B_P$ be the root set of $M_P$ and $m_P = |B_P| = \deg M_P$. The polynomial $N_P = \mathrm{Disc}/M_P$ has degree $R - m_P$ and takes the value $\delta(\beta)^2$ on $B_P$ and 0 on the other roots (Theorem 2 (3)): a list-recovery instance with two values per point and no error. Two of them differ, so $|B_1 \triangle B_2| \ge \min(m_1, m_2)$, and if every member has $m_P \ge m > R/2$ there are at most $2\lfloor m/(2m - R) \rfloor$ of them. For the slack-1 flat cubic $R = n = 2m$ at $m = n/2$ and the Plotkin bound at $d = R/2$ is $2R = 2n$, against the true value $2(n - 1)$; for the flat cubic $R = n - 1 = 2m + 1$.

**Why it stops there [proved].** This is the answer to the question of the brief ("does a Johnson bound for list recovery close the count?"): the condition is $m > R/2$, it fails by one unit at the top agreement of the flat cubic, and below the top agreement nothing of this kind can hold, because on $\mathbb{F}_{2^N}$ the count is superpolynomial (Section 4.2). The inequality $|B_1 \triangle B_2| \ge \min(m_1, m_2)$ is equivalent to $\deg(A_3(P_1 - P_2)^2) \le \max(m_1, m_2)$ and contains no more than "two list members agree on at most $\Delta$ points". The $\mathbb{F}_2$-space $\{N \in F[X] : \deg N \le K,\ N(\beta) \in \{0, \delta(\beta)^2\}\ \text{at the roots of } \mathrm{Disc}\}$ is a binary alternant code of designed distance $R - K$; the list members are its words whose weight equals $R - \deg N$. On $\mathbb{F}_{2^N}$ with the flat cubic it is the narrow-sense primitive BCH code of designed distance $m$ and the list members of agreement $m$ are its words of weight exactly $m$. Counting minimum-weight words of BCH codes of relative designed distance $\tau \in (a_1, 1/2)$ is the residual problem in its cleanest form; I know no bound for it beyond the second-order Reed-Muller range.

## 4. The flat cubic

### 4.1 Dictionary

$D$ an $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of dimension $N$, $L_D = X^n + X^2\gamma_D(X^2) + e_0X$, $E = XY^3 + (X\gamma_D(X^2) + e_0)Y + e_0X^{n/2 - 1}$, word $y = x^{n/2 - 1}$. Then $\delta = e_0$, $U = e_0X^{n/2 - 1}$, $Q = X^{n/2 - 1}$, $\mathrm{Disc} = e_0^2L_D/X$, $\omega = L_D/e_0^2$, $\lambda_P = (XP)' = g_P$, the even part of $P$ **[proved; checked on five domains, `43_structure.py B`]**.

**Proposition 4.1 [proved; checked].** Let $P$ be a polynomial section of degree $< n/2 - 1$.

1. $M_P = XP^2 + X\gamma_D(X^2) + e_0$ divides $L_D/X$; the agreement set of $P$ with $y$ on $D \setminus 0$ is the root set of $M_P$; the agreement is $\deg M_P = n/2 - 1 - 2\deg_\tau g_P$.
2. $z = g_P(X^2)(X^{n/2} + XP)/e_0$ satisfies $z^2 + z = L_D\,g_P(X^2)^2/e_0^2$; it is the indicator of the disagreement set, and $X^{n/2} + XP = \sigma_{B \cup 0}\cdot l^2$ with $l^2 \propto g_P(X^2)$: the roots of $X^{n/2} + XP$ outside $D$ form a perfect square.
3. $P \mapsto g_P$ is injective on polynomial sections ($P \oplus Q = P + e_0/(Xg_P(X^2))$ is not a polynomial).

**Corollary 4.2 [proved].** On every subspace domain, over every field: (a) every polynomial of degree $< n/4$ with $A \ge 3n/8 - 1$ agreements with $x^{n/2 - 1}$ on $D \setminus 0$ has an odd number $n/2 - 1 - 2j$ of them, where $j$ is the degree of its even part, and the list at agreement $\ge A$ has at most $|\Lambda_j| - 1$ members, $j = \lfloor (n/2 - 1 - A)/2 \rfloor$, $\Lambda_j = \{g \in F[\tau]_{\le j} : L_Dg(X^2)^2/e_0^2 \in \{z^2 + z\}\}$. (b) $|\Lambda_0| = n$ ($c \in \Lambda_0$ iff $c$ is a root of an additive polynomial of degree $n$, and the flats give $n - 1$ nonzero elements $e_0/\theta_V$), so **the list at agreement $\ge n/2 - 1$ is exactly the $n - 1$ flats**. (c) With the analysis degree: the slack-1 cubic $E' = Y^3 + (L_D - X^n)Y + e_0X^{n/2 + 1}$ of note 41 satisfies $E'(X, XY) = X^2E(X, Y)$, its sections are $X$ times the sections of $E$, both $XP_V$ and $X(P_V \oplus Q) = XP_V + \theta_V$ are polynomials, and the list of $x^{n/2}$ in degree $\le n/4$ at agreement $n/2$ is exactly these $2(n - 1)$ affine flats; in general the agreement is $n/2 - 2\deg(\text{odd part})$ and the list at agreement $\ge A$ has at most $2|\Lambda_j| - 2$ members **[checked: the 62 and 126 affine flats at $n = 32, 64$]**.

(b) improves the Johnson value $n^2/4$ of note 21 Section 2.1 to the exact count, for this word.

### 4.2 Dense domains: the Gold lists are the polynomial sections

**Proposition 4.3 [proved; the resulting dimensions checked against the kernel computation for $N \le 10$, 33 pairs $(N, j)$ with $j \le n/16$].** Let $D = \mathbb{F}_{2^N} \subseteq F$. For $i < n/4$ write $S(i)$ for the $N$-bit string of $4i + 1$. The condition defining $\Lambda_j$ is: for every $i \le j$, $g_i = g_{\iota(i)}^{2^{v + 2}}$ where $n/4 + i = 2^v o$ with $o$ odd and $\iota(i) = (o - 1)/4$, and $g_i = 0$ if $o \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, if $\iota(i) > j$, or if $i$ is not in the image of $\iota$. On strings, $\iota$ rotates $S(i)$ past its first 1. Hence $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}\Lambda_j$ is the number of nonzero cyclic binary strings of length $N$ without two cyclically adjacent ones, all of whose rotations ending in 1 are at most $4j + 1$. For $j = n/8 - 1$ this is the Lucas number $L_N - 1 \approx n^{0.694}$; for $j \le n/64$ (the window) it is about $n^{0.405}$.

So on a dense domain the group of sections with polynomial $\lambda$ has $2^{n^{0.4}}$ elements already in the window, and (5) is useless there. The polynomial ones are few, and they are a known family. For $g \in \Lambda_j$ the two sections are $P = (e_0z/g(X^2) + X^{n/2})/X$ and $P \oplus Q$; `43_flat_sections.py` enumerates $\Lambda_j$ and keeps the polynomials of degree $< n/4$:

| domain | $\dim\Lambda_j$ ($j$) | polynomial sections of degree $< n/4$, by agreement | $2^{2t}(2^\Delta - 1)\binom{\lfloor N/2 \rfloor}{t}_4 \cdot T/n$ of [DKTZ26, Thm 5.1] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathbb{F}_{16}$ | 6 (1) | 7: 15; 5: 18 | |
| $\mathbb{F}_{32}$ | 10 (2) | 15: 31; 11: 186 | $t = 2$: $496 \cdot 12/32 = 186$ |
| $\mathbb{F}_{64}$ | 15 (4) | 31: 63; 27: 196; 23: 1890 | $t = 3$: $448 \cdot 28/64 = 196$; $t = 2$: $5040 \cdot 24/64 = 1890$ |
| $\mathbb{F}_{128}$ | 14 (4) | 63: 127; 55: 3556 | $t = 3$: $8128 \cdot 56/128 = 3556$ |

**[checked]** The last column is the count of Gold sets $E$ (size $T = n/2 - n/2^{t+1}$, locator $U^2 + cX$) through 0; a Gold codeword $U$ with $0 \in E$ is $X(P - \gamma_D^{1/2})$ for a list member $P$ of the flat word at agreement $T - 1$ (because $x^{n/2 - 1} = \gamma_D^{1/2}(x) + \sqrt{e_0/x}$ on $D \setminus 0$ **[proved]**), and the counts agree in every case. By Section 3 they are words of weight equal to the designed distance in the narrow-sense BCH codes of lengths 31, 63, 127 and designed distances 11, 27 and 23, 55. **So the Gold lists are sections of the flat cubic, including at $t = 2$ where the agreement $3n/8 - 1$ is at the threshold of Proposition 5 part 4.**

**Inside the window [checked, `43_gold_window.py`].** $D = \mathbb{F}_{2^{11}}$, $n = 2048$, $a_1n = 960.1$. $\Lambda_{16}$ has dimension 22 ($\lambda = g_0 + g_8\tau^8 + g_{16}\tau^{16}$). Of 2000 sections drawn with $g_{16} \ne 0$, 257 are polynomials of degree $495 < 512$; each agrees with $x^{1023}$ on exactly 991 points ($0.4839\,n$), satisfies $E(X, P) \in F[X^2]$ and $XP^2 + 1 \mid X^{2047} + 1$. The observed density $0.129$ against $496/4096 = 0.121$ predicted by the formula ($t = 5$: $2^{10}\cdot 2047\cdot 992/2048 = 1015312$ codewords). So the list of the flat word at $0.4839\,n$ is about 500 times the number of flats at $n = 2048$, and by [DKTZ26, Cor. 5.2] **[quoted]** it is $n^{\Omega(\log n)}$ at every fixed $\tau < 1/2$ as $N \to \infty$ (the maximum over $t$ of the formula is about $2^{N^2/8}$: $2^{108}$ at $N = 25$). This is consistent with every row of the rate-1/4 table of note 21 and answers the dichotomy of the brief: **on dense subspace domains the sections of an economical cubic close to the word are superpolynomially many.**

## 5. Rate 1/4: classification, and what is and is not obtained

**Proposition 5.1 [proved, given Lemma C, Proposition F and Lemmas R, U of notes 35 and 41; the finite inequalities by `43_rates.py C`, exact integers].** $F$ perfect of characteristic 2, $n = 2^{25}$, $D \subset F$ any $n$ points, $\Delta = n/4 + 5$, $(a, \text{certificate}, B_{\mathrm{jet}}) = (0.4738, (43, 19), 81)$ or $(0.4788, (22, 9), 42)$, $y$ any word, $\mathbb{E}$ its explainer space. Let $\mathcal{G}_1$ be the set of fixed components $\partial E$ of $\mathbb{E}$ with $E$ cubic, $\mathrm{Disc}_E \ne 0$, total contact $\le 2n$ and $3\Delta \le W < 3.675\Delta$ (resp. $3.598\Delta$), and $\mathcal{G}_{>}$ the set of absolutely irreducible fixed components with total contact $> 2n$. Then
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, an)| \le C_0 + \sum_{G \in \mathcal{G}_1} |L_G| + \sum_{G \in \mathcal{G}_{>}} |L_G|, \qquad C_0 = 39528 \ (\text{resp. } 10584),$$
$|\mathcal{G}_1| \le B_{\mathrm{jet}}/3$, and each $L_G$, $G \in \mathcal{G}_1$ with $\delta_E \ne 0$, consists of tight polynomial sections in the sense of Theorem 2, with $|L_G| \le 2|\Lambda_E(\lfloor 0.84\Delta \rfloor)|$ (for $\delta_E = 0$ see Section 2.4).

*Proof.* Proposition F. A fixed factor with total contact $\le 2n$ and $b \ge 1$ satisfies $\sum_\alpha(R(m, B) - R(m - o_\alpha, B - b)) \le n(R(m, B) - R(m - 2, B - b))$ (Lemma U (a)), so it is cut as soon as $N(B, mA) - N(B - b, mA - W) > n(R(m, B) - R(m - 2, B - b))$ for one pair $(m, B)$ with $\Delta(m + B) \le mA - (m_0A - 1)$. If its leading coefficient involves $Y_0$ then $W \ge (b + 2)\Delta$ (Lemma L), and the script finds such a pair at $W = (b + 2)\Delta$ for every $2 \le b \le B_\partial$, and for $b = 1$ from $W = 3.675\Delta$ (resp. $3.598\Delta$) on. If its leading coefficient is in $F[X]$ then $W \ge b\Delta + sn$ with $s = \sum o_\alpha/2n \le 1$ (Lemma L), both sides are concave, respectively linear, in $s$, and the script verifies the inequality at $s = 0$ and $s = 1$ for one pair, for every $b \le B_\partial$. All pairs have $m \le 258$ (resp. 132), which gives at most $488\,\delta$ (resp. $252\,\delta$) members on a cut factor; factors with $b = 0$ give $\delta$, factors that are not absolutely irreducible $\delta^2/4$. A $b = 1$ factor with $W < 4\Delta$ and leading coefficient involving $Y_0$ is $\partial E$ with $E$ cubic (a monomial $X^iY^j$ of $E$ with $j \ge 4$ contributes weighted degree $\ge 4\Delta$ to $\partial E$). $\square$

The same computation on a grid ($x = n'/n$ the fraction of contained lines, simple contact, `43_rates.py D`) shows where the components of $\mathcal{G}_1$ live: at $a = 0.4738$ the open region for $W$ is empty for $x \le 0.8$, $[0.750, 0.775)\,n$ at $x = 0.85$, $[0.750, 0.823)\,n$ at $x = 0.9$ and $[0.750, 0.919)\,n$ at $x = 1$, so more than about $0.83\,n$ lines are contained. A naive parameter count (free coefficients of $A_3, A_1, c_2, c_0$ plus the $n$ values of the word, against two conditions per contained line) gives a family of words with an uncut cubic of dimension about $0.45\,n$ to $0.85\,n$ on any evaluation set **[heuristic]**: such components are not rare, and the flat cubic sits at the bottom corner $W = 3\Delta + 1$, $x = 1$.

**What is obtained at rate 1/4.** Not $H_K(\tau, b)$. Obtained: the reduction of the list above $a_1$ to (i) tight sections of at most $B_{\mathrm{jet}}/3$ fixed cubic components and (ii) the components of total contact $> 2n$ (open at every rate, notes 35 and 41), with the explicit constant $C_0$; the structure of (i) (Theorems 1 and 2, Lemma D); the exact lists of the flat word in the cases of Section 4; and the fact that (i) cannot be bounded by a polynomial on every evaluation set. **Check against the known lower bounds:** $n - 1$ at $n/2 - 1$ (equality, Corollary 4.2); $2(n - 1)$ at $n/2$ with slack 1 (equality); Gold lists on dense domains (they are sections; nothing here bounds them); $56n - 392$ at $3n/8$ for the complement word of note 21 (a different word, and below $a_1$); on the leanVM shape the predictions of note 01 for $t = 4$ ($15n/32 = 0.46875\,n$) are just below $a_1n = 0.468792\,n$, so in the window the only known members are the flats. Nothing claimed here is an upper bound that these could contradict.

**Proposition 6.2 (rate 1/8: the three examples carry nothing) [proved].** (a) $C4$: for every polynomial $P$, the agreements with $x^{n/2 - 1}$ on $D \setminus 0$ are the roots in $D$ of $M_P = X(P + \gamma_D^{1/2})^2 + e_0$, of degree $2\deg(P + \gamma_D^{1/2}) + 1$, and $\deg\gamma_D^{1/2} \in \{n/4 - 1, n/8 - 1, \dots\}$. So no polynomial of degree $< n/8$ has an agreement in the window. (b) $Q8$: a polynomial of degree $< n/4 - 1$ agrees with $x^{n/4 - 1}$ on at most $n/4 - 1$ points of $D \setminus 0$ (the roots of $X^{n/4} + XP$). ### 6.3 Rate 1/16: lists in the window

**Proposition 6.3 ($\mathbb{F}_4$-flats) [proved; checked at $n = 64$, all 21 hyperplanes, `43_structure.py D`].** Let $F \supseteq \mathbb{F}_4$ and $D \subset F$ an $\mathbb{F}_4$-subspace, $n = |D|$. For each of the $(n - 1)/3$ $\mathbb{F}_4$-hyperplanes $V$ of $D$, $L_V$ is a 4-polynomial, so $P_V = (L_V - X^{n/4})/X$ has degree $\le n/16 - 1$ and agrees with $x^{n/4 - 1}$ exactly on $V \setminus 0$: $(n - 1)/3$ codewords of the rate-1/16 code at agreement $n/4 - 1$. They are the sections with constant even part of the quintic $E_4 = X^3Y^5 + ((L_D - X^n)/X)\,Y + e_0X^{n/4 - 1}$ ($Q8$ with $\theta = 0$; for an $\mathbb{F}_4$-subspace $L_D$ is a 4-polynomial and $\deg((L_D - X^n)/X) = n/4 - 1$), which contains the $n - 1$ lines and has $W = 5\Delta + 3$, efficiency $0.156$ against $e^* \ge 0.20$: uncut in the whole window.

This is the flats lemma of note 00 for $\mathbb{F}_4$-linear $V$: agreement $4\rho n - 1$ instead of $2\rho n - 1$. As for the flats at rate 1/4, the exact Johnson bound still applies at $n/4 - 1$ (it gives about $3n/8$), so this is the boundary, not yet a list below it.

**Proposition 6.4 (a list inside the window) [proved; checked, `43_rate16_family.py`].** Let $n = 4^{N'}$ with $N' \ge 4$ even, $r = \sqrt n$, $j = r/4$, $D = \mathbb{F}_n$, $w(x) = x^{n/4}$. For $\mu_0 \in \mathbb{F}_n$ and $\mu_1 \in \mathbb{F}_n^*$ with $\mu_1^4 \in \mathbb{F}_r$ put $\mu = \mu_0 + \mu_1X^j$ and $z = \mathrm{Tr}_{n/4}(\mu_0^4X) + \mathrm{Tr}_{r/4}(\mu_1^4X^{r + 1})$ (as polynomials). Then there is exactly one $\eta \in \mathbb{F}_4$ such that $\mu^4$ divides $z + \eta$, and $P = X^{n/4} + (z + \eta)/\mu$ is a polynomial of degree $n/16 - 3r/16$ that agrees with $w$ on exactly $n/4 - 3r/4$ points of $D$. The pairs $(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ and $(\omega\mu_0, \omega\mu_1)$, $\omega \in \mathbb{F}_4^*$, give the same $P$ and no others do, so $w$ has at least $(r - 1)\,n/3$ codewords of degree $< n/16$ at agreement $(1/4 - 3/(4\sqrt n))\,n$. All are sections of $R$ with $c = 1$.

*Proof.* Telescoping, $z^4 + z = \mu_0^4(X^n + X) + \mu_1^4X^r(X^n + X) = (X^n + X)\mu^4$ (using $\mu_0^n = \mu_0$, $\mu_1^{4r} = \mu_1^4$, $r^2 = n$, $4j = r$), and $\deg z = n/4 + j$. So $\prod_{\eta \in \mathbb{F}_4}(z + \eta) = \mu_1^4(X - x_0)^r\prod_{x \in \mathbb{F}_n}(X - x)$ with $x_0 = (\mu_0/\mu_1)^{1/j} \in \mathbb{F}_n$ the only root of $\mu$. The four factors are pairwise coprime, each $x \in \mathbb{F}_n$ is a simple root of exactly one, and the factor $(X - x_0)^r$ goes entirely to the one with $\eta = z(x_0)$. For this $\eta$, $M = (z + \eta)/\mu^4$ is, up to a constant, the locator of $E = \{x : z(x) = \eta\}$, of size $n/4 + j - 4j$. The leading coefficient of $(z + \eta)/\mu$ is $\mu_1^{r - 1} = 1$ (its fourth power is $(\mu_1^4)^{r - 1} = 1$), so $\deg P < n/4$. Then $P^4 = X^n + (z^4 + \eta)/\mu^4 = X^n + M + X^n + X$, that is $P^4 + X = M$: $\deg P = \deg M/4$, and for $x \in \mathbb{F}_n$, $P(x) = x^{n/4}$ iff $P(x)^4 = x$ iff $M(x) = 0$. Finally $P' = \mu^3M' = \mu^3$ ($j$ is even, so $\mu' = 0$, and $M' = 1$), which determines $\mu$ up to $\mathbb{F}_4^*$, and $\mathrm{Tr}_{n/4}$ is $\mathbb{F}_4$-linear. $(P^4 + X)P' = \mu^3M = P + X^{n/4}$ is the section equation of $R$. $\square$

**[checked]** $n = 256$: all 3840 pairs $(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ give a polynomial, 1280 distinct ones, degree $13 < 16$, agreement 52. $n = 4096$: 12 random pairs, degree $244 < 256$, agreement $976 = 0.2383\,n > a_1(1/16)\,n = 893.3$; the proposition gives 86016 codewords there. For $N = 28$ it gives $2^{40.4}$ codewords at $0.24995\,n$. More Gold exponents (level sets of $\mathbb{F}_4$-quadratic forms of rank $2t < N'$) should give larger lists at agreement $1/4 - 3\cdot 4^{-t - 1}$, $t \ge 3$ **[conjectured; only $t = N'/2$ is proved]**.

**Consequence.** At rate 1/16 the statement "every list in $(a_1, \sqrt\rho)$ is bounded by a constant, on every evaluation set" is false: on $\mathbb{F}_{4^{N'}}$ ($N'$ even, $n \ge 4096$) there are lists of size $n^{3/2}/3$ in the window, all on one economical quintic first integral with $b = 1$. The structure is the 4-ary analogue of Section 4: $(\mu, z)$ with $z^4 + z = (X^n + X)\mu^4$ is an $\mathbb{F}_4$-vector space, $P = X^{n/4} + z/\mu$, agreement $n/4 - 3\deg\mu$. The analogue for rate $1/q^2$ is the word $x^{1/q}$ on $\mathbb{F}_q$-structured domains. Rate 1/8 is not of this form, and the paddings of these families to rate 1/8 reach $0.3125 < a_1(1/8)$.

## 8. The remaining cases, exactly

1. **Rate 1/4.** $H_K(\tau, b)$ for $\tau \in (a_1, 1/2)$ would follow, with $2^b = C_0 + 27\,T + (\text{item 4})$, from a bound $T$ on the number of **tight sections of a cubic**: for $A_3, A_1 \in F[X]$, $c_2, c_0 \in F[\tau]$ with $\deg A_3 < 0.68\Delta$, $\deg A_1 < 2.68\Delta$ and $\mathrm{Disc}$ vanishing on more than $0.8\,n$ points of $D$, the number of $P$ of degree $\le \Delta$ such that $A_3P^2 + A_1$ divides $\mathrm{Disc}$, has at least $0.27\,n$ roots in $D$, and $(\delta P + U)/(A_3P^2 + A_1) \in F[X^2]$. On $\mathbb{F}_{2^N}$, $T \ge n^{(\log_2 n)/8}$; on the leanVM shape the only known members are the flats and their translates. A proof must therefore use the domain, or aim at $2^{O(N^2)}$; it cannot be linear over $\mathbb{F}_2$ at level-0 sizes (Section 4.3), and by Section 3 it is a statement about minimum-weight words of alternant codes. Lemma D (differences are squares times common locators) is the one structural fact found here that has not been exploited.
2. **Rate 1/8.** Either an example or a bound for: $b = 1$ with $E = A_4Y^4 + \text{cubic}$, $W < 4.99\Delta$ (Theorems 1, 2 when $A_4 = 0$, Lemma T otherwise); $b = 2$ in the narrow region of Section 6.1. No structured word is known with a list above the trivial ones in $(0.319, 0.354)$.
3. **Rate 1/16.** The constant bound is false on $\mathbb{F}_4$-structured dense domains. On other domains: $b = 1$ with $\deg_Y E \le 6$ and $W < 6.85\Delta$, $b = 2$ with $W < 6.39\Delta$, $b = 3$ with $W < 5.76\Delta$ (closed for simple contact). The sparse-domain analogue of Proposition 6.4 (an $\mathbb{F}_4$-subspace of $K$, or a subspace with accidental $\mathbb{F}_4$-structure) should be counted as note 01 counted the Gold family.
4. **All rates.** Fixed absolutely irreducible components with total contact $> 2n$: untouched here, see note 41 Section 7 and researcher 40.
