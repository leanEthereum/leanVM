## 1. Setup and notation

$K$ a field of characteristic 2, $D \subset K$ an $\mathbb F_2$-subspace of dimension $N$, $n = 2^N$, $C = \mathrm{RS}_k(D) = \{f|_D : \deg f < k\}$. Three kinds of $D$ are distinguished throughout, because they do NOT behave alike (this is the first finding of the note): **(P)** the polynomial-basis subspace $D_N = \langle 1, x, \dots, x^{N-1}\rangle$ of $K = \mathbb F_2[x]/(x^{64}+x^4+x^3+x+1)$, which is today's code; **(G)** a random subspace of GF($2^{64}$) (this is what the letter S meant in notes 69 and 74: `69_tree.h` draws `beta[l] = gf_rand()`); **(F)** a subfield or another arithmetically special subspace. For a set $S \subseteq D$, $\Lambda_S(y) = \prod_{s \in S}(y + s)$; $\Lambda_D$ is linearized and $\Lambda_D' = c_0 = \prod_{d \ne 0} d$ is a constant. For a subspace $V$, $W_V = \Lambda_V$.

A pattern gives each point of $D$ a class (a set of at least two of the members $0..L$, or nothing); $S_l$ is the set of points whose class contains $l$; $w = \sum_x (c_x - 1)_+$ is the weight. $Q_t$: any $t$ distinct codewords have at most $(t-1)k - 1$ points at which at least two of them coincide.

## 2. Proposition 4.1 of note 74 in Reed-Solomon form (task 2, first half)

**Lemma 2.1 (relations are polynomial identities; proved, for every evaluation set, subspace or not).** Let $D \subseteq K$ be any set of $n$ points, $C = \mathrm{RS}_k(D)$, and a pattern with agreement sets $S_0, \dots, S_L$. The relations among the rows of the incidence system are exactly the tuples of polynomials $(p_0, \dots, p_L)$ with $\deg p_l < n - k$, $p_l = 0$ on $D \setminus S_l$, and $\sum_l p_l = 0$ as polynomials. Equivalently $p_l = \Lambda_{D \setminus S_l} h_l$ with $\deg h_l < |S_l| - k$ and $\sum_l \Lambda_{D \setminus S_l} h_l = 0$. So with $\mathrm{rel}$ the dimension of that space, the kernel of the pattern has dimension $s = Lk - w + \mathrm{rel}$ and the excess over the generalized Singleton bound is $w - (Lk - 1) = \mathrm{rel} - (s - 1)$. *Proof.* By note 74 Prop. 4.1 a relation is a tuple of dual codewords $\mu_l$ supported in $S_l$ with $\sum_l \mu_l = 0$. The dual of $\mathrm{RS}_k(D)$ is $\{(u_x p(x))_x : \deg p < n - k\}$ with $u_x = 1/\Lambda_D'(x) \ne 0$. The multiplier $u_x$ is the same in every $\mu_l$, so $\sum_l \mu_l = 0$ iff $\sum_l p_l(x) = 0$ for all $x \in D$, and a polynomial of degree $< n - k < n$ vanishing on $D$ is zero. $\square$ The dual multipliers play no role: **a list beyond the generalized Singleton bound is a linear relation between polynomials of degree below $n - k$ with prescribed zeros in $D$** (the zeros of $p_l$ contain the complement of the agreement set of member $l$). This is a "dual list": the $p_l$ are $L + 1$ codewords of the dual-dimension code $\mathrm{RS}_{n-k}(D)$ that sum to zero, $p_l$ vanishing where member $l$ disagrees.

**Corollary 2.2 (three members; proved).** For three members write $A, B, C$ for the points where exactly the pairs $\{0,1\}, \{0,2\}, \{1,2\}$ agree and $T$ for the points where all three agree ($a, b, c, t$ their sizes, $\sigma = a + b + c$). A syzygy of $(\Lambda_A, \Lambda_B, \Lambda_C)$ of degree $e$ is a triple $(u, v, w) \ne 0$ with $u \Lambda_A + v \Lambda_B + w \Lambda_C = 0$ and $\max(\deg u + a, \deg v + b, \deg w + c) = e$. The three polynomials are pairwise coprime, so (Hilbert-Burch, or the $\mu$-basis of a rational curve) the syzygy module is free on two generators of degrees $e_1 \le e_2$ with $e_1 + e_2 = \sigma$, and the syzygies of degree $\le d$ form a space of dimension $(d + 1 - e_1)_+ + (d + 1 - e_2)_+$. Then: (a) the kernel of the pattern (pairs of differences $(f, g)$, $f = 0$ on $A \cup T$, $g = 0$ on $B \cup T$, $f + g = 0$ on $C \cup T$, degrees $< k$) is $\{\Lambda_T \cdot (u \Lambda_A, v \Lambda_B)\}$ over the syzygies of degree $\le k - 1 - t$, of dimension $(k - t - e_1)_+ + (k - t - e_2)_+$; (b) the relations of Lemma 2.1 are the syzygies of degree $\le \sigma + t - k - 1$; (c) the pattern carries three distinct codewords iff some syzygy of degree $\le k - 1 - t$ has three nonzero components; when $a, b, c \le k - 1 - t$ this is the condition $e_1 \le k - 1 - t$ (a syzygy with a zero component is a multiple of a Koszul one such as $(0, \Lambda_C, \Lambda_B)$; if the generator of degree $e_1$ is of that kind then $e_2$ is the size of the third set, which is $\le k - 1 - t$, and the sum of the two generators has three nonzero components). *Proof.* (a) is the definition; (b) follows from Lemma 2.1 with $p_0 = \Lambda_E \Lambda_C w$, $p_1 = \Lambda_E \Lambda_B v$, $p_2 = \Lambda_E \Lambda_A u$, $E$ the points with no class. $\square$

**Corollary 2.3 (what $Q_3$ and the three-member weight are; proved).** For an evaluation set $D$ and a dimension $k \le n/2$: (a) $Q_3$ fails iff some disjoint $A, B, C \subseteq D$ of at most $k - 1$ points each have $e_1 \le k - 1$ and $e_2 \ge k + 1$ (take $t = \max(0, 2k - \sigma) \le k - 1 - e_1$ further points as $T$; conversely $\sigma + t \ge 2k$ and $e_1 \le k - 1 - t$ give $e_2 = \sigma - e_1 \ge k + 1$); (b) the largest weight of a pattern carrying three distinct codewords is the maximum of $\sigma + 2 \min(k - 1 - e_1,\, n - \sigma)$ over the disjoint triples with $e_1 \le k - 1$, which is $2k - 2 + (e_2 - e_1)$ when there is room for $k - 1 - e_1$ common zeros; since $a, b, c \le e_1$ forces $e_2 \le 2 e_1$, the extreme case $e_2 = 2 e_1$ is a pencil: three disjoint sets of the same size $e$ with $\Lambda_A, \Lambda_B, \Lambda_C$ linearly dependent; (c) in terms of the rational function $\varphi = f/g$: $Q_3$ fails iff some rational function of degree $e \in [k/2 + 1, k - 1]$ takes values in $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ on at least $k + 1 + e$ points of $D$ (a function of degree $e$ has at most $3e$ such points; the lifts $\varphi = \psi \circ W_V$, $|V| = k/2$, have $e = k/2$ and $3k/2$ points, one point short of a violation); (d) at rate 1/2, $Q_3$ fails iff there are $f, g$ of degree $< n/2$, $f, g, f + g \ne 0$, with $\Lambda_D \mid f g (f + g)$, that is, iff a two-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-space of polynomials of degree $< n/2$ has its Moore determinant $f g^2 + f^2 g$ vanishing on $D$.

## 3. $Q_3$ is FALSE for today's code (task 3)

**Theorem 3.1 ($Q_3$ fails for the polynomial-basis subspace; proved by the explicit instance, and exact at the stated size).** Let $D_3 = \langle 1, x, x^2 \rangle$, $k = 4$, $n = 8$ (rate 1/2), in any field of characteristic 2 in which $1, x, x^2$ are independent. Put $c = x^2 + x + 1$, $A = \{1, x^2, x^2 + 1\}$, $B = \{x, x + 1, x^2 + x\}$, $f = \Lambda_A$, $g = \Lambda_B$. Then $f + g = c\, y (y + c)$. So the three codewords $0, f, g$ of $\mathrm{RS}_4(D_3)$ coincide pairwise at all 8 points of $D_3$ (0 and $f$ on $A$, 0 and $g$ on $B$, $f$ and $g$ on $\{0, c\}$), against the $2k - 1 = 7$ that $Q_3$ allows. *Proof.* $A \cup \{0\} = V := \langle 1, x^2 \rangle$ and $B \cup \{c\} = c + V$, so $f = W_V(y)/y$, $g = (W_V(y) + W_V(c))/(y + c)$ with $W_V(y) = (y^2 + y)(y^2 + y + x^4 + x^2) = y^4 + c^2 y^2 + \alpha_0 y$, $\alpha_0 = x^4 + x^2$, and $W_V(c) = c^4 + c^4 + \alpha_0 c = \alpha_0 c$. Hence $f + g = \big(c W_V(y) + y W_V(c)\big) / \big(y (y + c)\big) = c (y^4 + c^2 y^2)/(y(y + c)) = c\, y (y + c)$. $\square$ Checked in GF($2^{64}$) by `scripts/77_q3_48.py` (zeros of $f$: $\{1, 4, 5\}$, of $g$: $\{2, 3, 6\}$, of $f + g$: $\{0, 7\}$ as integers).

The mechanism (proved): for a hyperplane $V$ of a subspace $D$ and $c \in D \setminus V$, the pair $f = W_V/y$, $g = (W_V + W_V(c))/(y + c)$ has $f + g = c\,(W_V(y) + W_V'(0)\, y)/(y(y+c))$ exactly when $W_V(c) = c\, W_V'(0)$ (the secant of $W_V$ from 0 to $c$ is its tangent at 0); in characteristic 2, $W_V(y) + W_V'(0) y = M_V(y)^2$ is the square of a linearized polynomial, the condition reads $M_V(c) = 0$, and then $f, g, f + g$ have $(n/2 - 1) + (n/2 - 1) + 2 = n$ zeros in $D$ with degrees $n/2 - 1$. For $V = \langle p, q \rangle$: $\ker M_V = \{0, p + q + \sqrt{pq}\}$, so the condition is that **$pq$ is the square of an element $r$ of $D$ with $D = \langle p, q, r \rangle$: a three-term geometric progression spanning $D$**. $D_3 = \langle 1, x, x^2 \rangle$ is one by construction. A random three-dimensional subspace is not. So $Q_3$ is a statement about the arithmetic of $D$, not about subspaces in general; two more instances of kind (F), for orientation (**proved** by inspection): on $D = \mathbb F_8$, $f = y^3 + y + 1$, $g = y^3 + y^2 + 1$, $f + g = y^2 + y$ have product $y^8 + y$, so $Q_3$ fails at $(4, 8)$; on $D = \mathbb F_{16}$, the three polynomials $y^5 + \zeta$, $\zeta^3 = 1$, lie in a pencil and have the 15 nonzero elements as roots, so $Q_3$ fails at $(8, 16)$ after adding one common zero.

**Theorem 3.3 ($Q_3$ fails for today's code at rate 1/2 at EVERY size $N \ge 3$, with about $2^{n/2}$ violating triples; proved).** Let $D$ be a subspace, $n = 2k$, $c \in D \setminus 0$, and $A \subset D \setminus \{0, c\}$ with $|A| = k - 1$, $B = D \setminus (A \cup \{0, c\})$. Then $0$, $f = \Lambda_A$, $g = \gamma \Lambda_B$ (for the right $\gamma$) coincide pairwise somewhere at every point of $D$ **iff $\prod_{a \in A} a = \prod_{a \in A} (a + c)$**. *Proof.* $f + g$ must vanish at $0$ and $c$, that is $\Lambda_A(0)/\Lambda_B(0) = \Lambda_A(c)/\Lambda_B(c)$. From $\Lambda_D = \Lambda_A \Lambda_B\, y (y + c)$ and $\Lambda_D' = c_0$ constant: $c\,\Lambda_A(0) \Lambda_B(0) = c_0 = c\,\Lambda_A(c) \Lambda_B(c)$. Multiplying the two identities, the condition is $\Lambda_A(0)^2 = \Lambda_A(c)^2$, and squaring is injective. $\square$ A pair $\{a, a + c\} \subset A$ contributes the same factor to both sides. So $A$ = (any union of pairs $\{a, a + c\}$) $\cup\ S$ with $S$ an odd set meeting each pair at most once and $\prod_{a \in S} a/(a + c) = 1$. For $|S| = 3$ the condition is $(a_1 + c)(a_2 + c) = a_3 (a_1 + a_2 + c)$: **every multiplicative quadruple $pq = rs$ in $D$ gives one** ($c = p + q + s$, $S = \{q + s, p + s, r\}$, provided $c \ne 0$ and the three elements lie in three different pairs other than $\{0, c\}$). In $D_N = \langle 1, x, \dots, x^{N-1} \rangle$, $N \ge 3$: $x \cdot (x + 1) = 1 \cdot (x^2 + x)$ gives $c = x^2 + x + 1$, $S = \{1, x^2, x^2 + 1\}$, $S \cup (S + c) \cup \{0, c\} = D_3$, and $D_N \setminus D_3$ is a union of $n/2 - 4$ pairs: any half of them, with $S$, is a set $A$. That is $\binom{n/2 - 4}{n/4 - 2}$ triples of codewords of $\mathrm{RS}_{n/2}(D_N)$ violating $Q_3$ from this one quadruple, at every $N \ge 3$, in particular at $N = 19$ to $26$ (no reduction modulo the field polynomial is involved: the identity lives in degree 4). At $N = 3$ this is Theorem 3.1.

**What the failure does and does not mean (proved).** (a) At rate 1/2, $Q_3$ only says that three codewords cannot have a coincidence at every point, and the three-member weight bound does not need it: $w = \sigma + 2t \le n + t$ and $w \le 3(k-1) - t$ give $w \le 5k/2 - 2$ for every MDS code with $n = 2k$. So Theorem 4.4 of note 74 **holds for today's code at rate 1/2** (trivially), and the lifted list of note 69 is optimal there. (b) The violating triples of Theorem 3.3 are not large lists: their agreements with the word they define are $(n - 2, n/2 + 1, n/2 + 1)$. They are three codewords with weight exactly $n = 2k$, one above the generalized Singleton value $2k - 1$. (c) What dies is the method: for today's code the incidence rows of a list with one functional per leaf are NOT independent, already for three members, at every size, so no proof of the type of Theorem 4.2 of note 74 (and none of its consequences: Corollary 4.3, the linear programs of notes 74 and 75, the list bound below Johnson at rates $\le 1/8$) can hold for today's code as stated; and the deficient patterns of today's code are not block-aligned (the sets $A$ above are arbitrary unions of pairs plus three special points). (d) A random subspace has no multiplicative quadruple at toy size (Experiment 3.2: no violation), and one expects about $n^4/2^{64}$ of them, $2^{12}$ to $2^{40}$ at $N = 19$ to $26$ (**estimated**; Cauchy-Schwarz proves a non-trivial one only for $N \ge 33$). This does not matter: at the real sizes $Q_3$ fails on every subspace, and for every MDS code, for a cruder reason (Theorem 3.4).

**Theorem 3.4 (over GF($2^{64}$), $Q_3$ fails for EVERY linear MDS code of dimension $k \ge 65$ and length $n \ge 2k$, and with slack up to $k/63$; proved).** Let $C$ be a linear MDS code of dimension $k$ over a field $K$ with $[K : \mathbb F_2] = m$, $A$ any $k - 1$ positions, $f$ the codeword (unique up to a scalar) vanishing on $A$, $s \ge 0$, and $S$ any $k + 1 + s$ positions outside $A$. If $k + 1 + s - m(s + 1) \ge 2$, there is a codeword $g \notin \{0, f\}$ with $g = 0$ or $g = f$ at every position of $S$; so $0, f, g$ coincide pairwise at $2k + s$ positions. *Proof.* The code restricted to $S$ is MDS of dimension $k$ and its dual $C^\perp(S)$ (dual codewords supported in $S$) has dimension $s + 1$, basis $\mu^{(0)}, \dots, \mu^{(s)}$. For $X \subseteq S$ the word ($f$ on $X$, $0$ on $S \setminus X$) extends to a codeword iff $\sum_{c \in X} \mu^{(i)}_c f_c = 0$ for all $i$. This is an $\mathbb F_2$-linear map from $\mathbb F_2^{S}$ to $K^{s+1} \cong \mathbb F_2^{m(s+1)}$ (characteristic 2: a subset sum is a linear combination), with kernel of dimension at least $k + 1 + s - m(s+1) \ge 2$, so it contains some $X \ne \emptyset, S$. Then $g \ne 0$ ($f \ne 0$ off $A$) and $g \ne f$ ($g = 0 \ne f$ on $S \setminus X$). $\square$ With $m = 64$: $s = 0$ needs $k \ge 65$ (so every MDS code of rate 1/2 and length $\ge 130$ over GF($2^{64}$) violates $Q_3$, for every $A$, in at least $2^{k - 63} - 2$ ways), and $s \le (k - 65)/63$: **at rates below 1/2 three codewords coincide on $(2 + 1/63) k$ positions.** For Reed-Solomon codes $\mu_c f_c = \Lambda_A(c)^2 / \Lambda_D'(c)$ at rate 1/2, and on a subspace the criterion is simply: $A$, $X$ with $|A| = n/2 - 1$ and $\sum_{c \in X} \Lambda_A(c) = 0$ (Theorem 3.3 is the case $|X| = 2$). Checked by construction and interpolation at $k = 128$ on $D_8$, on a random subspace, on 256 random points, and with $s = 1$ at $n = 512$ (`scripts/77_q3_pigeonhole.py`: 65 independent dependencies found, 65 predicted). **Consequence for the tree-code programme of notes 68 to 75 (proved):** the "random version of $Q_t$" asked for in note 75 cannot hold over GF($2^{64}$) without a slack of at least $k/63$ already for $t = 3$, whatever the twiddles (T1, T2, random, subspace): $Q_t$ is a statement about transcendental twiddles only. This is a property of the 64-bit field, not of today's code; the multiplicative violations of Theorem 3.3 are a property of today's code and exist from $N = 3$.

## 5. Which relations exist: the aligned class is closed, and where an arbitrary set escapes (task 2, second half)

**Lemma 5.1 (lists as a Hilbert function; proved, any evaluation set).** Let $R_l = D \setminus S_l$ be the disagreement set of member $l$ ($r_l = |R_l|$), and assume that every point has a class ($\bigcup_l S_l = D$) and $|S_l| \ge k$ for all $l$ (otherwise replace $D$ by the set $S$ of points that have a class: the code punctured to $S$ is the Reed-Solomon code on $S$, and a member with $|S_l| < k$ adds $k - |S_l|$ to the dimension). The kernel of the pattern has dimension $s = (n - k) - \dim \sum_l \Lambda_{R_l} \cdot K[y]_{< n - k - r_l}$. In words: **the pattern carries a list iff the polynomials of degree $< n - k$ that vanish on one of the disagreement sets do not span all polynomials of degree $< n - k$**; $s$ is the value at degree $n - k - 1$ of the Hilbert function of $K[y, z]/(F_0, \dots, F_L)$, $F_l$ the homogenized $\Lambda_{R_l}$ (a product of $r_l$ linear forms). *Proof.* $s = Lk - w + \mathrm{rel}$, $w = \sum_l |S_l| - n$, $\mathrm{rel} = \sum_l (|S_l| - k) - \dim \sum_l C^\perp(S_l)$, and $C^\perp(S_l)$ is $\Lambda_{R_l} K[y]_{< n - k - r_l}$ by Lemma 2.1. $\square$ For generic evaluation points the dimension is the one a uniform matrix gives (generic Reed-Solomon codes are higher-order MDS: Brakensiek, Gopi and Makam; cited, and consistent with the scans of note 69 at random points); a deficiency is a family of products of linear forms over subsets of $D$ whose ideal is smaller than expected in degree $n - k - 1$.

**Theorem 5.2 (the aligned class is closed: an aligned pattern has exactly the lifted relations; proved, any number of members).** Let $V \subseteq D$ be a subspace, $m = |V|$, $W = W_V$, $D' = W(D)$ (a subspace, $n' = n/m$), $m \mid k$, $k' = k/m$. Suppose every disagreement set is a union of cosets of $V$ up to one common set: $R_l = W^{-1}(R'_l) \sqcup G$ with $R'_l \subseteq D'$ and $G \subseteq D$ the same for all $l$, $g = |G| < m$ (the lifted lists of note 69 have $G$ = the one point of the zero block that is not a zero of the lower factor). Write $\mathrm{Rel}'_{<d}$ for the relations of the top pattern $(R'_l)$ in degree $< d$ (tuples $h_l \in K[t]$, $\deg(\Lambda_{R'_l} h_l) < d$, $\sum_l \Lambda_{R'_l} h_l = 0$). Then the relation space of the pattern is $\Lambda_G \cdot \bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1} y^i\, \big(\mathrm{Rel}'_{<d_i} \circ W\big)$ with $d_i = n' - k'$ for $i < m - g$ and $d_i = n' - k' - 1$ for $i \ge m - g$. In particular for $G = \emptyset$: $\mathrm{rel} = m\, \mathrm{rel}'$, $s = m s'$, and the kernel is the $m$-fold interleaving $\{\sum_{i<m} y^i A^{(i)}(W)\}$ of the kernel of the top pattern for $\mathrm{RS}_{k'}(D')$: **an aligned pattern is deficient exactly as much as its top pattern, every member of its lists is a sum of lifts, and its list size is that of the $m$-fold interleaved top code.** *Proof.* $\Lambda_{R_l} = \Lambda_G(y)\, \Lambda_{R'_l}(W(y))$ because $\prod_{v \in V} (y + u + v) = W(y) + W(u)$. $K[y]$ is a free $K[W]$-module on $1, y, \dots, y^{m-1}$ ($W$ is monic of degree $m$). Write $h_l = \sum_{i<m} y^i h_{l,i}(W)$; then $\sum_l \Lambda_{R'_l}(W) h_l = 0$ iff $\sum_l \Lambda_{R'_l} h_{l,i} = 0$ for every $i$, and $\deg(\Lambda_{R'_l}(W)\, y^i h_{l,i}(W)) < n - k - g = m(n' - k') - g$ iff $\deg (\Lambda_{R'_l} h_{l,i}) < n' - k'$ when $i < m - g$, and $< n' - k' - 1$ when $i \ge m - g$. The statement on kernels is the same computation on the primal side. $\square$

Consequences (proved). (a) The excess of every lifted list of notes 69 and 74 is accounted for: with $g = 1$ and a top pattern at its generalized Singleton weight whose block $q_0$ lies in all $R'_l$, removing $q_0$ from all $R'_l$ leaves a top system with $Lk' + L - 1$ equations in $Lk'$ unknowns, of full column rank, hence $L - 1$ forced relations in degree $< n' - k'$, lifted $m - 1$ times: excess $(L-1)(m-1)$, the value of note 74 Proposition 4.6. So in the dual picture **every lifted list of notes 69 and 74 beyond the generalized Singleton bound is a forced relation of a top code (one that every MDS code has), multiplied by $y^i$, $i < m - 1$.** (b) Classification inside the aligned class reduces to the top code, which is again a Reed-Solomon code on a subspace: top dimension $k' = 1$: no relation beyond the forced ones; $k' = 2$, three members: none; $k' = 2$, four members with six distinct pair points: a non-forced relation exists only if the three pairs of opposite points are in involution (**proved** by the identity of Section 4; that it then exists is **exact at the stated size**, family II); $k' = 4$: whatever the arithmetic of $D'$ allows (family IV). (c) A flag $V_1 \subset V_2 \subset \dots$ is not needed: $V$ is any subspace of $D$, which is why a subspace code has more deficient patterns than the tree code T2 (Section 4).

**Where an arbitrary set escapes (the precise gap).** The proof needs all $\Lambda_{R_l}/\Lambda_G$ in ONE subring $K[W_V]$. It says nothing when (i) the disagreement sets are aligned to different subspaces for different members, or to none; (ii) they are aligned up to sets $G_l$ that differ from member to member; (iii) the top code itself has a non-forced relation (families II and IV: these are relations of $\mathrm{RS}_2$ or $\mathrm{RS}_4$ on eight or fewer points, with no subring behind them). For (i) and (ii) the module of syzygies of $(\Lambda_{R_l})$ over $K[y]$ is not extended from a subring and I have no handle on it except for three members, where it is the $\mu$-basis of Section 2. The counterexamples of Section 3 are of type (ii): $\Lambda_A = \lambda(W) \cdot u(y)$, $\Lambda_B = \lambda'(W) \cdot u(y + c)$ with $W = y(y + c)$, two different cubic lower factors conjugate under the involution, and the relation exists because $u(0) = u(c)$, a multiplicative coincidence in $D$.

Numerical check of Theorem 5.2 (**exact at the stated sizes**, `scripts/77_aligned_check.py`): 400 random aligned patterns, $N = 4, 5$, $|V| = 2, 4$ with $V$ a random subspace of $D$ (not in the flag in general), three and four members, kinds P and G, $g \in \{0, 1, m - 1\}$: the number of relations equals $(m - g)\,\mathrm{rel}'_{<n'-k'} + g\,\mathrm{rel}'_{<n'-k'-1}$ in all 400 (153 of them with at least one relation).

## 7. Does today's information change the night's assessment? (task 5)

**No. It makes the assessment sharper and slightly worse.** The night concluded that no proof of a list bound below the Johnson radius for today's code is in reach. Today's information is a proof technique (independence of incidence rows, by differentiating in a transcendental twiddle) and a structure picture (every deficiency is a lift). For today's code:

1. **The technique has no analogue and its conclusion is false.** $Q_3$ fails for today's code at every size $N \ge 3$ (Theorem 3.3, explicit), and for every MDS code over GF($2^{64}$) of dimension $\ge 65$ (Theorem 3.4, pigeonhole over $\mathbb F_2$). There are no parameters to differentiate in, and no exact rank statement of the generic kind can be true over a 64-bit field at the real sizes: only statements with a slack of at least $k/63$, that is, counting statements. The night's methods were already of that kind.
2. **Where the generic theory is true it gives nothing at rate 1/2** ($Q_t$ returns the Johnson radius at rates 1/2 and 1/4; note 75). So even a perfect transfer would not have produced a bound at rate 1/2.
3. **The structure picture survives at toy size, with two amendments that matter for a proof.** (i) For a subspace code the lifts go through every subspace of $D$, not through a flag: there are about $n^3/168$ subspaces of index 8, each with 8 blocks, and a structure theorem has to say which few of these aligned classes a given word can use. (ii) Today's evaluation set is not a generic subspace: $\langle 1, x, \dots, x^{N-1} \rangle$ is closed under products of low-degree elements, which creates deficient configurations (family IV) that a random subspace does not have at toy size. None of those found is a larger list (at $(4,8)$, $(4,16)$, $(8,16)$ the largest weights and balanced agreements are those of the lifts), but they exist at every $N$ and they are not block-aligned, so "every deficient pattern is a lift" is false for today's code, and a proof cannot go through a classification of deficient patterns.
4. **What today adds on the positive side is only language and one closed class:** lists are relations between polynomials of degree $< n - k$ with prescribed zeros (Lemma 2.1), three members are a $\mu$-basis (Corollary 2.2), the aligned class is closed with exactly the lifted relations (Theorem 5.2), the conjectured bound is the size of one aligned class, and the first open case is $(16, 32)$ at agreement 20.

So: **no proof for today's code is in reach, and today's information does not bring one closer.** The open statement is a structure theorem for lists at large agreement (alignment), for which there is no technique here, on a domain that is measurably less generic than a random subspace.
