# 83. List stabilization for the generic tree code at agreement 5n/8

**Neither bounded nor unbounded is proved for $\lambda(n) = \lambda_{5/8}(n)$, the largest list of the generic tree code $[n, n/2]$ at agreement $5n/8$, and the simplest stabilization guess is refuted by new lists.** **Exact data:** $\lambda(8) = 4$ and $\lambda(16) = 5$ (note 76, exhaustive); here every maximal list at these sizes is described: at $n = 8$ the 160 lists of four are a product family of three at level 1 (differences $A \otimes B$, $B \in K^2$) and one more member; at $n = 16$ the 162 orbits of five are one product family of five (128 orbits: a list of the top code $C_8$ with two votes per coordinate and the zero leaf of $B$) or two product families of three sharing one member (34 orbits). **New lower bounds (exact at the stated size, checked by encoding with fresh twiddles): $\lambda(32) \ge 7$ and $\lambda(64) \ge 11$** (notes 74 and 76 had 5 and 9): a staircase of scale 8 on six blocks whose block word is a lift from scale 4, so that three interpolants share one free leaf; with the known 15 at $n \ge 128$ the sequence of best known values is 4, 5, 7, 11, 15. No exhaustive statement is made at $n = 32$ (out of reach; the search was an annealing over the class of lists with one lower factor at scale 8, where 8 was not found, and that annealer is shown to be unreliable at scale 16). **Proved:** (a) the pencil form of one level: a list at depth $v$ is a line of top words $W_\theta$ and lines of top codewords, full pairs are agreements for all $\theta$ and a half pair at leaf $x$ is an agreement at $\theta = t_x$ only (Lemma 2.1); (b) $\dim S(\pi) \le 2 \dim S'(\pi) - E$: every leaf whose coincidence partition is coarser than its pair's costs one dimension of the doubled top kernel (Lemma 2.2), so a list whose top pattern is rigid is one product family (Lemma 3.2); (c) a product family is exactly a list of the top code with two votes per coordinate plus one common leaf, families are the points of the pencil near the top code, and families with small error supports share one centre (Lemmas 3.1, 3.3); (d) the class of lists with one lower factor whose zeros fill a block at scale 8 has at most 30 members for every $n$ (Proposition 6.1), and at scale $N$ the same argument bounds it by $3\lambda(N) + P_2(N)$ plus members of low agreement, where $P_2(N)$ is a list size of the twice punctured top code at agreement $5N/8 - 1$ (not a closed recursion); (e) a conditional descent: if the top kernels stay bounded by $\sigma$ at every depth, the list is a voted list of $C_{n_0}$, $n_0 > 8(2\sigma - 1)$, hence bounded (Proposition 2.4); its hypothesis is **false** in general (lifting doubles kernels). **The recursion suggested by the data and what it needs:** a list at depth $v$ is a star of product families, each a voted list at depth $v - 1$; to bound $\lambda$ one needs (i) a bound on voted lists of $C_N$ at credit $5N/8 - 1 + \varepsilon$ on $N - 1$ coordinates that is uniform in $N$ (this is the same problem at agreement $5/8 - 1/N$, where 35 members exist, so the induction does not close on $\lambda$ alone), and (ii) a bound on the number of rays and of members outside families (at $n = 8, 16$: at most two rays, no member outside). **Open:** whether $\lambda(n)$ is bounded; the exact value at $n = 32$; (S8) at agreement $9n/16$ was not started.

## 1. Setup: frames, votes, heavy classes

**1.1 Frame form of the tree code (proved, restatement of note 69 Section 5).** For every split $n = N b$ ($b = 2^i$) the code is $C_N \otimes K^b$ read in $N$ private frames: a codeword is a $b \times N$ matrix $c$ with rows in the top code $C_N = [N, N/2]$, block $q$ has a basis $(\beta_x)_{x \in q}$ of $(K^b)^*$ (the bottom transform of that block, generic and private to the block), and the symbol at leaf $x \in q$ is $\langle \beta_x, c(q) \rangle$. A word is a matrix $W$ of columns $W(q) \in K^b$; a member has error columns $e(q) = c(q) - W(q)$ and its agreement is $\sum_q (b - \mathrm{wt}_q(e(q)))$, $\mathrm{wt}_q$ the Hamming weight in the frame of $q$. At $b = 2$: a member is a codeword $(u, v)$ of $C_{n/2}^2$; pair $p$ is **full** if $e_p = 0$ and **half at leaf $(p, \epsilon)$** if $e_p \in K^* (t_{p\epsilon}, 1)$, a slope condition with the private twiddle of that leaf; agreement $= 2 f + h$.

**1.2 Votes (proved, trivial).** If all members are $A_j \otimes B$ with one lower factor $B$, the word may choose at every leaf $x$ of block $q$ with $B(x) \ne 0$ any class of the partition of the top codewords $A_j$ by their value at $q$. So a lifted list is a **weighted list of the top code**: a distribution $\varphi_q$ over the classes at each block (denominator $b$), member credit $\sum_q \varphi_q([j]_q)$ plus the zeros of $B$. The free leaves of Proposition 6.1 of note 74 are the case where a whole block is spread over singletons.

**1.3 Lemma A (heavy classes; proved, any MDS top code, one lower factor).** Let $C'$ be an MDS code $[N', k']$ and let $M$ distinct codewords have weighted credit at least $k' + \eta$, $\eta > 0$ (a measure of total mass at most 1 on the symbols of each coordinate; the credit of a codeword is the mass of its own symbols). Then $M \le (N' - k' + 1) \binom{N'}{k'} / (1 + \eta)$. *Proof.* Let $H_q$ be a class of largest size at $q$ and $T(j) = \{q : j \in H_q\}$. The sum of the credits is at most $\sum_q |H_q| = \sum_j |T(j)|$. Members with $|T(j)| \ge k'$ are determined by any $k'$ elements of $T(j)$, so there are at most $\binom{N'}{k'}$ of them; the others have $|T(j)| \le k' - 1$. Hence $M (k' + \eta) \le (k' - 1) M + (N' - k' + 1) \binom{N'}{k'}$. $\square$ *Use.* A lift from scale $N$ with one lower factor whose $b - 1$ zeros sit in one block is a weighted list of the punctured top code $[N - 1, N/2]$ at credit $5N/8 - 1 + 1/b$: for $N = 8$ this is $k' + 1/b$ and the lemma gives $M \le 140$ for every $b$ (Proposition 6.1 improves this to 30); for $N = 16$ it gives $8 \cdot 6435 / 2 = 25740$. **So no family of products with one lower factor at a fixed scale grows with $n$**, but the bound grows exponentially with the scale.

**1.4 What the recursion must be about (sketched).** Three different list problems are nested: integer lists of $C_n$ at agreement $a$; weighted lists of $C_N$ (votes, 1.2); and frame lists of $C_N \otimes K^b$ (1.1), which is the true problem for every $N$. Lifting gives weighted($C_N$) $\subseteq$ lists($C_{Nb}$). A stabilization proof needs the converse up to a bounded number of members, at one level ($b = 2$): from a list of $C_{2n'}$ at agreement $5n'/4$, with member profile $(f_j, h_j)$, $2 f_j + h_j \ge 5n'/4$, produce a weighted list of $C_{n'}$. The projection $u + \theta v$ keeps the full pairs and loses every half pair unless $\theta$ is the private twiddle of that leaf, so the half pairs are the whole difficulty: Theorem 4.2 of note 74 says half-pair agreements with one functional a leaf are independent conditions.

**1.5 What was done about the data.** $n = 8, 16$: every maximal list analysed (Section 4). $n = 32$: a full search is out of reach ($\binom{32}{12} = 2.3 \cdot 10^8$ error supports, each cutting codimension 4 in $\mathbb P^{15}$, against 8008 supports cutting codimension 2 in $\mathbb P^7$ at $n = 16$); the search of Section 5 covers one class and is not exhaustive in it.

## 2. The one-level recursion: pencils, essential leaves, votes (proved unless tagged)

Notation: $n = 2n'$, $C' = C_{n'}$ the top code, $K_1$ the field of the twiddles of layers $\ge 1$, the bottom twiddles $t_x$ algebraically independent over $K_1$. A list is $M = L + 1$ distinct codewords $c_j = (u_j, v_j) \in C'^2$ and a word. For a pair $p$ let $\pi_p$ be the partition of the members by the value $(u_j(p), v_j(p)) \in K^2$, for a leaf $x$ let $\pi_x$ be the partition by the value at $x$ (coarser than $\pi_p$), $C_x$ the class of $\pi_x$ chosen by the word, $F_p = C_{p0} \cap C_{p1}$ (one class of $\pi_p$ or empty), $H_x = C_x \setminus F_p$. A leaf is **essential** if $\pi_x \ne \pi_p$, and **essential for the word** if $C_x$ contains at least two classes of $\pi_p$.

**Lemma 2.1 (pencil form; proved).** For $\theta$ in $K$ put $c_j(\theta) = u_j + \theta v_j \in C'$ and $W_\theta = U + \theta V$ (the word in the frame form). For all but finitely many $\theta$ the $c_j(\theta)$ are distinct, their coincidence partition at $p$ is $\pi_p$, and $c_j(\theta)$ agrees with $W_\theta$ exactly on the full pairs of $j$. At $\theta = t_x$, $x = (p, \epsilon)$, the partition at $p$ is $\pi_x$ and the class of the word is $C_x$; nothing changes at the other pairs. *Proof.* $c_j(\theta)(p) - W_\theta(p) = \langle (1, \theta), e^j_p \rangle$ vanishes for generic $\theta$ iff $e^j_p = 0$ and at $\theta = t_x$ iff $e^j_p \in K (t_x, 1)$ (characteristic 2). $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (essential leaves cost one dimension each; proved; a reformulation of Theorem 4.2 of note 74 in terms of kernel dimensions).** Let $S(\pi)$ be the solution space of the full coincidence pattern $(\pi_x)_x$ at depth $v$ (rows $\Lambda(\pi_x) \otimes g_x$, $\Lambda(\pi)$ spanned by the $e_j - e_{j'}$, $j \sim j'$) and $S'(\pi)$ the solution space of the pattern $(\pi_p)_p$ for the top code. Let $E$ be the number of essential leaves. Then $\dim S(\pi) \le 2 \dim S'(\pi) - E$. *Proof.* $S(\pi) \subseteq S'(\pi)^2$ (both slices $M_0, M_1$ satisfy the top system: $\lambda \in \Lambda(\pi_p)$ kills $M_0 g_p + t M_1 g_p$ at two values of $t$). For an essential leaf $x$ and $\lambda \in \Lambda(\pi_x) \setminus \Lambda(\pi_p)$, a point $M$ with exact pattern $\pi$ has $(\lambda^T M_0 g_p, \lambda^T M_1 g_p) \ne 0$, so $t_x = \lambda^T M_0 g_p / \lambda^T M_1 g_p \in K_1([M])$. Let $[M]$ be the generic point of $\mathbb P(S(\pi))$ over $K = K_1(t)$ (its pattern is exactly $\pi$ if $\pi$ is the pattern of some solution). Then $\mathrm{trdeg}_{K_1} K([M]) = 2n' + \dim S(\pi) - 1$, while $K([M])$ is generated over $K_1([M])$ by the $2n' - E$ inessential twiddles and $\mathrm{trdeg}_{K_1} K_1([M]) \le \dim \mathbb P(S'(\pi)^2) = 2 \dim S'(\pi) - 1$, because $S'(\pi)$ is defined over $K_1$. $\square$ Iterating from the root ($\dim S = L$ for the code $\{(m, m)\}$): $\sum_x \#\{\text{levels on the path of } x \text{ at which the partition coarsens}\} \le Lk - \dim S(\pi)$, which contains Corollary 4.3 (a) of note 74.

**Lemma 2.3 (votes; proved).** Let every leaf $x$ vote for a largest class $G_x$ of $\pi_p$ inside $C_x$. Then $(c_j(\theta))_j$, generic $\theta$, is a list of the top code with two votes per coordinate in which member $j$ has credit at least $\mathrm{agr}(j) - \ell_j$, where $\ell_j$ is the number of leaves essential for the word at which $j \in C_x \setminus G_x$; and $\ell_j \le E \le 2 \dim S'(\pi) - \dim S(\pi)$. The same holds for a list with $\beta$ votes per leaf at depth $v$ (each vote a symbol): it gives a list with $2\beta$ votes per coordinate at depth $v - 1$ and $\ell_j \le \beta E$. *Proof.* A vote at $x$ for the symbol of $C_x$ is passed to the symbol $(U, V)$-value of $G_x$, read through $\theta$; a member of $G_x$ keeps the vote. Only essential leaves lose votes. $\square$

**Proposition 2.4 (descent with bounded kernels; proved, conditional on its hypothesis).** Suppose that for a list at depth $v$ at agreement $5n/8$ the top kernels at every depth $i$, $v_0 \le i < v$, have $\dim S' \le \sigma$, where at each depth the list is the voted list of Lemma 2.3. Then the list is a weighted list of $C_{n_0}$, $n_0 = 2^{v_0 + 1}$, with credit at least $5n_0/8 - (2\sigma - 1)$ coordinates for every member (the loss at the depth with $m$ leaves is at most $(2\sigma - 1)/m$ of the length, a geometric series), and if $n_0 > 8(2\sigma - 1)$ Lemma A of 1.3 bounds the number of members by $(n_0/2 + 1)\binom{n_0}{n_0/2}$, whatever $n$. **This is a stabilization statement, but its hypothesis (bounded kernel of the top coincidence pattern) is not proved and is false for sub-lists of two or three members** (their kernels have dimension about $n/4$, $n/8$). The data of Section 4 say how large the kernels of actual lists are.

## 3. Product families are the near-codewords of the pencil (proved)

A **product family at level 1** is a set of members whose pairwise differences are $d \otimes B$ with one $B = (b_0, b_1) \in K^2$ (flattening rank 1 at level 1). Put $\theta_B = b_0 / b_1$.

**Lemma 3.1 (families merge on the pencil; proved).** The members of a family with factor $B$ are exactly the members with one common value $c^* = c_j(\theta_B)$ on the pencil of Lemma 2.1 ($c_j(\theta) - c_{j'}(\theta) = (b_0 + \theta b_1) d$ in characteristic 2). The top word $W_{\theta_B}$ agrees with $c^*$ on the union of the full pairs of all members of the family. Conversely, for any $\theta^*$ and any $c^* \in C'$ with $\varepsilon = W_{\theta^*} - c^*$, the members through $c^*$ at $\theta^*$ are $(c^* + \theta^* v, v)$, and such a member agrees with the word at the leaf $x$ of pair $p$ iff $v_p = V_p + \varepsilon_p / (\theta^* + t_x)$ (if $t_x \ne \theta^*$) or $\varepsilon_p = 0$ (if $t_x = \theta^*$). So **a product family is a list of the top code around $V$ with two votes per coordinate, equal votes outside $\Delta = \mathrm{supp}\, \varepsilon$ and two different votes on $\Delta$ (a split pair), plus one leaf $x_0$ common to all members when $\theta^* = t_{x_0}$ and $\varepsilon_{p(x_0)} = 0$** (the zero of $B$). *Proof.* The error of $(c^* + \theta^* v, v)$ at $p$ is $(\varepsilon_p + \theta^* \eta_p, \eta_p)$ with $\eta = V - v$, and its value at $x$ is $\varepsilon_p + (\theta^* + t_x) \eta_p$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (rigid top pattern; proved, trivial).** If the top kernel of the pair partitions has $\dim S'(\pi) = 1$, the whole list is one product family (both slices of the differences are multiples of one matrix), and it has at most one essential leaf, the zero of $B$.

**Lemma 3.3 (two families share their centre; proved).** Let two families of at least two members each sit at $\theta_1 \ne \theta_2$ with near-codewords $c_1^*, c_2^*$ and error supports $\Delta_1, \Delta_2$. The line $c_0(\theta)$ through $c_1^*$ at $\theta_1$ and $c_2^*$ at $\theta_2$ is a codeword of $C'^2$ that is full outside $\Delta_1 \cup \Delta_2$ (its error is $a(\theta) \varepsilon_1 + b(\theta) \varepsilon_2$ with $a, b$ the Lagrange weights). It belongs to both families, and it is a member of the list as soon as $|\Delta_1 \cup \Delta_2| \le 3n'/8$. A third family at $\theta_3$ with $|\Delta_1 \cup \Delta_2 \cup \Delta_3| \le n'/2$ passes through the same centre and has $\Delta_3 = \{p : a(\theta_3) \varepsilon_1(p) + b(\theta_3) \varepsilon_2(p) \ne 0\}$. *Proof.* $c_3^* - c_0(\theta_3)$ is a top codeword supported on $\Delta_1 \cup \Delta_2 \cup \Delta_3$, hence zero (distance $n'/2 + 1$). $\square$ So the families with small error supports form a **star**: one centre $c_0$, and for each slope $\theta^*$ at which the centre's error vector $e^0_p \in K^2$ is proportional to $(\theta^*, 1)$ on a set $\Delta^{\theta^*}$ of pairs, a family with split set $\Delta_0 \setminus \Delta^{\theta^*}$, $\Delta_0$ the set of pairs where the centre is not full.

**Lemma 3.4 (the price of half leaves; proved).** For a member $j$ let $R$ be the other members, $s'_R$ the dimension of the top kernel of the pair partitions of $R$, $f^{\rm sh}_j$ the number of pairs on which $j$ coincides with some member of $R$ (on both leaves), and $h^{\rm ess}_j$ the number of leaves where $j$ coincides with some member of $R$ at the leaf but not on the pair. Then $h^{\rm ess}_j \le 2 s'_R + 2 (n'/2 - f^{\rm sh}_j)_+ - 1$. *Proof.* Forgetting the difference of $j$ maps $S'(\pi)$ to $S'(\pi|_R)$ with fibres of dimension at most $(k' - f^{\rm sh}_j)_+$ (evaluations at distinct coordinates of an MDS code are independent conditions), and the $h^{\rm ess}_j$ leaves are essential, so Lemma 2.2 gives $h^{\rm ess}_j \le E \le 2 \dim S'(\pi) - 1$. $\square$ *Consequences.* (a) A member at agreement $5n'/4$ with $f \le n'/2$ full pairs, all shared, and all its half leaves essential forces $s'_R \ge n'/8 + 1/2$: **members that are not top members can only be added to a list whose top pattern has a kernel of dimension above $n/16$.** (b) A member with $f = 5n'/8 - m$ shared full pairs and $2m$ essential half leaves forces $s'_R \ge m + 1/2$. (c) At $n = 32$: let the five members of a list of $C_{16}$ at agreement 10 be lifted with a factor $B \in K^2$ (all 162 orbits have a full coincidence pattern with kernel 1 and cover every coordinate, Section 4). A sixth codeword at agreement 20 with the lifted word has all its full pairs shared and all its half leaves essential, so $20 - 2f \le h \le 1 + 2(8 - f)_+$: impossible for $f \le 8$, and $f \ge 10$ makes it a sixth member of the list of $C_{16}$. **So a pure lift of a maximal list of $C_{16}$ cannot be extended at $n = 32$**; larger lists at $n = 32$ must change the word (split votes, zero leaf), which is why Section 5 searches voted lists.

## 4. Data: every maximal list at n = 8 and n = 16 (exact at the stated sizes)

**Reading.** At $n = 8, 16$ a maximal list is a star of at most two product families at level 1, each a voted list of the top code. The quantity that grows from $n = 8$ to $n = 16$ is the size of one family (3 to 5) or the number of rays (1 to 2 with rays of three).

## 5. n = 32 and n = 64: seven and eleven codewords at agreement 5n/8 (exact at the stated sizes as lower bounds, checked by encoding)

**Search class.** `scripts/83_votes.c`: simulated annealing over voted lists of a top code $C_N$ with $b$ votes per block and the $b - 1$ zeros of one lower factor in block 0, that is over the lists of $C_{Nb}$ that are one product family at every level down to scale $N$ (Lemma 3.1 iterated). Feasibility of a state is an exact kernel test on the top code. Validation at $n = 16$ ($C_8$, $b = 2$): five members at credit 10 are found in every run within $6 \cdot 10^4$ tests, six members never (best objective 59 of 60), in agreement with $\lambda_{5/8}(16) = 5$.

**Result ($C_8$, $b = 4$, $n = 32$).** Eight runs of eight reach six members with credit 20 each, for instance (blocks of four leaves; `*3` three zeros of $B$; a block `[0 13 5 4]` gives one leaf to member 0, one to the class $\{1, 3\}$, one to 5, one to 4):

`[2 *3] [0 13 5 4] [0125 x4] [01345 x4] [0234 x4] [0145 x4] [2345 x4] [123 x4]`

Each member has four full blocks among blocks 2 to 7 (16 leaves), the three zeros, and one single leaf; the five single leaves (four of the free block 1 and the leaf left in block 0) serve six members because **two interpolants coincide on the free block** (members 1 and 3: one condition on the block word, which has one projective parameter once two blocks are free of it; kernel dimension 1). `scripts/83_verify.py` expands the votes to a pattern of $[32, 16]$, solves the incidence system for a fresh draw of the twiddles, encodes a kernel point and counts: kernel 1, six distinct codewords, agreements $(20, 20, 20, 20, 20, 20)$, for all eight runs. **So $\lambda_{5/8}(32) \ge 6$** (note 76 had 5 from $\min(15, n/8 + 1)$): the count of note 74, Proposition 6.1 (free leaves handed to members one by one) is not optimal, because a special block word lets several members share a free leaf.

**Seven members at $n = 32$ (exact at the stated size, checked by encoding).** The same search with seven members succeeds in eight runs of eight, for instance `[135 *3] [2 6 4 0] [023456 x4] [03456 x4] [0123 x4] [12346 x4] [145 x4] [01256 x4]`: members are 4-subsets of the six blocks 2 to 7, the free block 1 gives one leaf each to members 2, 6, 4, 0, and the leaf left in the zero block goes to the class $\{1, 3, 5\}$. These three members are the interpolants through two of the three sibling pairs of blocks $(2,3), (4,5), (6,7)$: the block word is a product $(h_1, h_2, h_3) \otimes B'$ at scale 4 on these six blocks (the one projective parameter of the block word is spent on this), so the three are the three-line family of scale 4 and coincide at the zero of $B'$, which is block 0. **So $\lambda_{5/8}(32) \ge 7$: a staircase of scale 8 whose block word is a lift from scale 4.** With 8, 9, 10, 11 members the search (four runs each, $4 \cdot 10^6$ steps) ends one, two, three, four units below the target (objective $20 M - (M - 7)$): **measured**, in this class (one lower factor with three zeros in one block of scale 8) seven is the largest list found. Outputs: `83_tmp/votes_c8_b4*.txt`; the check `83_verify.py` confirms every list reported at the target.

**Class $C_{16}$, $b = 2$ (one product family at level 1 with a zero leaf; measured).** It contains the class above. The annealer finds six members in one run of four and no seven in $1.5 \cdot 10^6$ steps, so in this larger class it is not a reliable optimizer; nothing above seven is known at $n = 32$. **No exhaustive statement at $n = 32$ is made**: the secant search of note 76 would have to treat $\binom{32}{12} = 2.3 \cdot 10^8$ supports cutting codimension 4 in $\mathbb P^{15}$, and by Lemma 2.2 the lists that are not one family need a top kernel of dimension at least 2, which the classes searched here do not have.

**Structure of the seven (exact at the stated size; `scripts/83_scales.py`).** For the list above the differences of all seven members have flattening rank $(1, 1, 2, 4)$ at levels 1 to 4: one lower factor in $K^4$ (scale 8), and no product at scale 4; the sub-list $\{1, 3, 5\}$ has ranks $(1, 1, 1, 2)$: it is a product at scale 4 as well, as described.

**$n = 64$ ($C_8$, $b = 8$; exact at the stated size as a lower bound, checked by encoding).** A run with twelve members ends with eleven members at agreement 40 and one at 39 (kernel 1, twelve distinct codewords, agreements checked by `83_verify.py`): the free block gives seven single leaves and one leaf to a class of three, the zero block one leaf. **So $\lambda_{5/8}(64) \ge 11$** (9 in notes 74, 76). Twelve at 40 was not found (one run of $6 \cdot 10^6$ steps; measured). Output `83_tmp/votes_c8_b8.txt`.

So the absence of larger lists in the runs on $C_{16}$ says nothing, and **the quantity $P_2(16)$ of Section 6 is not determined** (it is at least 15).

## 6. The single-factor class at scale 8 is bounded for every n (proved)

**Proposition 6.1 (proved, any MDS code $[8, 4]$ as top code).** Let $n = 8b$ and consider lists at agreement $5n/8$ whose members are $A_j \otimes B + c$ with one lower factor $B \in K^b$ having $b - 1$ zeros in one block $q_0$ (any distribution of the other leaves between the members, any block word). Such a list has at most 30 members, whatever $b$. *Proof.* Credits in block units: member $j$ needs $\sum_{q \ne q_0} \varphi_q(A_j(q)) + \frac{1}{b}[\text{leaf left in } q_0] \ge 4 + \frac1b$, $\varphi_q$ the distribution of the $b$ leaves of block $q$ over symbols. Let $H_q$ be a symbol of largest class at $q \ne q_0$ and $T(j) = \{q : A_j(q) = H_q\} \subseteq [7]$. (i) The members that agree with the leaf left in $q_0$ and have $|T(j)| = 4$ have pairwise $|T \cap T'| \le 2$ (two codewords agree on at most 3 coordinates), so there are at most 7 of them (4-subsets of a 7-set pairwise meeting in at most 2 points; 4 for the tree code, where they form a list at agreement 5). (ii) Big members, $|T(j)| \ge 5$: pairwise $|T \cap T'| \ge 3$, hence equal to 3, the complements are disjoint pairs: at most 3, with $\sum (|T(j)| - 4) \le 3$. (iii) Summing all credits, $\sum_j \mathrm{credit}_j \le \sum_j |T(j)| + h_{q_0}/b$ with $h_{q_0} \le M$ the size of the class of the leaf left in $q_0$, so the small members ($|T(j)| \le 3$) have total deficit at most 3: at most 3 of them. (iv) Let $\mathcal T$ be the remaining members: $|T(j)| = 4$, no credit from $q_0$, credit above 4. Summing their credits, $\sum_q \sum_a \varphi_q(a) |\mathrm{cl}_q(a) \cap \mathcal T| > 4 |\mathcal T| = \sum_q |\mathrm{cl}_q(H_q) \cap \mathcal T|$, so at some block $q$ some symbol $a \ne H_q$ has more members of $\mathcal T$ than $H_q$ has. The members of $\mathcal T$ with symbol $a$ at $q$ have $T(j) \subseteq [7] \setminus q$ and pairwise $|T \cap T'| \le 2$, so there are at most 3. Hence at most 2 members of $\mathcal T$ contain $q$, and the others are 4-subsets of the six other blocks: $|\mathcal T| \le 17$. Total $17 + 7 + 3 + 3 = 30$. $\square$ The families found reach 15 (one free block, $b \ge 14$, note 74) and, by the sharing of Section 5, $\min(15, b + 3)$ at intermediate $b$ (**exact at the stated sizes** as lower bounds: 5 at $b = 2$, 7 at $b = 4$, 11 at $b = 8$; **conjectured** to be the largest lists of this class).

**The same argument at scale $N$ (proved inequality, not a bound).** For lists with one lower factor whose $b - 1$ zeros lie in one block at scale $N$, call a member big if $|T(j)| \ge 5N/8$, tight if $|T(j)| = 5N/8 - 1$, small otherwise. Then the big members number at most $\lambda_{5/8}(N)$ (a list of $C_N$ around $H$), the tight members served by the leaf left in $q_0$ at most $\lambda_{5/8}(N)$, and the other tight members at most $\lambda_{5/8}(N) - 1 + P_2(N)$, where $P_2(N)$ is the largest number of codewords of $C_N$ agreeing with a word on at least $5N/8 - 1$ of $N - 2$ coordinates (two coordinates punctured). The small members are bounded only by their total deficit, at most $3N \lambda_{5/8}(N)/8$. **So the growth of lists of this class is governed by $P_2(N)$, a list size at agreement $5N/8 - 1$ on the twice punctured top code**: $P_2(8) = 15$ (interpolants), and $P_2(N) \ge 15$ for all $N$.

## 7. Conclusions: the recursion, and what a proof of (S8) needs

**7.1 The recursion in its correct form (proved parts: Lemmas 2.1 to 3.3; the completeness of the description is exact at $n = 8, 16$ only).** A list at depth $v$ is a pencil $W_\theta$ of top words with a line of top codewords for each member. Its members group into product families, one for each point $\theta^*$ of the pencil at which several lines meet; a family is a list at depth $v - 1$ with two votes per coordinate around the second component $V$ of the word, with equal votes outside the support $\Delta(\theta^*)$ of $W_{\theta^*} - c^*$ and split votes on it, and one extra common leaf if $\theta^*$ is a leaf twiddle. Families with $|\Delta_1 \cup \Delta_2| \le 3n'/8$ share a centre. A member outside every family of size at least two has all its agreements with the other members at essential leaves or at full pairs, and by Lemma 2.2 the essential leaves number at most $2 \dim S'(\pi) - \dim S(\pi)$. Iterating, the natural objects are **voted lists**: $\beta$ votes per leaf, a credit threshold $5/8$ of the votes, and wildcard votes (zeros of lower factors) of total mass below one coordinate at each scale. The class of voted lists is closed under the recursion; the class of ordinary lists is not.

**7.2 Why the induction does not close on $\lambda$ alone (proved obstruction).** One product family with a zero leaf, read at depth $v - 1$, is a voted list at credit $5n'/8 - 1/2$ per member on the $n' - 1/2$ coordinates left: after $i$ levels, credit $5N/8 - 1 + 1/b$ on the $N - 1$ coordinates of $C_N$ outside the zero block. So $\lambda(Nb)$ is bounded below by a list size of $C_N$ at agreement **just above $5N/8 - 1$**, not at $5N/8$, and at agreement $5N/8 - 1$ the staircase has 35 members. The gap between the two is exactly the question which members of a list at agreement $5N/8 - 1$ can be given a fraction of a coordinate more: Proposition 6.1 answers it at $N = 8$ (at most 30, found 15), and at scale $N$ the answer is governed by $P_2(N)$ (Section 6). A stabilization proof must therefore carry, as its inductive statement, a bound on **lists of the punctured code at agreement $5N/8 - 1$ on $N - 2$ coordinates together with lists at $5N/8$**, or more generally a bound on voted lists; $\lambda(n) \le F(\lambda(n/2))$ is not the right shape.

**7.3 What the data say about stabilization (measured, conjectured).** Best known values: $\lambda = 4, 5$ at $n = 8, 16$ (exact), at least 7, 11, 15 at $n = 32, 64, \ge 128$. All known lists at $n \ge 32$ are in the class of Proposition 6.1 (one lower factor at scale 8), which is bounded by 30. The statement "$\lambda(n) = \lambda(n_0)$ for $n \ge n_0$" with $n_0 = 16$ or 32 is **false**; "$\lambda(n) \le 30$ for all $n$" is **conjectured** only in the weak sense that nothing found contradicts it: no search here could see a list that needs scale 16 (the annealer fails its control there), and by 7.2 such lists are where growth would come from. A family growing with $n$ was **not found**.

**7.4 Smallest next steps (not done).** (i) $P_2(16)$ exactly: lists of $C_{16}$ at agreement 9 on 14 coordinates, a secant search with 2002 hyperplanes of $\mathbb P^5$ (supports of five among fourteen), feasible with the orbit reduction of note 76; if $P_2(16) = 15$ the class of one lower factor at scale 16 is bounded by about $3 \cdot 5 + 15$ plus the members of low agreement, and the pattern "$P_2(N) = 15$ for all $N$" would be the statement to prove by induction. (ii) Lists that are stars with two or more rays at $n = 32$ (top kernel at least 2): not searched. (iii) A bound on the members of low heavy agreement at scale $N$ that does not grow with $N$ (the only term of Section 6 without a candidate bound). (iv) The same analysis at agreement $9n/16$ (scale 16 staircases, credit $9N/16 - 1$): not started.
