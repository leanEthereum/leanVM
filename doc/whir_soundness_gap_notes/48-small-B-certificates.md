## 1. Certificates with small $B$

**Lemma 1.1 [proved; checked against the defining sums, `48_certs.py A`: 0 mismatches for $B \le 7$, $m < 60$, three values of $\Delta$].** For $m \ge 2B - 1$, $R(m, B) = (B + 1)\big[\tfrac{m(m+1)}2 - \tfrac{mB}2 + \tfrac{B(2B+1)}6\big]$. For $Z \ge (B - 1)\Delta$, with $Z = q\Delta + r$, $0 \le r < \Delta$ and $S(Z) = \sum_{t \ge 0}(Z - \Delta t)_+ = (q + 1)(q\Delta/2 + r)$: $N(B, Z) = (B + 1)S(Z) - Z\tfrac{B(B+1)}2 + \Delta\tfrac{(B-1)B(B+1)}6$. In particular $R(m, 1) = m^2 + 1$, $R(m, 2) = (3m^2 - 3m + 10)/2$, $R(m, 3) = 2m^2 - 4m + 14$, $R(18, 5) = 811$.

*Proof.* In the sum of note 35 the correction term is nonzero for $s = m - 1 - i$, $0 \le i \le B - 1$, where it is $(m - 1 - 2i)(B - i)$; $\sum_i (m - 1 - 2i)(B - i) = m\tfrac{B(B+1)}2 - \tfrac{B(B+1)(2B+1)}6$. For $N$: $\min(t, B) + 1 = (B + 1) - (B - t)_+$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.2 ($B = 1$) [proved].** $N(1, Z) = Z^2/\Delta + r(\Delta - r)/\Delta$. So for every $n$, every $\Delta < 4n$ and every $A$ with $A^2 \le n\Delta$ there is no certificate $(m, 1)$, for any $m$.

*Proof.* $N(1, Z) = 2S(Z) - Z = (q + 1)(q\Delta + 2r) - q\Delta - r = q^2\Delta + 2qr + r = (Z^2 - r^2)/\Delta + r$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.3 (asymptotic thresholds) [proved at leading order in $n$; exact integers for the table].** For fixed $B$ and $m \ge 2B - 1$, with $\rho = \Delta/n$, $\epsilon = 1 - a^2/\rho$ and $f$ the fractional part of $ma/\rho$,
$$\frac{N(B, mA) - nR(m, B)}{(B + 1)\,n} = -\frac{m^2\epsilon}2 + \frac{m(B - 1)(1 - a)}2 - \frac{B\big((2 - \rho)B + 1 + \rho\big)}6 + \frac{\rho f(1 - f)}2 + O(1/n).$$
Maximizing over real $m$ and dropping the term in $f$: a certificate with this $B$ exists iff $\epsilon < \dfrac{3(B - 1)^2(1 - a)^2}{4B\big((2 - \rho)B + 1 + \rho\big)}$. For $B \to \infty$ this is the condition $a^2/\rho + 3(1 - a)^2/(4(2 - \rho)) > 1$ of note 35; for $B = 1$ it is never satisfied below Johnson. At $\rho = 1/2$ the constants are $1/12$ ($B = 2$), $1/6$ ($B = 3$), $9/40$ ($B = 4$), $4/15$ ($B = 5$), $1/2$ ($B = \infty$). The smallest multiplicity of any certificate below the Johnson radius is $m = 11$ (for $B = 2$ and $B = 3$).

## 2. The fibre lemma

**Lemma F [proved; checked `48_fibre.py`: for $p \in \{2, 3, 5\}$, $1 \le m \le 9$, $0 \le b \le 4$, the space of all $Q \in \mathfrak{c}_m$ of $Y_1$-degree $\le b$ and $(T, S)$-degree $\le m + 2$ is computed from the definition; no monomial outside the stated support occurs, and for the leading coefficient every monomial of the stated support does occur].** Let $Q = \sum_{c \le b} Q_c(X, Y_0)Y_1^c$ lie in the contact ideal $\mathfrak{c}_m(\alpha, y_\alpha)$, in any characteristic. Then every monomial $T^rS^q$ of $Q_{b - e}$ with $r + q < m$ satisfies $2r + q \ge m - e$ (and $r + q \ge m - b$, which is Lemma N of note 40). In particular $Q_b$ lies in the ideal $(T^rS^q : 2r + q \ge m)$, and $(Y_0 - y_\alpha)^m$ divides $Q_b(\alpha, Y_0)$.

*Proof.* As in Lemma N of note 40, substitute $S = Ts$: a generator $T^iw^lY_1^e$ ($i + 2l \ge m$) becomes $T^{i + l}(s - Y_1)^lY_1^e$, so the coefficient $\Psi_N(s, Y_1)$ of $T^N$ in $Q(\alpha + T, y_\alpha + Ts, Y_1)$ is divisible by $(s - Y_1)^k$, $k = m - N$, for $N < m$. Write $\Psi_N = (s - Y_1)^k\Lambda_N$. The monomial $T^rS^qY_1^c$ of $Q$ contributes $T^{r + q}s^qY_1^c$, so $\deg_s\Psi_N \le N$; since $(s - Y_1)^k$ is monic of degree $k$ in $s$, $\deg_s\Lambda_N \le N - k = 2N - m$. And $\deg_{Y_1}\Lambda_N \le b - k$. The coefficient of $Y_1^{b - e}$ in $(s - Y_1)^k\Lambda_N$ is $\sum_i \binom ki(-1)^is^{k - i}\lambda_{b - e - i}$ with $\lambda_c$ the coefficients of $\Lambda_N$, which vanish for $c > b - k$; only $k - e \le i \le k$ contribute, so its $s$-degree is at most $e + 2N - m$. This coefficient is $\sum_q [T^{N - q}S^q]Q_{b - e}\cdot s^q$. So $[T^rS^q]Q_{b - e} = 0$ when $q > e + 2(r + q) - m$, that is when $2r + q < m - e$. $\square$

Sanity checks by hand: $Q = w = S - TY_1 \in \mathfrak{c}_2$ has $Q_1 = -T$ ($2r + q = 2$); the plane $\hat g_y = Y_0 - \Lambda_DY_1 - \psi_y$ of note 41 Section 4.2 has leading coefficient $\Lambda_D$, zero in every fibre; for $b = 1$, $m = 4$ the statement is "$(Y_0 - y_\alpha)^4$ divides $E_{Y_0}(\alpha, Y_0)$", which is note 40, Proposition 9 part 3. For $b \ge 2$ it is new.

**Corollary F [proved].** Let $G$ be a first integral with $b \ge 1$ and leading coefficient $\hat G_b = \sum_{k \le K} c_k(X)Y_0^{2k}$, $c_K \ne 0$ (it lies in $F[X, Y_0^2]$ by Lemma L of note 35). Put $\rho_K(j) = \max(j - K,\ 2j - b - 2K,\ 0)$ and $\nu(j) = \max(j, 2j - b)$.

1. Every $c_k$ vanishes to order $\ge \rho_K(j_\alpha)$ at $\alpha$. Hence $W \ge (b + 2K)\Delta + \sum_\alpha \rho_K(j_\alpha)$.
2. (Sudan step.) For a polynomial $P$ of degree $\le \Delta$ with agreement set $S_P$: either $\hat G_b(X, P) = 0$ (at most $2K \le \delta - b$ such $P$), or $W \ge b\Delta + \sum_{\alpha \in S_P}\nu(j_\alpha) + \sum_{\alpha \notin S_P}\rho_K(j_\alpha)$.
3. If $j_\alpha > K$ for $n'$ values of $\alpha$, then $\hat G_b(\alpha, Y_0) \equiv 0$ for these $\alpha$ and $W \ge (b + 2K)\Delta + n'$.

*Proof.* In characteristic 2, $\hat G_b(\alpha + T, y_\alpha + S) = \sum_l d_l(T)S^{2l}$ with $d_l = \sum_{k \ge l}\binom kl y_\alpha^{2(k - l)}c_k(\alpha + T)$. By Lemma F with $m = 2j_\alpha$ and Lemma N, the monomials $T^rS^{2l}$ have $2r + 2l \ge 2j_\alpha$ and $r + 2l \ge \nu(j_\alpha)$, so $d_l = O(T^{\rho_l(j_\alpha)})$, and $\rho_l$ is nonincreasing in $l$. $c_K = d_K$, and by descending induction $c_l = d_l - \sum_{k > l}\binom kl y_\alpha^{2(k-l)}c_k = O(T^{\rho_K})$. The monomial $c_KY_0^{2K}Y_1^b$ has weighted degree $\deg c_K + (b + 2K)\Delta \le W$: part 1. Part 2: $\hat G_b(X, P)$ has degree $\le W - b\Delta$, vanishes to order $\nu(j_\alpha)$ at the agreements (Lemma N) and to order $\rho_K(j_\alpha)$ elsewhere. Part 3 is part 1 with $\rho_K \ge 1$. $\square$

For $K = 0$ part 1 is Lemma L part 3 of note 35, and for $\rho_K = 0$ part 2 is Corollary N of note 40. What is new is the case $1 \le K < j$: a leading coefficient that involves $Y_0$ but has $Y_0$-degree below the contact order must vanish identically in the fibres. In the cheapest situation of notes 40 and 41 ($W$ close to $(b + 2)\Delta$, so $K = 1$) this costs $\sum_\alpha\max(j_\alpha - 1, 2j_\alpha - b - 2)$.

**Corollary 2.1 ($b = 2$, contact 4) [proved].** A first integral with $b = 2$, leading coefficient involving $Y_0$, and contact $\ge 4$ on $n_4$ lines has $W \ge \min(6\Delta,\ 4\Delta + n_4)$. (If $K \ge 2$, $W \ge 6\Delta$; if $K = 1$, part 3.) At rate 1/2 with $n_4 = n$: $W \ge 3n$, against the cut of Lemma C at $2.613\,n$ ($a = 0.70$) and the lower end $2.4\,n$ of notes 40 and 41.

## 3. Theorem 8'' (certificates with $B = 2$)

**Theorem 8'' [proved; the finite inequalities by `48_window.py A`, exact integers, $n = 2^{25}, 2^{26}, 2^{27}, 2^{28}$].** Let $F$ be a perfect field of characteristic 2, $D \subset F$ any $n$ points, $\Delta = n/2 + 5$, and $(a, m_0, B_{\mathrm{jet}}, C) \in \{(0.7065, 11, 15, 20340),\ (0.705, 14, 19, 25707)\}$, $A = \lceil an \rceil$. For every word $y$ the explainer space $\mathbb{E}$ of the certificate $(m_0, 2)$ is nonzero. Let $\mathcal{G}^*$ be the set of first integrals $G$ such that

- (i) $\hat G$ divides every element of $\mathbb{E}$; (ii) $G$ is absolutely irreducible over $F(\tau)$; (iii) $b \in \{1, 2\}$ and $\hat G_b$ involves $Y_0$, with $Y_0$-degree $2K \ge 2$; so $b + 2K \le \delta \le B_{\mathrm{jet}}$ and $W < m_0A$;
- (iv) the total contact $\sum_\alpha o_\alpha$ exceeds $2n$, and $j_\alpha \le \delta$ (Lemma M of note 40);
- (v) $W \ge (b + 2K)\Delta + \sum_\alpha\rho_K(j_\alpha)$, and $W \ge b\Delta + \min_{|S| = A}\big[\sum_{\alpha \in S}\nu(j_\alpha) + \sum_{\alpha \notin S}\rho_K(j_\alpha)\big]$ (Corollary F);
- (vi) if $b = 1$: $\sum_\alpha(o_\alpha^2 - o_\alpha - 1) \le W(W - \Delta)/\Delta$ (Lemma B of note 40), and if moreover $W < 6\Delta$, with $\hat G = \partial E$, $E$ a quintic: $\det\mathbf{E} = 0$ or $W \ge (4n_4 + 6n_6 + 8\Delta + 1)/3$ (Theorem 4 of note 45);
- (vii) inequality (2) of Lemma C of note 35 fails for $G$ for every pair $(m, B)$ with $m \le 960$ whose side condition $\Delta(m + B) \le mA - W$ holds.

Then $|\mathcal{G}^*| \le \lfloor B_{\mathrm{jet}}/3 \rfloor$ (5, respectively 6) and
$$|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \ \le\ C + \sum_{G \in \mathcal{G}^*}|L_G| ,$$
unless some first integral satisfying (i) to (iv) violates the second inequality of (v), in which case $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le B_{\mathrm{jet}} - 1$.

*Proof.* Proposition F of note 41: $|\mathrm{List}| \le (B_{\mathrm{jet}} - t)^2 + \sum_{G \mid H_{\mathbb{E}}}|L_G|$ with $t = \deg H_{\mathbb{E}}$, and a fixed factor has $b \le B = 2$. A factor that is not absolutely irreducible has at most $\delta^2/4$ sections (Lemma R of note 41) and one with $b = 0$ at most $\delta$. A factor with leading coefficient in $F[X]$ is cut by Lemma C with $(m, B) = (41, 1)$ for $b = 1$ and $(43, 2)$ for $b = 2$ at $a = 0.7065$ ($(51, 1)$ and $(54, 2)$ at $a = 0.705$), for every contact profile and every $W \le m_0A - 1$: Lemma U (b) of note 41, evaluated by `caseI_all_profiles`; the convexity hypothesis of Lemma U is trivial here because $B - b = 0$ and $R(m', 0) = m'(m' + 1)/2$. A factor with leading coefficient involving $Y_0$ and total contact $\le 2n$ is cut by the same pairs (Lemma U (a), `lemmaC_uniform`). A factor for which (vii) fails is cut by Lemma C with some $m \le 960$. Each cut factor has $|L_G| \le \delta\lfloor(960A - 1)/\Delta\rfloor = 1356\,\delta$ ($1353\,\delta$ at $a = 0.705$), which also dominates $\delta^2/4$ and $\delta$; so the cut factors and the Bezout term contribute at most $\max_t (B_{\mathrm{jet}} - t)^2 + 1356\,t = 1356\,B_{\mathrm{jet}}$. (iv) second part is Lemma M, (v) is Corollary F (if the Sudan inequality fails for the agreement set of every list member, every list member is a $Y_0$-root of $\hat G_b$, of $Y_0$-degree $\le \delta - b$), (vi) is Lemma B of note 40 and Theorem 4 of note 45. $\delta \ge b + 2K \ge 3$ and $\sum\delta \le B_{\mathrm{jet}}$ give the bound on $|\mathcal{G}^*|$. $\square$

**The degenerate case, working form [proved].** Let $E$ be a quintic with $\det\mathbf{E} = 0$ and $\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}$ independent over $F(X)$, so that $M_0\mathcal{C} = M_1\mathcal{A} + M_2\mathcal{B}$ with $M_i \in F[X]$, $M_0 \ne 0$ (note 45, Proposition 4.2), and put $N_\psi = M_2^2 + XM_1^2$, $f_P = M_0^2P + N_\psi$. Then a polynomial $P \ne N_\psi/M_0^2$ is a section of $\partial E$ iff $f_P\cdot E_Y(X, P)$ is a square in $F[X]$. If the contact is 4 on all lines and $P$ has agreement set $S$, $E_Y(X, P) = \Lambda_S^3r_P$ with $\deg r_P \le W - \Delta - 3|S|$, so
$$f_P\cdot\Lambda_S\cdot r_P \ \text{ is a square}, \qquad \deg r_P \le W - \Delta - 3A \le 0.016\,n \ \text{ at } a = 0.7065 .$$
*Proof.* With $P = \gamma^2 + X\eta^2$, $U = M_0\gamma + M_2$, $V = M_0\eta + M_1$: the section equation (1) of note 45 times $M_0$ is $V\mathcal{A}_P = U\mathcal{B}_P$, and $U^2 + XV^2 = f_P$. Hence $U^2E_Y(X, P) = U^2\mathcal{A}_P^2 + XV^2\mathcal{A}_P^2 = \mathcal{A}_P^2f_P$ and likewise $V^2E_Y(X, P) = \mathcal{B}_P^2f_P$; $(U, V) \ne 0$ because $f_P \ne 0$. Conversely $E \equiv (Y - N_\psi/M_0^2)E_Y$ modulo $F(X^2)[Y^2]$. $\square$

So a list member on a degenerate component is a polynomial $P$ of degree $\le \Delta$ such that the fixed affine function $f_P = M_0^2P + N_\psi$ of $P$ is $\Lambda_S$ times a square, up to a factor of degree at most $0.016\,n$: its odd-multiplicity roots are its agreement set, up to $0.016\,n$ points. I could not bound the number of such $P$. Attempts that did not work: Mason and Stothers on $f_{P_1} - f_{P_2} = M_0^2(P_1 - P_2)$ (the radical count leaves a margin of several tenths of $n$, as in note 45); the four-member relation given by the vanishing third divided difference of $E_Y(X, \cdot)$ in $Y^2$ at four list members (true, no contradiction found); a Pade count for $\tilde\psi = N_\psi/M_0^2$ (its degrees are bounded only by $2W - 7\Delta + 1 \approx 1.8\,n$ and $2W - 8\Delta \approx 1.3\,n$, above $n$, so "the word is a rational function of bounded degree" carries no information by itself). The suggestion of the brief (compare with the codewords close to $\psi$) therefore fails for a reason that can be stated exactly: the interpolating function of a degenerate component is not of low degree, and in the two known families (polynomial $\tilde\psi$ of degree about $0.7\,n$) it is the factor $\mathcal{B} \in F[X]$, not the degree of $\tilde\psi$, that kills the sections.

**The two known families.** Unchanged: the 11/16 family ($W = 2.75\,n$) and the 27/40 family ($W = 2.70\,n$) are degenerate and lie above the cut at both agreements ($2.6352\,n$ and $2.6498\,n$); they carry no list. Nothing here contradicts them.

## 7. Consistency with what is known

Theorem 8'' is conditional, so it cannot contradict the lower bounds of note 21 (families at agreement $5/8$, $9/16$ and $5/8 + \epsilon$, all below $0.69$; at $a \ge 0.705$ the padding family has no member) or the exhaustive data of note 18 (lists of at most 4 at $n = 16$). The unconditional statements here are Proposition 1.2 (verified by brute force and at toy size), Lemma F (verified in three characteristics on all $m \le 9$, $b \le 4$, and consistent with the flat cubic, the plane $\hat g_y$, the squares, and the $b = 1$ fibre statement of note 40), and Corollary 2.1, which is an existence bound. It is consistent with the squares $\hat g_y^2$ ($K = 0$, leading coefficient $\Lambda_D^2$, as Corollary F part 1 requires with $\rho_0(2) = 2$), and it is **sharp on the data of note 45, Section 5 [checked]**: in the lattice of first integrals with $b \le 2$, $(u, v)$-degree $\le 5$ and contact $\ge 4$ on all lines (there $K \le 1$), Corollary F gives $W \ge 4\Delta + n$ when the leading coefficient involves $Y_0$ and Lemma L gives $W \ge 2\Delta + 2n$ when it does not; with $\Delta = n/2 - 1$ the smaller bound is $44$ at $n = 16$ and $92$ at $n = 32$, and the first genuine $b = 2$ minimum computed by `45_b2.py` for the word $X^{3n/4 - 1}$ is exactly $44$ (rerun here) and $92$ (table of note 45); for random words it is $45$ and $93$. So the value $V_1 \approx 2.9\,n$ that note 45 observed is this bound, and it holds for every word.
