## 0. Where this comes from

The window-mixed code (notes 57 to 66) is not proved beyond pairs: researcher 62 leaves the first rung conditional on Hypothesis L, researcher 63 leaves rungs 4 and 5 open (the rigid overlapping stratum), and note 65 finds no version that beats the proven knobs by a useful margin. ## 1. Structural fact: level 0's evaluation set is free (checked)

`crates/verifier/src/whir.rs`: `INITIAL_FOLDING_FACTOR = 6` is "level 0's fold count, which is also the number of lanes a level-0 leaf holds". The stacked witness of $2^\mu$ words of $K$ is 64 lanes, each a table of $k = 2^{\mu-6}$ words, each lane encoded on its own on a domain $D_0$ of $n_0 = k/\rho$ points; a Merkle leaf is one point of $D_0$ and holds the 64 lane values. Level 0's fold is the linear combination of the lanes with the six sumcheck challenges. No fibre of a subspace polynomial is used at level 0.

Consequence: the level-0 row code can be any $K$-linear code $C_0: K^k \to K^{n_0}$ with (i) a fast encoder, (ii) rows the verifier can turn into a weight on the folded table cheaply (a tensor $\bigotimes_i (1, x_i)$ is the cheap case: today's rows are of that form with $x_i = \hat W_i(y)$), (iii) a distance bound, and (iv) for the DEEP fold, a proved list bound for $C_0 \otimes E$ on $E$-valued words. Levels 1 and later are untouched. Level 0 is about three quarters of an opening at rate 1/2 (218 of its queries, note 08).

## 2. Constrained lanes: a proved list bound below Johnson that does not survive the fold

**Setting.** $F = \mathbb F_Q$ a field containing the evaluation points, $\mathbb E \supset F$ of degree $m$, $\sigma$ the $Q$-Frobenius of $\mathbb E$. A leaf of $m$ lanes is one symbol of $\mathbb E \cong F^m$. Let $X_e \in F[Y]$ have degree exactly $e$ (the novel basis), $H_e \subset \mathbb E$ be $F$-subspaces, and $C_H = \{ (G(y))_{y \in D} : G = \sum_{e<k} G_e X_e,\ G_e \in H_e \}$, with $|D| = n$, $\rho = k/n$.

**Proposition 2.1 (proved; a special case of Guruswami and Xing's subcodes, with a leading-coefficient argument that works in any basis of exact degrees).** Let $s \le m$ and suppose every $F$-subspace $W \subset \mathbb E$ of dimension at most $s-1$ has $\sum_e \dim_F(H_e \cap W) \le A$. Then for every word $w: D \to \mathbb E$, the codewords of $C_H$ agreeing with $w$ on at least $(n + sk + 1)/(s+1)$ leaves lie in an $F$-affine subspace of $C_H$ of dimension at most $A$, and there are at most $(\tau - \rho)^{-A}$ of them at agreement $\tau n$.

*Proof.* Interpolation: there are $A_0, \dots, A_s \in \mathbb E[Y]$, not all zero, $\deg A_j \le \Delta$ for $j \ge 1$, $\deg A_0 \le \Delta + k - 1$, with $A_0(y) + \sum_{j=1}^s A_j(y)\, w(y)^{Q^{j-1}} = 0$ on $D$, as soon as $(s+1)(\Delta+1) + k - 1 > n$. For a codeword $G$ agreeing with $w$ at $y \in D \subset F$ one has $w(y)^{Q^j} = G^{\sigma^j}(y)$ where $G^{\sigma^j} = \sum_e G_e^{Q^j} X_e$, so $R = A_0 + \sum_j A_j G^{\sigma^{j-1}}$ has degree at most $\Delta + k - 1$ and vanishes on the agreement set; with $\Delta = \lfloor (n-k+1)/(s+1) \rfloor$ it is zero. Not all of $A_1, \dots, A_s$ vanish. Let $d^*$ be their largest degree, $\bar a_j$ the coefficient of $Y^{d^*}$ in $A_j$, $\Lambda(z) = \sum_j \bar a_j z^{Q^{j-1}}$, $W = \ker \Lambda$, an $F$-subspace of dimension at most $s-1$. The difference $\Gamma$ of two solutions satisfies $\sum_j A_j \Gamma^{\sigma^{j-1}} = 0$; if $\Gamma$ has top index $e_1$, the coefficient of $Y^{d^* + e_1}$ is $\mathrm{lc}(X_{e_1}) \Lambda(\Gamma_{e_1})$, so $\Gamma_{e_1} \in W \cap H_{e_1}$. Filtering the $F$-space of such $\Gamma$ by degree gives dimension at most $\sum_e \dim (W \cap H_e) \le A$. Pruning: pick $A$ leaves uniformly at random and intersect the affine space with agreement at each; for a list member the event "every pick lies in its agreement set and lowers the dimension while it is positive" has probability at least $(\tau - \rho)^A$ (a nonzero difference of codewords vanishes on at most $\rho n$ leaves), and these events are disjoint for distinct members. $\square$

With $Q$ astronomically larger than $k$, codimension-$c$ subspace designs with $A \approx (s-1)(m-s+1)/(c-s+2)$ exist (the generic count). Numbers at information rate 1/2: $m = 64$, $s = 5$, $c = 5$ gives agreement $0.619$ (156 queries instead of 218) for a rate loss of $5/64$; $m = 128$, $s = 8$, $c = 8$ gives $0.585$ (140 queries).

**Why it is not the answer (proved, by the structure of the argument).** The constraints must be linear over the field the fold challenges come from, so $F = E$ after folding and the $m$ components are lanes that stay unfolded. The bound is for the code whose symbol is the $m$-tuple. The DEEP fold lemma uses the list bound of the code the next oracle is compared against, so the next commitment must still hold $m$ lanes: a level with leaves of $2^\lambda$ words folds only $\lambda - \log_2 m$ variables. With $\lambda = 6$ and $m \ge 32$ that is one variable per level. If instead all 64 lanes are folded, the folded code is plain Reed-Solomon over $E$ and its list bound is the open one: an adversary can commit a word close to an unconstrained tuple $u'$ and no bounded number of batched constraint claims excludes all such $u'$ without a bound on their number. Folded Reed-Solomon codes (orbit leaves $\{\gamma^i a\}$) fail the same way and also cost $m$ kernel evaluations a query and an encoder that is not an additive NTT. **Closed: any list-decoding argument that needs big symbols pays leaf size over fold arity.**

A by-product (proved by the same computation with $m = 2$, $F = K$): for agreement above $(1 + 2\rho)/3$, the list of $\mathrm{RS}_E$ around an $E$-valued word is in bijection with a list of $\mathrm{RS}_K$ around a $K$-valued word. So the alphabet $E$ is not where lists can hide.

## 3. Random tree codes (open; the proposal)

**Definition.** Message: a table $m \in K^{2^v}$ indexed by $e \in \{0,1\}^v$. Leaves: $j = (j_{v+r-1}, \dots, j_0)$, rate $2^{-r}$. For every layer $i < v$ and every prefix $p = (j_{v+r-1}, \dots, j_{i+1})$ the random oracle gives twiddles $t_i(p, 0) \ne t_i(p, 1)$ in $K$. Put $x_i(j) = t_i(p_i(j), j_i)$ and $c_j = \sum_e m_e \prod_i x_i(j)^{e_i}$: the multilinear polynomial with coefficient table $m$ evaluated at the point $x(j) \in K^v$. The top $r$ bits of $j$ select one of $2^r$ independent trees.

**Facts (proved, elementary).**
- Encoding is the additive NTT's butterfly network: at layer $i$, $(u, w) \mapsto (u + t_i(p,0) w,\ u + t_i(p,1) w)$. With $t_i(p,1) = t_i(p,0) + 1$ (variant T1) the arithmetic is exactly today's; with independent twiddles (variant T2) it is one more multiplication a butterfly.
- Today's Reed-Solomon code on the subspace domain is the specialization $x_i(j) = \hat W_i(y_j)$. Hence every $k \times k$ minor of the generator is a nonzero polynomial in the twiddles: the generic tree code is MDS.
- Each row is the tensor $\bigotimes_i (1, x_i(j))$, so a level-0 query claim is a weight of the same form as today.
- Within one tree the map $m \mapsto (c_j)$ is a bijection $K^{2^v} \to K^{2^v}$; the redundancy is that the $2^r$ trees see the same table through independent transforms. Between two trees the transfer matrix is a product over layers of Lagrange coefficients $(t' + s)/(t + t')$, $(t + s)/(t + t')$.
- A leaf has private randomness: its bottom twiddle. Given the upper twiddles its row is $(u, s u)$ with $s$ uniform.
- A codeword vanishes on a whole block of $2^i$ leaves only if the partial evaluation at the block's prefix is the zero polynomial, an event one level up: zeros on blocks are paid at full price in the count of codewords.

**Costs (estimate).** Prover: the NTT is unchanged in T1; the twiddle table has about $n_0$ words, one sixty-fourth of the level-0 codeword, and costs about $n_0/15$ Keccak permutations to derive once per size. Verifier: one permutation squeezes the 15 twiddles of a four-level subtree, so a query needs about $v/4$, five or six, more permutations (today about sixteen a level-0 query natively), and the kernel as today.

**Target theorem (open).** In the random oracle model, except with probability $2^{-130}$ over the oracle, at $q = 2^{64}$, $n_0 = 2^{19}$ to $2^{25}$: (a) two codewords agree on at most $(\rho + \varepsilon_0) n_0$ leaves; (b) for $L$ up to about 16, no word of $E^{n_0}$ has more than $L$ codewords of $C_0 \otimes E$ at agreement $\tau_L = (1 + L\rho)/(L+1) + \varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon$ a small multiple of $1/64$. The rank of a pattern's incidence matrix is a rank over $K$, so $E$-valued words cost nothing extra; the union bound is over $2^{(L+1) n_0}$ patterns, which is where $1/64$ comes from. At rate 1/2 that would be about 138 queries at $L = 8$ and 125 at $L = 16$, instead of 218; at rate 1/4 about 72 instead of 109.

**Why it might be provable where the window-mixed code was not.** The window-mixed code put randomness on fixed windows of a structured code, so the windows on which a tuple is degenerate were chosen by the adversary. Here the degeneracy of a tuple at a block is itself an event over the twiddles one level up, with the same shape, and sibling subtrees are independent given their parents: an induction on the tree is available. The corresponding theory for Reed-Solomon codes at random points (GM-MDS, then quantitative versions) exists but its constants are too loose for a 64-bit field, and its encoder is a general multipoint evaluation.

**What could kill it.** (1) Generic tree codes may fail higher-order MDS for some patterns (to be tested at toy size first). (2) The proof may lose a constant factor in the slack, and $\varepsilon = c/64$ with $c = 10$ is useless. (3) The guest's cost of deriving twiddles.

## 4. What the lead worked out by hand after writing Section 3 (proved unless marked)

**4.1 The slack of about 1/64 is intrinsic.** The top $j$ levels form a small tree code of length $2^{j+r}$ on the level-$(v-j)$ blocks, whose symbols are the partial evaluations at those blocks, interleaved $2^{v-j}$ times. If one square minor of this top code vanishes, a family of messages of dimension $k 2^{-j}$ vanishes on $k$ leaves and can add $k 2^{-j} - 1$ more zeros: one coincidence wastes a whole block of constraints. A scale with $N$ blocks has $2^N$ block patterns, so at $N$ about 64 some coincidence exists with constant probability, and the code has codewords with about $(\rho + 1/64) n$ zeros. The count is the same at every scale, $2^{N(L+1)}$ patterns against $q^{-(\text{slack in blocks})}$, so the list thresholds are $\tau_L = (1 + L\rho)/(L+1) + c/64$ with $c$ at least about $(L+1)/L$ times an entropy, whatever the proof. (Estimate for the constant.)

**4.2 The top levels must be fixed by checkable conditions.** Two equal top-level twiddles across trees (probability about $2^{-62}$) drop the distance to $n/4$ at rate 1/2; a block pattern with slack one block at a scale of $N \le 32$ blocks is deficient with probability about $2^N q^{-2}$, between $2^{-96}$ and $2^{-126}$. Neither is below $2^{-130}$. So the ensemble is: top four or five levels satisfying explicit finite conditions (twiddles of a level pairwise distinct, the top code MDS, and whatever higher-order condition the proof needs, checked exhaustively once), random below.

**4.3 Frozen-block model.** With a virtual top variable the transform is a bijection of $K^{2^{v+r}}$, a product of random $2 \times 2$ kernels in butterfly topology, and the code is its restriction to inputs whose top-variable coefficients vanish. For the bijection, the sum over all inputs of the probability of a zero pattern is multiplicative over the two children of a node; the whole difficulty is the transversality of the frozen subspace with the subspace cut out by the pattern.

**4.4 A family every proof must handle.** For $W_0, W_1 \in K^{L \times r}$ and any $r$-tuple $\Psi$ of polynomials in the upper variables, the tuple of differences $M = (W_0 + x_0 W_1)\Psi$ has, at a leaf with bottom twiddle $b$, its $L$ values in the column space of $W_0 + b W_1$. Hence the statement "every system of constraint subspaces has the rank a generic matrix would give" is false for tree codes when the subspaces may depend on the twiddles (for $r = 1$, $L = 2$, rate 1/2: $2k$ constraints on $2k$ unknowns and $k/2$ dimensions of solutions). For list-decoding patterns the constraint subspaces are graphic and chosen from a fixed finite set, each multiply covered leaf still costs such a family one coincidence, and the family has dimension $rk/2$ against more than $k$ multiply covered leaves, so it is subcritical. An induction on the tree must therefore restrict the class of subspaces or count tuples by the profile of the column spaces of their block partial evaluations.

## 5. The splitting lemma (proved by the lead; not refereed)

Write $T_j$ for the code formed by the top $j$ levels: positions are the level-$j$ nodes ($n_j = 2^{j+r}$ of them), dimension $k_j = 2^j$, generator rows $g_p \in K^{k_j}$. $T_{j+1}$ has positions $(p, b)$, $b \in \{0,1\}$, messages $(a_0, a_1) \in (K^{k_j})^2$ and symbols $\langle g_p, a_0\rangle + t_{p,b} \langle g_p, a_1 \rangle$, with the twiddles $t_{p,b}$ of level $j+1$ independent of $T_j$. For a set $Z$ of positions of a code let $\mathrm{null}(Z)$ be the dimension of the space of messages whose codeword vanishes on $Z$. For a set $Z$ of positions of $T_{j+1}$ let $F$ be the nodes $p$ with both children in $Z$ and $H$ those with exactly one.

**Lemma 5.1.** In variants T1 and T2, if $|H| < q/2$, then for every $\nu \ge 1$,
$$\Pr\big[\mathrm{null}_{j+1}(Z) \ge \nu \,\big|\, T_j\big] \;\le\; 2 \sum_{R \subseteq H,\ \mathrm{null}_j(F \cup R) \ge 1} q^{\,2\,\mathrm{null}_j(F \cup R) \,-\, \nu \,-\, |H \setminus R|}.$$

*Proof.* A full pair forces $\langle g_p, a_0 \rangle = \langle g_p, a_1 \rangle = 0$ because its two twiddles differ. Let $S$ be the solution space and $R$ the set of $p \in H$ at which every element of $S$ has both $\langle g_p, a_0\rangle$ and $\langle g_p, a_1 \rangle$ zero; then $S \subseteq V_{F \cup R}$, of dimension $2\,\mathrm{null}_j(F \cup R)$. For $p \in H \setminus R$ the elements of $S$ with zero symbol pair at $p$ form a proper subspace, so, a space over $K$ not being a union of fewer than $q$ proper subspaces, at least $q^{\dim S}(1 - |H|/q)$ elements of $S$ have a nonzero symbol pair at every $p \in H \setminus R$, and for such an element $\langle g_p, a_1 \rangle \ne 0$ and $t_p = -\langle g_p, a_0\rangle / \langle g_p, a_1 \rangle$. For a slope vector $\rho \in K^{H \setminus R}$ let $S_\rho^\circ$ be the elements of $V_{F \cup R}$ with nonzero symbol pairs on $H \setminus R$ and slopes $\rho$. These sets are disjoint, so at most $2 q^{2\mathrm{null}_j(F \cup R) - \nu}$ slope vectors have $|S_\rho^\circ| \ge q^\nu/2$, and each is the realized one with probability $q^{-|H \setminus R|}$, the used twiddles of distinct half pairs being independent and uniform. Sum over $R$. $\square$

If $T_j$ is MDS the right side is at most $2(1 + 1/q)^{|H|} q^{-(s + \nu)}$ with $s = |Z| - k_{j+1}$: the bound of a uniformly random code, up to the linear rather than quadratic dependence on $\nu$.

**What it shows.** (1) Nullity at level $j+1$ is twice the nullity at level $j$ of a rounded set, minus the dropped half pairs, plus a fresh excess that costs one coincidence a unit: a defect born at level $i$ doubles at every level below, which is 4.1. (2) Distance is provable by induction on the levels, but not with the invariant "every set has excess nullity at most $e_j$": that gives $e_{j+1} = 2e_j + n_{j+1}/64$, a slack of $1/64$ a level. The invariant must be sensitive to scale: a set with few full pairs inherits nothing (if $|F| \le k_j - 2e_j$ no term of the sum carries a loss, given a two-sided invariant: sets of size at least $k_j + e_j$ have full rank and sets of size at most $k_j - e_j$ are independent), and a set made of full pairs has no fresh entropy. The honest count is: a bad set needs coincidences $\gamma_i$ at levels $i$ with $\sum_i 2^{v-i} \gamma_i \ge s + 1 + \text{(leaves added by rounding up)}$, at probability $q^{-\sum \gamma_i}$, and $\gamma_i$ is at most the number of half pairs dropped at level $i$ minus one. (3) The dual side (small sets are independent) is where the lemma is weak: it gives $q^{-1}$ where the truth is $q^{-(k - |Z| + 1)}$, because the count of slope vectors with large solution spaces is much smaller than the disjointness bound. (4) For $L \ge 2$ the same decomposition applies with $K^L$-valued symbols: at a half pair with class size $c$ one coincidence is paid and $c - 2$ conditions are structural (the two symbol vectors are proportional modulo the pattern subspace), a determinantal condition on the tuple, so the count that was linear for $L = 1$ becomes the number of points of a determinantal variety. That is the real content of the list-decoding theorem.

**Deterministic top.** For distance alone the top $J$ levels can be the Reed-Solomon specialization, which is MDS at every depth, so that coarse defects exist only below level $J$: slack about $1/64 + 130/(64 \cdot 2^{J+r+1})$. For lists the top code needs a higher-order property that is a finite check only at very small size.

## 6. Erratum to Section 1 and the findings of note 71 (checked in the code by researcher 71; consequences not refereed)

**Erratum.** Section 1 says level 0's fold is the linear combination of the lanes. In today's protocol it is not: every level, level 0 included, folds with the affine combiner $G + (\alpha + \beta X) F$ of [CWYZ26], where $X$ is the column's evaluation point (`crates/verifier/src/whir.rs:173-183`, `:244-251`, `:304-319`), sends a high part, and rests on a correlated agreement theorem for Reed-Solomon codes. What is right: no fibre is used, level 1 is a fresh encoding of the folded message, a query claim is a tensor weight, and ring switching, the stacked opening, the rotation of the terminal point, the batching of query claims and the final check do not look at level 0's domain. A tree code is not closed under multiplication by a column label, so with a tree code level 0 must fold with a scalar, which has a proof only inside the DEEP fold (class lemma sketched in note 71 Section 4.2: a loss of $g$ columns a round with at most $n/(g+1)$ bad challenges, so a 12-bit fold grind keeps the loss at $n_0/2^{13}$ a round).

**Costs (note 71, estimates).** Prover: T1 changes proving time by less than one percent, plus about 1.5 percent for the DEEP fold's points. Guest: 3 to 5 more permutations and 5 to 10 percent more cycles a level-0 query. Multilinear out-of-domain points save 2 or 3 points a level.

**Payoff against configuration C (bytes and Keccak exact in note 08's model, cycles estimated).** At rate 1/2 with C's knobs: $L = 2$ never pays; $L = 3$, $\varepsilon = 2/64$ is 11 to 13 percent below C with the guest 1 to 5 percent faster; $L = 4$, $\varepsilon = 2/64$ is 14 to 17 percent below; $L = 8$, $\varepsilon = 2/64$ is 20 to 23 percent below with the guest 12 to 16 percent faster; $L = 16$, $\varepsilon = 1/64$ is 24 to 27 percent below. At rate 1/4 the win starts at $L = 8$. With $\varepsilon = 4/64$ the win needs $L \ge 8$. So the theorem is worth having only for $L \ge 3$ with $\varepsilon \le 2/64$, or $L \ge 8$.

**The extractor (note 71 Section 6.1; the lead's reading).** `formal/`'s end statement is knowledge soundness with a deterministic straight-line extractor of proved running time, which descends from the final polynomial and decodes each previous tuple on the agreement columns with at most one error. Two things change. (1) Erasure decoding of a tree code is a dense linear system. Writing it in the leaf coordinates of one tree leaves about $(1 - \tau) k$ unknowns, so Gaussian elimination costs about $0.04\,k^3$ field operations, $2^{49}$ at $\mu = 24$ and $2^{67}$ at $\mu = 30$, inside today's budget $2^{\max(58, 2\mu + 9)}$ but only just at the largest size, and it has to be proved for the cost machine. (Lead's estimate, not checked.) (2) With a scalar fold a wrong column is repaired by one value of the challenge, the adversary can give all wrong columns the same value, so the agreement set contains wrong columns with probability about $6 n_0/q$ a level and no decoder can absorb them. Knowledge soundness at 125 bits then needs fold grinds of $\log_2 n_0 - 2$ bits a round at level 0, an estimated 4 to 7 percent of proving time at rate 1/2 and 6 to 11 at rate 1/4; an alternative is to fold the lanes twice with independent challenges and carry two folded lanes into level 1, which makes the probability $n_0/q^2$ at the cost of a level-1 polynomial twice as large. Neither is free.

**All or nothing (note 71 Section 6.2).** A tree code with a scalar fold has no proved mode without the DEEP fold and its own list theorem: at unique decoding it needs about 260 queries at rate 1/2. There is no partial deployment.

## 7. Correction to the target theorem of Section 3 (after note 69, Sections 4 and 5; exact at toy size, mechanism proved there)

The generic tree code is not higher-order MDS. For a split level $i$, a message $A \otimes B$ with $A$ in the top code (the $N = n/2^i$ blocks, dimension $k_{\rm up} = k/2^i$) and $B$ a lower factor has codeword $A(\mathrm{block}(j))\,B(\mathrm{lower}(j))$. A list of the top code around a block word lifts to a list of the full code whose members all agree with the word, in addition, on the $2^i - 1$ zeros of $B$, which can be put on any leaves. With a generic block word every $k_{\rm up}$-subset of blocks interpolates a member: $\binom{k_{\rm up}/\rho}{k_{\rm up}}$ codewords at agreement $\rho + \rho(1-\rho)/k_{\rm up} - O(1/n)$. At rate 1/2: 6 below $5/8$, 70 below $9/16$, 12870 below $17/32$, $6 \cdot 10^8$ below $33/64$. Reed-Solomon on a subspace has the same lists (the same lemma with $A(\hat W_i(y)) B(y)$); Reed-Solomon at random points has none, because its upper points are all distinct. In note 69's exhaustive and sampled scans every rank-deficient pattern is block-aligned and of this lifted form (lower-factor spaces of dimension one or two); patterns scattered over leaves are never deficient.

Consequences.
1. Target (b) of Section 3, "at most $L$ codewords at $(1 + L\rho)/(L+1) + \varepsilon$", is false for small $\varepsilon$, and so is any proof by full rank of every pattern's incidence system. Section 5's item (4) describes a proof of a false statement and is withdrawn; Lemma 5.1 and the distance discussion stand.
2. The application survives: the DEEP fold tolerates lists up to about $2^{300}$, and the query count depends on the threshold only. The staircase costs $\log_2 \Lambda \approx 1/(2\varepsilon)$ bits at rate 1/2: 14 bits at $\varepsilon = 1/32$, 29 at $1/64$, less than one out-of-domain point.
3. New target: except with probability $2^{-130}$ (top levels satisfying checked conditions), every word of $E^n$ has at most $\Lambda(\varepsilon)$ codewords at agreement $\rho + \varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon \ge c_0/64$, with $\Lambda(\varepsilon) \le 2^{300}$. If that held with $c_0 = 2$, level 0 would need about 120 to 130 queries at rate 1/2 instead of 218.
4. What must be controlled is the number of solutions, not the rank: a structure statement (every list is, up to few members, a lifted list of a top code plus a fresh part) or a direct induction on depth for the list-size function.
5. A remark on the original problem: the same lifted lists are the known lists of Reed-Solomon on the deployed domain, so the attack frontier of note 38 and the staircase are the same object; the generic tree code has, at toy size, slightly fewer deficient patterns than the subspace specialization and none of a different kind.

What the lead worked out on the questions put to researcher 72:
- **Why fast encoders have lifted lists.** A row that is (upper part shared by a block) tensor (lower part) gives the product codewords $A \otimes B$ of note 69, and sharing upper coordinates at every scale is what makes the butterfly network cost $\log n$ a symbol. An ensemble whose upper points are all distinct at the scale of the gain (Reed-Solomon at random points, or multilinear evaluation at independent random points) has no such lists and, for Reed-Solomon, an existing theory, but its encoder is a general multipoint evaluation: about $2 n \log_2^2 n$ products against $\tfrac12 n \log_2 n$, 30 to 70 times the NTT. Dead on cost.
- **Dense random node matrices** (a uniformly random $2^a \times 2^a$ matrix at the bottom of a block) do not remove the lifts and do not make the proof elementary: the count that matters moves to the code one level up.
- **An intermediate candidate, not analysed further.** Level 0 on a union of $J$ cosets $a_j + V$ of one subspace, with independent random shifts $a_j$ and $|V| = n_0/J$: encoder $\rho J$ products a symbol for the reductions plus the NTTs inside cosets (about 2.4 times the NTT at $J = 32$, rate 1/2), verifier as today with a table of $J \log n$ constants and no hashing, top code a Reed-Solomon code at $J$ random points, and lifts worth at most one coset, $(1 - \tau)/J$ in agreement. Inside a coset the structure is today's, so a list bound would need exactly the reduction to the quotient that was open for today's code (the period statements of notes 39 and 44), and the randomness is only $J$ field elements. Not better founded than the tree code.
- **Zyablov and Pinsker style arguments** (among a large list take $\log_q$ of it linearly independent members) need the joint law of several codewords at one leaf; for tensor rows those lie on a line given the upper twiddles, so the argument does not start.

## 10. Report 73: the distance theorem (proved there, one author, not refereed)

With $T_J$ MDS and the twiddles of levels $J+1$ to $v$ random (T1 or T2, same constants), except with probability $p$ every nonzero codeword has fewer than $(\rho + \varepsilon_0) n$ zeros and every set $Z$ of leaves has rank above $\min(|Z|, k) - \varepsilon_0 n$, with $\varepsilon_0$ explicit (note 73 Section 5). At $p = 2^{-130}$, $n = 2^{19}$ to $2^{25}$, $64\,\varepsilon_0$ is:

| $J$ | rate 1/2 | rate 1/4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 5.3 to 5.7 | 3.1 to 3.2 |
| 5 | 4.1 to 4.5 | 2.4 to 2.6 |
| 6 | 3.5 to 3.9 | 2.1 to 2.3 |
| 8 | 2.9 to 3.3 | 1.8 to 2.0 |

"$T_J$ is MDS" is checkable by enumeration only for $J = 4$ at rate 1/2; a larger $J$ means a top that is MDS by construction, in practice the Reed-Solomon specialization of the top $J$ levels. Lemma 5.1 of this note is re-proved there with exact hypotheses and is correct (the factor 2 is $1/(1 - |D|/q)$). The proof: every flat has a chain of flats without rounding along which the excess in leaf units only grows, by $u_{j+1} G_j$ at level $j$; defect events are indexed by prefixes of chains with a prefix code satisfying Kraft; conditioning is top-down, level by level. The main term (about $3/64$ at rate 1/2) is an artifact of the lemma's bound being blind to the nullity left; the conjectured truth is about $h(\rho)/64 + 2/n_{J+1}$. The dual statement (small sets are independent) is proved only at the first random level. One measured difference between variants, unexplained: two-level T1 parents behave like Reed-Solomon parents for nullity 2, T2 parents like a random MDS code.

This settles the easy half. The list bound of Section 8 is untouched by it.

## 11. Report 74 (generic lists) and a consequence at low rates

**Report 74 (one author, not refereed; exact checks at toy size).** For the generic tree code (T2, algebraically independent twiddles):
- Theorem 4.2 there (proved by induction on the levels with a derivative lemma): with one functional kept at each leaf, the incidence rows of a list of distinct codewords are linearly independent. Hence, for any $t$ distinct codewords, the number of leaves at which at least two of them coincide is at most $(t-1)k - 1$ (call this $Q_t$; $Q_2$ is the MDS property), and every pattern has weight at most $Lk - 1 + \sum_x (c_x - 2)_+$: all the excess over the generalized Singleton bound sits at leaves where three or more members agree.
- Three members: the largest weight is exactly $5k/2 - 2$, so three codewords at $3n/4 - 1$ are optimal at rate 1/2.
- Every deficient kernel of note 69's four exhaustive scans is spanned by products $A \otimes B$ with a one-dimensional lower factor. The lead's generic rank recursion (Section "what must be controlled", item 3 of the brief to 74) is proved only when every class has two members, is false for arbitrary subspaces, and held on all 760000 patterns tested.
- Lower bounds move up (one-sided staircase): $\binom{N-1}{\rho N}$ codewords at agreement $\rho + 1/N - 1/n$. Frontier at rate 1/2: 16 to 35 members at $5/8$, 36 to 64 at $0.566$, 6435 at $9/16$, $3 \cdot 10^8$ at $17/32$, $9 \cdot 10^{17}$ at $33/64$; a budget of $2^{300}$ is exceeded below $1/2 + 1/512$.
- No list bound below the Johnson radius at rate 1/2, and a weight bound uniform in the rate cannot give one below agreement $3\rho/2$. Conjecture 5.2 there: at most $\binom{N-1}{\rho N} + nN$ codewords at agreement $\rho + 1/N$.
- Random twiddles: a top codeword with $\rho N + b - 1$ zero blocks at a scale of $N$ blocks gives a subcode of dimension $n/N$ vanishing on $k + (b-1) n/N$ leaves, so lists of any size at agreement $\rho + b/N$; with the top checked to $N = 32$ this forces $\varepsilon \ge 2/64$ (first-moment estimate). The list threshold can never be below the distance slack.

**Consequence worked out by the lead (computation, not refereed): the properties $Q_t$ give a list-decoding threshold below Johnson at rates 1/8 and lower, and nothing at rates 1/2 and 1/4.** If a word has unboundedly many codewords at agreement $\tau n$, let $\mu$ be the distribution over leaves of the fraction $\theta$ of members agreeing there; averaging $Q_{j+1}$ over sub-lists gives $\mathbb E_\mu[h_j(\theta)] \le j \rho$ for all $j \ge 1$, with $h_j(\theta) = \Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(j+1, \theta) \ge 2]$, and $\tau = \mathbb E_\mu[\theta]$. The linear program (`scripts/68_lp_twise.py`, grid of 2001 points) gives:

| rate | $2^{-1}$ | $2^{-2}$ | $2^{-3}$ | $2^{-4}$ | $2^{-5}$ | $2^{-7}$ | $2^{-10}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| threshold from $Q_t$ | 0.7071 | 0.5000 | 0.3276 | 0.2051 | 0.1237 | 0.0416 | 0.0072 |
| Johnson | 0.7071 | 0.5000 | 0.3536 | 0.2500 | 0.1768 | 0.0884 | 0.0312 |
| queries at 108 bits | 216 | 108 | 67 | 47 | 36 | 24 | 15 |
| Johnson queries | 216 | 108 | 72 | 54 | 43 | 31 | 22 |

For $j = 1$ alone the program is the Johnson bound. This is the limit of large lists; an explicit list size at a threshold slightly above these values is still to be derived, as is the passage from generic to random twiddles.

**What it is worth (estimate).** Level 0 at rate 1/2 or 1/4 gains nothing. The later levels of an opening have rates $2^{-4}$, $2^{-7}$, $2^{-10}$ and their leaves hold lanes too, so they could be tree codes; their queries would drop by about 13, 24 and 30 percent, but they are only about a quarter of an opening, so the opening shrinks by about 5 percent before the DEEP fold's own costs. Not a practical win. The statement that would be one is still Conjecture 5.2 at $N = 8$ or 16 (thresholds $5/8$ and $9/16$ at rate 1/2), with random twiddles.

## 13. Two constructions to remove the lifts (lead, round 18; nothing here is tested or proved)

**Why.** Every list beyond the generalized Singleton bound found for the tree code (and for today's code) is a lift: a list of a small top code on blocks, times a lower factor whose zeros all members share. A lift needs (i) rows that are (upper part shared by a block) tensor (lower part), and (ii) agreement sets that are unions of blocks. The counting technique is provably too weak in the presence of lifts (note 74). So: keep the butterfly encoder, and break (ii) by a cheap operation that no block structure survives. If the resulting code has no list beyond the generalized Singleton bound, the target becomes the classical one (at most $L$ codewords at $(1 + L\rho)/(L+1) + \varepsilon$), for which full rank of incidence systems is the right technique again.

**V1, the mixed tree code.** After the tree encoding $c$, output $c'(x) = c(x) + \sum_{a=1}^{d} \mu_{x,a}\, c(\pi_a(x))$, with $\pi_1, \dots, \pi_d$ fixed permutations of the leaves derived from the oracle (far apart at every scale) and $\mu_{x,a} \in K$ from the oracle. The same $\pi$, $\mu$ for all 64 lanes, so a leaf of 64 words is read once per partner. Row of leaf $x$: $g_x + \sum_a \mu_{x,a} g_{\pi_a(x)}$, a sum of $d + 1$ tensors: $d + 1$ kernels a query, no extra Merkle opening. Facts (elementary): the code is MDS for generic parameters (specialize $\mu \to 0$); a product message $A \otimes B$ has $c'(x) = A(\mathrm{bl}\,x) B(x) + \sum_a \mu_{x,a} A(\mathrm{bl}\,\pi_a x) B(\pi_a x)$, so a lifted member agrees at $x$ only if $x$ and all its partners lie in its blocks or in the zeros of $B$; for a union of blocks of density $\varphi$ at a scale of $N \le \sqrt n$ blocks the fraction of such leaves is about $\varphi^{d+1}$ for every union (the partner counts between blocks are uniform), so with $d = 1$ a lifted member at agreement $\rho + 1/N$ falls to about $\rho^2$, below capacity; a coarse coincidence in the tree (a top minor vanishing) no longer gives a family with many common zeros, for the same reason. Each leaf has $d$ private random coefficients in addition to its bottom twiddle: for a fixed tuple of codewords the probability that leaf $x$ satisfies $\kappa_x$ conditions is exactly $q^{-r}$ with $r$ the rank, modulo the pattern subspace, of the plain leaf vectors at the partners, when the plain leaf vector at $x$ lies in their span. This is the window-mixed code of note 57 on a base code that is itself random at every scale, which is what that analysis lacked.

**V2, the twisted trees.** Tree number $t$ reads the message table through an $\mathbb F_2$-linear change of the index bits $M_t \in \mathrm{GL}_v(\mathbb F_2)$ ($M_1 = I$): $c^{(t)}(j) = \sum_e m_e \prod_i x^{(t)}_i(j)^{(M_t e)_i}$. As a function of $e$ this is still a tensor, with coordinates $z_i = \prod_{l : (M_t)_{li} = 1} x^{(t)}_l(j)$, so the verifier pays a few products a query for a sparse $M_t$; the prover pays a bit-linear permutation of the table per tree and no multiplication. A message that is a product for the split of one tree is not one for the others, so a lift cannot collect block agreements in two trees. Not known: whether the code is MDS for a given $M_t$ (specialization does not show it).

**What must be found out, in this order.** (a) At toy size, exactly: are V1 and V2 MDS, and higher-order MDS (no rank-deficient pattern against a uniform matrix)? (b) At $n = 64$ to $256$: what do the lifted lists of notes 69 and 74 become, and does a search find any new structured list? (c) If a variant is clean: prove the generic statement (full rank of every pattern's incidence system), then the random one with a slack. (d) Cost in the prover (random leaf reads) and in the guest ($d + 1$ kernels and twiddle paths a query).

## 21. Report 82: an exact rank formula (one author, not refereed; proved in special cases and by exhaustion at small sizes, otherwise a tested conjecture)

**Formula (C\*).** For a pattern $P$ of the generic T2 tree code let $Q^*$ be the pattern one level up whose space at a bottom pair $p$ is the set of functionals with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$ that kill both bottom slices of every solution at $p$, and $\Delta_p$ the span of the difference vectors of the solutions of $Q^*$ at $p$. Then $r(P) = 2\, r_{\rm up}(Q^*) + \sum_x \mathrm{rank}(\Lambda_x|_{\Delta_{p(x)}})$; equivalently $r(P) = \min_Q [2 r_{\rm up}(Q) + \sum_x (r_{\rm up}(Q \vee \Lambda_x @ p(x)) - r_{\rm up}(Q))]$; iterated, a generalized Singleton count in which a node of height $i$ counts $2^i$ times and costs the rank of its space on the difference vectors of the solutions one level up. The recursion does not close on class patterns; it closes on binary patterns (a node carries any subspace of the even-weight code of length $L + 1$ over $\mathbb F_2$).

Proved there: the upper bound for every $Q$; (C\*) when at most two leaves have a full part not spanned by binary functionals; note 74's one-level identity when at most four leaves have a class of three or more (sharp); for three members a closed expression with one unknown integer (the number of copies of "three lines in a plane"), the twiddle-dependent functionals being the points of a conic; by exhaustive exact computation, the formula for all three-member patterns at $(4, 8)$, all four-member binary patterns at $(2, 4)$, all five-member systems on five and six leaves at $k = 2$. ## 23. Report 85: the list bound reduced to one missing inequality (one author, not refereed)

No bound is proved. The rank formula, even read as an inequality, gives no list bound: a random profile with exponentially many members at agreement $5n/8$ satisfies every refereed inequality (MDS, $Q_t$ at every size and scale, the three-member weight, the matching inequality); it is not realized at $n = 8, 16$ and nothing refereed excludes it at large $n$.

Proved there, unconditionally (it uses only that any $N/2$ blocks determine a codeword): for every list at agreement $k + e$ and every scale $N = n/b$, $\sum_l (e + b - \beta_l(N)) \le b\,F(N)$ with $F(N) = (N/2 + 1)\binom{N}{N/2}$, where $\beta_l(N)$ is the number of leaves at which member $l$ agrees with the word together with a member from which it differs somewhere in the block of scale $N$ (incoherent leaves). So if the members have on average at most $e$ incoherent leaves at some scale $N$ the list has at most $F(N)$ members (350 at $N = 8$, $2^{17}$ at 16, $2^{259}$ at 256); members whose agreement set contains at most ten full blocks covering $15n/32$ leaves number fewer than $n^{10}$; members with at least $5N/8$ full blocks number at most the list size at scale $N$.

**The missing inequality (M):** at some scale $N \le 256$ the members of a list at agreement $k + e$ have on average fewer than $e + n/N$ incoherent leaves, with a margin $n^{-c}$. A sufficient one-level form: a member lies in the splitting classes of at most $m_0$ blocks of each height. On every list computed (M) holds at scale 8 with one leaf to spare on the staircases, except a six-member list at $n = 32$ which needs scale 16. No refereed statement bounds the number of incoherent leaves: that needs a statement in which a leaf carrying several members costs more than one condition, and the general statement of that kind (the rank formula) is false.

So the hard result is now: (M) for the plain T2 tree code, then its version with slack for twiddles in GF($2^{64}$). It says that a list cannot be "fine": most common agreements of a member must be shared with members that agree with it on the whole block.

## 24. Report 86: the rank formula is proved at small sizes and false from $(16, 32)$ on (one author, not refereed; the counterexample was found independently by referee 87)

The binary recursion (C\*) of note 82 is proved, with a computer-assisted proof in characteristic 2, for three members at $(k, n) = (4, 8), (8, 16), (4, 16)$ and for four members at $(4, 8)$. It is false at $(16, 32)$: a three-member pattern of weight 30 has rank 29 while the binary count is 30 for every binary pattern one level up, and a variant of weight 32 has rank 31, that is, a list of three distinct codewords (agreements 19, 18, 18 of 32) that the recursion says cannot exist. So no proof at every size exists, and parts (ii) to (iv) of note 82's Theorem 5.1 with its two corollaries fail for $k \ge 16$.

What is true at every size (proved there): for a saturated pattern the deficiency splits as $w - r = \sum_i 2^i (b_i + \Delta_i)$ with $b_i$ the binary term and $\Delta_i \ge 0$ the number of relations among the large nodes of height $i$ modulo the meet pattern above; for three members $\Delta \le l - 2$ with $l$ the number of lonely triples whose full part contains a line that is not binary, and this is attained. The object that replaces the binary pattern is, at each node, the space of all functionals with coefficients in the field of the upper twiddles that kill the solutions there: finite-dimensional data, but not one of finitely many states, and the scalar recursion does not close on it. No bound on the number of such nodes per level was found, so no list bound follows.

## 25. What the defect is: scalar twiddles commute (lead; exact rank checks at one draw, not refereed)

**Observation (checked on every failing pattern of note 86).** Replace every twiddle of the T2 tree code by an independent random $d \times d$ matrix over the field (symbols become vectors of $K^d$, a butterfly is $(a, b) \mapsto (a + Tb, a + T'b)$, the leaf map is the product of the matrices on its path). On all 102 patterns of `scripts/86_tmp/all_fail_16_32.txt` whose rank with scalar twiddles is one below the weight, the rank with $2 \times 2$ matrix twiddles is exactly $2 \times$ the weight: the defect is gone. On note 86's first counterexample this was also checked with $d = 3$ and over a prime field (ranks 29, 60, 90 for $d = 1, 2, 3$), and replacing only the bottom twiddles by matrices does not remove it (29 per unit for every $d$). Script: `scripts/68_nc_rank.py` (characteristic 2 over GF($2^{16}$), two draws a pattern, `python3 68_nc_rank.py <pattern file> 1 2`).

**Reading.** The binary recursion is the rank formula of the tree code with twiddles that do not commute, and the defects $\Delta$ of note 86 are identities between products of twiddles along different paths, which hold only because scalars commute. This is the situation of Edmonds' problem: a matrix depending linearly on variables has a rank given by a min-max formula when each variable sits in a matrix of rank one (matroid intersection: this is the one-functional case, property $Q_t$, where everything was proved), and has no such formula when the variables sit in matrices of higher rank (here a leaf carrying three or more members); what does have a min-max formula in general is the non-commutative rank, obtained by replacing the variables by generic matrices, and the commutative rank can be smaller. So the failure of every exact formula tried in notes 76, 82 and 86 is structural: they were formulas for the non-commutative rank. One level alone is not the cause (the one-level blow-up changes nothing, in agreement with note 86's exact one-level formula); the collapse needs two levels. **Consequence for the proof search:** for the scalar code the binary count is only an upper bound on the rank, the lists of the scalar code are the lists of the non-commutative code plus extra ones, and an upper bound on lists must bound the extra ones, for which there is no technique. **Consequence for constructions (not proposed, recorded):** matrix twiddles are compatible with the fold (the fold acts on lanes, the code stays linear over $K$, its extension to $E$ has symbols in $E^d$), cost $d$ multiplications a word in the encoder and one fold variable less a level for $d = 2$; they would make the generic theory finite-state, but the list bound would still need the missing inequality (M), and Section 26 applies to them as well.

## 26. A ceiling for every proof of the present kind over a 64-bit field (lead; estimate, not refereed)

Every proof in this programme, and every proof in the literature for codes with few random elements (random Reed-Solomon codes), has two steps: a statement for generic twiddles (a list of $M$ members at agreement above $\tau_{gen}(M)$ does not exist), and a union bound over agreement patterns for twiddles in the real field, where a pattern at agreement $\tau_{gen}(M) + \varepsilon$ is bad only if about $\varepsilon n$ leaves each see an independent event of probability about $2^{-64}$ (a leaf has one private twiddle; this is the accounting of notes 73 and 84, and the best one known for Reed-Solomon codes at random points). There are $2^{M h(\tau) n}$ patterns, so the slack is at least $\varepsilon \ge M\,h(\tau)/62$.

Lifted lists exist for every code whose top levels form a small code with vector symbols, whatever lies below (Section 7): this is any code with a butterfly encoder and tensor evaluation functionals, so any code the protocol can use. With the lifted lists of Section 8 as lower bounds on $\tau_{gen}(M)$, the best threshold this kind of proof can reach is (`scripts/68_transfer_ceiling.py`):

| rate | Johnson | 62 bits an event, one event a leaf | 126 bits an event (twiddles in $E$, or $2 \times 2$ matrix twiddles), one event a leaf | one event a surplus incidence (the count of a random linear code) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.7071 (217 queries) | 0.733 at $M = 6$: above Johnson | 0.694 at $M = 8$ (205 queries) | 0.652 at $M = 8$ (176 queries), lower with larger lists |
| 1/4 | 0.5000 (108 queries) | 0.535 at $M = 7$: above Johnson | 0.473 at $M = 8$ (101 queries) | 0.426 at $M = 8$ (88 queries), lower with larger lists |

For comparison, a code with no lifted lists at all (generic Reed-Solomon points, no fast encoder) would reach 0.672 at rate 1/2 with one event a leaf (189 queries).

**So even if the missing inequality (M) and the whole generic theory were proved, the transfer to GF($2^{64}$) by any known accounting gives nothing below the Johnson radius at rates 1/2 and 1/4, for the plain tree code or any variant of it.** Notes 73 and 84 are above even this ceiling (their slack is two to five times the first-moment value, and note 84 covers one row a leaf only). A useful result needs an accounting with about one independent event for each surplus incidence, which is what a random linear code has because each column is $k$ independent elements; a tree code has $2n$ random elements in all and a leaf with $c$ members has $c - 1$ rows on one private twiddle, the others falling on twiddles shared with its neighbours. I believe the truth for the tree code is close to the last column, but proving it needs both things that are missing: the generic rank of systems with several rows at a leaf (Section 25: no formula), and a robust version in which those rows are separate events. A first-moment count over messages instead of patterns is impossible for the same reason: it needs about $Mk + n$ independent events and the code has $2n$ random elements.

**Status of the goal.** Not reached. The list bound below Johnson at rate 1/2 is not proved for any code the protocol can use, and after Sections 23 to 26 the obstacle is the same in both steps: leaves carrying several members. For today's code (no random element at all) there is no technique (note 77).

## 27. Codes with a private perturbation at each position: equal to random Reed-Solomon on every pattern sampled, but sparse lifts survive (lead; exact rank checks at one draw, not refereed)

Section 25 suggested a code with scalar symbols whose encoder carries a small vector state (matrix twiddles, a private read-out vector at each leaf: the leaf functional is a tensor train instead of a rank-one tensor). Cheaper members of the same family were tested with it: two independent tree codes added with a private coefficient a leaf; a tree code, or today's Reed-Solomon code on a subspace in the novel basis, plus $r$ private multiples of raw message symbols at each position, $c_x = (Gm)_x + \sum_{i<r} \tau_{x,i}\, m_{\pi_i(x)}$. The last one costs $r$ multiplications a symbol and leaves the NTT alone.

**What the sampling shows.** On about five million near-critical list patterns (weight within 3 of $Lk$; uniform patterns and block patterns at every scale with a few perturbed leaves; 3 to 6 members; sizes $(4, 8)$ to $(64, 128)$; exhaustive for three members at $(4, 8)$), every variant with a private coefficient at each leaf has exactly the rank of a Reed-Solomon code at random points, including today's code over GF($2^{64}$) with $r = 1$, while the plain tree code and today's code lose rank on thousands of them, by up to 6. A two-dimensional state on the top levels only does not suffice. Scripts: `scripts/68_tt_rank.c` (prime field: tree code, tensor trains, sums, message perturbations, top-only states), `scripts/68_smoothed_rs.c` (GF($2^{64}$): today's code plus $r$ message symbols).

**Why that is not the truth at real sizes (checked, `scripts/68_sparse_lift.c`).** Take a list of the top code, lift it with a bottom factor that is a single basis vector, and drop the leaves that read a coordinate in its support. The difference messages vanish on every coordinate the remaining leaves read, so the perturbation does not see them and the list of the plain code survives there. With the three-member list of weight 18 at $(8, 16)$ as top: at $(64, 128)$, $(128, 256)$ and $(256, 512)$ with $r = 1$, and at $(256, 512)$ with $r = 3$, the pattern has weight above $2k$ and the perturbed tree code has rank $2k - 1$ where random Reed-Solomon has $2k$. Only a fraction $r/b$ of a lifted list is lost ($b$ the block size), so at real sizes the perturbed code keeps the lifted lists of every top code almost in full. Random patterns at small sizes cannot contain this structure, which is why the sampling above saw nothing.

**The first-moment proof and where it stops (lead; the one-direction bound is proved here, the rest is analysis).** With $r \ge L$ private coefficients a leaf, a fixed tuple of difference messages $\Delta$ satisfies a pattern with probability $\prod_x q^{-\rho_x(\Delta)}$, $\rho_x$ the rank of the read rows of $\Delta$ on the leaf's functionals, so a first moment over message tuples gives the bound of a random linear code, $(1 + L\rho)/(L+1) + H(\tau)/64$, provided the tuples with blind directions are few: this is hypothesis KH of note 79, and it has the exact form $\sum_\Delta q^{w_{\rm free}(\Delta)} = q^{Lk} \sum_\vartheta q^{-\dim \sum_x \theta_x \otimes M_x^\perp}$ over assignments $\vartheta = (\theta_x \in \Lambda_x)$, with $M_x^\perp$ the span of the base row and the read coordinates of $x$. For one direction it holds with $C = 2$ for any MDS base code: a nonzero message is blind with a zero of the base code at fewer than $k$ leaves, so $\sum_{\mu \ne 0} q^{b(\mu)} \le \sum_{|X| < k} q^{|X|} q^{k - |X|} \le 2^n q^k$. For several directions the blind tuples are lists of the base code whose difference messages vanish on the coordinates their agreement leaves read, and the sparse lifts above are exactly such tuples: KH fails for a message-side perturbation of a code with lifts. With a perturbation that reads codeword symbols instead (the mixed code V1 of Section 13), blind means that the base codeword vanishes on the leaf and its partners, which an MDS code pays for in full, so there the first moment may close with $d \ge L$ partners and an expanding partner graph; that is the construction the user declined, and it was not pursued.

**State when the work stopped (2026-11-21, at the user's request).** No list bound below the Johnson radius at rate 1/2 or 1/4 is proved for any code the protocol can use. What is proven and usable is unchanged: configuration C of note 08 and the rate trade.
