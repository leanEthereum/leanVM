## 1. The section equation and when a centre exists

Write $E = E_1(X, Y^2)\,Y + E_0(X, Y^2)$, so $E_Y = E_1(X, Y^2)$, and split in $X$: $E_i = E_i^0(\tau, Z) + XE_i^1(\tau, Z)$. For $P \in F(X)$, $Z_P = P^2 \in F(\tau)$ and $\varepsilon_P = E_1(X, Z_P)$, and the odd part of $E(X, P)$ gives **[proved]**
$$P \text{ is a section} \iff P \wedge \varepsilon_P = E_0^1(\tau, P^2). \tag{S}$$
This is the lead's equation $E_1^1(Z)g + E_1^0(Z)h + E_0^1(Z) = 0$ ($P = g + Xh$), and the square of equation (1) of note 45 ($E_1^0 = \mathcal{A}^2$, $E_1^1 = \mathcal{B}^2$, $E_0^1 = \mathcal{C}^2$). $F(X)$ is a plane over $F(\tau)$, $\wedge$ is its determinant, $f \wedge g = 0$ iff $f \equiv g$, and (S) says that $P$ lies on the line $\ell(Z_P)$, where $\ell(Z) = \{Q : Q \wedge E_1(X, Z) = E_0^1(\tau, Z)\}$ is a family of lines parametrized by $Z \in F(\tau)$.

**Lemma 1.1 (trichotomy) [proved].** View $E_1^0, E_1^1, E_0^1$ in the $F(\tau)$-vector space $F(\tau)[Z]$ and let $K_Z$ be the largest of their $Z$-degrees.

- **(I) concurrent.** $E_1^0, E_1^1$ independent and $E_0^1 = g_\psi E_1^1 + h_\psi E_1^0$ with $g_\psi, h_\psi \in F(\tau)$. Then $\psi = g_\psi + Xh_\psi$ is unique, $E \equiv (Y + \psi)E_Y$ modulo $F(\tau)[Y^2]$, equivalently $E_X = (\psi E_Y)_X + YE_{XY}$, and (S) reads $(P + \psi) \wedge \varepsilon_P = 0$: all lines $\ell(Z)$ pass through $\psi$.
- **(II) parallel.** $E_1^0, E_1^1$ dependent: $E_Y = \rho(X)\,q(\tau, Y^2)$ with $\rho \in F[X]$, and $\varepsilon_P \equiv \rho$ for every $P$ with $\varepsilon_P \ne 0$: all lines have the same direction.
- **(III)** otherwise. Then no point lies on more than $K_Z$ of the lines $\ell(P^2)$, $P$ a section.

*Proof.* (I): $\psi \wedge E_1(X, Z) = g_\psi E_1^1(Z) + h_\psi E_1^0(Z)$ for $\psi = g_\psi + Xh_\psi$, identically in $Z$, and $(g_\psi, h_\psi)$ is unique because $E_1^0, E_1^1$ are independent. (III): if $\psi_0$ lies on $\ell(Z)$ for $K_Z + 1$ values of $Z$, the polynomial $E_0^1(Z) - \psi_0 \wedge E_1(Z)$ of degree at most $K_Z$ vanishes identically, which is case (I) or (II); distinct sections have distinct squares. $\square$

For a quintic modulo squares (all three of $Z$-degree at most 2) case (III) is $\det\mathbf{E} \ne 0$, case (I) is (D2) of note 51, case (II) is the dependent sub-case. Every cubic with $\delta = (A_1A_3)' \ne 0$ is in case (I) with $\psi = U/\delta$, the section $Q$ of note 43. So the lead's reading of "degenerate" is right. The referee's example $E = XY^6 + Y$ (note 52) has $E_1 = 1$, $E_0^1 = Z^3$: case (II). A centre requires $\deg_ZE_0^1 \le \deg_ZE_1$.

## 2. The pencil, the intrinsic identity, and the divisor

### 2.1 Consequence 1

**Lemma 2.1 [proved].** In case (I): $P \in F(X)$ is a section iff $(P + \psi)\,\varepsilon_P$ is a square in $F(X)$. $\psi$ is a section, and so is every $P$ with $\varepsilon_P = 0$; if $G$ is absolutely irreducible there is no such $P$. For a section $P \ne \psi$: $P + \psi = \varepsilon_P\theta_P^2$ with $\theta_P \in F(X)^\ast$, so $\mathrm{dlog}(P + \psi) = \mathrm{dlog}\,\varepsilon_P$ and $\mathrm{Odd}(P + \psi) = \mathrm{Odd}(\varepsilon_P)$. For sections $P_1, P_2$ with $(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)' \ne 0$ and three sections:
$$\psi = \frac{(P_1\varepsilon_1)'\varepsilon_2 + (P_2\varepsilon_2)'\varepsilon_1}{(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)'} = P_1 + \varepsilon_1\frac{((P_1 + P_2)\varepsilon_2)'}{(\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)'}, \qquad \sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}(P_1\varepsilon_1)'(\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3)' = 0 .$$

*Proof.* The first sentence is (S). If $\varepsilon_P = 0$ then $E_{XY}(X, P) = (\varepsilon_P)' = 0$, so $E_X(X, P) = 0$ and $Y - P$ divides $G$ over $F(X)$. The formulas are Cramer's rule for the two lines $\{Q : Q \wedge \varepsilon_i = P_i \wedge \varepsilon_i\}$ and the vanishing of the $3 \times 3$ determinant. $\square$

For any two pairs $(P_i, \varepsilon_i)$ with non-parallel directions the formula defines a point, so pairs carry no information (Proposition 4.4 of note 51); with a fixed potential, five sections determine nothing either: for any $\psi$ and any five polynomials there is a quintic $E_Y = e_1 + e_3Y^2 + e_5Y^4$ with centre $\psi$ of which they are sections (five $F(\tau)$-linear equations on six unknowns; this is how `53_planted.py` builds its tests). What is not free is the weighted degree and the contact.

### 2.2 The intrinsic identity

Let $E_Y = \sum_{i \le K}e_{2i+1}Y^{2i}$ and let $\mu_{ij} \in F[X]$ be defined by $\mu_{ij}^2 = (e_{2i+1}e_{2j+1})'$ (a derivative is a square). In terms of note 45, $\mu_{ij} = \mathcal{A}_i\mathcal{B}_j + \mathcal{A}_j\mathcal{B}_i$, the minors of the first two rows of $\mathbf{E}$. Put $g_\mu = \gcd_{i<j}\mu_{ij}$.

**Proposition 2.2 [proved; checked on random non-sections, `53_planted.py`, `53_families.py`].** For every potential and all $P, Q \in F(X)$:
$$(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_Q)' = \sum_{i<j}\mu_{ij}^2\,(P^{2i}Q^{2j} + P^{2j}Q^{2i}) = \big((P + Q)\,\mathsf{B}(P, Q)\big)^2, \qquad \mathsf{B} = \mu_{01} + \mu_{02}(P + Q) + \mu_{12}PQ \ \text{ for a quintic.}$$
Hence, writing $\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_Q = \Lambda_Oh^2$ with $O = \mathrm{Odd}(\varepsilon_P) \triangle \mathrm{Odd}(\varepsilon_Q)$: $\Lambda_O'\,h^2 = ((P + Q)\mathsf{B}(P, Q))^2$, and $g_\mu^2(P + Q)^2$ divides $\Lambda_O'h^2$.

*Proof.* $P^{2i}Q^{2j} \in F(\tau)$, so $(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_Q)' = \sum_{i,j}(e_{2i+1}e_{2j+1})'P^{2i}Q^{2j}$; the diagonal terms vanish and $x^{2i}y^{2j} + x^{2j}y^{2i}$ is the square of a multiple of $x + y$. $\square$

Note 51, Section 6 mentions this identity (in the variable $\sqrt X$) as giving "only $M_0 \mid \mathsf{D}(\lambda_1\lambda_2)$". Its content for the lead's idea is the following correction of Consequence 2: **the logarithmic derivatives $\mathrm{dlog}(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_Q)$ vanish on $2\,\mathrm{div}(g_\mu)$ (where $g_\mu$ is prime to $\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_Q$) for all polynomials $P, Q$, sections or not, for every potential, degenerate or not.** The divisor is intrinsic; a centre and a list member are needed only to prove that it is large (Section 2.4). For a cubic $\mathsf{B} = \sqrt{\delta}$ and the identity is $(M_PM_Q)' = \delta(P + Q)^2$ of note 43.

### 2.3 Consequence 2, exact

In case (I) write $\psi = N/M$ in lowest terms, $M = b_0m^2$ with $b_0$ squarefree, $p_\beta = \mathrm{ord}_\beta M$, $p_\infty = \deg N - \deg M$.

**Proposition 2.3 [proved; checked with equality in every instance, `53_planted.py`].** Let $P \ne P'$ be polynomial sections, both different from $\psi$.

1. At every finite $\beta$: $\mathrm{ord}_\beta\,\mathrm{dlog}(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_{P'}) \ge 2\lfloor (p_\beta + \mathrm{ord}_\beta(P + P'))/2 \rfloor$. So the order is at least $2\,\mathrm{ord}_\beta m$ at a root of $m$, and at least $2\,\mathrm{ord}_\beta(b_0m)$ at a root of $b_0$ where $P(\beta) = P'(\beta)$.
2. If $p_\infty > \max(\deg P, \deg P')$: $\deg\mathrm{dlog}(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_{P'}) \le -2\lfloor (p_\infty - \deg(P + P'))/2 \rfloor - 2$.
3. $\mathrm{dlog}\,\varepsilon_P \equiv N'/N + b_0'/b_0$ modulo $m^2$ at the roots of $m$ that are not roots of $b_0$.

*Proof.* $u = (P + \psi)/(P' + \psi) = 1 + (P + P')/(P' + \psi)$ satisfies $u \equiv \varepsilon_P\varepsilon_{P'}$, so $\mathrm{dlog}(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_{P'}) = u'/u$ (dlog is injective on $F(X)^\ast/F(X)^{\ast 2}$). $P' + \psi$ has a pole of order exactly $p_\beta$, so $\mathrm{ord}_\beta(u - 1) = p_\beta + \mathrm{ord}_\beta(P + P') =: k \ge 1$, and in characteristic 2 a function of order $k$ has a derivative of order at least $2\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$. At infinity the same with degrees: $\deg f = -k$ gives $\deg f' \le -2\lfloor k/2 \rfloor - 2$. (3): $Q_P := PM + N = M\varepsilon_P\theta_P^2$, so $\mathrm{dlog}\,Q_P = \mathrm{dlog}\,\varepsilon_P + \mathrm{dlog}\,b_0$, and $Q_P \equiv N$, $Q_P' = m^2(Pb_0)' + N' \equiv N'$ modulo $m^2$. $\square$

The lead's residue $N'/N$ is wrong by $b_0'/b_0$ **[checked: with $b_0 \ne 1$ the residue $N'/N$ fails at every (section, root) pair tested, and $N'/N + b_0'/b_0$ holds]**. By Proposition 2.2 the bound at the roots of $m$ is not special to sections; the gain at a root of $b_0$ and the exponent $\mathrm{ord}_\beta(P + P')$ are the factor $(P + Q)^2$ of the identity.

### 2.4 What polynomiality and one list member force

**Proposition 2.4 [proved; the degree drops checked on the two families, `53_families.py`].** In case (I) with $E \in F[X, Y]$ of weighted degree $W$:

1. $b_0$ divides every $e_{2i+1}$; with $e_{2i+1} = b_0\tilde e_{2i+1}$: $m^2 \mid (N\tilde e_{2i+1})'$; and $b_0m$ divides every $\mu_{ij}$. So $b_0m \mid g_\mu$, $\deg b_0 \le W - (2K + 1)\Delta$, and every $\varepsilon_P$ is divisible by $b_0$.
2. $\deg(e_{2i+1}e_{2j+1})' \le (W - (2i + 1)\Delta) + (W - (2j + 1)\Delta) - (p_\infty - \Delta)$: the minors drop by at least $(p_\infty - \Delta)/2$ below their generic degree.

*Proof.* The coefficient of $Y^{2i}$ in $E_X = (\psi E_Y)_X + YE_{XY}$ is $c_i := (\psi e_{2i+1})'$, a polynomial of degree at most $W - 2i\Delta$. (1) $(\psi e)' = (Ne/b_0)'/m^2$ and $b_0^2(Ne/b_0)' = (Ne)'b_0 + Neb_0'$, so $b_0 \mid Neb_0'$, hence $b_0 \mid e$; then $(\psi e)' = (N\tilde e)'/m^2$. $N^2(\tilde e_i\tilde e_j)' = (N\tilde e_i)'N\tilde e_j + N\tilde e_i(N\tilde e_j)' \equiv 0$ modulo $m^2$ and $(e_ie_j)' = b_0^2(\tilde e_i\tilde e_j)'$. (2) $\psi^2(e_ie_j)' = c_i\psi e_j + c_j\psi e_i$. $\square$

On the 11/16 family ($\psi = X^{3n/4 - 1}$, $p_\infty - \Delta = n/4$) the three drops are $n/4, n/4, n/2$ at $n = 16, 32, 64$; on the 27/40 family ($p_\infty - \Delta = 3, 13$) the one nonzero minor drops by $4, 14$ **[checked]**. On the 27/40 family $g_\mu = X^{2j}(X^n + X)$: the whole domain is in the intrinsic divisor.

**Proposition 2.5 (contact 4) [proved; checked on both families].** If $E_Y \in \mathfrak{m}_\alpha^3$ at $(\alpha, y_\alpha)$ (contact at least 4, quintic): $E_Y(\alpha, Y) = e_5(\alpha)(Y - y_\alpha)^4$, so $e_3(\alpha) = 0$ and $e_1(\alpha) = e_5(\alpha)y_\alpha^4$; and $\mu_{02}(\alpha) = \mu_{12}(\alpha)y_\alpha$, $\mu_{01}(\alpha) = \mu_{12}(\alpha)y_\alpha^2$. With contact 4 on all lines: $\Lambda_D \mid e_3$, the word is the rational function $\mu_{02}/\mu_{12}$ wherever $\mu_{12} \ne 0$, $\mathsf{B}(P, Q) \equiv \mu_{12}(P + \hat y)(Q + \hat y)$ modulo $\Lambda_D$, and a root of $\varepsilon_P$ in $D \setminus S_P$ is a root of $e_5$.

*Proof.* $E_Y(\alpha, Y)$ is a polynomial in $Y^2$ of degree at most 4 in $Y$ vanishing to order 3 at $y_\alpha$, so its root $y_\alpha^2$ in $Y^2$ is double. For constants $y_1 = y_\alpha$ and $y_2$: $\varepsilon_{y_1}$ vanishes to order 3 at $\alpha$, so by Proposition 2.2 $\mathsf{B}(y_\alpha, y_2)(\alpha) = 0$ for every $y_2$. $\square$

**The degree of the divisor [proved].** Let $P$ be a polynomial section of degree at most $\Delta$ in case (I), and $O_P = \mathrm{Odd}(\varepsilon_P)$. Since $Q_P = M\varepsilon_P\theta_P^2$ is a polynomial prime to $M$: $\mathrm{Roots}(b_0) \subseteq O_P$, $\mathrm{Odd}(Q_P) = O_P \setminus \mathrm{Roots}(b_0)$, and $|O_P| - \deg b_0 \le \deg Q_P \le \deg M + \max(\Delta, p_\infty)$, that is
$$2\deg(b_0m) + (p_\infty - \Delta)_+ \ \ge\ |O_P| - \Delta. \tag{2.1}$$
If $G$ has contact $2j_\alpha \ge 2$ on the lines of $S_P^c \subseteq S_P$ then $\varepsilon_P = r_P\prod_{S_P^c}(X - \alpha)^{2j_\alpha - 1}$ with odd exponents, $O_P = S_P^c \triangle \mathrm{Odd}(r_P)$, and $|O_P| \ge |S_P^c| - s_P$, $s_P := W - \Delta - \sum_{S_P^c}(2j_\alpha - 1) \ge \deg r_P$. Define $\mathfrak{M} = 2\,\mathrm{div}(b_0m) + e_\infty(\infty)$, $e_\infty = 2\lfloor (p_\infty - \Delta)/2 \rfloor + 2$ if $p_\infty > \Delta$ and $0$ otherwise. Then $\deg\mathfrak{M} \ge |S_P^c| - s_P - \Delta + [p_\infty > \Delta]$. At rate 1/2, $a = 0.7065$, contact 4 on all lines and $W < 2.6352\,n$ (exact integers, `53_window.py`, $n = 2^{25}$ and $2^{28}$): $s \le 0.0157\,n$, $\deg\mathfrak{M} \ge 0.2065\,n$ when the $r_P$ are constants and $\ge 0.1908\,n$ in general; without the roots of $b_0$ the bound loses $2\deg b_0$, which can be $0.27\,n$. (Note 51 uses the part $M_0^\star$ of the denominator prime to the numerator; $2\mu^\star \le 2\deg m$.)

### 2.5 The parallel case

**Proposition 2.7 [proved].** In case (II), let $D_c$ be the set of contained lines and $x_0n = n - |D_c|$. For every polynomial $P$ of degree at most $\Delta$ with $\varepsilon_P \ne 0$: $(S_P \cap D_c) \triangle \mathrm{Odd}(r_P) = \mathrm{Odd}(\rho)$, the same set for all $P$. Hence two such polynomials satisfy $|S_P \triangle S_{P'}| \le s_P + s_{P'} + x_0n$, and among the polynomials with at least $A$ agreements, $s_P \le s$ and $2(A - \Delta) > 2s + x_0n$ there is at most one, besides the at most $\deg_Zq$ polynomials with $q(\tau, P^2) = 0$.

*Proof.* $\varepsilon_P = \rho\,q(\tau, P^2) \equiv \rho$, and two distinct polynomials of degree at most $\Delta$ with at least $A$ agreements each have $|S \triangle S'| \ge 2(A - \Delta)$. $\square$

In the window ($s \le 0.166\,n$ when contact 4 holds on at least $0.925\,n$ lines and contact 2 elsewhere, $A - \Delta = 0.2065\,n$) this closes the dependent sub-case for mixed contact, which note 48 listed as "not on proved ground"; note 51, Lemma 3.1 closes it by a degree bound instead. It is the pencil argument with the centre at infinity of the plane: all odd sets are equal, the code is trivial.

## 3. Consequence 3: the code

For a finite support $\Sigma \subset \overline F$, a monic $g$ and $e \ge 0$ let $\Gamma(\Sigma; g, e)$ be the set of $T \subseteq \Sigma$ such that $\mathrm{dlog}\Lambda_T$ has order at least $2\,\mathrm{ord}_\beta g$ at every root $\beta$ of $g$ (so $T$ avoids them) and degree at most $-e$ (that is, $p_k(T) = 0$ for $0 \le k \le e - 2$). It is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear code, the binary Goppa code with Goppa polynomial $g^2$ and $e - 1$ power-sum conditions; a nonzero word has weight at least $2\deg g + e$. For a subspace $D$, $1_T(X) = (L_D/e_0)\,\mathrm{dlog}\Lambda_T$ is the polynomial of degree below $n$ that is the indicator of $T$ on $D$, and for $g$ prime to $L_D$: $T \in \Gamma(D; g, e)$ iff $g^2 \mid 1_T$ and $\deg 1_T \le n - e$. $\Gamma_\infty(g) = \Gamma(D; 1, g + 2)$.

### 3.1 The clean case

**Theorem 3.1 [proved].** In case (I) let $\mathcal{L}$ be a set of polynomial sections of degree at most $\Delta$, different from $\psi$, with $\mathrm{Odd}(\varepsilon_P) = S_P$ and $|S_P| \ge A > \Delta$ for $P \in \mathcal{L}$ (true if every line is contained and every $r_P$ is a constant times a square). Then: (a) $\mathrm{Roots}(b_0) \subseteq S_P$ for all $P \in \mathcal{L}$: the simple poles of the centre are common agreement points; (b) $S_P \triangle S_{P'} \in \Gamma(D; b_0m, e_\infty)$ for all $P, P' \in \mathcal{L}$; (c) $P \mapsto S_P$ is injective, so $|\mathcal{L}| \le |\Gamma(D; b_0m, e_\infty)|$, and the differences have weight in $[2(A - \Delta),\ 2(n - A)]$; (d) $2\deg(b_0m) + e_\infty \ge \max_{\mathcal{L}}|S_P| - \Delta + [p_\infty > \Delta]$.

*Proof.* (a) $\mathrm{Roots}(b_0) \subseteq O_P = S_P$. (b) Proposition 2.3 with $\mathrm{dlog}(\varepsilon_P\varepsilon_{P'}) = \mathrm{dlog}\Lambda_{S_P \triangle S_{P'}}$; at a root of $b_0$ both sections take the value $y_\beta$. (c) $|S_P| > \Delta$. (d) is (2.1). $\square$

The code depends on the word only through the divisor of poles of $\psi$, so $\sup$ over the divisors of degree at least $A - \Delta + 1$ of the number of admissible families in one coset is a property of the domain. This is the lead's Consequence 3. The proof uses only the relations $\mathrm{Odd}(P + \psi) = S_P$ for one common $\psi$; the potential is not needed, so the theorem applies to any family of polynomials with a common centre in this sense.

### 3.2 With the cofactors $r_P$

**Theorem 3.2 [proved].** In case (I) let $Z$ be the set of odd roots outside $D$ of the $\varepsilon_P$, $P$ a polynomial section. Then:

1. Every odd root of $\varepsilon_P$ that is not a pole of $\psi$ is a point where $P = \psi$, and a root of $\varepsilon_\psi = E_Y(X, \psi)$; so $Z \subseteq \mathrm{Roots}(\varepsilon_\psi) \cup \mathrm{Poles}(\psi)$, a finite set that does not depend on $P$. On $D \cup Z$ with the word $\psi$ every section is "tight": all its odd-order agreements with $\psi$ are in $D \cup Z$.
2. $O_P \triangle O_{P'} \in \Gamma(D \cup Z;\ m\,b_{P,P'},\ e_\infty)$ where $b_{P,P'}$ is the product over the roots of $b_0$ at which $P = P'$; at most $s_P + s_{P'}$ roots of $b_0$ are missing from it.
3. $|O_P \cap D| \ge |S_P^c| - s_P$, $|O_P \setminus S_P^c| \le s_P$, and $P \mapsto O_P$ is injective on the sections with $|O_P| > \Delta$ (note 51, Proposition 4.2 (ii)).

This is Proposition 4.2 (iii) of note 51 in code form, with the roots of $b_0$ added. So the list members on the component split into classes by $O_P \setminus D$ (at most $s$ points of $Z$ each); inside a class the sets $O_P \cap D$ lie in one coset of a Goppa code on $D$ alone, with a divisor of degree at least $A - \Delta - 3s$ ($0.159\,n$). **I could not bound the number of classes [open, (G1)].** The lead's suggestion "sections in the same coset have the same $r$" is item 4; the converse direction (few cosets) is what is missing. ## 5. Task D: the size of the code

### 5.1 The formal model

**Proposition 5.1 (Consequence 4) [proved].** Let $D$ have a basis $b_1, \dots, b_N$ algebraically independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and $w(g) = \max\{\mathrm{wt}_2(k) : k \le g\}$. Then $\Gamma_\infty(g) = \mathrm{RM}(N - 1 - w(g), N)$: $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ for $n/8 - 1 \le g < n/4 - 1$, $\mathrm{RM}(1, N)$ for $n/4 - 1 \le g < n/2 - 1$, $\{\emptyset, D\}$ for $n/2 - 1 \le g < n - 1$.

*Proof.* For $k = \sum_{j \in J}2^j$, $\alpha^k = \prod_{j \in J}(\sum_ia_ib_i^{2^j}) = \sum_Ic_I^{(k)}a_I$ with $c_I^{(k)} = \sum_{\phi : J \twoheadrightarrow I}\prod_jb_{\phi(j)}^{2^j}$, nonzero exactly for $1 \le |I| \le |J|$. The $c_I^{(k)}$, for all $I$ and $k$, are sums over disjoint sets of monomials in the $b_i$, hence linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$. So the dual of $\Gamma_\infty(g)$, spanned by the coordinate functions of $a \mapsto \alpha^k$, $k \le g$, is the span of all monomials $a_I$ with $|I| \le w(g)$. $\square$

### 5.5 Finite places on a generic subspace

**Proposition 5.3 [proved].** Let $D$ be generic (formal model). (a) If $\beta \in D$, or $\beta$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_2$, or $\beta$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_2(b_1, \dots, b_N)$, the elements $1/(\beta - \alpha)$, $\alpha \in D \setminus \{\beta\}$, are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$, so $\Gamma(D; X - \beta, 0) = \{\emptyset\}$. (b) (Lifted divisors.) For a subspace $U \subseteq D$, $\bar D = L_U(D)$ and $g = \bar g(L_U(X))$: $T = L_U^{-1}(\bar T)$ lies in $\Gamma(D; g, \cdot)$ when $\bar T \in \Gamma(\bar D; \bar g, \cdot)$, because $\mathrm{dlog}\Lambda_T = e_0(U)\,(\mathrm{dlog}\Lambda_{\bar T}) \circ L_U$; the degree of the divisor relative to the size of the domain is the same, and $\bar D$ is again generic. (c) For every $T$ of even size, $T \in \Gamma(D; m_T, 0)$ with $m_T^2 = \Lambda_T'$ of degree up to $|T| - 2$.

*Proof.* (a) For $\beta \in D$ translate to $\beta = 0$: $\sum_{a \in T}1/(a \cdot b)$ is a nonzero rational function of $b$ when $T \ne \emptyset$, because the linear forms $a \cdot b$, $a \in \mathbb{F}_2^N \setminus 0$, are pairwise non-proportional and each term has its own polar hyperplane; the same with the affine forms $\beta + a \cdot b$ for $\beta$ algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_2$, and in the variable $\beta$ for $\beta$ transcendental. (b), (c): computation. $\square$

Consequences. In the formal model, in the clean case, a simple pole of the centre in $D$ ($b_0$ with a root in $D$) leaves at most one section, by (a). By (c) one-dimensional codes with divisors of any degree exist for trivial reasons, so the question is the dimension: whether $\Lambda_T'$ and $\Lambda_{T'}'$ can share a factor of degree $0.1\,n$ for many $T$. By (b) lifted divisors give nothing new (the same question on a smaller generic subspace). **Whether $|\Gamma(D; m, e)| \le 2^{\mathrm{poly}(N)}$ for every divisor of degree at least $0.19\,n$ on a generic subspace is [open, (G2)]**; I have no example of a code of dimension 2 with a finite, non-lifted divisor of that degree, and no proof. If the part at infinity alone has $2\lfloor (p_\infty - \Delta)/2 \rfloor \ge n/8 - 1$, the finite part can only shrink the code and Section 6 applies whatever $m$ is.

## 6. The refined count: weight gap and second moment

**Lemma 6.1 (Johnson step; note 51, Proposition 4.3, and the lead) [quoted, proof checked].** If $O_1, \dots, O_M \subseteq D$ have $|O_i| \ge A' > n/2$ and pairwise distances at least $w_1$ with $w_1n > 2A'(n - A')$, then $M \le w_1n/(w_1n - 2A'(n - A'))$. Sizes larger than $A'$ only help.

**Theorem 6.2 [proved; only in the formal model].** Let $D$ be a subspace with algebraically independent basis, $n = 2^N$, $25 \le N \le 28$, $\Delta = n/2 + 5$, $A = \lceil 0.7065\,n \rceil$, and let $G^\star = \partial E^\star$ be in case (I) with contact 4 on all lines and $W < 2.6352\,n$ (the hard core of class $\mathcal{D}$ of note 51). Assume (H1) $\deg N - \deg M \ge \Delta + n/8$ for the centre $\psi = N/M$ (true if $\psi$ is a polynomial), and (H2) the sets $O_P \setminus D$ are the same for all $P \in L_{G^\star}$ (true if every odd root of every $r_P$ lies in $D$). Then $|L_{G^\star}| \le 42$, and $|L_{G^\star}| \le 19$ if the $r_P$ are constants. If $\deg N - \deg M \ge \Delta + n/4$ the bounds are 6 and 5.

*Proof.* In the hard core every list member is a section (Lemma 0, $W < 4A$) and $s_P \le W - \Delta - 3A < 0.0158\,n$. Put $O_P^{\mathrm{in}} = O_P \cap D$. By (H2) and Theorem 3.2, $O_P^{\mathrm{in}} \triangle O_{P'}^{\mathrm{in}} = O_P \triangle O_{P'}$ has vanishing power sums up to $g = 2\lfloor (p_\infty - \Delta)/2 \rfloor \ge n/8 - 1$, so it lies in $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ by Proposition 5.1 (finite poles only add conditions). $|O_P^{\mathrm{in}}| \ge A - s$ and $|O_P^{\mathrm{in}} \triangle O_{P'}^{\mathrm{in}}| \ge 2(A - \Delta) - 2s > 0.3816\,n > 3n/8$; the weights of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ are $n/2$ and $n/2 \pm n/2^{h+1}$, so the distance is at least $7n/16$. Lemma 6.1 with $A' = A - s \ge 0.6908\,n$, $w_1 = 7n/16$ gives 42 (exact integers, `53_window.py`, both sizes); with $s = 0$: 19. For $g \ge n/4 - 1$ the differences are affine hyperplanes, $w_1 = n/2$. $P \mapsto O_P^{\mathrm{in}}$ is injective because $|O_P^{\mathrm{in}}| > \Delta$. $\square$

Remarks. (i) This confirms the lead's reading of the generic model with the divisor at infinity, with the factors $r_P$ handled by passing from $S_P$ to $O_P \cap D$, on the whole window (the lead's count $|T| \ge 7n/16 - 2s$ applied to the $S_P$ would stop at $W < \Delta + 3A + 0.0114\,n$); it does not need the roots of $\theta_P$ to lie in $D$, which Theorem A of note 12 does. What it does not cover is (G1): odd roots of $r_P$ outside $D$ that differ between members. (ii) With the thresholds of Theorem S: the clean bound is 24, 21, 20, 19 at $a = 0.70084, 0.703, 0.705, 0.7065$. (iii) The mechanism is narrow: Lemma 6.1 needs no codeword with weight in $[(2a - 1)n,\ 2a(1 - a)n)$, an interval of width $(1 - 2a^2)n = 0.0017\,n$ at $a = 0.7065$.

**Proposition 6.3 (the gap fails for the crude code on small fields) [proved; found at toy size].** Let $D$ be any $N$-dimensional subspace of $\mathrm{GF}(2^m)$, $g/n \in [0.1875, 0.2187)$, and $\binom{N-1}2 - 2m - 4(N - 1) + 10 > N - 6$ (for $N = 25, 26, 27, 28$: $m \le 85, 94, 104, 115$). Then $\Gamma_\infty(g)$ contains a word of weight $n/2 - n/16 - n/2^{h+2}$ with $4 \le h \le (N - 1)/2$, which lies in $[27n/64,\ 7n/16)$.

*Proof.* Exactly two odd $k \le g$ have weight at least $N - 3$ ($2^{N-3} - 1$ and $2^{N-2} - 1 - 2^{N-4}$), and on $\mathrm{RM}(3, N)$ the functionals $p_k$ for these $k$ depend only on the cubic part. So the cubic forms $x_0\bar Q$, $\bar Q$ a quadratic form modulo linear forms in $x_1, \dots, x_{N-1}$, that are cubic parts of words of $\Gamma_\infty(g)$ form a space $\mathcal{X}$ of dimension at least $\binom{N-1}2 - 2m$. Asking that a fixed 4-dimensional subspace lies in the radical of the alternating form of $\bar Q$ costs $4(N - 1) - 10$ conditions; a linear space of alternating forms of rank at most 2 on a space of dimension $N - 5$ has dimension at most $N - 6$; so $\mathcal{X}$ contains $\bar Q$ of rank $2h' \in [4, N - 5]$ with that radical. Take $q_0 = x_1x_2 + x_3x_4$ in radical coordinates. A word of $\Gamma_\infty(g)$ with cubic part $x_0\bar Q$ can be changed by any element of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$, so its two halves $x_0 = 0, 1$ can be made any quadratic functions with quadratic parts $q_0$ and $\bar Q + q_0$, of ranks 4 and $2h' + 4 =: 2h$; choose the affine parts so that the weights are $n/4 - n/16$ and $n/4 - n/2^{h+2}$. $\square$

**[checked, `53_gap.c`]** The space $\mathcal{X}$ has dimension exactly $\binom{N-1}2 - 2m$ in the four toy cases where this is positive ($N = 10$: $m = 12, 16$; $N = 12$: $m = 20, 24$), and the construction (with $q_0$ on four random coordinates) gives words of weight $27n/64$ in more than 3000 of 4000 trials at $N = 10$, and of weight $27n/64$ or $55n/128$ in all but 2 of 4000 trials at $N = 12$. (The counting hypothesis of the proposition fails at these sizes; the construction works because $\mathcal{X} \ne 0$.) A randomized descent in the whole code also finds, at $N = 10$, $m = 12$ and $16$, words of weight $424 = 53n/128$ with a nonzero part of degree at least 3: this is inside the narrow interval of remark (iii) ($[424, 424.2]$ at $n = 1024$). At $N = 12$ in $\mathrm{GF}(2^{32})$ and $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$, where $\mathcal{X} = 0$, 100 descents found no word below $7n/16 = 1792$ (smallest 1816 and 1824); this proves nothing. So the answer to the lead's question is: **at $N = 25$ to $28$ over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$ the code at infinity has words in the gap of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ [proved]**, the ones exhibited have weight at least $27n/64$, for which Lemma 6.1 would still give 58, and whether the code has words in the narrow interval $[0.4130, 0.4147)\,n$ is **[open; yes at toy size with small fields]**. The explicit words are sums over two half-spaces; sums of two of them ($x_0\bar Q_0 + x_1\bar Q_1$) have weights $n/2 - (n/8)\sum_{i \le 4}2^{-h_i}$, which reaches $53n/128$ for $(h_i) = (1, 3, 5, 5)$, so I expect the narrow interval to be populated at real sizes **[conjectured]**.

## 7. The lead's questions

**(i) Divisor at infinity, generic subspace, with $r_P$.** If the odd roots $R$ of $r_Pr_{P'}$ lie in $D$: yes, and more: Theorem 6.2 bounds the component by 42 on the whole window. If $R \not\subseteq D$: $T' = (S \triangle S') \triangle (R \cap D)$ has the power sums of the set $R \setminus D$, it is not a codeword and no weight gap applies to it; by Section 3.2 it is a Boolean function of degree at least 5, and one phantom point is impossible. Nothing replaces the weight gap at present: **[open, (G1)]**. In the hard core this is the only thing missing for the generic model with a polynomial centre.

**(ii) Mixed divisors $2(m) + e(\infty)$, $m$ prime to $\Lambda_D$.** Closed, with the same constants, when $e \ge n/8 + 1$ (Theorem 6.2 under (H1)). For a short part at infinity the code at infinity is $\mathrm{RM}(3, N)$ or larger, which has no gap near $0.41\,n$, and the finite part must do the work: **[open, (G2)]**, with Proposition 5.3 as the only results.

**(iii) The real domain over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$.** $\dim\Gamma_\infty(g) \ge 47938$ at $N = 25$; the gap of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ is populated (Proposition 6.3). The code relaxation is too weak there, and the true list is controlled by the even conditions that it drops (for the flat cubic at rate 1/4 the exact count on the real shape is the $n - 1$ flats for $N \le 14$, note 43 **[quoted]**, while the stratum bound for its code $\Gamma_\infty(n/4)$ at $N = 25$ is $2^{1154}$). **[open, (G3)]**

**Other rates (clean, divisor at infinity, formal model) [proved for the code; exact numbers `53_window.py`].** At the thresholds of Theorem S, $g = A - \Delta - 1$ is $0.2381\,n$, $0.2151\,n$, $0.1750\,n$ at rates 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, all in $[n/8 - 1, n/4 - 1)$: the code is $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ and $|L| \le 2^{1 + N + N(N-1)/2}$ ($2^{326}$ to $2^{407}$), or $\mathrm{RM}(1, N)$ and $|L| \le 2n$ if the pole at infinity has order at least $\Delta + n/4$ (the flat cubic: $n - 1$ flats, sharp up to the factor 2). The Johnson step for sets of size $A < n/2$ and distance at least $w_1$ needs $w_1/2 > A(n - A)/n$: at rate 1/16 the next weight of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ above $2(A - \Delta)$ is $3n/8$ and the bound is 29; at rates 1/4 and 1/8 it is void (the weights of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)$ accumulate at $n/2$), as it must be at rate 1/4 where the flats are $n - 1$ members.

**Attacks.** The coset condition alone never gives a list (Section 3.3). An attack through this structure needs sets whose multisets $O_i + 2\,\mathrm{Roots}(h_i)$ share all elementary symmetric functions up to $p - \Delta - 1$; the extra codewords that a small field creates satisfy the odd half of these conditions by pigeonhole and the even half only by accident ($m\,(p - \Delta)/2$ more bits per member). I did not find a family that grows with the field deficiency, and I did not run a search for one beyond the exhaustive coset enumerations of `53_tight.py` at $n = 32, 64$ (which find only the known lists).

## 8. The statement as it stands, and the gap

**Theorem 8.1 [proved, given Theorem S of note 52 (refereed) and Theorem 2.1 of note 51 (its covering computation and Lemma B of note 40, Theorem 4 of note 45, Corollary F of note 48 are not refereed)].** Let $n = 2^N$, $25 \le N \le 28$, $\Delta = n/2 + 5$, $A = \lceil 0.7065\,n \rceil$, $F$ a perfect field of characteristic 2, $D \subset F$ any $n$ points, $y$ any word.

1. (Every evaluation set.) $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le 19578$ unless one absolutely irreducible $G^\star = \partial E^\star$ of class $\mathcal{D}$ exists, and then $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le 576 + |L_{G^\star}|$ (note 51). $E^\star$ is in case (I): it has a centre $\psi = N/M$, $M = b_0m^2$; every $P \in L_{G^\star}$ that is a section satisfies $P + \psi \equiv \varepsilon_P$; the sets $O_P = \mathrm{Odd}(\varepsilon_P)$ lie in $D \cup Z$, $Z \subseteq \mathrm{Roots}(E_Y^\star(X, \psi)) \cup \mathrm{Poles}(\psi)$, they are pairwise distinct, contain $\mathrm{Roots}(b_0)$, and $O_P \triangle O_{P'} \in \Gamma(D \cup Z; m\,b_{P,P'}, e_\infty)$, a binary Goppa code whose divisor has degree at least $|S_P^c| - s_P - \Delta - 2(s_P + s_{P'}) + [p_\infty > \Delta]$ (at least $0.128\,n$ in the hard core, $0.2065\,n$ if the $r_P$ are constants). The parallel case (II) carries at most one member.
2. (Formal model.) If $D$ is a subspace with algebraically independent basis, $G^\star$ is in the hard core (contact 4 on all lines, $W < 2.6352\,n$), and (H1), (H2) of Theorem 6.2 hold, then $|L_{G^\star}| \le 42$ and $|\mathrm{List}(y, A)| \le 576 + 42 = 618$.

Item 2 does not hold on the real domain by this proof, and its hypotheses (H1), (H2) are not known to hold for any word.

**What remains, exactly.**

- **(G1) Phantom roots.** In case (I) with the divisor at infinity on a generic subspace: bound the number of classes $O_P \setminus D$, or prove that for $T' \subseteq D$ and $R \subset \overline F \setminus D$ with $1 \le |R| \le 0.032\,n$ the equalities $p_k(T') = p_k(R)$, $k \le 0.19\,n$, are impossible. Equivalent form: no $T' \subseteq D$ and squarefree $\rho$ prime to $L_D$ of degree $t \le 0.032\,n$ with $\deg(1_{T'}\rho + L_D\rho'/e_0) \le n + t - g - 2$. Known: $t \ne 1$; $T'$ has Boolean degree $r \ge 5$ with $2^{N - r} = 1 + k_0(R)$.
- **(G2) Short pole at infinity.** $p_\infty < \Delta + n/8$: then $2\deg(b_0m) \ge 0.065\,n$ at least, and the question is the size (or the weights in $[0.38, 0.43]\,n$) of $\Gamma(D; m, e)$ on a generic subspace for a finite divisor. Known: Proposition 5.3. By Proposition 2.4 such a potential has all three minors $\mu_{ij}$ divisible by $b_0m$, a strong condition on $E^\star$ that I could not turn into a contradiction (it gives only $W \ge 4\Delta + \deg(b_0m)$).
- **(G3) The real field.** Word-independent property that would suffice for the hard core with (H1), (H2): *for the real $D$ and every $g \ge 0.19\,n$, every family of subsets of $D$ of size at least $0.6908\,n$, pairwise at distance at least $0.3816\,n$, with all differences in $\Gamma_\infty(g)$, has at most $B$ members.* For a generic subspace $B = 42$. For the real domain the code has dimension at least 47938 and words in the gap, so this is a list-decoding question for an explicit binary code at radius $0.31\,n$, just beyond its Johnson radius. A sufficient condition would be that $\Gamma_\infty(g)$ has no word of weight in $[0.3816\,n,\ 0.4272\,n]$ ($[0.4130\,n, 0.4147\,n)$ if the $r_P$ are constants); Proposition 6.3 shows that the larger gap $(3n/8, 7n/16)$ is not empty, and its words with $h = 4$ (weight $27n/64 = 0.4219\,n$, which the proof does not guarantee at $N = 25$ but which occur at toy size) violate the first condition. How it could be examined: the part of the code of Boolean degree 3 is the kernel of two explicit $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$-linear functionals on the 2300 cubic monomials (Moore determinants $\det[x^{i2^j}]$ of size 22, computable), a space of dimension at least 2172, and its words of low "partition rank" are the ones with weights away from $n/2$; degrees 4 to 6 are similar with 44, 462, 3087 functionals (the numbers $c_g(d)$ of the stratum count at $N = 25$). I did not do this. A better route is to keep the even conditions (Theorem W of note 02 on a generic subspace; nothing known on the real one).
- **(G4) Other profiles of class $\mathcal{D}$.** Contact 4 on at least $0.9102\,n$ lines only: $s_P$ and $x_0$ enter the inequalities of Theorem 6.2 ($2(A - \Delta) - 2s - x_0n > 3n/8$ and the Johnson step with $A - s - x_0n$); with the outer bounds of (D3) these fail, and a cell-by-cell evaluation is needed. Not done.
- Not used and possibly useful: the triple relation, and the fact that $\varepsilon_P$ is a quadratic polynomial in $P^2$ with fixed coefficients satisfying Proposition 2.5 (all arguments above use pairs only, except through the common centre).

**Tests a proof must pass**, in addition to those of notes 48 and 51: it must fail at rate 1/4 on field domains (Gold) and give at least $n - 1$ on subspaces (flats); a bound through $|\Gamma|$ must be at least $2^{n^{0.7}}$ on field domains and at least $2^{47938}$ on 25-dimensional subspaces of $\mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$; a bound through the weight gap must use more than membership in $\Gamma_\infty(g)$ on small fields.
