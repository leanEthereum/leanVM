### 1.1 Lemma 2.1 (splitting lemma with one functional per leaf): confirmed

Re-derived line by line. (a) $S(Y,D)$ is cut out of $N_j(Y)^2$ (dimension $m = 2\,\mathrm{null}_j(Y)$) by $|D|$ linear conditions, so $m - |D| \le \dim S \le m$. (b) Two siblings in $D$ with the same $\lambda$: the difference of the two conditions is $(t_{p,0} - t_{p,1})\lambda^T\beta_p \equiv 0$ on $S$, so the label is not tight. (c) On "tight and $\dim S = \delta$" each $S_x$ is a proper subspace, so $|S^\circ| \ge q^\delta - d q^{\delta - 1}$. The sets $W_\sigma$ depend only on $T_j$ and on the label; they are pairwise disjoint because a member of $W_\sigma$ determines every $\sigma_x$ as the slope $\lambda_x^T\alpha_p/\lambda_x^T\beta_p$ (this is the only place where "one functional per element of $D$" is used, and it is used correctly); $S^\circ \subseteq W_{(t_x)}$. So $|\Sigma_\delta| \le q^{m - \delta}/(1 - d/q)$, the $\Sigma_\delta$ are nested, and the event "tight and $\dim S \ge \delta$" is contained in "$(t_x)_{x \in D} \in \Sigma_\delta$". The law of $(t_x)_{x \in D}$ given $T_j$: a node with one child in $D$ contributes a uniform element (the marginal of a uniform ordered distinct pair), a node with two children in $D$ contributes a uniform ordered distinct pair, nodes are independent and independent of $T_j$; so each value has probability at most $q^{-d}(1 - 1/q)^{-d_2}$, $d_2 \le d/2$. Finally $(1 - d/q)(1 - 1/q)^{d/2} \ge (1 - d/q)(1 - d/(2q)) \ge 1 - 2d/q$. The constant is as stated.

Three remarks, none of them an error. (1) The lemma is a statement about one fixed label; the tight elements of $D$ may sit below nodes of $Y$ and the twiddle of a rounded sibling (not in $D$) never enters $S(Y,D)$, so no hidden dependence. (2) The independence used is exactly: the pairs $(t_{p,0}, t_{p,1})$, $p \in P_j$, are mutually independent and independent of $T_j$. Nothing is assumed across cousins, and twiddles of different nodes may collide (see 1.6 for $J = 0$). (3) For T1 (siblings $t, t+1$) a node with two children in $D$ has only $q$ values, not $q(q-1)$: the proof correctly fails there.

### 1.6 The four points singled out by the brief

### 2.3 Theorem 4.4 (one-level identity with at most four large leaves): confirmed, with the four-arm step re-proved here without the citation

The reduction to at most four leaves is as in Proposition 4.1 and is correct: $Y^K$ is defined over $K_1$ with $Y^K \otimes F^2 \subseteq R$, so the derivative argument gives $q_x \in E_x$; where $E_x$ is spanned by rational vectors these lie in $\mathcal Q^K_x \subseteq Y^K$ and $q_x \in (Q_x \cap Y^K) \otimes F = 0$. What the proof then needs is the following, which the note takes from Dlab and Ringel.

**Lemma (referee; four leaves, elementary).** Let $\bar U_1, \dots, \bar U_4$ be subspaces of a $K_1$-space $\bar V$ and $t_1, \dots, t_4$ independent transcendentals. There are $K_1$-subspaces $\bar\Lambda'_i \subseteq \bar U_i$ with $\bar\Lambda'_i \otimes F^2 \subseteq \bar R = \sum_i \bar U_i \otimes z_i$ such that every relation $\sum_i u_i \otimes z_i = 0$, $u_i \in \bar U_i \otimes F$, has $u_i \in \bar\Lambda'_i \otimes F$.

*Proof.* For $g \in \mathrm{GL}_2(F)$, $(1 \otimes g)\bar R_t = \sum_i \bar U_i \otimes g z_i$, relations correspond with components rescaled, and $u \otimes F^2 \subseteq \bar R$ is invariant (3.3 (c) of the note).
