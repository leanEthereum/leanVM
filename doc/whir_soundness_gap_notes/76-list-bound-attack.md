# 76. Attack on the list bound (S8) for the generic tree code at rate 1/2

**(S8) is not proved, and no list bound below the Johnson radius is proved here for general $n$.** **Proved:** (a) at rate 1/2 the dual of a tree code is the sibling-exchanged tree code with column multipliers (Lemma 1.1), so (S8) says that no point $s \in K^k$ lies with all coefficients nonzero in more than $\Lambda_8$ of the spans of at most $3k/4$ evaluation vectors $g_x$ (Lemma 1.2); the largest list $\lambda_{5/8}(n)$ at agreement $5n/8$ is non-decreasing in $n$ (Lemma 2.1), so (S8) is exactly $\sup_n \lambda_{5/8}(n) \le \Lambda_8$. (b) A new rank inequality for every pattern (Theorem 4.1): sending one twiddle of each bottom pair to $0$ and the other to $\infty$ splits the code into two copies of the code one layer up, so the rank is at least the sum of the two upper ranks. Hence, for **every** bijection $\mu$ between the leaves of the two trees, for the sibling matching and for every cut matching, $\mathrm{rank} \ge \sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\mu(x)})$ (Corollaries 4.2, 4.3): a pattern in which no two sibling leaves, or no two matched leaves of some bijection, share two members has independent rows whatever its class sizes (a proved case of the structure statement that allows classes of any size at every leaf); the deficiency of any pattern is at most the least overlap of a matching; and any three codewords around any word satisfy a Hall-type condition. (c) A top-down derivative lemma (Lemma 2.3). **Exact at the stated size** (exhaustive, two draws of the twiddles with identical counters): the generic tree code has at most 4 codewords at agreement 5 when $n = 8$, and at most **5 codewords at agreement $10 = 5n/8$ when $n = 16$** (162 orbits of five-member lists, no list of six). These are list bounds at agreement $0.625 n$, below the Johnson radius $0.707 n$, but for these two lengths only; Conjecture 5.2 of note 74 allows 163 at $n = 16$. The recursion of Theorem 4.1 gives the exact rank of all 1281 deficient three-member orbits of note 69, of the 1740 deficient four-member orbits of note 74 and of 3300 random three-member patterns. **Refuted:** that this recursion is an exact rank formula (it fails for 122 of 3000 random four-member patterns at $(4,8)$ and gives at most 237 against 269 for the 35-member staircase at $(8,16)$); that the splitting bounds, the decomposition by full blocks or a one-functional theorem with twiddle-dependent directions give a list bound (the first lose all but one unit of deficiency against a list, the second leaves sub-problems below their own Johnson radius, the third is false); that weights of three or four members can ever give a bound below Johnson (their true values exceed the bars $2.29k$ and $3.67k$). **Conjectural:** (S8) itself, in the form $\sup_n \lambda_{5/8}(n) < \infty$, with $\lambda_{5/8} = 4, 5$ at $n = 8, 16$, at least $\min(15, n/8 + 1)$ for $n \ge 32$ and nothing known above 15; the three-member rank formula (Conjecture 4.4). The nearest target for a first bound below Johnson is the five-member weight at rate 1/2: $W_5 \le 5.07 k$ would do, and $4.5k - 4 \le W_5 \le 16k/3$ is what is known (Section 5).

## 1. Setup, and the secant form of (S8)

Notation of notes 68, 69, 74: $k = 2^v$, $n = 2k$ (rate 1/2, two trees), variant T2, twiddles algebraically independent over the prime field ("generic"), leaf $x$ has evaluation vector $g_x = \bigotimes_{i<v}(1, x_i(x)) \in K^k$, the code is $C = \{(g_x \cdot m)_x\}$. A block at scale $N$ is one of the $N$ sets of $b = n/N$ leaves under a node; the top code at scale $N$ is the generic tree code $[N, N/2]$.

**Lemma 1.1 (the dual of a rate-1/2 tree code is a tree code; proved, any twiddles with $t(p,0) \ne t(p,1)$; checked exactly over GF($2^{64}$) for $v = 1, \dots, 5$ by `scripts/76_dual.py`: $G^T \Delta\, G^{\rm sw} P = 0$).** Let $C^{\rm sw}$ be the tree code obtained by exchanging the two twiddles of every sibling pair, $P$ the permutation of $K^k$ that complements every index bit, and $\Delta_x = \prod_{i<v} (t_i(p_i(x),0) + t_i(p_i(x),1))^{-1}$. Then $C^\perp = \{(\Delta_x\, g^{\rm sw}_x \cdot z)_x : z \in K^k\}$: the dual is $C^{\rm sw}$ with column multipliers. In particular the supports of dual codewords are the supports of codewords of a generic tree code (the exchanged twiddles are again algebraically independent). *Proof.* One tree is the bijection $T = \mathrm{diag}(T_a, T_b)(X \otimes I)$, $X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t_a \\ 1 & t_b \end{pmatrix}$, with $T_a, T_b$ the subtrees. $X^{-T} = (t_a + t_b)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} t_b & 1 \\ t_a & 1 \end{pmatrix} = (t_a+t_b)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t_b \\ 1 & t_a \end{pmatrix} J$ with $J$ the exchange of the two coordinates. By induction $T^{-T} = \Delta\, T^{\rm sw} P$ with the same $P$ for every tree. $C = \{(T_0 m, T_1 m)\}$ has dual $\{(y_0, y_1) : T_0^T y_0 + T_1^T y_1 = 0\} = \{(T_0^{-T} z, T_1^{-T} z)\}$ (characteristic 2). $\square$ (At rate $2^{-r}$, $r \ge 2$, the dual has dimension $n - k$ and is not of this form.)

**Lemma 1.2 (secant form; proved, for any MDS code with parity-check columns $\gamma_x$).** Let $w$ be a word with syndrome $s = \sum_x w_x \gamma_x$. For $a \ge k$ the codewords at agreement at least $a$ with $w$ are in bijection with the sets $E$ of at most $n - a$ positions such that $s = \sum_{x \in E} e_x \gamma_x$ with all $e_x \ne 0$ (the error supports). By Lemma 1.1, at rate 1/2 the $\gamma_x$ are, up to scalars, the evaluation vectors $g_x$ of a generic tree code. Hence

**(S8) is equivalent to:** for every $s \in K^k$, the number of sets $E$ of at most $3k/4$ leaves with $s \in \mathrm{span}^*(g_x : x \in E)$ (all coefficients nonzero) is at most $\Lambda_8$. Geometrically: the $2k$ points $g_x$ lie on the Segre variety $(\mathbb P^1)^v \subset \mathbb P^{k-1}$, and one counts the secant spaces spanned by $3k/4$ of them through one point $s$. Equivalently, with $\Sigma = s^\perp$ (a hyperplane of the message space of $C^{\rm sw}$) and $I(Z)$ the space of codewords of $C^{\rm sw}$ vanishing on $Z$: the number of minimal $Z$, $|Z| \le 3k/4$, with $I(Z) \subseteq \Sigma$.

## 2. What (S8) is, in one line (proved reductions)

**Lemma 2.1 (monotonicity; proved, from the lifting lemma of note 69).** Let $\lambda_\tau(n)$ be the largest number of codewords of the generic $[n, n/2]$ tree code at agreement at least $\tau n$ with one word ($\tau n$ an integer). Then $\lambda_\tau(n) \le \lambda_\tau(2n)$. *Proof.* Lift the list by one layer with a lower factor $B \in K^2$ without zeros: block agreement $\tau n$ becomes leaf agreement $2 \tau n$, members stay distinct. $\square$ So (S8) is exactly the statement $\sup_n \lambda_{5/8}(n) \le \Lambda_8$, and every exact value $\lambda_{5/8}(n)$ at small $n$ is a lower bound for all larger lengths.

**Lemma 2.2 (decomposition by full blocks; proved, trivial, valid for every MDS code with a block structure).** At scale $N$ (blocks of $b = n/N$ leaves) call a block full for a member if the member agrees with the word on all of it. A member with a set $Q$ of $f$ full blocks is $c_Q + c'$ with $c_Q$ fixed and $c'$ in the subcode vanishing on $Q$, an MDS code of length $n - fb$ and dimension $k - fb$. Hence the members with at least $N/2$ full blocks number at most $\binom{N}{N/2}$, and the members with exactly $f < N/2$ full blocks are, for each of the $\binom{N}{f}$ choices of $Q$, a list of the shortened code $[(N - f)b, (N/2 - f)b]$ at agreement $a - fb$ with no full block. At $N = 8$, $a = 5b$: $f = 3$ gives the 5-tree code $[5b, b]$ at agreement $2b$ (rate $1/5$, agreement $2/5$, Johnson radius $0.447$), $f = 2$ gives $[6b, 2b]$ at $1/2$ (Johnson $0.577$), $f = 1$ gives $[7b, 3b]$ at $4/7$ (Johnson $0.655$), $f = 0$ is the problem itself. Every sub-problem is again below its own Johnson radius: **the decomposition does not reduce (S8) to known bounds; it only isolates the members with no full block at any scale.** (For thresholds $\tau > \phi + \sqrt{(1/2 - \phi)(1 - \phi)}$, $\phi = f/N$, the members with $f$ full blocks are bounded by the Johnson bound of the shortened code: $0.698$ at $\phi = 1/8$, $0.683$ at $1/4$, $0.655$ at $3/8$. This uses nothing about the tree.)

**Lemma 2.3 (top-down derivative lemma; proved).** Write $g_x = (1, t_q) \otimes g'_x$ with $q$ the child of a root containing $x$ and $g'_x$ the row of the subtree. For a relation $\sum_x \nu_x \otimes g_x = 0$ among incidence rows ($\nu_x \in \Lambda_x \otimes F$) and every top block $q$, the block part $\rho_q = \sum_{x \in q} \nu_x \otimes g'_x$ satisfies $K^2 \otimes \rho_q \subseteq R$: every solution $M = (M^0, M^1)$ (split by the top variable) has $\langle M^0, \rho_q \rangle = \langle M^1, \rho_q \rangle = 0$. *Proof.* $\Lambda_x$ and $g'_x$ do not involve $t_q$; differentiate the relation in $t_q$ (Hasse derivative in characteristic 2): $(0,1) \otimes \rho_q + \sum_x \partial_q \nu_x \otimes g_x = 0$, and $(1, t_q) \otimes \rho_q$ is itself a combination of rows. $\square$ Consequence: the row space is $R = \sum_q z_q \otimes R_q$ with $z_q = (1, t_q)$ and $R_q \subseteq K^L \otimes K^{k/2}$ the row space of block $q$, of dimension $w_q$ (the rows of one subtree are independent) and independent of the top twiddles. At rate 1/2 there are four top blocks, so the top layer is an instance of the four-subspace problem with one generic cross-ratio. This is the same closure problem as the bottom-up recursion of note 74 (the pushed-down constraints $\rho_q$ mix the leaves of a block and depend on the twiddles), seen from the top; it is recorded because the four-subspace quiver is tame, while the five-subspace quiver, where note 74 found Segre's counterexample, is wild. **Not exploited further here.**

## 4. Splitting by degeneration: a combinatorial lower bound on the rank of every pattern (proved)

Notation of note 74: a system gives every leaf $x$ a subspace $\Lambda_x \subseteq K_0^L$ of functionals with constant entries (for a pattern, the span of the differences inside the class $C_x$, of dimension $\kappa_x$), its rows are $\Lambda_x \otimes g_x$, $w = \sum_x \kappa_x$, and the deficiency is $\delta = w - \mathrm{rank}$. A list of $L + 1$ distinct codewords needs a nonzero solution, hence $\mathrm{rank} \le Lk - 1$ and $\delta \ge w - Lk + 1$.

**Theorem 4.1 (splitting; proved, any number of trees, any constant subspaces).** Choose in every bottom pair $p$ one leaf $\chi(p)$. Let $\mathcal P^0$ and $\mathcal P^1$ be the systems on the upper code (same trees, one layer less) with $\Lambda^0_p = \Lambda_{(p, \chi(p))}$ and $\Lambda^1_p = \Lambda_{(p, 1 - \chi(p))}$. Then $\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P) \ge \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P^0) + \mathrm{rank}(\mathcal P^1)$, all ranks generic. The same holds if in some pairs both leaves are sent to the same side (with $\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1}$ there and $0$ on the other side). *Proof.* The rows of a leaf $(p, b)$ are $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (1, t_{p,b})$. Multiply the rows of the leaves sent to side 1 by $\tau = 1/t$: they become $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (\tau, 1)$. The matrix now has polynomial entries in the bottom parameters, and its rank over the function field is at least its rank at $t = 0$ (leaves of side 0) and $\tau = 0$ (leaves of side 1), the upper twiddles untouched. The specialized matrix is block diagonal: the rows $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (1, 0)$ see only the slice $M_0$, the rows $\lambda \otimes g_p \otimes (0, 1)$ only $M_1$. $\square$ In words: sending one twiddle of a pair to $0$ and the other to $\infty$ turns the tree code into two independent copies of the code one layer up, and ranks can only drop.

**Corollary 4.2 (matching bound; proved, rate 1/2).** For **every** bijection $\mu$ from the leaves of tree 0 to the leaves of tree 1, $\mathrm{rank} \ge \sum_{x \in T_0} \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\mu(x)})$, that is $\delta \le \sum_x \dim(\Lambda_x \cap \Lambda_{\mu(x)})$, which for a pattern is $\mathrm{ov}(\mu) := \sum_x (|C_x \cap C_{\mu(x)}| - 1)_+$. *Proof.* Induction on $v$; for $v = 0$ the code is $\{(m, m)\}$ and the rank is $\dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_y)$. For $v \ge 1$ consider the graph on the leaves whose edges are the sibling pairs and the pairs $\{x, \mu(x)\}$. It is a disjoint union of cycles alternating between the two kinds of edges; an edge of $\mu$ changes tree, so every cycle has an even number of them, hence an even number of sibling edges. So the leaves can be 2-coloured with every sibling pair bichromatic and every $\mu$-pair monochromatic. Let $\chi(p)$ be the leaf of colour 0 in $p$. Then $\mu$ induces bijections $\mu^0, \mu^1$ between the pairs of tree 0 and those of tree 1 (through the leaves of each colour), and Theorem 4.1 with the induction hypothesis for $(\mathcal P^c, \mu^c)$ gives the claim. $\square$ So the generic tree code satisfies the bound that a pair of generic bijections $(T_0, T_1)$ satisfies trivially (specialize $T_1 = P_\mu T_0$), although $T_1 = P_\mu T_0$ is not a specialization of a tree code unless $\mu$ is a tree isomorphism.

**Corollary 4.3 (sibling and cut bounds; proved, rate 1/2).** $\mathrm{rank} \ge \sum_{\text{pairs } p} \dim(\Lambda_{p0} + \Lambda_{p1})$; more generally, for a fixed height $h \ge 1$ and a tree isomorphism $\iota_u$ between the two child subtrees of every node $u$ of height $h$, $\mathrm{rank} \ge \sum_u \sum_{x \in u0} \dim(\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\iota_u(x)})$ (different heights may be used in different parts of the tree, as long as the chosen nodes partition the leaves). *Proof.* Specialize the twiddle of the second child of $u$ and every twiddle below it to the corresponding twiddle of the first child's subtree: the rows of $x$ and $\iota_u(x)$ become equal, and the $k$ surviving rows are rows of a generic tree code at $k$ leaves, independent because the code is MDS. $\square$

**Consequences (proved).**
1. *First step of the structure statement (suggestion 1 of the brief).* A pattern in which no two sibling leaves have two members in common has independent rows, whatever the sizes of its classes; so does a pattern for which some bijection between the two trees matches no two leaves with two members in common. Equivalently: **a rank-deficient pattern has, for every sibling matching, every cut matching and every bijection between the trees, a matched pair of leaves at which the same two members agree with the word**, and its deficiency is at most the least total overlap. This complements Corollary 4.3 (b) of note 74 (deficiency at most $\sum_x (c_x - 2)_+$): there the excess sits at leaves with three members, here at pairs of matched leaves sharing two. Example: the three-member list of note 69 at $(4, 8)$, `. 012 | 12 12 | 02 02 | 01 01`, has $w = 8 = Lk$, deficiency 1, sibling overlap 3 and cross-tree overlap exactly 1 (the leaf `012` meets every leaf of the other tree in two members).
2. *Hall form.* If a system has a nonzero solution, no bijection $\mu$ has all its pairs spanning ($\Lambda_x + \Lambda_{\mu(x)} = K^L$). By Koenig's theorem there are $A \subseteq T_0$, $B \subseteq T_1$ with $|A| + |B| \ge k + 1$ and no spanning pair in $A \times B$. For $L = 1$ this is the MDS property (a new proof that does not go through Reed-Solomon). For three distinct codewords $a, b, c$ and any word it reads: with $\mathrm{light}_j$ the number of leaves of tree $j$ where at most one of the three agrees with the word and $\mathrm{full}_j$ the number where all three do, **either $\mathrm{light}_1 > \mathrm{full}_0$, or $\mathrm{light}_0 > \mathrm{full}_1$, or one of the three agrees with the word on at most $k - 1$ leaves outside the set where it agrees alone** (the three maximal cross-non-spanning pairs of families of classes). The one-sided staircase satisfies the first two with equality up to one leaf for the triples checked by hand ($\mathrm{light}_1 = 4b$, $\mathrm{full}_0 = 4b - 1$ for $S_i = \{1,2,3,3+i\}$).
3. *What it does not give.* For a pattern in which every matched pair covers all members, $\mathrm{ov}(\mu) = w - Lk$ exactly, and a list needs $\delta \ge w - Lk + 1$: the corollaries forbid only the last unit, and one leaf with at most one member repairs it. So they change the weight bounds of note 74 by $O(1)$, not by a multiple of $k$, and **they give no list bound below the Johnson radius**. The reason is structural: the fully split code is the interleaved code $C_{\rm up} \otimes K^b$ with equal frames, whose lists at agreement $5/8$ are products of top lists and exponentially large; all the information about distinct members is lost in the limit. The recursive bound of Theorem 4.1 is not an exact rank formula either: for the 35-member staircase at $(8, 16)$ ($w = 300$, $Lk = 272$) the rank is 269 and the deficiency 31 (**exact at the stated size**, `scripts/76_stair_rank.py`: solution space of dimension 3), while no partition of the 16 leaves into 8 cells, realizable or not, gives more than $34 + 7 \cdot 29 = 237$ (a cell with leaves of $j$ blocks other than the zero's has value $19, 29, 33, 34$ for $j = 1, 2, 3, \ge 4$).
4. *Random-like sub-lists are excluded (sketched).* If the classes of a $j$-subset of a list, at the leaves of the two trees, were independent samples in which each member agrees with probability $\theta$, a leaf with $c$ members would need a partner with at least $j - c + 1$, and Hall's condition for the families "at most $s$ members" holds as soon as $\theta > \theta^*(j) = 0.653, 0.625, 0.607, 0.594, 0.584, 0.577$ for $j = 3, \dots, 8$, tending to $1/2$ (computed; Hall's condition was checked for these families only). A perfect spanning matching then exists and the $j$ members coincide. So at every agreement above $1/2$ a large list must be far from random on its small sub-lists: this is the proved mechanism behind the observation of note 69 that scattered patterns are never deficient, and it is where the block structure of the known lists is forced (the staircase has leaves of two kinds only: all members, or the members containing one block).

**Numerical check of Corollaries 4.2 and 4.3 (exact at the stated sizes; `scripts/76_match.py`, GF($2^{64}$), T2, random patterns, the best bijection by the Hungarian method).** At $(4, 8)$ with 3, 4, 5 members and at $(8, 16)$ with 3, 4, 5 members, 10900 patterns in all: the rank is never below either bound; it equals the matching bound in 8673 of them (for instance 2744 of the 3000 patterns at $(4,8)$ with three members, 931 of which have dependent rows) and the sibling bound much less often.

**Is the recursion exact? (exact at the stated sizes; `scripts/76_scheme.py`, `76_scheme_rand.py`, `76_scheme16.py`, `76_match_def.py`.)** Let $r_v(\mathcal P)$ be the best value of Theorem 4.1 applied recursively down to the code $\{(m, m)\}$, each leaf of each pair being sent to either side at every level (a graphic space is a set partition of the members, a sum of spaces is the join, so the recursion is combinatorial). So the split-and-merge recursion gives the exact rank of **every three-member pattern tested** and of every deficient four-member orbit of the exhaustive scan, and it is **not exact from four members on** (first instance: classes `012 023 013 3 | 123 2 013 12` at $(4,8)$, rank 11, bound 10). **Conjecture 4.4 (three members; conjectured).** For three members the generic rank of every pattern is $r_v$: a combinatorial formula. It contains the one-level identity $\dim R = \max_{\text{sides}} (\dim R^0 + \dim R^1)$ inside a class of systems closed under the recursion (spaces $0$, three lines and $K^2$), the primal counterpart of the identity $\dim R = \min_{\Lambda'} \Phi(\Lambda')$ of note 74 (Theorem 2.5 there, unproved when several leaves carry three members); for one functional a leaf both are the matroid union theorem. It would settle suggestion 1 of the brief for three members; it is not proved here.

## 5. What does not work, and the nearest targets

1. **Weights of small sub-lists (computed, limit of large lists).** A list of $M$ codewords at agreement $\tau n$ has, on average over its $t$-subsets, weight at least $n f_t(\tau)$ with $f_t(\theta) = t\theta - 1 + (1 - \theta)^t$ (Jensen; exact as $M \to \infty$). So a proved bound $W_t$ on the weight of $t$ members gives, by this averaging, a threshold below the Johnson radius exactly when $W_t < 2 f_t(1/\sqrt 2)\, k$: $2.29 k$, $3.67 k$, $5.08 k$, $6.49 k$, $7.90 k$, $9.31 k$ for $t = 3, \dots, 8$. The true values are $W_3 = 5k/2 - 2$ and $W_4 \ge 3.75 k - 3$ (note 74), both above the bar: **weights of three and four members can never do it**. For five members $W_5 \ge 4.5 k - 4$ (proved: lift of four concurrent lines of the top $[4,2]$ code and a fifth meeting two of them, all zeros of the lower factor in the free block) against the bar $5.08 k$ and the pair bound $16k/3$; for eight members the pair bound is $9.6 k$ against $9.31 k$. **The first weight theorem that would give a list bound below Johnson is $W_5 \le 5.07 k$ at rate 1/2** (threshold $0.6995$ in the limit of large lists if $W_5 = 5k$, $0.649$ if $W_5 = 4.5 k$); nothing proved here reaches it, because the bounds of Section 4 lose all but one unit of deficiency against a list.
2. **Decomposition by full blocks** (Lemma 2.2): every sub-problem is again below its own Johnson radius.
3. **One functional a leaf with moving directions.** Theorem 4.2 of note 74 needs functionals with constant entries. It is false for functionals depending on the twiddles even with a bounded number of parameters: at rate 1/4 with a top $[8, 2]$ code, $M = (A_1, A_2) \otimes B$ and $\lambda_u = (A_2(u), A_1(u))$ on the leaves of block $u$ give $8b - 1$ independent-looking constraints with a genuine solution, against $Lk - 1 = 4b - 1$ (the family of note 68, 4.4). This blocks the natural attempt to prove that the members of a product word $w_{\rm up} \otimes B$ are products (sketched): it works when the member's error is a product (MDS alone) or fills the whole coset space on its support (Theorem 4.2 of note 74 applied to the $N$-tree code), and fails in between, where a product at the next scale appears.
4. **Limits of lists.** Under the degeneration of Theorem 4.1 the limit of a list is a list of the interleaved code with equal frames, in which members may have merged; the merged members cannot be counted, and the interleaved code has product lists of exponential size. A proof along these lines needs an invariant that survives the limit (a degree, not a rank).
