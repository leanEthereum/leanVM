Notation. $F$ is a perfect field of characteristic 2 ($K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{64})$ in the application, $m = [F : \mathbb{F}_2]$ when $F$ is finite), $W = W(F)$ its ring of Witt vectors ($W/2^\ell$ is the Galois ring $\mathrm{GR}(2^\ell, m)$), $[\cdot] : F \to W$ the Teichmüller lift, $\sigma$ the Frobenius of $W$ ($\sigma[s] = [s]^2$), $v_2$ the 2-adic valuation on $W$. $D \subset F$ is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace with basis $b_1, \dots, b_N$, $n = 2^N$, $s(x) = \sum_i x_i b_i$ for $x \in \mathbb{F}_2^N$. For a finitely supported $c : F \to \mathbb{Z}$: $\Lambda_c(X) = \prod_s (1 - sX)^{c(s)} \in F[[X]]$ and $\tilde p_k(c) = \sum_s c(s)[s]^k \in W$. For $j \ge 1$: $\mathrm{wt}(j)$ is the binary weight and $v_2(j)$ the 2-adic valuation. For $c \in \mathbb{Z}^D$ and $\emptyset \ne A \subseteq \{1..N\}$: $\gamma_A(c) = \sum_{x \supseteq A} c(x)$, the mass of $c$ on the subcube $Q_A = \{x : x_i = 1 \text{ for } i \in A\}$; $n_\varphi(c) = \sum_{\varphi(x) = 1} c(x)$ for a functional $\varphi$. The **Walsh level** of $c$ is the largest $r$ with $c \in L^{(r)}$,
$$L^{(r)} = \{c : n_\varphi(c) \equiv 0 \bmod 2^{r-1} \text{ for all } \varphi\} = \{c : \gamma_A(c) \equiv 0 \bmod 2^{r - |A|} \text{ for all } A \ne \emptyset\}$$
(the two descriptions agree by step (i) of the proof of Theorem W in note 02; $L^{(r)}$ is $L_{r-1}$ in the notation of notes 13 and 19). Two sets of one size are in one "previous-level class" of note 19 when their difference has level at least $N - 2$, and are formally equal at a depth $g \in [n/8, n/4)$ when it has level at least $N - 1$. Depth $g$ means $\Lambda_c \equiv 1 \bmod X^{g+1}$, that is $e_j$ equal for $j \le g$, that is $\deg(Z_S - Z_{S'}) < p - g$ for two sets of size $p$.

## 1. The exact statement

**Theorem 1 [proved].** For every finitely supported $c : F \to \mathbb{Z}$,
$$\mathrm{ord}_X\bigl(\Lambda_c(X) - 1\bigr) = \min_{k \ge 1 \text{ odd}}\ k\,2^{v_2(\tilde p_k(c))}$$
($\infty$ on both sides when all $\tilde p_k(c)$ vanish). Equivalently, for $g \ge 1$: $\Lambda_c \equiv 1 \bmod X^{g+1}$ iff $\tilde p_k(c) \equiv 0 \bmod 2^{\ell_k(g)}$ for every odd $k \le g$, $\ell_k(g) = 1 + \lfloor\log_2(g/k)\rfloor$, iff $\tilde p_j(c) \equiv 0 \bmod 2^{1 + v_2(j)}$ for every $j \le g$.

*Proof.* The three forms are equivalent because $\tilde p_{k2^v}(c) = \sigma^v\tilde p_k(c)$ and $\sigma$ preserves $v_2$. Let $E(X) = \exp(\sum_{i \ge 0} X^{2^i}/2^i) \in \mathbb{Z}_{(2)}[[X]]$ be the Artin-Hasse series (integrality is classical: $E = \prod_{j \text{ odd}}(1 - X^j)^{-\mu(j)/j}$). For $a \in W$ put $\mathcal{A}_a(Y) = \exp(\sum_{i \ge 0}\sigma^i(a)Y^{2^i}/2^i)$. (i) $\mathcal{A}_{a + b} = \mathcal{A}_a\mathcal{A}_b$, $\mathcal{A}_{[s]}(Y) = E([s]Y)$, $\mathcal{A}_{za} = \mathcal{A}_a^z$ for $z \in \mathbb{Z}_2$, and every $a \in W$ is a convergent sum $\sum_i 2^i[a_i]$, so $\mathcal{A}_a \in 1 + YW[[Y]]$ for all $a$ and $\mathcal{A}_a = 1 + aY + O(Y^2)$. (ii) $\mathcal{A}_{2a}(Y) = \exp(2aY)\,\mathcal{A}_{\sigma a}(Y^2)$, and $\exp(2aY) \equiv 1 \bmod 2$ because $v_2(2^t/t!) = \mathrm{wt}(t) \ge 1$. Hence $\mathcal{A}_{2^v a}(Y) \equiv \mathcal{A}_{\sigma^v a}(Y^{2^v}) \bmod 2$, and for $a \ne 0$ with $v = v_2(a)$ and $u = (a/2^v \bmod 2) \in F^\ast$: $\mathcal{A}_a(Y) \equiv 1 + u^{2^v}Y^{2^v} + O(Y^{2^v + 1}) \bmod 2$. (iii) $-\log(1 - X) = \sum_k X^k/k = \sum_{j \text{ odd}} j^{-1}\log E(X^j)$, so the lift $\tilde\Lambda_c = \prod_s(1 - [s]X)^{c(s)}$ equals $\prod_{j \text{ odd}}\mathcal{A}_{-\tilde p_j(c)/j}(X^j)$. Reduce modulo 2: the factor $j$ is $1 + (\text{unit})\,X^{j2^{v_2(\tilde p_j)}} + \dots$ by (ii), and the exponents $j2^{v}$ are pairwise distinct for distinct odd $j$, so the order of the product is the least of them. $\square$

**Corollary 1.1 (sets, multisets, pencils) [proved].** (a) For two sets $S, S' \subset F$ of size $p$: $\deg(Z_S - Z_{S'}) = p - \min_{k \text{ odd}} k\,2^{v_2(\sum_S[\alpha]^k - \sum_{S'}[\beta]^k)}$. In particular $e_1, e_2$ agree iff $\sum_S[\alpha] \equiv \sum_{S'}[\beta] \bmod 4$, and $e_j(A) = e_j(B)$ for $j \le g$ iff $\sum_A[\alpha]^k \equiv \sum_B[\beta]^k \bmod 2^{1+v}$ whenever $k$ is odd and $k2^v \le g$. (b) The same for multisets, with multiplicities as coefficients. (c) Pencil at infinity: let $M_i = Z_{S_i}\theta_i^2$ be monic of degree $p$, $\theta_i \in F[X]$ with roots (with multiplicity) in $\overline F$. Then $\deg(M_i - M_j) < p - g$ iff
$$\sum_{\alpha \in S_i}[\alpha]^k + 2\sum_{\theta_i(\rho) = 0}[\rho]^k \ \equiv\ \sum_{\alpha \in S_j}[\alpha]^k + 2\sum_{\theta_j(\rho) = 0}[\rho]^k \pmod{2^{\ell_k(g)}} \qquad (k \text{ odd},\ k \le g)$$
in $W(\overline F)$ (both sides lie in $W(F)$). Digit 0 involves the $S_i$ only: $p_k(S_i) = p_k(S_j)$ for odd $k \le g$, the code condition of note 53. The digits from 1 on say $\Lambda_{\theta_j}/\Lambda_{\theta_i} \equiv \sqrt{\Lambda_{S_i}/\Lambda_{S_j}} \bmod X^{\lfloor g/2\rfloor + 1}$, a Padé condition. With $\Delta$ the degree bound of the code ($\deg(M_i - M_j) \le \Delta$, so $g = p - \Delta - 1$), $t = \deg\theta_i$ and $g_S = \lvert S_i\rvert - \Delta - 1$ (so $g = g_S + 2t$), the set $S_i$ carries, once $\theta_i$ is eliminated, $\lceil g/2\rceil$ digit-0 conditions and $\lfloor g/2\rfloor - t$ higher ones, $g_S + t$ in all: phantom roots trade higher-digit conditions for digit-0 conditions one for one and never lower the total below $g_S$.

*Proof.* Theorem 1 for $c = 1_S - 1_{S'}$, for a difference of multisets, and for $\mu_i - \mu_j$ with $\mu_i = 1_{S_i} + 2\cdot\mathrm{Roots}(\theta_i)$ over $\overline F$, which is perfect. $\square$

## 2. Digit degrees, and Theorem W as their shadow

**Lemma 2 [proved].** Write $[s(x)]^k = \sum_{A \ne \emptyset} h_A^{(k)}x^A$ (multilinear expansion on $\{0,1\}^N$, coefficients in $W$, $k \ge 1$). Then (i) $v_2(h_A^{(k)}) \ge (\lvert A\rvert - \mathrm{wt}(k))^+$. (ii) For $\lvert A\rvert \ge \mathrm{wt}(k)$ the leading coefficient is
$$\eta_A^{(k)} := \bigl(h_A^{(k)}/2^{\lvert A\rvert - \mathrm{wt}(k)}\bigr) \bmod 2 = \sum_{(t_i) \in \mathbb{Z}^A,\ \sum_i 2^{t_i} = k}\ \prod_{i \in A} b_i^{2^{t_i}} \in F ,$$
the sum over the ordered ways of writing $k$ as a sum of $\lvert A\rvert$ powers of two, negative exponents allowed (for $k = 1$, $A = \{1, 2\}$: $\sqrt{b_1b_2}$, the carry of $[a + b] = [a] + [b] + 2[\sqrt{ab}] + \dots$). For $\lvert A\rvert = \mathrm{wt}(k)$ it is the generalized Moore determinant $\det[b_i^{2^{t_l}}]_{i \in A, l}$, $k = \sum_l 2^{t_l}$. (iii) If the $b_i$ are algebraically independent (formal model), (i) is an equality and the $\eta_A^{(k)}$, $A$ varying, are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

*Proof.* Let $\tilde s(x) = \sum_i x_i[b_i]$. Then $\tilde s \equiv [s(x)] \bmod 2$, so $\tilde s^{2^t} \equiv [s(x)]^{2^t} \bmod 2^{t+1}$ and $[s(x)]^k \equiv \sigma^{-t}(\tilde s(x)^{k2^t}) \bmod 2^{t+1}$. The multinomial expansion of $\tilde s^{k2^t}$ gives $h_A^{(k)} \equiv \sum_\mu \binom{k2^t}{\mu}\prod_{i \in A}[b_i^{2^{-t}}]^{\mu_i} \bmod 2^{t+1}$, the sum over the compositions $\mu$ of $k2^t$ with support exactly $A$. By Legendre $v_2\binom{k2^t}{\mu} = \sum_i\mathrm{wt}(\mu_i) - \mathrm{wt}(k) \ge \lvert A\rvert - \mathrm{wt}(k)$, with equality iff every $\mu_i$ is a power of two. This gives (i) and (ii) for $t \ge \lvert A\rvert$. (iii): Teichmüller lifts of $\mathbb{F}_2$-independent elements are $\mathbb{Z}_2$-independent modulo every power of 2, and distinct $A$ give disjoint sets of monomials. $\square$

**How the digit-$v$ part depends on the indicator.** For $T \subseteq D$, $\sum_{\alpha \in T}[\alpha]^k = \sum_A h_A^{(k)}\,\lvert T \cap Q_A\rvert$. Modulo $2^{v+1}$ only the subcubes with $\lvert A\rvert \le \mathrm{wt}(k) + v$ matter, and $\lvert T \cap Q_A\rvert$ enters modulo $2^{v + 1 - (\lvert A\rvert - \mathrm{wt}(k))^+}$. The binary digit $i$ of the integer $\lvert T \cap Q_A\rvert$ is $e_{2^i}$ of the indicator restricted to $Q_A$ (Lucas), a polynomial of degree $2^i$ in the indicator: these are the carries. So digit $v$ of the Witt sum is a function of the indicator of degree $2^v$, and in the coordinates of $D$ it pairs the indicator with monomials of degree up to $\mathrm{wt}(k) + v$, not $\mathrm{wt}(k)$. This is the point where the heuristic "$\alpha \mapsto \alpha^k$ has algebraic degree $\mathrm{wt}(k)$, so few strata see it" stops being true beyond digit 0.

**Corollary 2.1 (Theorem W of note 02 and its converse) [proved; new proof].** $\tilde p_k(c) = \sum_A h_A^{(k)}\gamma_A(c)$, so for $c \in L^{(r)}$: $v_2(\tilde p_k(c)) \ge \min_A[(\lvert A\rvert - \mathrm{wt} k)^+ + (r - \lvert A\rvert)^+] \ge r - \mathrm{wt}(k)$, and by Theorem 1 $\mathrm{ord}(\Lambda_c - 1) \ge \min_k k2^{(r - \mathrm{wt} k)^+} = 2^{r-1}$ ($k \ge 2^{\mathrm{wt}(k) - 1}$, equality for $k = 1$). That is Theorem W in every field. In the formal model $v_2(\tilde p_1(c)) = \min_A[v_2(\gamma_A(c)) + \lvert A\rvert - 1]$ by Lemma 2 (iii), so the single congruence $\sum c(s)[s] \equiv 0 \bmod 2^{a+1}$ (that is $e_1, e_2, e_4, \dots, e_{2^a}$ trivial) already forces $c \in L^{(a+2)}$: the converse. So Theorem W is exactly the valuation statement of Lemma 2, and the formal model is the case where no cancellation occurs between the $\eta_A^{(k)}$.

## 3. The stratum count for every digit

### 3.1 Walsh levels

**Theorem 3 [proved].** Let $c \in L^{(r)}$ and $\bar g_A = \gamma_A(c)/2^{r - \lvert A\rvert} \bmod 2$ for $1 \le \lvert A\rvert \le r$ (the class of $c$ modulo $L^{(r+1)}$).

1. The congruence $\tilde p_k(c) \equiv 0 \bmod 2^{v+1}$ holds automatically when $\mathrm{wt}(k) + v < r$. With $j = k2^v$: $\mathrm{wt}(k) + v = 1 + \mathrm{wt}(j - 1)$. So the units $\Lambda_c \bmod X^{g+1}$, $c \in L^{(r)}$, form a group of order at most $\lvert F\rvert^{C_g(r)}$,
$$C_g(r) = \#\{(k, v) : k \text{ odd},\ k2^v \le g,\ \mathrm{wt}(k) + v \ge r\} = \#\{0 \le i < g : \mathrm{wt}(i) \ge r - 1\} .$$
2. (Leading digit.) For odd $k$ with $\mathrm{wt}(k) \le r$ and $j = k2^{r - \mathrm{wt}(k)}$: $\bigl(\tilde p_k(c)/2^{r - \mathrm{wt} k} \bmod 2\bigr)^{2^{r - \mathrm{wt} k}} = \sum_{\mathrm{wt}(k) \le \lvert A\rvert \le r}\bar g_A\,M_A(j)$, where $M_A(j) = \sum_{t \in \mathbb{N}^A,\ \sum 2^{t_i} = j}\prod_{i \in A}b_i^{2^{t_i}}$ is the sum of all monomials of degree $j$ in the $b_i$, $i \in A$, with every exponent a power of two. It is $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear in the class. For $j < 2^r$ this is the coefficient of $X^j$ in $\Lambda_c$ (the sparse key of note 13).
3. Consequences. $C_g(r) = 0$ iff $g < 2^{r-1}$ (Theorem W). For $2^{r-1} \le g < 2^r$, $C_g(r)$ is the number $i(g)$ of note 13 (positions $2^r - 2^t$). For $g \ge 2^r$ it continues through all later blocks: the coefficient $e_j$ costs nothing at level $r$ exactly when $\mathrm{wt}(j - 1) < r - 1$. Pigeonhole: any family $\mathcal{F}$ of sets of one size in one class modulo $L^{(r)}$ contains at least $\lvert\mathcal{F}\rvert/\lvert F\rvert^{C_g(r)}$ sets that pairwise share their top to depth $g$; and two sets whose difference has level exactly $r$ share a top to depth $g$ only if $C_g(r)$ elements of $F$ vanish.

*Proof.* (1) is Corollary 2.1: $\tilde p_k(c) \in 2^{(r - \mathrm{wt} k)^+}W$, so the image of $L^{(r)}$ in $\prod_k W/2^{\ell_k(g)}$ has at most $\lvert F\rvert^{\sum_k(\ell_k - (r - \mathrm{wt} k)^+)^+}$ elements, and the exponent counts the pairs $(k, v)$ with $(r - \mathrm{wt} k)^+ \le v < \ell_k$. For odd $k$, $j - 1 = (k-1)2^v + 2^v - 1$ has weight $\mathrm{wt}(k) - 1 + v$. (2) Lemma 2 (ii): the terms with $\lvert A\rvert < \mathrm{wt}(k)$ have valuation at least $r - \lvert A\rvert > r - \mathrm{wt}(k)$; raise $\eta_A^{(k)}$ to the power $2^{r - \mathrm{wt} k}$, which turns the decompositions of $k$ with exponents at least $-(r - \mathrm{wt} k)$ into all decompositions of $j$. For $j < 2^r = 2j_{\min}$ no product of two factors of the proof of Theorem 1 reaches $X^j$. (3) is (1) and Theorem 1. $\square$

**Proposition 3.1 (candidates at high levels) [proved].** A difference $c = 1_S - 1_{S'} \in L^{(N - c)}$ of two sets of one size is $2^{-c}\sum_{\varphi \ne 0}d_\varphi\chi_\varphi$ with $d \in \mathbb{Z}^{D^\vee \setminus 0}$ and $\sum d_\varphi^2 = 4^c\lvert S \triangle S'\rvert/n$ (Parseval). So there are at most $(1 + 2n)^{4^cw} \le 2^{(N+2)\,4^c\,w}$ such differences of weight at most $wn$ (with $Q = 4^cw$: at most $\binom nt2^t\binom Qt$ vectors $d$ with $t$ nonzero entries and $\sum\lvert d_\varphi\rvert \le Q$), and never more than $3^n$. In the windows $w \le 2(1 - a)$ at rate 1/2 and $w \le 2a$ at the other rates.

For every $c \ge 3$ and every rate, $64\,C_g(N - c)$ exceeds the bound on the candidates at the lower end of the window, by at least 360 bits (rate 1/2, $c = 3$, 23 percent), 1215, 543, 767 bits (the other rates, $c = 3$), and by a growing margin for $c \ge 4$. ### 3.2 Boolean degree

**Corollary 3.2 [proved, given McEliece's theorem, quoted].** If the indicator of $T \subseteq D$ has algebraic degree at most $d$, then $v_2(\sum_{\alpha \in T}[\alpha]^k) \ge \lceil(N - \mathrm{wt}(k))/d\rceil - 1$. Hence for two sets $A, B$ of degree at most $d$ the condition $(k, v)$ is automatic when $\mathrm{wt}(k) + d(v + 1) < N$, and the keys $(e_1..e_g)$ of the sets of degree at most $d$ take at most $\lvert F\rvert^{R_d(g)}$ values, $R_d(g) = \#\{j \le g : \mathrm{wt}(j) + d\,(v_2(j) + 1) \ge N\}$. For $v = 0$ this is the stratum count $c_g(d)$ of note 53, Proposition 5.2.

*Proof.* $\lvert T \cap Q_A\rvert$ is the weight of a function of degree at most $d$ in $N - \lvert A\rvert$ variables, divisible by $2^{\lceil(N - \lvert A\rvert)/d\rceil - 1}$ (McEliece). ## 4. What can be proved about the real field

### 4.1 Low Walsh levels are impossible on every domain

**Lemma 4.1 (widening) [proved].** Let $\omega$ be an alternating $q$-form on $\mathbb{F}_2^N$, extended $F$-multilinearly, and $b^{(t)} = (b_i^{2^t})_{i \le N} \in F^N$ for $t \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $\omega(b^{(t_1)}, \dots, b^{(t_q)}) = 0$ for all integers $t_1 < \dots < t_q$ with $t_q - t_1 \le L$, and $2^{q-1}(N - 1 - L) \le N - 1$, then $\omega = 0$.

*Proof.* We may assume $L \le N - 1$. Put $S_0 = L$, $S_{j+1} = 2S_j + 1 - N$, so $S_j = (N - 1) - 2^j(N - 1 - L)$ is nonincreasing and the hypothesis is $S_{q-1} \ge 0$. Call a $q$-set $T \subset \mathbb{Z}$ $j$-good if some $(q - j)$-subset of it has spread (largest minus smallest element) at most $S_j$. Claim: $\omega(b^{(T)}) = 0$ for every $j$-good $T$, $0 \le j \le q - 1$. For $j = 0$ this is the hypothesis. Let $T = Q \cup E \cup \{t^\ast\}$ be $(j+1)$-good, $\lvert Q\rvert = q - j - 1 \ge 1$ of spread $\rho \le S_{j+1}$, $\lvert E\rvert = j$. The integers $t$ with $\mathrm{spread}(Q \cup \{t\}) \le S_j$ form an interval of $2S_j - \rho + 1 \ge N$ integers; choose $N$ consecutive ones, $B$. The $b^{(t)}$, $t \in B$, are a basis of $F^N$ (Moore determinant, the $b_i$ being independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$), so $b^{(t^\ast)} = \sum_{t \in B}\mu_tb^{(t)}$ and $\omega(b^{(T)}) = \pm\sum_t\mu_t\,\omega(b^{(Q \cup E \cup \{t\})})$. A term with $t \in Q \cup E$ vanishes by alternation; in the others $Q \cup E \cup \{t\}$ is $j$-good. Every $q$-set is $(q-1)$-good (a point has spread $0 \le S_{q-1}$), so $\omega$ vanishes on the basis $b^{(T)}$, $T \subseteq \{0..N-1\}$, of $\Lambda^qF^N$. $\square$

**Theorem 4 [proved].** Let $D$ be an $N$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of any perfect field of characteristic 2 and $r \ge 1$. For $q \ge 2$ let $\lambda_q(G)$ be the largest $L \ge q - 1$ with $2^{L+1} - 2^{L+2-q} + 1 \le G$, the left side being the largest value $\sum_{t \in T}2^t$ of a $q$-set $T \ni 0$ of spread at most $L$ ($\lambda_q(G) = -\infty$ if there is none). Suppose that $g \ge 2^{r-1}$ and that for every $v \in \{0, \dots, r - 2\}$, with $q = r - v$: $2^{q-1}\bigl(N - 1 - \lambda_q(\lfloor g/2^v\rfloor)\bigr) \le N - 1$. Then no $c \in \mathbb{Z}^D$ of Walsh level exactly $r$ has $\Lambda_c \equiv 1 \bmod X^{g+1}$.

*Proof.* Let $c$ have level $r$, so $\bar g \ne 0$; let $q = \max\{\lvert A\rvert : \bar g_A \ne 0\}$, $v = r - q$, and $\omega = (\bar g_A)_{\lvert A\rvert = q} \ne 0$, an alternating $q$-form. For odd $k$ of weight $q$ with $k2^v \le g$, Theorem 1 gives $v_2(\tilde p_k(c)) \ge v + 1$, and Theorem 3 (2) gives $\tilde p_k(c)/2^v \equiv \sum_{\lvert A\rvert = q}\eta_A^{(k)}\bar g_A = \omega(b^{(t_1)}, \dots, b^{(t_q)})$ with $k = \sum_l2^{t_l}$ (the terms with $\lvert A\rvert > q$ vanish by the choice of $q$, and for $\lvert A\rvert = \mathrm{wt}(k)$ the coefficient is the Moore minor). So $\omega(b^{(T)}) = 0$ for every $q$-set $T \ni 0$ of value at most $g/2^v$, and for every translate $T + s$, because $\omega$ has coefficients in $\mathbb{F}_2$ and $\omega(b^{(T + s)}) = \omega(b^{(T)})^{2^s}$. For $q = 1$ this is $\sum_i\bar g_ib_i = 0$, impossible. For $q \ge 2$ all $q$-sets of spread at most $\lambda_q(\lfloor g/2^v\rfloor)$ are covered and Lemma 4.1 gives $\omega = 0$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 [proved].** If $g \ge n/8$ then $\lambda_{r - v}(\lfloor g/2^v\rfloor) \ge N - 4 - v$ and the hypothesis holds for every $r$ with $3 \cdot 2^{r-1} \le N - 1$. So on every subspace domain of dimension $N \ge 25$, in every field: **two sets (or multisets, or any integer vectors) that share $e_1..e_g$ for some $g \ge n/8$ have a difference of Walsh level at least 5: all their hyperplane counts agree modulo 16.** In general the level is at least $2 + \lfloor\log_2((N - 1)/3)\rfloor$. The formal model gives level $N - 1$ (modulo $n/4$).

This is the first statement of the form "a real-field coincidence forces formal congruences" that holds on leanVM's domain without a hypothesis. It is weak (4 levels of the 23 that are needed at $N = 25$) and the method cannot reach far: it uses only the leading digit, which is linear in the class, and by counting the leading-digit system stays overdetermined on the 64-bit field only up to about $r = 15$ at $N = 25$, $g = 0.2\,n$ ($64\,\#\{i < g : \mathrm{wt}(i) = r - 1\}$ bits against $\sum_{1 \le t \le r}\binom Nt$ unknowns: 35.8 million against 29.7 million at $r = 15$, 19.5 against 31.7 at $r = 16$). Lemma 4.1 is far from that limit (it is sharp only in fields of degree close to $N$, `56_lowlevel.py A`), so there is room for a better interpolation argument between levels 5 and 15 **[open]**. Levels 16 to $N - 3$ need the higher digits, which are not linear.

### 4.2 The first block of a deep accident, in the dual space

Notation of note 19: for a functional $\varphi \ne 0$, $\delta_\varphi = \prod_{\varphi(x) = 1}x = L_{\ker\varphi}(t)$, $a_\varphi = 1/\delta_\varphi$ (additive in $\varphi$), and for $c \in L^{(r)}$ with $\hat c = 2^rd$, $\Omega' = \{\varphi : d_\varphi \text{ odd}\}$.

**Proposition 4.3 [proved, from note 19 Theorem 1 (refereed in note 21)].** For $c \in L^{(r)}$ with $\sum c = 0$ and $r \le N - 1$: $\Lambda_c \equiv 1 \bmod X^{2^r}$ iff the linearized polynomial $\sum_{\varphi \in \Omega'}L_{\ker\varphi}(Y)$ has degree less than $n/2^{r+1}$, iff the $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear map $T_{\Omega'} : D \to F$, $y \mapsto \sum_{\varphi \in \Omega'}\delta_\varphi\,\varphi(y)$, is the restriction of a linearized polynomial of 2-degree at most $N - 2 - r$. For $r = N - 3$: $T_{\Omega'}(y) = \alpha y^2 + \beta y$. If moreover $\lvert\Omega'\rvert < 2(N - 1)$ then $T_{\Omega'} = 0$: for every $y \in D$ the set $\{\varphi \in \Omega' : \varphi(y) = 1\}$ is empty or is a set of at least 6 functionals with $\sum\varphi = 0$ and $\sum 1/a_\varphi = 0$.

*Proof.* Note 19: $\theta_i(c)^{2^{N-r}} = \sum_{\Omega'}\delta_\varphi c_i(\varphi)$ for $0 \le i \le r - 1$, with $L_{\ker\varphi} = \sum_ic_i(\varphi)Y^{2^{N-1-i}}$ and $c_{i+1}(\varphi)^2 = D_{i+1} + \delta_\varphi c_i(\varphi)$. $\lvert\Omega'\rvert$ is even, so $\sum_{\Omega'}\delta_\varphi c_i(\varphi) = (\sum_{\Omega'}c_{i+1}(\varphi))^2$: the first block vanishes iff the coefficients of $Y^{2^{N-1-j}}$, $j \le r$, of $\sum_{\Omega'}L_{\ker\varphi}$ vanish, and $L_{\ker\varphi}(y) = \delta_\varphi\varphi(y)$ on $D$. For $r = N - 3$: $\alpha y^2 + \beta y$ has a kernel of dimension at most 1 in $D$ unless $\alpha = \beta = 0$, while the rank of $T_{\Omega'}$ is at most the rank of $\Omega'$, which is at most $\lvert\Omega'\rvert/2$ because $\Omega'$ is a word of $\mathrm{RM}(2, N)^\perp$ (the matrix of its points has $\Phi^{\mathsf T}\Phi = 0$). The sets $\{\varphi(y) = 1\} \cap \Omega'$ are zero-sum and even, and the Vandermonde argument of note 19, Theorem 2 gives at least 6 points. $\square$

At $N = 25$ in the rate 1/2 window $\lvert\Omega'\rvert \le 64\lvert S \triangle S'\rvert/n \le 43 < 48$, so **a pair of level $N - 3$ in the window is a set of at most 43 points of the dual space, a word of $\mathrm{RM}(22, 25)$, all of whose sections by hyperplanes off the origin are dual words with at least six points, of the kind of Section 5.2**, and then $N - 2$ further conditions hold. A single affine flat fails (its sections are flats, and $\sum_{u \in U}1/(a_0 + u) = \prod_{u \ne 0}u/L_U(a_0) \ne 0$). I could not exclude the general configuration **[open]**; by the count of Section 3.1 it has probability below $2^{-360}$.

## 6. Answers to the tasks

**Task 1.** Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1: the lead's statement is exact, for sets, multisets and the pencil at infinity ($\sum_{S_i}[\alpha]^k + 2\sum_{\theta_i(\rho) = 0}[\rho]^k$ fixed modulo $2^{\ell_k(g)}$). Theorem W is the valuation bound of Lemma 2 and its converse is the equality case for $k = 1$ (Corollary 2.1).

**Task 2.** Section 3. Digit $v$ of $\sum_T[\alpha]^k$ is a $W$-linear combination of the subcube counts $\lvert T \cap Q_A\rvert$, $\lvert A\rvert \le \mathrm{wt}(k) + v$, taken modulo $2^{v + 1 - (\lvert A\rvert - \mathrm{wt} k)^+}$; the carries are the binary digits of these counts. For indicators of Boolean degree at most $d$ the condition $(k, v)$ is active iff $\mathrm{wt}(k) + d(v + 1) \ge N$; for differences of Walsh level $r$ iff $\mathrm{wt}(k) + v \ge r$. The formal model and the real field have the same active conditions; the real field gives each 64 bits. Tables at $N = 25..28$ in Sections 3.1 and 3.2. Classes where the real field imposes fewer bits than the class has, all digits counted: only the previous-level classes ($r = N - 2$, 1 to 3 conditions), and they are bounded by note 19.

**Task 3.** Non-formal top-type configurations exist on the real field at real sizes below the Johnson radius: 352 explicit pairs at depth $3n/16 - 1$ (22 structural words, Section 5.2), star families of 4 to 11 sets (Section 5.3), and with high probability pairs up to depth $7n/32 - 1$. All are inside one previous-level class, where at rate 1/2 every list has at most 7 members for $g \ge 3n/16$ and at most $2^{32}$ below (note 19). Outside a previous-level class: none at Walsh levels 1 to 4 (Theorem 4, every domain); none found and none expected at levels 5 to $N - 3$ (Section 3.1). At toy sizes the numbers of non-formal pairs grow as $2^{-mC_g(r)}$ when $m$ decreases, never faster, and vanish where Theorem 4 or note 19 says they must.

**Task 4.** No growing family, so no new list sizes: the largest agreements with lists above $2^{200}$ and $2^{1000}$ on the real parameters are those of note 38 ($17/32$ and the pigeonhole value 0.5156 at rate 1/2), and the frontier does not move. The strongest transfer statements: Theorem 4 (levels 1 to 4, every domain), Theorem 3 (exact price of every other level), and the reduction below.

**Proposition 6.1 (what (G3) reduces to, clean case) [proved, with note 19 Theorem 3a].** Let $D$ be any subspace domain of dimension $N \ge 25$, and $S_1, \dots, S_M$ sets of one size $p$ with $\min(p, n - p) < n/2$ that pairwise share their top to depth $g \ge 3n/16$. Then $M \le JM'$, where $J = \lfloor 1/(1 - 2\min(p, n - p)/n)^2\rfloor$ (7 for $p \ge 11n/16$ at rate 1/2), $M'$ is the number of previous-level classes among them, and two members in different classes have a difference $c$ of Walsh level $r \in [5, N - 3]$ whose $C_g(r) \ge C_g(N - 3) \ge 2N - 5$ Witt coordinates of weight at least $r$ all vanish in $K$. So for a clean pencil at infinity (list members $\psi + \lambda_i\Lambda_{S_i}$ with one polynomial centre $\psi$ of degree $p \ge \Delta + 1 + 3n/16$, the situation of note 53, Theorem 6.2 with constant cofactors), the bound $\lvert L_{G^\star}\rvert \le J$ on the real domain follows from the absence of such $c$ of weight in $[2g + 2, 2(n - p)]$: a statement about at most $2^{1176}$ candidates at level $N - 3$ and correspondingly few at the next levels (Proposition 3.1), each constrained by at least $64(2N - 5) = 2880$ bits at $N = 25$. It is not proved.

What this does not touch: cofactors $r_P$ with odd roots outside $D$ ((G1) of note 53). By Corollary 1.1 (c) such roots enter the Witt congruences with coefficient 2 at points that are not in $D$, and the Walsh-level calculus (which lives on $\mathbb{F}_2^N$) has no place for them, the same limitation as in note 19, Section 8. And it does not touch arbitrary words.

**For notes 12, 19, 21.** The formal bound for strict common tops above the second position (note 02, Proposition 6) holds on the real domain up to the factor $M'$ above, which is note 19's Corollary 3.3 with the classes now restricted to levels 5 to $N - 3$. For multiset tops (note 12, Theorem A) only Remark 6.1 of note 19 is available inside a class; Theorems 1, 3 and 4 here are statements about integer vectors and apply to multisets verbatim. Note 13's Corollary 3.2 (3) and note 19's Proposition 4.1 ("$e' + 1$ field elements, before anything is asked of the later blocks") become "$C_g(r)$ field elements in all", and note 19's question about differences below the previous level gets the answer: impossible at levels 1 to 4, priced at levels 5 to $N - 3$.

**Hostile summary of the lead's guess.** The formula is right. The hope attached to it is not: (1) it is not new (note 13); (2) the 64-bits-per-digit accounting does not reveal a shortage of conditions, it reveals that digit 0 is a small minority of them, so the alarming number 47938 of note 53 is irrelevant to lists; (3) it gives no handle on the one thing that is missing, a proof that an explicit $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice of index $2^{64C}$ has no short $\{0, \pm1\}$ vector in the middle levels; the only new proof it produced (Theorem 4) uses the first digit of each stratum, which is linear, and stops at level 4.
