# 16. Fewer points by a sharper proof: what is true, what is provable

Researcher 16, round 3. Target: the probability that the $s$ points of a DEEP fold round are exceptional (note 07 Theorem 3, note 10 Theorem A, note 11 Lemma D4, note 14 Lemma F), which the proofs bound by about $\Lambda^3 q^3 \epsilon^s$ and note 10 conjectures to be about $(n/q)^s + \binom{\Lambda}{2}\epsilon^s$. Statuses: **[proved]** (complete proof in this note, by hand, not machine-checked), **[checked]** (numerically, sizes given), **[conjectured]**. Scripts: `scripts/16_sharp.c` (exact block search on toy fields, written from scratch), `scripts/16_checks.py` (three algebraic checks), `scripts/16_params.py` (point counts). ## 0. Verdict

1. **The conjecture of note 10 is false as stated, by one point.** For the statement the protocol uses (Lemma F(2) of note 14, any admissible $K_0$), there are words whose exceptional set has probability at least $s\,(r/q)^{s-1}(1 - o(1))$ with $r$ a constant fraction of $d$, and, with points uniform in $E$, at least $s\,(n/q)^{s-1}(1 - o(1))$ (Propositions 3.1 to 3.3, **[proved]**; **[checked]** exhaustively over all point pairs at $q = 64$, where the measured frequency equals the prediction exactly). The mechanism: a list member that misses the answers at exactly one point wins a whole line of fold challenges, so $k$ members that share the answers at $s - 1$ points win $k$ lines with one prover message, with the zero weight or with a weight that every one of them violates. So only $s - 1$ of the $s$ points separate list members. The families exist unconditionally for $k \le (n - d)/(A - d)$ members (so for $K_0 = 2.72q$ at every threshold up to $(n + 2d)/3$), for the flats word at rate 1/4 with $n/2^{s-1}$ members, and otherwise whenever a list of $K_0/q$ members exists at a slightly lower degree. This is not an attack on the protocol (after the round the prover is honest for one member, and a member costs $1/q$ to reach), but it is a floor for every proof that uses the state function of notes 11 and 14: where lists can reach $K_0/q = 2^9$ members, the point message costs at least $s\,(r/q)^{s-1}$, not $\epsilon^s$, and two points are not enough at levels 0 and 1 with the 15-bit grind (for the flats word at rate 1/4, unconditionally: $2n/q = 2^{-101}$).
2. **Best bound I can prove: Theorem B (section 5).** $\Pr[\text{points exceptional}] \le \binom{s}{2}/q + s\binom{L}{2}\epsilon^{s-1} + |\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|^3\,\epsilon^s / (K_u(K_u-1)(K_u-q))$, and off that set fewer than $\max(2q, sq) + L + K_u$ challenges win, for lists $L < K_0$. Here $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}$ is the set of pairs (challenge, close codeword) that are not the fold of a near pair. **[proved]** The first two terms match the floor of item 1 up to the list factor. The third is the triple count of note 07 restricted to the unexplained pairs: the factor $q^3$ of Lemma F becomes $(|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|/q)^3$. Unconditionally $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le \Lambda q^2$ and Theorem B gives the counts of note 14, no better. So: **no unconditional improvement of the point count.** What Theorem B says is that the whole loss $q^3$ is the price of not knowing how many fold challenges have an unexplained close codeword, and that a very weak bound on that number removes it: if at most $2^{75}q$ pairs are unexplained (a fraction $2^{-53}$ of the challenges at list size one, where today's analysis needs $2^{-118}$), the count drops to the floor.
3. **A barrier (section 4).** Lemma F uses the lists only through their sizes, the sizes of classes and Schwartz-Zippel. For abstract systems with those properties, two points never suffice (every pair of points is exceptional, with three candidates per challenge), and each point beyond the second gains at most $128 - j$ bits for the largest $j \mid 128$ with $2^j \le d + 1$ (112 bits at levels 0 to 2). **[proved]**, **[checked]** at $q = 64$. So any proof of a two-point statement, and any proof that brings the unexplained term to $\mathrm{poly}(\Lambda, n)\,\epsilon^s$, must use the code. What the code gives (Lemmas 2.1, 2.2, 4.2, **[proved]**): a class on a line is a full line or has at most $n - A + 1$ elements; a section of dimension $k$ contains at most $\sum_{t \le k}\binom{n}{t}$ unexplained elements off full lines; so an unexplained block of $q$ elements must span a section of dimension at least 5 at $n = 2^{28}$ and at least 8 at $n = 2^{20}$. I could not turn this into a probability bound, and I explain in section 6 why the missing statement is a mutual correlated agreement statement beyond Johnson, with error $2^{-53}$ instead of $2^{-118}$.

## 1. The event that matters

Lemma F of note 14 has two parts: (1) a set $\mathrm{Exc}_j(O)$ of point tuples of small probability, (2) for $\mathbf z$ outside it, for every weighted claim $(W, \sigma)$, carried values $v$, predicate $B$ and prover message, under the hypothesis that every near pair that satisfies the carried point claims violates the running claim, fewer than $K_0$ challenges admit a close $P$ with (p), (w), (b). I call a pair (set, integer) *valid for $O$* if (2) holds for it. The quantity to bound is the least probability of a valid set, for the worst word. Note that (2) quantifies over all weights, the zero weight included, and that its hypothesis is void when no near pair satisfies the carried claims.

With answers $y^F_m := \varphi_m$, $y^G_m := v_m + \theta_m^{2^{j-1}}\varphi_m$, (p) reads $\mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(P) = y^G_m + a_c(\zeta_m)y^F_m$. For an answer vector $m = (y^F, y^G)$ the *block* $\mathcal W(m, \mathbf z)$ is the set of elements of $\mathcal T$ satisfying (p). Geometrically, the point $z$ maps an element to $(a_c(\zeta), \mathrm{ev}_z(P)) \in E^2$, an answer is a non-vertical line of that plane, and a block is the set of elements whose $s$ images lie on $s$ given lines.

*Near pairs.* $\mathcal N := \{(F, G) \in \mathrm{List}(O; A - 1, d) : \deg F \le d - 1\}$, $N_P := |\mathcal N| \le L$. An element is *explained* if it lies in $\mathcal C(U)$ for some $U \in \mathcal N$. $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}$ is the set of unexplained elements. The *unexplained mass* is $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|/q$, in units of lines of challenges. In the language of `doc/whir_soundness_gap.md` section 3.2, $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le \Lambda\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{un}}\,q^2$.

## 2. Structure of $\mathcal T$

**Lemma 2.1 (a class on a line is full or tiny). [proved]** Let $\mathcal C = \mathcal C(F, G)$ have at least two elements, with $F$ not a polynomial tuple or of degree at least $d$, so that its challenges lie on a line $\lambda \mapsto c_\lambda$ of $E^2$ (note 14, L2(a), (b)). Then either $\mathcal C$ contains the element over every challenge of the line ($q$ elements, a *full line*), and then all its elements agree with their folds on one common set of at least $A$ columns; or $|\mathcal C| \le n - A + 1$.

*Proof.* On the line, $a_{c_\lambda} = a_0 + \lambda\Delta$ with $\Delta = X + x_0$ (pole $x_0$) or $\Delta = 1$ (common $\beta$). For two elements, $P_\lambda + P_\mu = (\lambda + \mu)\Delta F$, so $F' := \Delta F$ is a polynomial tuple of degree at most $d$, and so is $G_0 := P_\lambda + \lambda F' = G + a_0F$. So $P_\lambda = G_0 + \lambda F'$ is a polynomial tuple of degree at most $d$ for every $\lambda \in E$. The fold along the line is $O_{c_\lambda} = u + \lambda v$ with $u := O_1 + a_0O_0$, $v := \Delta O_0$. At a column $x$, $P_\lambda(x) = O_{c_\lambda}(x)$ reads $(G_0 + u)(x) = \lambda\,(F' + v)(x)$ in $E^h$: it holds for every $\lambda$ if both sides vanish (call $S_0$ the set of such columns), for at most one $\lambda$ if $(F' + v)(x) \ne 0$, for none otherwise. If $|S_0| \ge A$ every $\lambda$ gives an element of $\mathcal T$, and it lies in $\mathcal C$. Otherwise every element needs at least $A - |S_0|$ agreement columns outside $S_0$, each of which serves at most one element: $|\mathcal C| \le (n - |S_0|)/(A - |S_0|) \le n - A + 1$. $\square$

So $\mathcal T$ is the union of the classes of near pairs, the full lines (the pole lines of note 00 section 3, and their analogues with a common $\beta$), and *sporadic* elements, all of whose classes have at most $\binom{n}{2}$ elements.

**Lemma 2.2 (sections). [proved]** Let $\Sigma$ be an affine subspace of dimension $k$ of the $E$-vector space $V := E^2 \times (E[X]_{\le d})^h \ni (c, P)$. The elements of $\mathcal T \cap \Sigma$ that are neither explained nor on a full line number at most $\sum_{t=0}^{k}\binom{n}{t}$.

*Proof.* For a column $x$ let $H_x := \{(c, P) \in V : P(x) = O_1(x) + a_c(x)O_0(x)\}$, an affine subspace. For $p \in \mathcal T \cap \Sigma$ with agreement set $S_p$ ($|S_p| \ge A$) let $I_p := \Sigma \cap \bigcap_{x \in S_p}H_x \ni p$. Every point $(c', P')$ of $I_p$ has $P'$ of degree at most $d$ agreeing with $O_{c'}$ on $S_p$, so $I_p \subseteq \mathcal T$; two of its points over the same challenge agree on more than $d$ columns and are equal, so $I_p$ projects injectively to $E^2$ and has dimension at most 2. If $\dim I_p = 0$: adding the columns of $S_p$ one at a time, at most $k$ of them strictly decrease the dimension, so $p$ is the unique point of $\Sigma \cap H_{x_1} \cap \dots \cap H_{x_t}$ for some $t \le k$ columns, and there are at most $\sum_{t \le k}\binom nt$ such points. If $\dim I_p \ge 1$, take an affine line $\{(c_0 + \lambda\delta, P_0 + \lambda\Pi)\}$ of $I_p$ through $p$: for two of its points $P_\lambda + P_\mu = (\lambda + \mu)\Pi$ and $a_{c_\lambda} + a_{c_\mu} = (\lambda + \mu)(\delta_\alpha + \delta_\beta X)$, so all its $q$ points lie in the class of $F = \Pi/(\delta_\alpha + \delta_\beta X)$. If $F$ is not a polynomial tuple of degree at most $d - 1$ this is a full line. If it is, then $(G + O_1) + a_{c_\lambda}(F + O_0) = 0$ on $S_p$ for all $\lambda$, and at every column where $\delta_\alpha + \delta_\beta x \ne 0$ the scalar $a_{c_\lambda}(x)$ takes all values, so $F = O_0$ and $G = O_1$ there: the pair agrees with $O$ on at least $A - 1$ columns and $p$ is explained. $\square$

Consequence. An unexplained block with $K$ elements off full lines spans a section of dimension $k$ with $\sum_{t \le k}\binom nt \ge K$. For $K = q$: $k \ge 5$ at $n = 2^{28}$ ($\binom{n}{4} = 2^{107.4}$), $k \ge 6$ at $n = 2^{24}$, $k \ge 8$ at $n = 2^{20}$.

## 3. Lower bounds: one point is always lost

**Proposition 3.1 (members that share $s - 1$ answers win a line each). [proved]** Let $U_1, \dots, U_k \in \mathrm{List}(O; A, d)$ have halves $(F, G_a)$ with one common $F$ of degree at most $d - 1$. Let $\mathbf z$ and an index $i$ be such that $\mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_m}(G_a)$ does not depend on $a$ for every $m \ne i$, and the values $g_a := \mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_i}(G_a)$ are pairwise distinct. Suppose $|\mathrm{List}(O; A - 1, d)| < q$. Then there are a weighted claim, carried values and a prover message for which the hypothesis of Lemma F(2) holds and at least $kq$ challenges admit a $P$ with (p) and (w) (with $B$ true). Hence every pair $(\mathrm{Exc}, K_0)$ valid for $O$ with $K_0 \le kq$ has $\mathbf z \in \mathrm{Exc}$.

*Proof.* Take $W = 0$, $\sigma = 0$ and the message $v_G = v_F = \sigma_0 = \sigma_1 = 0$: (w) reads $0 = 0$ for every $P$. Write $\theta'_m := \theta_m^{2^{j-1}}$. For $m \ne i$ put $\varphi_m := \mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(F)$ and $v_m := \theta'_m\mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(F) + \mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(G_a)$, the same for all $a$. Choose $v_i$ outside the fewer than $q$ values $\mathrm{ev}^{(j-1)}_{z_i}(U)$, $U \in \mathrm{List}(O; A - 1, d)$: no tuple of that list satisfies the carried claims, so the hypothesis of Lemma F(2) holds vacuously. Put $e_a := v_i + \theta'_i\mathrm{ev}_{z_i}(F) + g_a$, nonzero and pairwise distinct, and $\varphi_i := \mathrm{ev}_{z_i}(F) + \phi$ with any $\phi \ne 0$. For every $c$, $P_{a,c} := G_a + a_cF$ is in $\mathrm{List}(O_c; A, d)$. At $m \ne i$, (p) holds for $P_{a,c}$ identically in $c$. At $m = i$ the two sides of (p) differ by $e_a + (\theta'_i + \alpha + \beta\zeta_i)\phi$, which vanishes exactly on the line $\alpha + \beta\zeta_i = \theta'_i + e_a/\phi$. The $k$ lines are parallel and distinct. $\square$

The same holds with a nonzero weight that every $U_a$ violates: with $\delta_F = 0$, $\delta_0 = \delta_1 = \delta \ne 0$ and, for the pair $a$ with line $\alpha + \beta\zeta_i = \gamma_a$, $\delta^a_G = \gamma_a\delta/\zeta_i$ and running-claim error $(1 + \gamma_a)\delta/\zeta_i$, the residual of note 14 (the difference of the two sides of (w) for $G_a + a_cF$) vanishes identically on the line of pair $a$; these are two prescribed values of $\langle W_0, \cdot\rangle$ and $\langle W_1, \cdot\rangle$ per pair (for $\zeta_i \ne 0$ and $\gamma_a \ne 1$), which a weight realises as soon as the $G_a$ are linearly independent. **[checked]** by brute force over all challenges at $q = 64$ for $k = 3, 4, 5$ and $s = 2, 3$: $kq$ winning challenges with the zero weight and with a solved weight that every pair violates (`16_checks.py lines`).

**Corollary 3.2 (the floor $s(r/q)^{s-1}$). [proved]** Let $R$ be a scalar polynomial with $r$ distinct roots in $E$, and suppose some $h$-lane word $w$ has $k$ tuples $H_1, \dots, H_k$ of degree at most $d - r$ within agreement $A$ (that is, $\Lambda_{d - r}(A) \ge k$). Let $O_0 := F$ and $O_1 := G + R\,w$ for any $F, G$ of degrees $d - 1$, $d$. Then every pair $(\mathrm{Exc}, K_0)$ valid for $O$ with $K_0 \le kq$ has
$$\Pr[\mathbf z \in \mathrm{Exc}] \ \ge\ s\Bigl(\frac rq\Bigr)^{s-1}\Bigl(1 - \frac rq - \binom k2\epsilon\Bigr).$$
*Proof.* $U_a := (F, G + RH_a)$ agrees with $O$ where $H_a$ agrees with $w$. If $\zeta_m$ is a root of $R$ then $\mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(RH_a) = R(\zeta_m)\,\mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(H_a) = 0$ for every $a$ and every $\theta_m$. So Proposition 3.1 applies when exactly $s - 1$ of the $\zeta_m$ are roots of $R$ and the $k$ values $\mathrm{ev}_{z_i}(RH_a)$ at the remaining point are distinct. $\square$

When does the family exist? **[proved]** for $k \le (n - d)/(A - d)$, by the construction behind the generalized Singleton bound: let $d' := \max(0, \lceil(kA - n)/(k - 1)\rceil) \le d$, let $R$ have degree $d$ with $d'$ roots in $D$ and $d - d'$ in $E \setminus D$, take $H_a$ distinct constants, and let $O_1 := G + H_aR$ on the $a$-th of $k$ equal parts of the columns that are not roots. Each member agrees with $O_1$ on the $d'$ roots and on its part, at least $d' + (n - d')/k \ge A$ columns (up to rounding). All $d$ roots are common zeros: $r = d$ for uniform points, and $r = d - d'$ if the points avoid $D$. For $k = 3$ this covers every $A \le (n + 2d)/3$, with $d - d' = d - (3A - n)/2$ (at rate 1/2 and $A = 0.6n$: $0.2d$). For $k = K_0/q = 2^9$ it needs $A \le d + (n - d)/2^9$; above that, a list of $2^9$ members at degree $d - r$ and agreement $A$ is needed, which is exactly the kind of list whose possible existence is the reason for the DEEP fold. If such lists exist at degree $d$ but not at degree $d(1 - 2^{-7})$, the floor weakens by 7 bits a point, not more. So the floor is unconditional for small $K_0$, and for $K_0 = 2^9q$ it is conditional on large lists at slightly lower degree (or, with uniform points, on large lists spread over the columns, Proposition 3.3); a proof that does not know the structure of the lists cannot do better.

**Proposition 3.3 (points in $D$: the floor $s(n/q)^{s-1}$). [proved]** If $\zeta_m \in D$, every $U \in \mathrm{List}(O; A, d)$ that agrees with $O$ at the column $\zeta_m$ has $\mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(U) = \mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(O(\zeta_m))$, the value of the committed column. So if $s - 1$ points have $\zeta_m \in D$ and $k$ members with a common first half agree with $O$ at these $s - 1$ columns, Proposition 3.1 applies. For the flats word of note 00 section 1 at rate 1/4 ($O_0 = F$, $O_1 = G + x^{2^{\kappa+1}-1}$, the $n - 1$ members $(F, G + Q_V)$ at agreement $n/2 - 1$), a column $x \ne 0$ lies in $n/2 - 1$ of the flats and $s - 1$ independent columns in about $n/2^{s-1}$: every pair valid for it with $K_0 \le 2^{-(s-1)}nq$ has $\Pr[\mathrm{Exc}] \ge s\,(n/q)^{s-1}(1 - o(1))$ when the points are uniform in $E$. **[checked]** on the toy flats word (section 7).

**Remark 3.4 (a natural family: the high parts). [proved]** The members of a list that share their low part and differ in the high part are a family of Corollary 3.2 with $R = \widehat W_\kappa$ (the subspace polynomial of the message space, $r = 2^\kappa$, roots in $D$) and $H_a$ of degree below $\ell$, and they satisfy every weighted claim together, since a weight sees only the low part: the prover message that is honest for one is honest for all. A word has up to $\lfloor(n - 2^\kappa)/(A - 2^\kappa)\rfloor$ of them (agreement on the message subspace and on disjoint sets elsewhere), 5 at rate 1/2 and $A = 0.6n$. So with $K_0 \le 5q$ and uniform points the floor $s(2^\kappa/q)^{s-1}$ is met by a prover who is dishonest only in one answer. With $K_0 = 2^9q$ it is not.

**Remark 3.5 (trivial floors). [proved]** (a) Each element is consistent with $q^s$ of the $q^{2s}$ answer vectors, and two elements over one challenge share an answer vector only if they agree at every point, so (when that does not happen) the average block has $\min(q^2, |\mathcal T|/q^s)$ challenges: a statement with the zero weight needs $|\mathcal T| < K_0q^s$. With lists of $2^{300}$ at every challenge this forces $s \ge 4$; with lists up to $2^{100}$ it forces nothing beyond $s \ge 2$. (b) With one lane per half (the last round of a level), two points with the same $\zeta$ are one point: probability $\binom s2/q$ of having $s - 1$ effective points.

*What this refutes and what it does not.* Note 10, section 2.3, lists the exceptional events it could build: all $s$ points in $D$, and two members sharing all $s$ answers, and concludes $(n/q)^s + \binom\Lambda2\epsilon^s$. The events above need only $s - 1$ points. Note 10 saw the mechanism (its strategy S2: a candidate that misses the answers at one point "can be given the line of that point") and dismissed it because one round message aligns the running claim for one such candidate only. That is true for a weight in general position, and false for the weights Lemma F must cover: the zero weight, a weight that does not separate the members, or the solved weight above. In the protocol the weight is a random batch and the members' values are not at the prover's disposal, so I do not claim an attack: when the members violate the running claim, a second line is aligned with probability about $q^{-2}$; when they satisfy it, they are valid openings and the prover is honest for one of them after the round. But the state function of notes 11 and 14 calls the state doomed in both cases, so its fold transition has error $k/q$ there, and the proof must put these point tuples in the exceptional set. A state function that avoids this would have to treat a member that satisfies the running claim and all but one point claim as not doomed, and then the batching message pays the list factor that the unbatched points were meant to remove. Either way the separation that matters is by every $s - 1$ of the points, as Lemma 0 of note 07 already has it.

## 4. A barrier: abstract list systems

An *abstract list system* is a set $\mathcal T \subseteq E^2 \times E[X]_{\le d}$ with at most $\Lambda$ elements over each challenge. Classes, blocks, $Q$, Lemma L1, Lemma L2(a), (b) and Lemma F(1) of note 14 make sense and hold for it verbatim; the only place where Lemma F uses the word is L2(c), which bounds the polynomial classes that are not near pairs.

**Proposition 4.1 (two points never suffice abstractly). [proved]** Let $2^j \le d + 1$ with $j \mid 128$, and $\Lambda \ge 1$. Take polynomials $G, F_1, F_2$ of degree at most $d$ with $F_2 + XF_1 = X^{2^j}$, and for each challenge a set $\mathcal X_c \subseteq E$ of $\Lambda$ constants. The system $\mathcal T := \{(c, G + \alpha F_1 + \beta F_2 + x) : x \in \mathcal X_c\}$ has the following properties.

- (a) For every pair of points with $\zeta_1 \ne \zeta_2$ there is an answer vector whose block has at least $\Lambda q$ elements with pairwise distinct challenges, in which every class lies on a line of challenges. So for $\Lambda \ge 3$ no statement "fewer than $2.72q$ consistent challenges unless a class has more than $2q$" holds at any pair of points, and none with $K_0 \le \Lambda q$ in general.
- (b) For $s \ge 3$ the same holds at every $\mathbf z$ with $\zeta_m \in \zeta_1 + (\zeta_1 + \zeta_2)\,\mathrm{GF}(2^j)$ for all $m$: probability $(2^j/q)^{s-2}$ given distinct $\zeta_1, \zeta_2$.

*Proof.* Let $u_1 := (\zeta_1^{2^j} + \zeta_2^{2^j})/(\zeta_1 + \zeta_2) = (\zeta_1 + \zeta_2)^{2^j - 1}$ and $u_2 := \zeta_1^{2^j} + u_1\zeta_1$. The polynomial $X^{2^j} + u_1X + u_2$ is affine over $\mathrm{GF}(2^j)$ and vanishes at $\zeta_1$; its linear part $X^{2^j} + u_1 X$ vanishes on $(\zeta_1 + \zeta_2)\mathrm{GF}(2^j)$, so its roots are exactly the $2^j$ points of $\zeta_1 + (\zeta_1 + \zeta_2)\mathrm{GF}(2^j)$. For $v_0 \in E$ let $y^F_m := F_1(\zeta_m) + u_1$ and $y^G_m := G(\zeta_m) + v_0$ at each such root $\zeta_m$. The element $(c, G + \alpha F_1 + \beta F_2 + x)$ satisfies $P(\zeta_m) = y^G_m + (\alpha + \beta\zeta_m)y^F_m$ if and only if $x = v_0 + u_1\alpha + \beta(\zeta_mu_1 + \zeta_mF_1(\zeta_m) + F_2(\zeta_m)) = v_0 + u_1\alpha + u_2\beta$, using $\zeta_m^{2^j} = u_1\zeta_m + u_2$. So the block of this answer vector is $\{(c, x) : x \in \mathcal X_c,\ x = v_0 + u_1\alpha + u_2\beta\}$, with one element at most per challenge. Summing over $v_0$ gives $\Lambda q^2$ elements, so some $v_0$ has at least $\Lambda q$. The block lies in the plane $c \mapsto (G + v_0) + \alpha(F_1 + u_1) + \beta(F_2 + u_2)$, and for three of its elements $Q = \Delta\cdot(X^{2^j} + u_1X + u_2)$ with $\Delta$ the determinant of the three challenges (Cramer's rule for $F_1 + u_1$ and $F_2 + u_2$): a triple is dependent only if its challenges are collinear. $\square$

For abstract systems the truth is between $(2^j/q)^{s-2}$ (for $\Lambda \ge K_0/q$) and the $\Lambda^3q^3\epsilon^s$ of Lemma F: the triple count is within two or three points of optimal for what it uses (it needs 5 to 6 fresh points at level 0 where the abstract floor allows 3), and with $j = 16$ three points give at best $2^{-112}$. **Lemma 4.2 (the code excludes it). [proved]** For a real $\mathcal T$ and a plane $\Pi = \{(c, G + \alpha F_1 + \beta F_2)\}$ with $F_2 \ne XF_1$: $|\mathcal T \cap \Pi| \le q\,\lfloor(n - d - 1)/(A - d - 1)\rfloor + \binom n2 + n$.

*Proof.* On $\Pi$ the agreement at a column $x$ is an affine condition on $c$: all of $E^2$, a line, a point or nothing. It is all of $E^2$ only if $G = O_1$, $F_1 = O_0$, $F_2 = xO_0$ at $x$, and then $x$ is a root of the nonzero $F_2 + XF_1$: at most $d + 1$ such columns, a set $Z_\Pi$. An element of $\mathcal T \cap \Pi$ has at least $A - |Z_\Pi|$ agreement columns outside $Z_\Pi$. If two of them have different solution sets, the challenge is determined by a pair of columns, or by one column whose solution set is a point: at most $\binom n2 + n$ elements. Otherwise all have the same line, a line carried by at least $A - |Z_\Pi|$ columns; different such lines use disjoint sets of columns, so there are at most $(n - |Z_\Pi|)/(A - |Z_\Pi|)$ of them. $\square$

So the abstract counterexample needs a twisted plane with $\Lambda q$ elements, and a real one has at most $5q + \binom n2 + n$ at rate 1/2 and $A = 0.6n$. Lemma 2.2 is the same statement in every dimension. This is the information a sharper proof has to use, and section 6 says why I could not.

## 5. Theorem B

**Theorem B (the explained part exactly, the unexplained part by triples). [proved]** In the setting of Lemma F of note 14, let $M_u := \max(q, \binom n2)$, let $K_u > M_u$ be an integer, let $N_{\mathrm{un}}(\mathbf z)$ be the number of ordered triples of elements of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}$ with pairwise different challenges, $Q \ne 0$ and $\mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_m}(Q) = 0$ for all $m$, and let $\mathrm{Exc}^\ast_j(O)$ be the set of $\mathbf z$ with at least one of:

- (X1) $\zeta_m = \zeta_{m'}$ for some $m \ne m'$;
- (X2) two distinct near pairs $(F, G), (F', G') \in \mathcal N$ and an index $i$ with $\mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_m}(F) = \mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_m}(F')$ and $\mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_m}(G) = \mathrm{ev}^{(j)}_{z_m}(G')$ for all $m \ne i$;
- (X3) $N_{\mathrm{un}}(\mathbf z) \ge K_u(K_u - 1)(K_u - M_u)$.

(1) For independent uniform points,
$$\Pr[\mathbf z \in \mathrm{Exc}^\ast_j(O)] \ \le\ \frac{\binom s2}{q} + s\binom{N_P}{2}\epsilon^{s-1} + \frac{|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|^3\,\epsilon^s}{K_u(K_u-1)(K_u-M_u)} .$$
(2) Let $\mathbf z \notin \mathrm{Exc}^\ast_j(O)$ and let $(W, \sigma)$, $v$, $B$ satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma F(2). Then for every prover message, fewer than
$$K^\ast := \max\bigl(2q, \tbinom n2, sq\bigr) + N_P + K_u$$
challenges admit a $P \in \mathrm{List}(O_c; A, d)$ with (p), (w), (b).

*Proof.* (1) (X1) is a union over pairs of points. For (X2) and fixed $U \ne U'$, $i$: one of $F + F'$, $G + G'$ is a nonzero tuple of degree at most $d$, so by (E4) of note 14 and independence the $s - 1$ points agree with probability at most $\epsilon^{s-1}$; union over $i$ and the pairs. For (X3): each triple counted has probability at most $\epsilon^s$ as in Lemma F(1), so $\mathbb E[N_{\mathrm{un}}] \le |\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|^3\epsilon^s$; Markov.

(2) Choose one $P_c$ for each winning challenge: a set $\mathcal W$ with pairwise different challenges. Split it into $\mathcal W_{\mathrm{un}} := \mathcal W \cap \mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}$ and the explained elements, each assigned to one $U \in \mathcal N$ with $(c, P_c) \in \mathcal C(U)$.

*Unexplained elements.* A class that contains two elements of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}$ is not the class of a near pair, so by L2 it has at most $q$ elements (cases (a), (b)) or at most $\binom n2$ (case (c) with $|Z| \le A - 2$): at most $M_u$. If $|\mathcal W_{\mathrm{un}}| \ge K_u$, then by L1(d), (b), (c) at least $K_u(K_u-1)(K_u-M_u)$ ordered triples of $\mathcal W_{\mathrm{un}}$ are counted by $N_{\mathrm{un}}(\mathbf z)$, against (X3). So $|\mathcal W_{\mathrm{un}}| < K_u$.

*Explained elements.* For $U = (F, G) \in \mathcal N$ and each $m$ let $\delta_m(U) := (\mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(F) + y^F_m,\ \mathrm{ev}_{z_m}(G) + y^G_m)$ and $t(U) := \#\{m : \delta_m(U) \ne 0\}$. An element $(c, G + a_cF)$ satisfies (p) at $m$ if and only if $\delta^G_m + (\alpha + \beta\zeta_m)\delta^F_m = 0$.
- $t(U) = 0$: $U$ satisfies the carried claims. As in the proof of Lemma F, if more than $\binom n2$ elements of $\mathcal W$ are assigned to $U$ then (b) gives $B(U)$, the hypothesis gives $\langle W, U\rangle \ne \sigma$, and (w) holds on at most $2q$ challenges for the folds of $U$. So at most $\max(2q, \binom n2)$.
- $t(U) = 1$: the elements lie on one line of $E^2$: at most $q$.
- $t(U) \ge 2$: two lines of slopes $\zeta_m \ne \zeta_{m'}$ (not (X1)), or an empty set: at most one element.

Two distinct near pairs with $t \le 1$ and the same exceptional index (or one of them with $t = 0$) agree with the answers, hence with each other, at $s - 1$ points with a common omitted index, against (X2). So either one near pair has $t = 0$ and no other has $t \le 1$, or none has $t = 0$ and at most one has $t = 1$ for each index $i$: the near pairs with $t \le 1$ hold at most $\max(2q, \binom n2, sq)$ elements of $\mathcal W$. The others hold at most one each: at most $N_P$. Total $|\mathcal W| < K^\ast$. $\square$

Remarks.

- **Theorem B contains Lemma F, for lists below $K_0$.** With $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le \Lambda q^2$ and $K_u := K_0 - sq - L$ the third term is that of Lemma F up to the factor $(K_0/K_u)^3$ (0.1 bit at $s = 12$), and the first two are far below it. For $L \ge K_0$ the count $N_P$ of single elements is useless and Lemma F stays the statement to use.
- **It drops into Theorem D' of note 14 unchanged.** The proof of Theorem D' uses Lemma F only through its two parts and through Lemma T, which needs a bound on $\Pr[\mathrm{Exc}_j(O')]$ uniform in the word. (X1) does not depend on the word and is charged once. So the point message of level $i$ has error $\binom{L_i}{2}\epsilon_i^{s_i} + \binom{s_i}{2}/q + \bigl[s_i\binom{L_i}2\epsilon_i^{s_i-1} + 2^{3u}q^3\epsilon_i^{s_i}/(K_u(K_u-1)(K_u-q))\bigr]\sum_{j<\ell_i}\tau^{-j}$ for any $2^u \ge |\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|/q$ valid for all words of the level's rounds, and the fold message $(K^\ast - 1)/q^2 + \tau$. **[proved]** given Theorem D' of note 14, whose transitions I read and did not re-derive.
- **The second term is the floor.** Corollary 3.2 gives $s(r/q)^{s-1}$ from below and (X2) gives $s\binom{N_P}2\epsilon^{s-1}$ from above. I do not know whether the factor $\binom{N_P}{2}$ is attained: it needs about $N_P^2$ families of $K_0/q$ members with different common factors, and one list of $N_P$ members has room for $N_P q/K_0$ disjoint ones.
- **Full lines can be separated too.** If the full lines are counted apart ($N_L$ of them), two of them, or one and a near pair, are in one block only if their rational pairs agree at all $s$ points: probability $(\binom{N_L}{2} + N_LN_P)((d + 2^\ell + 1)/q)^s$, each other full line gives one element, and the triple count runs over the sporadic elements only with $M = \binom n2$. A full line that agrees with the answers at $s - 1$ points gives one element, not a line (toy check: section 7, pole word), so full lines do not have the $\epsilon^{s-1}$ floor. This variant is better than the lumped one only when $N_L^3 > 2^{27}s\binom{N_P}{2}/\epsilon$, that is for more than about $2^{70}$ full lines at $L = 2^{40}$, and it is not needed for any count below.
- **Johnson regime.** `thm:mca-affine` says $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le \Lambda\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{aff}}\,q^2$. Where today's analysis applies ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{aff}} \le 2^{-118}$), the unexplained winners are fewer than $K_u$ outright and (X3) is not needed: the point count is that of the first two terms. This uses the MCA theorem that note 14 was glad to drop, so it is a trade, not a gain.

## 6. The unexplained term

What Theorem B leaves is (X3): a block of $K_u \approx 2^9q$ unexplained elements, no $2^{-9}$ fraction of them in one class. Four equivalent or partial views, all **[proved]** unless marked.

- **Quotient view.** Let $h = 1$, $\zeta_m \notin D$ distinct, $Z := \prod_m(X + \zeta_m)$, and $I_F, I_G$ the polynomials of degree below $s$ through the answers. Then $(c, P)$ is in the block of $m$ if and only if $P = I_G + a_cI_F + Z\,P'$ with $(c, P')$ in the $\mathcal T$ of the word pair $((O_0 + I_F)/Z, (O_1 + I_G)/Z)$ at degree $d - s$ and agreement $A$ (the difference $P + I_G + a_cI_F$ has degree at most $d$ and vanishes at every $\zeta_m$). Dependent triples correspond ($Q' = Q/Z$). The near pairs of the quotient are the near pairs of $O$ that match the answers; a near pair of $O$ that misses the answers at one point becomes a full line of the quotient with its pole at that point, which is Proposition 3.1 seen from the quotient. So Lemma F(2) at $\mathbf z$ says: every DEEP quotient of the word, for every choice of the $2s$ interpolation values, has fewer than $K_0$ fold challenges with an unexplained close codeword. That is mutual correlated agreement for the quotients with error $K_0/q^2 \approx 2^{-119}$, at the same threshold, beyond Johnson, where nothing is known for any word. The random points must supply it.
- **Lift.** If some pair $(h_0, h_1)$ at degree $d - r$ has an unexplained block of $K_u$ elements (at no points at all: $K_u$ fold challenges with close codewords, no large class), then $O := R\cdot(h_0, h_1)$, with the roots of $R$ away from the poles of the classes involved, has (X3) at every $\mathbf z$ made of roots of $R$ (answers zero): probability $(r/q)^s$. This is the only source of (X3) that I know, it needs all $s$ points, and every instance known to the swarm is a family of pole lines (note 10, section 5.4). An unexplained event of probability $N\epsilon^s$ built this way needs about $N$ different $R$, each with its own $K_u$ elements: at most $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|/K_u$ disjoint ones. This is the heuristic for $\Pr[\text{X3}] \lesssim (|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|/K_u)\,\epsilon^s$, a first power where Theorem B has a cube. **[conjectured]**; I could not prove it because an element can serve many overlapping structures, and bounding that overlap is again a statement about common roots of many $Q$.
- **Dimension.** By Lemma 2.2 an unexplained block off full lines spans a section of dimension at least 5 ($n = 2^{28}$) to 8 ($n = 2^{20}$), and by Lemma 4.2 it is not planar when $K_u$ exceeds that lemma's bound. The whole section is then consistent with the answers: its plane part has $F_2 + XF_1$ vanishing at every $\zeta_m$, and its vertical space, of dimension at least 3, consists of polynomials vanishing at every $\zeta_m$. For one fixed section this has probability at most $\epsilon^s$, the same as for one triple, so the dimension does not shorten the union bound by itself; it would if one could count the sections that carry $K_u$ elements, and Lemma 2.2 bounds the elements per section, not the sections.
- **What suffices.** $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le 2^uq$ with $u \le 75$ (no nesting loss) or $u \le 77$ to $105$ depending on the level (points reused) makes (X3) cheaper than (X2) at $L = 2^{40}$ (`16_params.py`, last table). For the pole pairs of note 00 the unexplained mass is the number of pole lines, at most the list size at degree $d + 1$.

**Hypothesis $\mathrm U(u)$. [conjectured] for $u = 64$ at lists up to $2^{40}$.** For every word of every round of the level, $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le 2^u q$: the unexplained close codewords of all folds fill at most $2^u$ lines of challenges. Evidence: every construction in notes 00 to 13 (pole pairs over flats, over the $5n/8$ word, over padded words) has $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}| \le \Lambda_{d+1}(A)\,q + \binom n2\Lambda$, the number of pole lines being the list size at degree $d + 1$; Lemma 2.1 (nothing between $n$ and $q$ on a line); toy sizes (section 7), where no unexplained block above two merged lines appears at generic points. Against: nothing I know; but the number of poles a word can have (the number of $x_0$ for which $(X + x_0)O_0$ is close to a polynomial of degree $d$ while $O_0$ is not close to one of degree $d - 1$) is a proximity-gap count for the pencil $XO_0 + x_0O_0$ beyond Johnson, on which I have no bound, so I do not attach a constant to this with confidence. The statement is a mutual correlated agreement bound with error $2^{u - 128}$ where today's analysis needs $2^{-118}$ and the capacity conjectures ask for $\mathrm{poly}(n)/q$: as open as they are, but about 60 bits weaker.

## 9. What I tried that does not work

- **Fix a pair of candidates and union over pairs (idea 3 of the brief). [proved] useless.** A pair fixes the rational answers, and the further elements consistent with them number at most $|\mathcal T|\epsilon^s$ in expectation, so one pair is bad with probability $|\mathcal T|\epsilon^s/(K_0 - M)$; the union over $|\mathcal T|^2$ pairs is $q^2$ times worse than triples, and dividing by the $K_0^2$ pairs of a block gives the triple bound back exactly. A pair statistic cannot exist: any two elements are consistent with some answer vector.
- **Incidences or a second moment over answer vectors (idea 2). [proved] useless.** For every $\mathbf z$, two elements whose challenges differ at every $a_c(\zeta_m)$ lie in exactly one block, so $\sum_m|\mathcal W(m)|^2 = (1 + o(1))|\mathcal T|^2$ whatever the system: the second moment is a constant. The third moment is $N(\mathbf z)$, and Proposition 4.1 shows it cannot tell a system with blocks of $\Lambda q$ from one with blocks of $\Lambda$. Moments of order $k \ge 4$ can, but their expectation is a sum over $k$-tuples of (number of common roots of $k - 2$ polynomials$/q)^s$, and bounding it is the classification problem.
- **A rank argument.** "At most two independent candidates share an answer at one point" is false: at one point every answer is consistent with $|\mathcal T|/q$ elements in general position. What is true is Lemma 2.2, and it bounds elements per section.
- **Two batches of points with the answers in between.** If the answers at the first $s'$ points are sent before the remaining points are drawn, the block is fixed after the first batch and the triple count can be run inside it on the second: the first batch bounds the block size $B$ with error $|\mathcal T|^3\epsilon^{s'}/B^3$, the second costs $B^3\epsilon^{s - s'}/K_0^3$, and the best $B$ gives twice the square root of the one-shot bound. Worse, and one more verifier message.
- **Classification (idea 1).** Done for the explained part (Theorem B: the exceptional points are common zeros, at $s - 1$ points, of one of $\binom{N_P}{2}$ explicit differences) and for full lines (remark after Theorem B). For sporadic elements the classification "every exceptional tuple is a tuple of common roots of one of $N$ polynomials" is true with $N$ the number of maximal sections that carry $K_u$ sporadic elements and are consistent at some point tuple, and I have no bound on $N$ below the triple count.
- **A counterexample with a factor of $q$ (idea 4).** A word whose $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}$ is covered by about $\Lambda q/2^9$ disjoint blocks of $K_u$ elements with different common factors would give $\Pr[\text{X3}] \approx \Lambda q\,\epsilon^s/2^9$. Through pole pairs it needs a degree-$(d+1)$ list of $\Lambda q$ members grouped in families with different common factors; Lemma 2.2 does not forbid it and I could not build it. The true exponent of $q$ in the unexplained term (as a multiple of $\epsilon^s$) is between 0 and 3 for real words, and between 2, up to powers of $d$, and 3 for abstract systems (Proposition 4.1).

  - `./sharp16 m=6 N=4 d=2 A=5 word=kpairs k=3 r=2 s=2 zmode=all` (two minutes), the same with `word=random` and `word=polek` (under a minute each);
  - `./sharp16 m=6 N=4 d=2 A=5 word=kpairs k=3 r=2 s=3 zmode=roots` (a minute and a half) and `... s=3 zmode=uniform samples=3000` (two minutes);
  - `./sharp16 m=6 N=4 d=3 A=7 word=flatlist s=2 zmode=uniform samples=200 kmin=175` (two minutes);
  - `./sharp16 m=6 N=4 d=2 A=5 word=random s=3 zmode=generic samples=300` and the same with `word=polek k=3 r=2`: no exceptional triple among 300 generic ones, largest unexplained block 128 for the pole word (two merged lines).
  - Options: `m` (field degree 5 to 8), `N` ($\log_2 n$, at most 5), `d`, `A`, `word` (`random`, `kpairs`, `polek`, `flatlist`), `k`, `r`, `s` (1 to 3), `zmode` (`all`, `uniform`, `generic`, `roots`), `samples`, `k0`, `kmin` (blocks below it are not examined), `seed`, `threads`. Keep $\binom nA q^{d+1-A}$ near 1, or the lists of random folds swamp the run. The first lines of the output give $|\mathcal T|$, the number of near pairs and $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{un}}|$.
- `python3 doc/whir_soundness_gap_notes/scripts/16_checks.py` (two seconds): the lines with a claim, the barrier, the roots.
- `python3 doc/whir_soundness_gap_notes/scripts/16_params.py [bits]` (instant): section 8, for a point budget of $2^{-\mathrm{bits}}$ (default 110).
