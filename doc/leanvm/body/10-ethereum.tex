\section{Ethereum use cases}\label{sec:ethereum}

An aggregate proof certifies signature claims and the correct encoding of blob data. One guest program establishes these claims by checking raw signatures, checking a blob matrix, or recursively verifying child proofs of the same program.

\subsection{Recursive aggregation}\label{sec:recursion}\label{sec:xmss-agg}\label{sec:sphincs-agg}

A signature claim identifies what was signed and by whom. For both leanXMSS, two layers of XMSS~\cite{rfc8391}, and SPHINCS+~\cite{sphincs}, it is a $(\text{public key},\text{message})$ pair. Each leanXMSS signature carries the leaf index it was made at, so its Merkle path is its own, and the guest derives that signature's addresses from the index's bits; the claim does not record it.

A node may publish any subset of the signature claims supported by its raw signatures and verified children. The default is their sorted, deduplicated union. The essential condition is coverage: every published claim must be justified, either directly at this node or recursively through a child.

The DA claim is at most one LeanDA root $\daroot$, paired with the challenge $\dachal$ of its membership test (\S\ref{sec:da-membership}). A node that checks blobs publishes their root; otherwise it may pass on one child's root, or none.

\subsubsection{The public input}\label{sec:rec-stmt}

Digests bind the two variable-length claim lists into a fixed-size statement. The VM's public input is the hash (Keccak-256, \S\ref{sec:prog-hashing}) of the following fields, in order:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rl@{}}
\hline
$32$ bytes & Fiat-Shamir IV\\
$32$ bytes & leanXMSS claim list digest\\
$32$ bytes & leanSPHINCS claim list digest\\
$32$ bytes & DA digest $H(\daroot,\dachal)$ (zeros if none)\\
$(\kbc+5)\times16$ bytes & deferred bytecode point and value\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The deferred claim lets recursion postpone an expensive polynomial evaluation until the final verification (\S\ref{sec:deferred-claims}).

Each list digest is the hash of that scheme's sorted claims, $64$ bytes per $(\text{public key},\text{message})$ pair (\S\ref{sec:prog-hashing}). Every field above has a fixed width, so the preimage parses one way and the statement needs no length fields.

\subsubsection{Checking coverage}

Every published claim needs support from a direct check or a recursively verified child. The guest builds a table with one region for leanXMSS and one for SPHINCS+. Each region starts with the claims the parent publishes, followed by extra slots for duplicates and omitted claims.

Each occurrence of a supporting claim needs its own slot. If two children prove the same published claim, one occurrence uses its published slot and the other uses an extra slot. Every occurrence of a claim the parent omits uses an extra slot. These claims are still verified; only the published prefixes enter the parent's list digests.

The prover supplies the extra claims and assigns each supporting occurrence to a slot. The guest checks that they match, confines the assignment to the correct region, and writes a distinct counter value there. Write-once memory prevents slot reuse. Requiring as many writes as slots ensures that every slot is covered, including every published claim.

\subsubsection{Deferred evaluation claims}\label{sec:deferred-claims}

Verifying a child requires one evaluation of a fixed polynomial: the bytecode multilinear, in $\kbc+4$ variables (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}). Computing it inside the guest would be very expensive. Instead, the guest carries one evaluation claim in its statement, a point and a value. This expensive computation is moved `outside of the SNARK': only the root's claim is checked, natively, by the final verifier. Nothing else is deferred, the Keccak argument's weights being cheap to evaluate in the guest (Annex~\ref{annex:keccak}).

A node with $n$ children combines the claim each child carries with the new claim produced while verifying it, $2n$ claims on the one polynomial. One sumcheck over its $\kbc+4$ variables reduces their random combination to a single evaluation, which is the claim the node carries. A node with no child carries the claim at the all-zeros point. The Fiat-Shamir for the batching and the sumcheck is seeded by every child's statement and both of its claims, the child count and each child's words padded with zeros to whole rates of the sponge (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}).

\subsection{Data availability: LeanDA}\label{sec:leanda}

Erasure coding makes data recoverable from a subset of its encoded symbols. Sampling checks whether those symbols can be retrieved, but does not establish that they form a valid encoding. An approach using leanVM was introduced in \href{https://frisitano.github.io/slides/presentations/leanvm-fold-in-circuit-das-2026-05-08/index.html}{Risitano's presentation} and developed in LeanDA~\cite{LeanDA} by Meng, Wagner, Kadianakis and Risitano: commit to an encoded matrix and prove every row is a codeword, then sample authenticated cells. A formal security proof can be found in \cite{LeanDASecurity}.

Our construction uses Keccak-256 and the additive Reed-Solomon encoder over $\K$ already used by the PCS, with a different commitment (\S\ref{sec:da-commit}) and codeword membership test (\S\ref{sec:da-membership}) from LeanDA's. The polynomial basis, transform, and membership lemmas are collected in Annex~\ref{app:ntt}.


\subsubsection{The commitment}\label{sec:da-commit}

A blob is a polynomial in $\K[X]$ of degree less than $\kdim$, represented by its $\kdim$ evaluations on $\subsp_{\log_2\kdim}$. Its Reed-Solomon encoding is the vector of evaluations on the larger domain $\dom=\subsp_{\log_2\blen}$, where $\blen=2\kdim$. The first $\kdim$ symbols are the original blob, so the encoding is systematic; the remaining symbols provide redundancy. Any $\kdim$ encoded evaluations recover the blob.

One commitment covers a nonempty matrix of $\nblob$ encoded blobs, one per row.

Split each encoded row into cells of $\cellsz$ symbols. The guest fixes these dimensions, matching the byte sizes of an \href{https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4844}{EIP-4844} blob and an \href{https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7594}{EIP-7594} sampling cell:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rll@{}}
\hline
$\kdim$ & $2^{14}$ symbols & $128$ KiB of payload per row\\
$\blen$ & $2^{15}$ symbols & $256$ KiB per encoded row\\
$\cellsz$ & $2^8$ symbols & $2$ KiB per cell\\
$\blen/\cellsz$ & $128$ cells & any $64$ reconstruct a row\\
$\nblob$ & $1$ to $1024$ & dynamic parameter\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Zero codewords pad the row count to the next power of two. The root binds this padded matrix.

Let $H$ denote the hash and $e_{i,j}$ the hash of cell $j$ in row $i$. Each row has a Merkle tree of its own: its leaves are $e_{i,0},\ldots,e_{i,127}$, each inner node is the hash of its two children, and its root is $\dablob{i}$. The left child of $\dablob{i}$ covers the first $\kdim/\cellsz$ cells, the original blob. The commitment $\daroot$ is the root of the Merkle tree whose leaves are the row roots of the padded matrix, each padding row having the zero codeword's root (Figure~\ref{fig:da-hashing}). A sampled cell is authenticated by its path to its row's root, then that root's path to $\daroot$.

\subsubsection{Codeword membership}\label{sec:da-membership}\label{sec:da-circuit}

A row is valid exactly when it consists of evaluations of a polynomial of degree less than $\kdim$. On our additive domain at rate $1/2$, such rows are orthogonal to every codeword, and this condition also characterizes them: the code is self-dual (Corollary~\ref{cor:rs-dual}). This gives an efficient membership test.

The challenge is one element $\dachal\in\E$, drawn from the VM's transcript (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}) with the hash of \texttt{leanDA/rs-membership/v1} as IV and $\daroot$ as public input, after a grind of $\lceil\log_2(\kdim-1)\rceil-5=9$ bits: the transcript absorbs the smallest nonce whose permutation leaves the low $9$ bits of lane $2$ zero, and $\dachal$ is that permutation's challenge. The nonce is not sent: whoever derives $\dachal$ finds it, and a prover trying roots for a bad $\dachal$ pays $2^{9}$ permutations per root. It defines $\dual$ by \eqref{eq:dual-product} and $\davec=(\dual(x))_{x\in\dom}$. The statement carries $H(\daroot,\dachal)$, $\dachal$ serialized as its two little-endian $64$-bit limbs and two zero words.

The guest recomputes $\daroot$, takes $\dachal$ as a hint, computes $\davec$ and checks
\[
  \inner{\davec}{w_i}
  =\sum_{x\in\dom}\dual(x)w_i(x)
  \;\qeq\;0
\]
for every row $w_i$. Factor $j$ of \eqref{eq:dual-product} depends only on the bits of $x$ from $j$ up, so $\davec$ is a product tree over those bits, built once for all rows.

The native verifier derives $\dachal$ from $\daroot$ outside the SNARK, as the security proof requires, and checks the proof against $H(\daroot,\dachal)$. A node passing on a child's root passes its digest unchanged.

Every valid row passes. Under a uniform challenge, a fixed invalid matrix passes with probability at most $(\kdim-1)/|\E|<2^{-114}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:rs-membership}), which the $9$-bit grind brings to $2^{-123}$ per root tried (\S\ref{sec:grinding}).
