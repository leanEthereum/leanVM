\section{End-to-end protocol}\label{sec:e2e}

\subsection{Bytecode encoding}\label{sec:e2e-bc}

The program is public, so nothing is decoded in a table: both parties decode each of the $\nprog=2^{\kbc}$ instruction words once, into the fields the rows read (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}), and encode the result as one $\K$-valued multilinear $P$ in $\kbc+4$ variables:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item lay instruction $z$ out as $16$ slots of $\K$: the class tag $\opc{j}$ in slot $2$ (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}); the flags in slot $3$, a word of selector bits naming the class's function, the finalization word for \texttt{HASH}, the accumulate and base-field bits for \texttt{EXT}; the register numbers $a_1,a_2,a_d$ in slots $4$ to $6$, $\intk{\sink}=\intk{32}$ for an instruction with no destination or with $\regs[0]$ as one; the immediate in slot $7$, sign-extended, folded with $\pc$ for \texttt{auipc}, and the link $\pc+4$ for \texttt{jal}; the successor $\intk{\tbase+4z+4}$ in slot $8$; the jump offset $\Delta$ in slot $9$, the fixed target of a branch, a \texttt{jal} or an \texttt{ecall} XOR the successor, and $0$ for an entry with none (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}); the exit selector $\exitflag$ in slot $11$, $1$ only for \texttt{ecall}; and $0$ everywhere else: slot $10$, where a division's row puts its verdict (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}), and slots $0$, $1$ and $12$ to $15$, which the separator, the address and nothing occupy in the bus tuple. A slot of the text holding no instruction the machine defines, the halt slot included, is all zeros: its tag is no table's.
\item read the resulting $2^{\kbc}\times16$ table as $P$, the low $\kbc$ variables indexing the instruction and the high four its slot, bits low first.
\end{enumerate}
So $P(z,0,1,0,0)$ is instruction $z$'s tag, $P(z,0,0,1,0)$ its first register, $P(z,0,0,0,1)$ its successor, and $P(z,0,0,0,0)=P(z,1,1,1,1)=0$.

\paragraph{Well-formedness.} The proof system is sound for any table $P$, a malformed one included: it proves that the rows read $P$ and did what its fields say. What makes $P$ a RISC-V program is checked once, by both parties, where a program enters: every entry with a tag reads two registers below $32$ and writes a cell in $1..32$, its successor is its own address plus $4$, its flags are ones its class defines, its exit selector is a bit, $1$ only for the one form of \texttt{ecall}, only a branch, a \texttt{jal} or an \texttt{ecall} has a jump offset, a \texttt{jal} reads $\regs[0]$ twice and has its successor as immediate, a \texttt{LOAD} or \texttt{LD} entry reads $\regs[0]$ as its second register, a \texttt{STORE} or \texttt{SD} entry writes the sink, an \texttt{LD} or \texttt{SD} entry has flags $0$, a \texttt{HASH} entry writes the sink and has immediate $0$, and an \texttt{EXT} entry reads its address from a register, not the sink, and has immediate $0$. The two verifiers check the same rules, and the digest below is of the decoded table, so a verifier is never handed a program it did not check. A table of $\nprog=2^{\kbc}$ slots, the padding and the halt slot included, makes the bus grind $\max(0,\kbc-21)$ bits before its challenges (\S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}); the text region caps it at $2^{26}$ slots.

\subsection{The statement}\label{sec:e2e-pi}

The \emph{statement} is a program and an output: that the program, run on RAM holding its image, halts on \texttt{exit} with $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$ holding the output (\S\ref{sec:vm}). The program is one digest, BLAKE2s of everything public and fixed: the table $P$, the entry point, the halt slot, $\kmem$, $\kadv$ and the image, each variable-length part length-framed. The digest names the Fiat-Shamir transcript's session (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}), so every challenge depends on the exact statement; the output does too, as the transcript's instance, its first message.

RAM's initial words need no claim: its seed column $\meminit$ is public, the image then zeros, and the verifier evaluates its extension at $\zeta$ itself, in time proportional to the image rather than to $\nmem$, the column being zero past the image (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}). The output and the exit are five claims on the committed final registers $\regfin$ at Boolean points, one per register, whose values both parties know and neither transmits:
\[
  \mle{\regfin}(\mathrm{bits}(17))\;\qeq\;\intk{93},\qquad \mle{\regfin}(\mathrm{bits}(10+i))\;\qeq\;\textsf{output}_i\quad(i<4),
\]
with $\mathrm{bits}(r)\in\cube{6}$ the number of the register, low bit first. The PCS opening binds them to the committed column, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem} makes that column what the run's last writes left, so the run executed \texttt{exit} with those values in place. The initial registers are the constant zero, the advice's initial words are the prover's, and nothing else about the run is public.

\subsection{Filling the tables}\label{sec:e2e-pad}

A table is proven over a power-of-two number of rows. The prover reaches one with \emph{padding rows}, whose clock is $0$. The text carries, past the program and an illegal word that keeps a run from falling into them, for each table and each of a few sizes, a block of that many no-ops of the table's class followed by a \texttt{jal} back to the block's first instruction, which no program code jumps into. A padding row is a row at one of those instructions. A clock without the live bit does not advance, so the states pushed and pulled around a block cancel among themselves, for any number of traversals: these are the closed walks of Proposition~\ref{prop:walk}, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:clock} they are the only ones. A traversal of the block of size $n$ adds $n$ rows to its table and one to \texttt{ALU}'s, so every table can be brought to its next power of two exactly, \texttt{ALU}, where every closing jump lands, being solved last.

The verifier has no notion of a padding row, and needs none: a row is inert exactly when its clock lacks the live bit, and the filler blocks are only how an honest prover comes by such rows. A prover may close such rows around any cycle of the program's own control flow instead, and they are inert all the same, by the same two facts: their state tuples cancel because their clocks do not advance, and their accesses meet nothing of the run because the order check \eqref{eq:order} keeps the live bit off their previous timestamps (Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem}). The filler blocks earn their place by making the fill exact, not by making it safe.

A padding row's accesses cost nothing either. Its access in slot $k$ pushes the timestamp $\intk{0}+\intk{k}=\intk{k}$, and the prover takes $\tsprev=\intk{k}$ and $v_{\mathrm{old}}=v_{\mathrm{new}}$, so the access pulls the very tuple it pushes and the two cancel, whatever the address and the value: the order check \eqref{eq:order} asks a row without the live bit only that its previous timestamps lack it too. A no-op reads $\regs[0]$ twice and writes the sink, a load or a store touches the byte at address $0$, and a compression's block is at address $0$: on zero registers, so the reads are of zeros, and a write rewrites what it writes, which is why a row can rewrite a cell but never update one in place (\S\ref{sec:tables}). Its circuit instances are honest instances on those zeros, and its read of the bytecode an ordinary one. None of this bears on soundness: by Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem} a tuple whose timestamp lacks the live bit meets no tuple of the run, so the padding rows constrain nothing and are constrained by nothing, which is what a padding row should be. The live bit plays the part of an \emph{is-real} selector, at the cost of no column of its own.

\subsection{Fiat Shamir instantiation}\label{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}

The transcript follows the duplex-sponge Fiat-Shamir transformation of Chiesa and Orr\`u~\cite{CO25}, in the interface of the IRTF draft~\cite{IRTF-FS} and of spongefish~\cite{spongefish}. Only the sponge differs: the one hash the machine proves is the BLAKE2s compression function, so the duplex is built from it rather than from a permutation. The native prover, the native verifier and the verifier in rows (\S\ref{rec:verifier}) implement the same transcript.

\paragraph{Interface.} A transcript has four operations. A \emph{prover message} is absorbed and written to the proof in one call, so that no message reaches the proof unbound or is bound without being sent. A \emph{public message} is absorbed and not sent. A \emph{verifier message} is squeezed. A \emph{hint} is written to the proof and never absorbed; only data a bound value authenticates may be a hint, which here means a Merkle opening against a sent root (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}).

\paragraph{Session and instance.} A transcript starts from a 32-byte session identifier, which names the protocol and the relation. It is derived from a tag $t$ as the draft prescribes: the duplex is started from the 32 bytes \texttt{irtf-cfrg-fiat-shamir/session-id}, absorbs $t$ and squeezes 32 bytes. The first message is then the \emph{instance}, absorbed by both sides. For a leanVM proof the tag is \texttt{leanvm/rv64im-proof/blake2s-duplex/gf2-192/v1} followed by the program's digest, and the instance is the four output words. For a recursion proof the tag is \texttt{leanvm-tree-11} followed by the leaves' program digest, their table heights, both arities, the statement's length, the nodes' heights and both rates; the instance is the statement's limbs, their count first. A node's reduction (\S\ref{rec:reduce}) has the tag \texttt{leanvm-tree-reduction-5} followed by its tree's session identifier, and its instance is every value its claims rest on, their count first. So every challenge depends on the protocol, the program or circuit, and the statement.

\paragraph{Codecs and the proof.} Every message has one fixed-length encoding, and the sponge absorbs exactly the bytes the proof carries. An element of $\E$ is its three coordinates, lowest first, each eight little-endian bytes; a digest is its 32 bytes; a nonce is eight little-endian bytes; a list whose length varies is its length as eight little-endian bytes, then its items. Every absorbed encoding is therefore a whole number of 64-bit words, which is how the rows absorb it. A verifier message in $\E$ is 24 squeezed bytes read the same way: the map is a bijection, so the challenge is uniform with no bias. A query index of a tree of $2^d$ leaves is $d$ bits of such a challenge, uniform for the same reason. The proof is the NARG string, the prover messages in order, then the hints, each its length as four little-endian bytes followed by its bytes. The verifier refuses a message it cannot read, a hint whose length runs past the end, and any byte left unread; a failed read poisons the transcript, so every later read fails too.

\paragraph{The duplex.} The state is a 256-bit chaining value. Every step is one BLAKE2s compression of the chaining value, a 64-byte block and a 64-bit counter, with the final flag set. The counter is the disjoint bit concatenation \texttt{role[8] || length[7] || previous[49]}, the role in the most significant bits. Absorb calls concatenate until a squeeze, and squeeze calls continue one output stream until an absorb, so splitting a call never changes the state. The last full input block is held back until it is known whether another byte follows, so chunking cannot change finality. An absorb after a squeeze binds the number of bytes actually consumed, not the rounded number generated. Empty calls do nothing. A squeeze run is limited to $2^{49}-1$ bytes, refused before any change.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{r l p{0.56\linewidth}}
\textbf{Role} & \textbf{Node} & \textbf{Block} \\
1 & seed & The session identifier, then zeros. Its chaining input is the BLAKE2s parameter IV. \\
2 & first, nonfinal absorb & The first full block of an absorb run. \\
3 & middle absorb & A later full block of the run. \\
4 & first, final absorb & The whole run, zero padded if short. \\
5 & last absorb & The run's last block, zero padded if short. \\
6 & output & The 64-bit index of the 32-byte output block, from zero. \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For an absorbed block, \texttt{length} is its meaningful bytes, 1 to 64, and \texttt{previous} is the byte count of the squeeze run before it, on a run's first block only. Both fields are zero for the other roles. Output nodes are terminal: their result is never a chaining value. A plain BLAKE2s hash, a Merkle leaf and a Merkle node set no bit of the counter's top byte, so none is ever a transcript node.

\paragraph{Proof of work.} A grinding step of $g\ge1$ bits is spongefish's. Both sides squeeze a 32-byte challenge $c$; the prover finds the smallest 64-bit nonce $n$ such that the first eight bytes of $\mathrm{BLAKE2s}(c\,\|\,n\,\|\,0^{16}\,\|\,\texttt{FS-POW-1})$, read little-endian, have their top $g$ bits zero, and sends $n$ as a prover message, which absorbs it before the next challenge. The verifier recomputes the trial and refuses a nonce short of the work. A step of zero bits draws and sends nothing. The trial is an ordinary one-block hash, so it never collides with a transcript node, and its last eight bytes are a fixed tag, which a Merkle node of the same shape matches only by chance. Counting trials is separate from claiming a soundness improvement for the grinding.

\paragraph{Published theorem and model.} Model the BLAKE2s compression interface, including adversarially chosen chaining values, blocks, counters and flags, as a random compression function with 256 output bits. The applicable result is Daemen, Mennink and Van Assche~\cite[Theorem 1, \S4.2]{DMV18}, not an ideal-permutation sponge theorem. It applies to a hashing mode that is subtree-free, radical-decodable and message-decodable (Definitions 4 to 6), and its simulator makes at most $\fsqueries$ random-oracle queries. For the mode above, whose only terminal family is the output node, it gives
\[
  \fsloss \;\le\; \min\!\left\{1,\frac{\binom{\fsqueries}{2}}{2^{256}}\right\}.
\]
Here $\fsqueries$ counts direct compression queries and all compressions induced by construction queries, across every instance and branch, honest verification and auxiliary hashing included, conservatively summing the full root-to-terminal path of every distinct terminal coordinate. The bound has a classical 128-bit birthday scale: it is about one half at $\fsqueries=2^{128}$, not a $2^{-128}$ guarantee for every query budget.

\paragraph{Why the hypotheses hold.} A template's message is the session identifier, then the absorbed bytes in order. Its frame bits encode the run structure, run lengths, prior squeeze counts and the terminal's output index; its chaining pointers fill the whole chaining-value field of every node but the seed. A complete chain has exactly one seed at one end and one terminal at the other, so any connected subtree containing both is the whole chain: the mode is subtree-free. For radical-decodability take the proper final subchains: a recognizer checks the chain's topology, a terminal at its final end, no seed, canonical padding and bounded fields, and whether the visible suffix admits a valid prefix; an absorb run has role sequence 4 or $2,3^*,5$, which a finite scan recognizes. It returns the chaining-value positions of the oldest node and never inverts the compression. A complete-chain recognizer requires the unique seed and rebuilds every meaningful byte and frame bit in linear time, the final lengths removing the padding: the mode is message-decodable. Proof-of-work nonces are ordinary absorbed bytes, so the syntactic analysis covers them.

\paragraph{From the mode to the protocol.} The theorem's ideal oracle is indexed by message and template, not by calls: chunkings and empty calls that give one template coincide. It is exactly a prefix-consistent XOF on an injective encoding of the completed history: output index $i$ selects bytes $32i$ to $32i+31$ of that history's XOF, and an absorb after a partial squeeze records the exact count consumed. A clone shares the old prefix's outputs, and distinct later histories use different ones. The 24-byte challenge codec is a bijection onto $\E$, so disjoint slices give unbiased challenges.

The verifier's schedule is a function of the session, the instance and the messages read so far: the announcement, absorbed first, fixes every later length. So the transcript is a full-transcript random oracle over canonical prefixes, and the doomed-state argument of~\cite[Definition 5.3 and Theorem 5.8]{CCHLRR18} applies to the maximal squeeze runs as challenge vectors. Let $\fsbatches$ bound the challenge-batch depth over every admissible statement, announcement and history, $\fscoords$ the coordinate requests before the one final verification, and $\fsvectors$ the distinct challenge vectors allocated, $\fsvectors\le\fsbatches(\fscoords+1)$. With $\fsbatcherr$ the largest grouped round-by-round error, the union bound adds at most $\fsvectors\fsbatcherr$ to $\fsloss$ and the binding and auxiliary losses, capped at one. In WHIR the query positions and the batching scalar after them form one batch, whose error is that of the whole stratified query set. Merkle hints stay covered by the PCS binding argument rather than becoming absorbed messages.

\paragraph{Scope of Fiat-Shamir claims.} For a matching classical canonical-ROM soundness theorem, a single-stage acceptance experiment transfers with bound $\fsromloss+\fsloss$, where $\fsromloss$ is evaluated at the simulator's actual resources and includes, or separately budgets, statement binding, authenticated hints, the proof system and any claimed grinding improvement. Indifferentiability transfers soundness only~\cite[\S2.3]{CO25}: knowledge soundness, zero knowledge, quantum security and programming or rewinding guarantees do not follow and are not claimed.

\paragraph{Why not a permutation sponge.} CO25~\cite{CO25}, SAFE~\cite{SAFE23} and its full-capacity initialization~\cite[Theorem 2]{KMM23} assume an ideal permutation, and the IRTF draft's suites are XOFs built from one. The BLAKE2s compression maps a chaining value, a block and tweaks to a shorter digest and is not invertible, so substituting it for a permutation does not meet their hypotheses; spongefish's own hash-based duplex is documented as heuristic. The interface, the session and instance binding, the codecs and the grinding step are the standard's; the sponge underneath is the compression mode above, whose theorem is the one cited. Ideal compression is itself an assumption on concrete BLAKE2s. Luykx, Mennink and Neves~\cite[Theorem 1]{LMN16} give a separate weakly ideal BLAKE-cipher bridge, with bound $\binom{\fsqueries}{2}/2^{512}+\binom{\fsqueries}{2}/2^{256}+\fsqueries/2^{128}$ for the mapped cipher-query budget, whose linear term cannot be dropped in that stronger model.

\paragraph{Compression accounting and versions.} The seed of a fixed session is a constant, which the rows take without a row. After it, an absorb run of \texttt{A} bytes followed by a squeeze run of \texttt{S} bytes costs \texttt{ceil(A/64) + ceil(S/32)} compressions; consecutive calls of one kind add none. A grinding step of $g\ge1$ bits adds its challenge's output block and one trial; its nonce joins the next absorb run. Proof envelopes are \texttt{LVMP} version 16 and \texttt{LVMT} version 14, and older formats are refused, with no legacy transcript path. The leanDA host's membership tag is \texttt{leanDA/rs-membership/v3}.

\subsection{The unrolled protocol}\label{sec:e2e-unrolled}

Each reduction below adds evaluation claims on columns to a shared \emph{claim pool}, proved by the final PCS \textsc{Opening} phase.

\paragraph{Setup.}

The Fiat-Shamir transcript starts from the program's session and the output (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}). The prover sends the eleven table log-heights $\tau_j$, the PCS rate and the final clock $\tsfinal\in\K$ (\S\ref{sec:state}); the verifier checks each $\tau_j$ against the cap $2^{32}$ and the batch floors (\S\ref{sec:tables}), and that $\tsfinal$ is a live clock at slot $0$, and derives the layout of every stack (PCS and GKR leaves) and the producer's bit count $\nbit$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}) from those sizes and the program's.

\paragraph{Commitment.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover.} Stack (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) every column: the read-write arrays' final columns and timestamps, $\regfin,\memfin,\advfin$ and their $\tsfin$, the advice's initial words $\advinit$ (\S\ref{sec:memchan}), the bytecode's multiplicity column $\mlt$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), the twenty-one packed witnesses, $\qcls{j}$ for the ten tables with a class circuit and $\qclk{j}$ for all eleven (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}, \S\ref{sec:clock}), every committed column of the eleven tables, and the register words $\regword{w}$ (\S\ref{sec:regpack}), into $q$, and send its commitment (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}).
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Receives the commitment to $q$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Bus.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} When $\kbc>21$, the prover sends a proof of work of $\kbc-21$ bits and the verifier checks it (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}, \S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}). A smaller program sends nothing here.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Samples and sends the fingerprint challenge $\alpha\in\E^{4}$ and the multiset challenge $\beta\in\E$ (\S\ref{sec:gp}).
\item \textbf{Prover.} Forms the push and pull leaf vectors (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}), the push side ending on the bytecode producer's bit blocks (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), stacked into blocks and padded with $1$s. Sends one bus root $R$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run the one batched GKR (\S\ref{sec:gkr}) over the two trees, at the depth $\mu$ of the taller. The verifier checks each side's product against $R$; one $R$ for both sides is the balance check (\S\ref{sec:gp}). The pass yields two leaf claims $\widetilde V^{s}_0(\zeta)$ at one shared $\zeta$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Decompose each leaf claim (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}). The verifier forms every public part itself: the block selectors, the constant entries, the index columns (\S\ref{sec:idxcol}) and RAM's seed column (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}). Four blocks per side belong to no table: the state boundary, plus the registers', RAM's and the advice's seed (push side) or finalization (pull side). For their committed columns the prover sends one evaluation each, entering the claim pool: on the push side the advice's initial column alone, on the pull side every final column and timestamp. Every other block belongs to a table or to a producer, and is discharged below.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Linear tables.} The ten tables with a class circuit flush only tuples linear in their columns (\S\ref{sec:tables}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover.} Sends, table by table, the value of every column of $T_j$ at $\zeta_{<\tau_j}$ but its register numbers, which the table sumcheck folds (\S\ref{sec:regpack}); they enter the claim pool, a port's routed to its packed witness (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}).
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Evaluates each such table's form $B^{s}_j$ at those values, its register numbers taken as zero, its share of side $s$ short of the numbers' part, and takes it off what the tables owe (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Table sumcheck.} One run proves what is left of the bus, \texttt{EXT}'s share, the linear tables' register numbers' part and the producer's (\S\ref{sec:air}), and \texttt{EXT}'s three identities, the only constraints a table has (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Samples and sends the batch challenge $\xi\in\E$. Its first $\nside=2$ powers weight the bus forms, one per side and shared by every table and the producer, and the next three, $\xi^{2}$ to $\xi^{4}$, \texttt{EXT}'s identities, whose sums are zero, so the target is the bus's alone. It draws no point: the batch runs at the bus point, $T_j$ tested at $\zeta_{<\tau_j}$ and a producer of $2^{\kappa}$ entries at $\zeta_{<\kappa}$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run the sumcheck over the eleven tables, a linear table's forms restricted to its register numbers, and then the bytecode's producer, $\taumax$ rounds, $\taumax$ the largest of their log heights, each joining at round $\taumax$ minus its own, against the target the verifier derived from the two leaf claims. Its summand has individual degree three and the round check pins one of the four coefficients (\S\ref{sec:prelim}), so a round costs three $\E$-elements.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} The prover sends one value per column of \texttt{EXT} and per register number of a linear table, but for a register number its bits' values (\S\ref{sec:regpack}), and each producer's bit values $\widetilde{\mbit{k}}(\fc_{<\kappa})$, $k<\nbit$; the verifier computes each producer's public columns $\widetilde{\pubp{k}}(\fc_{<\kappa})$ itself (\S\ref{sec:lookup}). It rebuilds each $B^{s}_j$ and \texttt{EXT}'s identities from them and checks that they reproduce the sumcheck's final value; \texttt{EXT}'s column values then enter the claim pool at $\fc_{<\tau_j}$. A circuit's ports are columns of its table but live in $\qcls{j}$ or $\qclk{j}$, so their claims route there (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}); a producer's bits are slices of its multiplicity column, and a table's register numbers' bits slices of the register word that packs them, so their claims go to ring switching (below).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The exit.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Pool the five claims on $\regfin$ at the Boolean points naming $\regs[17]$ and $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$, with the values $\intk{93}$ and the output; the prover sends nothing (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Flock validity.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run Flock's reduction (Annex~\ref{annex:flock}) once over the twenty-one circuits, batched under shared challenges (\S\ref{flock:batch}): every class circuit in table order, over $\qcls{j}$, \texttt{EXT} having none, then every clock circuit in table order, over $\qclk{j}$, each with $2^{\tau_j}$ instances. One zerocheck, its round-one message shared by all twenty-one, and one lincheck leave one evaluation claim on each packed witness, and one claim on each circuit's matrix form, which the verifier settles by one walk of the circuit once the proof is read.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Sends $\rsgamma\in\E$, drawn after every ring-switched claim is bound. The claims are the twenty-one witnesses', then the bytecode's multiplicity column's, its suffix point being its $\fc_{<\kappa}$ and its $64$ slices the $\nbit$ bit values it sent, then zeros, which are not transmitted (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), then each register word's, its suffix point being its tables' shared $\fc_{<\tau_j}$ and its slices the bit values sent for its tables, in its order, then zeros (\S\ref{sec:regpack}); claim $j$, from zero, takes the scale $\rsgamma^{\,j}$, and both sides form the family's slices $\sfam{i}=\sum_j\rsgamma^{\,j}s_{j,i}$ (\S\ref{rs:family}). The prover sends nothing.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} The ring switching of \S\ref{sec:ringswitch}, its map drawn after $\rsgamma$, reduces the family to one weighted-sum claim on the stack, target $\sum_ix^i\Phi(\sfam{i})$, with an MLE-friendly $\E$-valued weight (Definition~\ref{def:ipcs}) supported on the ring-switched regions, claim $j$'s being $\Phi\bigl(\rsgamma^{\,j}\eq(r_j,\cdot)\bigr)$; it joins the pool, ahead of every column claim. The circuits of one block size leave points that are prefixes of one another, so the verifier evaluates their weights in one pass (\S\ref{rs:weight}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Opening.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Each pooled claim is one evaluation of a column at a point, its value already supplied; via the stacking selectors (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) it becomes a weighted sum $\sum_w W_j(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=c_j$ over the stacked witness. (The ring-switched claim arrives already in this form, its weight supported on the ring-switched regions.)
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Sends one batching challenge $\lambda\in\E$, drawn after every claim value is bound. Claim $j$ takes the weight $\lambda^{j-1}$, the ring-switched claim first, with $\lambda^0=1$, and the pooled column claims after: the $J$ weights are distinct powers of the one challenge (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}).
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Fold the $J$ claims into $W_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda^{j-1} W_j$ and $C_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda^{j-1} c_j$, and run the inner-product PCS opening on $\sum_w W_\lambda(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=C_\lambda$ (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}), the verifier evaluating $W_\lambda$ itself. This one opening discharges every pooled claim; there is no separate reduction sumcheck.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Accepts if every check above passed: the caps, the one bus root for both sides, every sumcheck's rounds and final value, flock's batched reduction, and the PCS opening.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Soundness ledger}\label{sec:e2e-ledger}

The commitment takes no out-of-domain sample (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}), so its root binds the prover to the list $\polyset_{\mathsf{cm}}$ of at most $L_0=\tfrac{1}{2\slack_0\sqrt{\rate_0}}$ polynomials (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}), not to one of them. Every challenge drawn after the root and before the \textsc{Opening} is a test the prover may pass with any member of that list: the bus's $\alpha$ and $\beta$ and its GKR's challenges, the table sumcheck's $\xi$ and rounds, Flock's, $\rsgamma$ and the ring-switching map. The opening holds the prover only to some member satisfying every pooled claim, so a test that a fixed polynomial passes with probability $\varepsilon$ is passed by some member with probability at most $L_0\,\varepsilon$, by a union bound over the list. Each such challenge's error is therefore $L_0$ times the one its own section states. Over every size and rate the PCS configures, $\log_2L_0\le12$, a bound the implementation pins to the derivation of the PCS parameters; so a challenge before the opening keeps $128$ bits when its own error is at most $2^{-140}$.

The algebraic round-by-round error and grinding-adjusted work targets, in bits, at their smallest over every configured size and rate follow. The grinding adjustments are work targets, not error improvements established by the ordinary-soundness adapter above.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Bus} (\S\ref{sec:gp}, \S\ref{sec:gkr}): $4N/|\E|$ for the fingerprint, $N$ the larger side's linear factors, plus at most $8(\mu+1)^2/|\E|$ for the GKR; times $L_0$, at least $128$. The bytecode producer dominates $N$ with up to $2^{\kbc}(2^{\nbit}-1)$ factors (\S\ref{sec:lookup}). In a layout one commitment holds, the multiplicity column and every table's packed witness share the $2^{28}$ committed words, so $\nbit\le28$ and every block has at most $2^{28}$ rows. With $\kbc\le21$ the whole degree is below $2^{52}$, which leaves $140$ bits before the union and $128$ after. Each further bit of $\kbc$ doubles the degree and costs a bit, down to $135$ and $123$ at the text region's $\kbc=26$. The configured prover therefore grinds $g=\max(0,\kbc-21)$ bits before $\alpha$ and $\beta$, targeting a work factor of $2^g$. Converting this target into a per-oracle-query error improvement requires the separate joint PoW/Fiat-Shamir work argument and is not established here. The GKR's own term stays above $160$ bits after the union. The native verifier also refuses an announced layout whose bus would miss the configured grinding-adjusted target.
\item \emph{Table sumcheck} (\S\ref{sec:air}): $(3\taumax+\nside+\nconstot)/|\E|$ with $\taumax\le32$; at least $172$ after the union.
\item \emph{Flock} (Annex~\ref{flock:soundness}): below $2^{-183}$; at least $171$ after the union.
\item \emph{Ring switching} (Lemma~\ref{lem:rs-family}): $(\nrs-1)/|\E|+2^{-160}$; at least $147$ after the union.
\item \emph{Opening} (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}): each row already carries its level's list size $L_i$, so no further union applies. At the configured parameters the fold challenges' MCA errors clear $128$ bits, the batching challenges $168$, and the OOD challenges $150$. Each query phase has a $111$-bit query-error target and a separate $17$-bit grinding-work target.
\end{itemize}
These are round-by-round algebraic and query-work targets, not a sum-free end-to-end failure probability. Any Fiat-Shamir claim must also account for its actual oracle-query budget, the matching ROM compilation theorem, and the separate compression-mode and binding losses in \S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}; clearing a per-round target does not by itself prove whole-protocol 128-bit security.
