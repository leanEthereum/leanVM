\section{End-to-end protocol}\label{sec:e2e}

\subsection{Bytecode encoding}\label{sec:e2e-bc}

The program is public, so nothing is decoded in a table: both parties decode each of the $\nprog=2^{\kbc}$ instruction words once, into the fields the rows read (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}), and encode the result as one $\K$-valued multilinear $P$ in $\kbc+4$ variables:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item lay instruction $z$ out as $16$ slots of $\K$: the class tag $\opc{j}$ in slot $2$ (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}); the flags in slot $3$, a word of selector bits naming the class's function, the finalization word for \texttt{HASH}, the accumulate and base-field bits for \texttt{EXT}; the register numbers $a_1,a_2,a_d$ in slots $4$ to $6$, $\intk{\sink}=\intk{32}$ for an instruction with no destination or with $\regs[0]$ as one; the immediate in slot $7$, sign-extended, folded with $\pc$ for \texttt{auipc}, and $0$ for \texttt{jal}, whose link is its row's successor; the successor $\intk{\tbase+4z+4}$ in slot $8$; the jump offset $\Delta$ in slot $9$, the fixed target of a branch, a \texttt{jal} or an \texttt{ecall} XOR the successor, and $0$ for an entry with none (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}); the exit selector $\exitflag$ in slot $11$, $1$ only for \texttt{ecall}; and $0$ everywhere else: slot $10$, where a division's row puts its verdict (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}), and slots $0$, $1$ and $12$ to $15$, which the separator, the address and nothing occupy in the bus tuple. A slot of the text holding no instruction the machine defines, the halt slot included, is all zeros: its tag is no table's.
\item read the resulting $2^{\kbc}\times16$ table as $P$, the low $\kbc$ variables indexing the instruction and the high four its slot, bits low first.
\end{enumerate}
So $P(z,0,1,0,0)$ is instruction $z$'s tag, $P(z,0,0,1,0)$ its first register, $P(z,0,0,0,1)$ its successor, and $P(z,0,0,0,0)=P(z,1,1,1,1)=0$.

\paragraph{Well-formedness.} The proof system is sound for any table $P$, a malformed one included: it proves that the rows read $P$ and did what its fields say. What makes $P$ a RISC-V program is checked once, by both parties, where a program enters: every entry with a tag reads two registers below $32$ and writes a cell in $1..32$, its successor is its own address plus $4$, its flags are ones its class defines, its exit selector is a bit, $1$ only for the one form of \texttt{ecall} (a \texttt{JUMP} entry with flags $0$ that reads $\regs[0]$, writes the sink and has immediate $0$), only a branch, a \texttt{jal} or an \texttt{ecall} has a jump offset, a \texttt{jal} reads $\regs[0]$ and has immediate $0$, a \texttt{LOAD}, \texttt{LD} or \texttt{JUMP} entry reads $\regs[0]$ as its second register, a \texttt{STORE} or \texttt{SD} entry writes the sink, an \texttt{LD} or \texttt{SD} entry has flags $0$, a \texttt{BRANCH} or \texttt{HASH} entry writes the sink and has immediate $0$, and an \texttt{EXT} entry reads its address from a register, not the sink, and has immediate $0$. The two verifiers check the same rules, and the digest below is of the decoded table, so a verifier is never handed a program it did not check. A table of $\nprog=2^{\kbc}$ slots, the padding and the halt slot included, makes the bus grind $\max(0,\kbc-21)$ bits before its challenges (\S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}); the text region caps it at $2^{26}$ slots.

\subsection{The statement}\label{sec:e2e-pi}

The \emph{statement} is a program and an output: that the program, run on RAM holding its image, halts on \texttt{exit} with $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$ holding the output (\S\ref{sec:vm}). The program is one digest, BLAKE2s of everything public and fixed: the table $P$, the entry point, the halt slot, $\kmem$, $\kadv$ and the image, each variable-length part length-framed. The digest seeds the Fiat-Shamir transcript (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}), so every challenge depends on the exact statement; the output does too, as the words the transcript starts from.

RAM's initial words need no claim: its seed column $\meminit$ is public, the image then zeros, and the verifier evaluates its extension at $\zeta$ itself, in time proportional to the image rather than to $\nmem$, the column being zero past the image (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}). The output and the exit are five claims on the committed final registers $\regfin$ at Boolean points, one per register, whose values both parties know and neither transmits:
\[
  \mle{\regfin}(\mathrm{bits}(17))\;\qeq\;\intk{93},\qquad \mle{\regfin}(\mathrm{bits}(10+i))\;\qeq\;\textsf{output}_i\quad(i<4),
\]
with $\mathrm{bits}(r)\in\cube{6}$ the number of the register, low bit first. The PCS opening binds them to the committed column, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem} makes that column what the run's last writes left, so the run executed \texttt{exit} with those values in place. The initial registers are the constant zero, the advice's initial words are the prover's, and nothing else about the run is public.

\subsection{Filling the tables}\label{sec:e2e-pad}

A table is proven over a power-of-two number of rows. The prover reaches one with \emph{padding rows}, whose clock is $0$. The text carries, past the program and an illegal word that keeps a run from falling into them, for each table and each of a few sizes, a block of that many no-ops of the table's class followed by a \texttt{jal} back to the block's first instruction, which no program code jumps into; \texttt{JUMP}'s no-op is a jump to the next instruction, so its block falls through like any other. A padding row is a row at one of those instructions. A clock without the live bit does not advance, so the states pushed and pulled around a block cancel among themselves, for any number of traversals: these are the closed walks of Proposition~\ref{prop:walk}, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:clock} they are the only ones. A traversal of the block of size $n$ adds $n$ rows to its table and one to \texttt{JUMP}'s, so every table can be brought to its next power of two exactly, \texttt{JUMP}, where every closing jump lands, being solved last.

The verifier has no notion of a padding row, and needs none: a row is inert exactly when its clock lacks the live bit, and the filler blocks are only how an honest prover comes by such rows. A prover may close such rows around any cycle of the program's own control flow instead, and they are inert all the same, by the same two facts: their state tuples cancel because their clocks do not advance, and their accesses meet nothing of the run because the order check \eqref{eq:order} keeps the live bit off their previous timestamps (Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem}). The filler blocks earn their place by making the fill exact, not by making it safe.

A padding row's accesses cost nothing either. Its access in slot $k$ pushes the timestamp $\intk{0}+\intk{k}=\intk{k}$, and the prover takes $\tsprev=\intk{k}$ and $v_{\mathrm{old}}=v_{\mathrm{new}}$, so the access pulls the very tuple it pushes and the two cancel, whatever the address and the value: the order check \eqref{eq:order} asks a row without the live bit only that its previous timestamps lack it too. A no-op reads $\regs[0]$ twice and writes the sink, a load or a store touches the byte at address $0$, and a compression's block is at address $0$: on zero registers, so the reads are of zeros, and a write rewrites what it writes, which is why a row can rewrite a cell but never update one in place (\S\ref{sec:tables}). Its circuit instances are honest instances on those zeros, and its read of the bytecode an ordinary one. None of this bears on soundness: by Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem} a tuple whose timestamp lacks the live bit meets no tuple of the run, so the padding rows constrain nothing and are constrained by nothing, which is what a padding row should be. The live bit plays the part of an \emph{is-real} selector, at the cost of no column of its own.

\subsection{Fiat Shamir instantiation}\label{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}

The transcript is a chain of BLAKE2s compressions over $\K$-words. Its initial state is the compression, from the parameter IV, of the program's digest (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}) and the four output words, so the state depends on the whole statement before the prover sends anything. Every scalar the prover sends is absorbed, and a value that rode the stream is never absorbed a second time; the commitment's Merkle root is a scalar like any other. A \emph{step} compresses a 64-byte block into the state, as its chaining value, at counter $64$ and final, and its output is the next state. The block holds up to two absorbed scalars, the last in words $4$ to $6$ and the one before it in words $0$ to $2$, their count in word $3$, and a tag naming the step's role in word $7$. An absorbed scalar waits for the next step: a challenge is the output's first three words of a \emph{squeeze} step, which absorbs the waiting scalars, and a third scalar first closes an \emph{observe} step over the two before it. A block names its role and its scalars, so the steps encode the transcript injectively and every challenge is a compression of a state that hashes everything sent before it. A proof of work of $g$ bits is a nonce the prover sends: the waiting scalars are absorbed by an observe step, the \emph{base} is the output of a step that absorbs nothing under its own tag, and the nonce's hash with the base, from the parameter IV, has its $g$ low bits zero; the verifier checks it and absorbs it in a step of its own, so the next challenge depends on it. The prover sends the smallest such nonce. With $g=0$ nothing is sent.

\paragraph{Why a step is sound.} A step is the BLAKE2s compression function keyed by the transcript state, not BLAKE2s applied to the state and the block: the state is the compression's chaining value, as in the second and later blocks of an ordinary BLAKE2s hash, and the whole 64-byte block is free for the scalars, the count and the tag. The compression is modelled as a random function of its chaining value, block, counter and final flag, which is the assumption the HAIFA analysis of BLAKE2 makes about it for any chaining value; the state is public, so the argument needs no secret. The encoding is injective: the protocol fixes the order of the steps, each block names its role by its tag and its scalars by their count, and a waiting scalar is absorbed before anything is derived from the state (a challenge, the base of a proof of work, the state a recursive verifier hands on), so the blocks determine everything the prover sent and every challenge is the image of a state that hashes all of it. Steps stay apart from every other use of BLAKE2s (the seed, Merkle leaves and nodes, the proof-of-work predicate, the program's and a tree's digests): an ordinary BLAKE2s hash sets the final flag on its last block only, with the message's length as its counter, so the only ordinary compressions at counter $64$ with the final flag are those of one-block messages of exactly 64 bytes, from the parameter IV. A step coincides with one only if the state equals the parameter IV, a preimage of 256 bits under the compression, and two steps of different roles differ in their tags.

\subsection{The unrolled protocol}\label{sec:e2e-unrolled}

Each reduction below adds evaluation claims on columns to a shared \emph{claim pool}, proved by the final PCS \textsc{Opening} phase.

\paragraph{Setup.}

The Fiat-Shamir state is seeded by the statement (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}). The prover sends the fourteen table log-heights $\tau_j$, the PCS rate and the final clock $\tsfinal\in\K$ (\S\ref{sec:state}); the verifier checks each $\tau_j$ against the cap $2^{32}$ and the batch floors (\S\ref{sec:tables}), and that $\tsfinal$ is a live clock at slot $0$, and derives the layout of every stack (PCS and GKR leaves) and the producer's bit count $\nbit$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}) from those sizes and the program's.

\paragraph{Commitment.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover.} Stack (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) every column: the read-write arrays' final columns and timestamps, $\regfin,\memfin,\advfin$ and their $\tsfin$, the advice's initial words $\advinit$ (\S\ref{sec:memchan}), the bytecode's multiplicity column $\mlt$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), the twenty-seven packed witnesses, $\qcls{j}$ for the thirteen tables with a class circuit and $\qclk{j}$ for all fourteen (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}, \S\ref{sec:clock}), every committed column of the fourteen tables, and the register words $\regword{w}$ (\S\ref{sec:regpack}), into $q$, and send its commitment (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}).
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Receives the commitment to $q$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Bus.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} When $\kbc>21$, the prover sends a proof of work of $\kbc-21$ bits and the verifier checks it (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}, \S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}). A smaller program sends nothing here.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Samples and sends the fingerprint challenge $\alpha\in\E^{4}$ and the multiset challenge $\beta\in\E$ (\S\ref{sec:gp}).
\item \textbf{Prover.} Forms the push and pull leaf vectors (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}), the push side ending on the bytecode producer's bit blocks (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), stacked into blocks and padded with $1$s. Sends one bus root $R$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run the one batched GKR (\S\ref{sec:gkr}) over the two trees, at the depth $\mu$ of the taller. The verifier checks each side's product against $R$; one $R$ for both sides is the balance check (\S\ref{sec:gp}). The pass yields two leaf claims $\widetilde V^{s}_0(\zeta)$ at one shared $\zeta$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Decompose each leaf claim (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}). The verifier forms every public part itself: the block selectors, the constant entries, the index columns (\S\ref{sec:idxcol}) and RAM's seed column (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}). Four blocks per side belong to no table: the state boundary, plus the registers', RAM's and the advice's seed (push side) or finalization (pull side). For their committed columns the prover sends one evaluation each, entering the claim pool: on the push side the advice's initial column alone, on the pull side every final column and timestamp. Every other block belongs to a table or to a producer, and is discharged below.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Linear tables.} The thirteen tables with a class circuit flush only tuples linear in their columns (\S\ref{sec:tables}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover.} Sends, table by table, the value of every column of $T_j$ at $\zeta_{<\tau_j}$ but its register numbers, which the table sumcheck folds (\S\ref{sec:regpack}); they enter the claim pool, a port's routed to its packed witness (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}).
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Evaluates each such table's form $B^{s}_j$ at those values, its register numbers taken as zero, its share of side $s$ short of the numbers' part, and takes it off what the tables owe (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Table sumcheck.} One run proves what is left of the bus, \texttt{EXT}'s share, the linear tables' register numbers' part and the producer's (\S\ref{sec:air}), and \texttt{EXT}'s three identities, the only constraints a table has (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Samples and sends the batch challenge $\xi\in\E$. Its first $\nside=2$ powers weight the bus forms, one per side and shared by every table and the producer, and the next three, $\xi^{2}$ to $\xi^{4}$, \texttt{EXT}'s identities, whose sums are zero, so the target is the bus's alone. It draws no point: the batch runs at the bus point, $T_j$ tested at $\zeta_{<\tau_j}$ and a producer of $2^{\kappa}$ entries at $\zeta_{<\kappa}$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run the sumcheck over the fourteen tables, a linear table's forms restricted to its register numbers, and then the bytecode's producer, $\taumax$ rounds, $\taumax$ the largest of their log heights, each joining at round $\taumax$ minus its own, against the target the verifier derived from the two leaf claims. Its summand has individual degree three and the round check pins one of the four coefficients (\S\ref{sec:prelim}), so a round costs three $\E$-elements.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} The prover sends one value per column of \texttt{EXT} and per register number of a linear table, but for a register number its bits' values (\S\ref{sec:regpack}), and each producer's bit values $\widetilde{\mbit{k}}(\fc_{<\kappa})$, $k<\nbit$; the verifier computes each producer's public columns $\widetilde{\pubp{k}}(\fc_{<\kappa})$ itself (\S\ref{sec:lookup}). It rebuilds each $B^{s}_j$ and \texttt{EXT}'s identities from them and checks that they reproduce the sumcheck's final value; \texttt{EXT}'s column values then enter the claim pool at $\fc_{<\tau_j}$. A circuit's ports are columns of its table but live in $\qcls{j}$ or $\qclk{j}$, so their claims route there (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}); a producer's bits are slices of its multiplicity column, and a table's register numbers' bits slices of the register word that packs them, so their claims go to ring switching (below).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The exit.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Pool the five claims on $\regfin$ at the Boolean points naming $\regs[17]$ and $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$, with the values $\intk{93}$ and the output; the prover sends nothing (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Flock validity.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run Flock's reduction (Annex~\ref{annex:flock}) once over the twenty-seven circuits, batched under shared challenges (\S\ref{flock:batch}): every class circuit in table order, over $\qcls{j}$, \texttt{EXT} having none, then every clock circuit in table order, over $\qclk{j}$, each with $2^{\tau_j}$ instances. One zerocheck, its round-one message shared by all twenty-seven, and one lincheck leave one evaluation claim on each packed witness, and one claim on each circuit's matrix form, which the verifier settles by one walk of the circuit once the proof is read.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Sends $\rsgamma\in\E$, drawn after every ring-switched claim is bound. The claims are the twenty-seven witnesses', then the bytecode's multiplicity column's, its suffix point being its $\fc_{<\kappa}$ and its $64$ slices the $\nbit$ bit values it sent, then zeros, which are not transmitted (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), then each register word's, its suffix point being its tables' shared $\fc_{<\tau_j}$ and its slices the bit values sent for its tables, in its order, then zeros (\S\ref{sec:regpack}); claim $j$, from zero, takes the scale $\rsgamma^{\,j}$, and both sides form the family's slices $\sfam{i}=\sum_j\rsgamma^{\,j}s_{j,i}$ (\S\ref{rs:family}). The prover sends nothing.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} The ring switching of \S\ref{sec:ringswitch}, its map drawn after $\rsgamma$, reduces the family to one weighted-sum claim on the stack, target $\sum_ix^i\Phi(\sfam{i})$, with an MLE-friendly $\E$-valued weight (Definition~\ref{def:ipcs}) supported on the ring-switched regions, claim $j$'s being $\Phi\bigl(\rsgamma^{\,j}\eq(r_j,\cdot)\bigr)$; it joins the pool, ahead of every column claim. The circuits of one block size leave points that are prefixes of one another, so the verifier evaluates their weights in one pass (\S\ref{rs:weight}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Opening.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Each pooled claim is one evaluation of a column at a point, its value already supplied; via the stacking selectors (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) it becomes a weighted sum $\sum_w W_j(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=c_j$ over the stacked witness. (The ring-switched claim arrives already in this form, its weight supported on the ring-switched regions.)
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Sends one batching challenge $\lambda\in\E$, drawn after every claim value is bound. Claim $j$ takes the weight $\lambda^{j-1}$, the ring-switched claim first, with $\lambda^0=1$, and the pooled column claims after: the $J$ weights are distinct powers of the one challenge (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}).
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Fold the $J$ claims into $W_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda^{j-1} W_j$ and $C_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda^{j-1} c_j$, and run the inner-product PCS opening on $\sum_w W_\lambda(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=C_\lambda$ (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}), the verifier evaluating $W_\lambda$ itself. This one opening discharges every pooled claim; there is no separate reduction sumcheck.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Accepts if every check above passed: the caps, the one bus root for both sides, every sumcheck's rounds and final value, flock's batched reduction, and the PCS opening.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Soundness ledger}\label{sec:e2e-ledger}

The commitment takes no out-of-domain sample (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}), so its root binds the prover to the list $\polyset_{\mathsf{cm}}$ of at most $L_0=\tfrac{1}{2\slack_0\sqrt{\rate_0}}$ polynomials (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}), not to one of them. Every challenge drawn after the root and before the \textsc{Opening} is a test the prover may pass with any member of that list: the bus's $\alpha$ and $\beta$ and its GKR's challenges, the table sumcheck's $\xi$ and rounds, Flock's, $\rsgamma$ and the ring-switching map. The opening holds the prover only to some member satisfying every pooled claim, so a test that a fixed polynomial passes with probability $\varepsilon$ is passed by some member with probability at most $L_0\,\varepsilon$, by a union bound over the list. Each such challenge's error is therefore $L_0$ times the one its own section states. Over every size and rate the PCS configures, $\log_2L_0\le12$, a bound the implementation pins to the derivation of the PCS parameters; so a challenge before the opening keeps $128$ bits when its own error is at most $2^{-140}$.

The round-by-round errors, in bits ($-\log_2$ of the error), at their smallest over every configured size and rate:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Bus} (\S\ref{sec:gp}, \S\ref{sec:gkr}): $4N/|\E|$ for the fingerprint, $N$ the larger side's linear factors, plus at most $8(\mu+1)^2/|\E|$ for the GKR; times $L_0$, at least $128$. The bytecode producer dominates $N$ with up to $2^{\kbc}(2^{\nbit}-1)$ factors (\S\ref{sec:lookup}). In a layout one commitment holds, the multiplicity column and every table's packed witness share the $2^{28}$ committed words, so $\nbit\le28$ and every block has at most $2^{28}$ rows. With $\kbc\le21$ the whole degree is below $2^{52}$, which leaves $140$ bits before the union and $128$ after. Each further bit of $\kbc$ doubles the degree and costs a bit, down to $135$ and $123$ at the text region's $\kbc=26$. So the prover grinds $g=\max(0,\kbc-21)$ bits before $\alpha$ and $\beta$: after Fiat-Shamir, a random-oracle query then yields them only with probability $2^{-g}$, so the fingerprint's error per query is $2^{-g}$ times the above (\S\ref{sec:iopcs}), and the row is back to $128$ bits. The GKR's challenges come after the grinding and need none: their own term stays above $160$ bits after the union. The native verifier also refuses an announced layout whose bus would miss this margin at its grinding.
\item \emph{Table sumcheck} (\S\ref{sec:air}): $(3\taumax+\nside+\nconstot)/|\E|$ with $\taumax\le32$; at least $172$ after the union.
\item \emph{Flock} (Annex~\ref{flock:soundness}): below $2^{-183}$; at least $171$ after the union.
\item \emph{Ring switching} (Lemma~\ref{lem:rs-family}): $(\nrs-1)/|\E|+2^{-160}$; at least $147$ after the union.
\item \emph{Opening} (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}): each row already carries its level's list size $L_i$, so no further union applies. At the configured parameters the fold challenges' MCA errors clear $128$ bits, the batching challenges $168$, the OOD challenges $150$, and each query phase $111$ bits from its queries and $17$ from grinding.
\end{itemize}
Every row clears $128$ bits, so the Fiat-Shamir compilation of the whole protocol keeps the $128$-bit target (\S\ref{sec:iopcs}).
