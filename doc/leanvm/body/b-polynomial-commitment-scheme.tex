\section{The polynomial commitment scheme: WHIR \slash Ligerito with affine folds}\label{annex:pcs}

A self-contained description of the WHIR\slash Ligerito polynomial commitment scheme \cite{ACFY25,NA25} over binary fields, with a round-by-round soundness analysis in the Johnson list-decoding regime. The two schemes coincide when WHIR is instantiated with interleaved codes and Ligerito with the Reed-Solomon codes of Annex~\ref{app:ntt}, which gives the novel polynomial basis and additive NTT used for encoding. Each commitment takes one out-of-domain (OOD) sample. The two fields are those of \S\ref{sec:vm}: we commit to a $\K$-valued multilinear, and opening claims are in $\E$, with $|\K|=2^{64}$ and $|\E|=2^{128}$.

A fold round combines two halves with the \emph{affine combiner} of~\cite{CYWZ26}: the coefficient is a random polynomial $\afa+\afb X$ of degree one, not a random scalar as in the original schemes. Its mutual correlated agreement error is of order $1/|\E|$ instead of $\blen/|\E|$ for block length $\blen$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:mca-affine}, \S\ref{sec:affine-intuition}), so $128$-bit security is now possible with a $128$-bit field. We target 125 for practical reasons.

\subsection{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelims}
%=====================================================================

\subsubsection{Codes, interleaving, and list decoding}\label{sec:codes}

We use the Reed-Solomon code $\RS[\E,\dom,\kdim]$ of Definition~\ref{def:rs-code}, with $|\dom|=\blen$, dimension $\kdim=2^\kappa$, and rate $\rate=\kdim/\blen$. Messages are indexed by $\cube{\kappa}$; the linear encoder $\Enc$ of \S\ref{sec:binary-rs} maps them onto the code and preserves $\K$-valued words. Polynomials are written in the novel basis $(\novel_c)_c$ of Annex~\ref{app:ntt}: one of degree below $2^\kappa$ is a message $g:\cube{\kappa}\to\E$, with coefficient $g(u)$ on $\novel_{\langle u\rangle}$. A fold round raises degrees by one, so the analysis also uses tuples of polynomials of degree at most some $\dgr\ge2^\kappa$.

\begin{definition}[Interleaved words, lists]\label{def:interleaved}
An \emph{interleaved word} is a matrix $\orc \in \E^{\cube{\ell} \times \dom}$, of $2^\ell$ \emph{lanes}. A \emph{polynomial tuple} $U=(U_u)_{u\in\cube{\ell}}$ has degree at most $\dgr$ if every $U_u$ does; it \emph{agrees} with $\orc$ on $\aset\subseteq\dom$ if $U_u(x)=\orc[u,x]$ for all $u$ and all $x\in\aset$. For an agreement $\agr$, $\lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr)$ is the set of polynomial tuples of degree at most $\dgr$ agreeing with $\orc$ on at least $\agr$ coordinates. A tuple \emph{decodes} to the message $g_U:\cube{\ell+\kappa}\to\E$, where $g_U(u,\cdot)$ are the coefficients of $U_u$ below $2^\kappa$, its \emph{low part}; tuples of degree below $2^\kappa$ decode to distinct messages.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weighted claims on tuples]\label{def:tuple-claim}
For a weight $W:\cube{\ell}\times\cube{\kappa}\to\E$ and a polynomial tuple $P$ whose lane $P_u$ has coefficients $p_{u,c}$ on $\novel_c$, put $\inner{W}{P}:=\sum_{u\in\cube{\ell}}\sum_{c<2^\kappa}W(u,c)\,p_{u,c}$: coefficients of index $2^\kappa$ and above carry no weight. On a tuple of degree below $2^\kappa$ this is $\inner{W}{g_P}$ (Definition~\ref{def:inner}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Affine fold]\label{def:affine-fold}
Let $\orc$ have $\ell\ge1$ lane variables, and split a lane index as $(\iota,u')$, with $\iota\in\{0,1\}$ the variable folded first. For $\afa,\afb\in\E$, the \emph{affine fold} is the interleaved word with $\ell-1$ lane variables whose column at $x\in\dom$, the evaluation point of that column, is
\[
\orc_{\afa,\afb}[u',x]\;:=\;\orc[(1,u'),x]+(\afa+\afb x)\,\orc[(0,u'),x].
\]
The same formula, with the indeterminate $X$ for $x$, folds a polynomial tuple $U$ into $U_{\afa,\afb}$, of degree at most one more. Evaluation commutes with the fold, so a tuple agreeing with $\orc$ on $\aset$ folds to one agreeing with $\orc_{\afa,\afb}$ on $\aset$.
\end{definition}

In a fold round, $F:=U_{(0,\cdot)}$ and $G:=U_{(1,\cdot)}$ are the halves whose folded bit is $0$ and $1$, and the fold is $G+(\afa+\afb X)F$.

\begin{theorem}[Johnson bound]\label{thm:johnson-interleaved}
For every interleaved word $\orc$ over a domain of size $\blen$, every degree bound $\dgr<\blen$ and every agreement $\agr$ with $\agr^2>\blen\dgr$: $\ |\lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr)| \le \jl(\agr,\dgr) := \bigl\lfloor \blen(\agr-\dgr)/(\agr^2-\blen\dgr) \bigr\rfloor$. If $\agr\ge(\sqrt{\rate}+\slack)\blen$ with $\rate:=\dgr/\blen$, this is at most $\frac{1}{2\slack\sqrt{\rate}}$. (Proof in \S\ref{app:johnson}.)
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{Round-by-round soundness, and grinding}\label{sec:iopcs}

\begin{definition}[RBR soundness, informal; cf.~\cite{CCHLRR19,ACFY25}]\label{def:rbr}
A public-coin protocol is \emph{round-by-round (RBR) sound with errors} $(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \dots)$, one per verifier message, if there is an \emph{invariant} on partial transcripts (not necessarily efficiently computable) marking the positions where a cheating prover has already lost:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item on a false statement, the invariant holds before any message is sent;
\item the prover alone cannot escape it: it survives every prover message;
\item the $i$-th verifier message lets it escape with probability at most $\varepsilon_i$;
\item if it survives to the end of the interaction, the verifier rejects.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Interactive soundness error is then at most $\sum_i \varepsilon_i$. After Fiat-Shamir, it is $\max_i \varepsilon_i$ per random-oracle query~\cite{CCHLRR19,BCS16}.

\paragraph{Grinding.} The security target is $2^{-125}$ per verifier message. A message whose RBR error $\varepsilon_i$ exceeds it is preceded by a grind of $b_i$ bits, which counts the error as $\varepsilon_i\,2^{-b_i}\le2^{-125}$ (\S\ref{sec:grinding}); the protocol below leaves these grinds implicit.

\subsection{Reed-Solomon codes over binary fields}\label{sec:binary-rs}
%=====================================================================

The encoder $\Enc_{\kappa,\rexp}$ of Definition~\ref{def:enc} evaluates $2^\kappa$ novel-basis coefficients on an additive domain of size $\blen=2^{\kappa+\rexp}$. Here $\rexp\ge1$ and $\kappa+\rexp\le64$. The protocol uses three properties, proved in Annex~\ref{app:ntt}:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{fast encoding, for the prover:} $\Enc_{\kappa,\rexp}$ is computed by the additive NTT with $\tfrac12 \kappa \blen$ multiplications and fewer than $(\kappa + 1)\, \blen$ additions (Proposition~\ref{prop:ntt});
\item \emph{MLE-friendly columns, for the verifier:} for every $x \in \dom$, the weight $W_x : \cube{\kappa} \to \K$, defined by $\Enc_{\kappa,\rexp}(g)[x] = \inner{W_x}{g}$ (the relation being valid for arbitrary $g$), admits an MLE efficiently evaluable anywhere in $\E^{\kappa}$ in $O(\kappa)$ field operations (Lemma~\ref{lem:colweight});
\item \emph{multiplication by $X$, for the prover:} $X\cdot\novel_c$ is a short combination of $\novel_{c+1}$ and of the $\novel_{c'}$ whose index $c'$ is $c$ with low bits cleared, so the low $2^\kappa$ coefficients of $XH$, the only ones a claim weighs, depend only on those of $H$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mulx}).
\end{itemize}
Since $\novel_{2^\kappa+j}=\Wn{\kappa}\,\novel_j$ for $j<2^\kappa$ (Definition~\ref{def:enc}), a polynomial of degree below $2^{\kappa+1}$ splits as
\[
P=P_{\mathrm{low}}+\Wn{\kappa}\cdot\textstyle\sum_{j<2^\kappa}p_{2^\kappa+j}\,\novel_j :
\]
its low part $P_{\mathrm{low}}$ is a message of the code, and its high part costs one evaluation of $\Wn{\kappa}$ and of a few $\novel_j$.

\subsection{The protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
%=====================================================================

\begin{protocol}[WHIR/Ligerito with affine folds]\label{fig:protocol}
\ \par\smallskip
\emph{Parameters.} The scheme commits to $f : \cube{\nv} \to \K$ across $\nlev \ge 1$ \emph{levels} $i = 0, \dots, \nlev-1$, with variable counts $\nv = \nv_0 > \nv_1 > \dots > \nv_\nlev \ge 0$, fold factors $\ell_i := \nv_i - \nv_{i+1} \ge 1$ and $\nv_\nlev$ small (e.g.\ $\nv_\nlev \le 5$). Level $i$ has a rate $\rate_i = 2^{-\rexp_i}$, $\rexp_i \ge 1$, giving the encoder $\Enc_i := \Enc_{\kappa_i, \rexp_i}$ of \S\ref{sec:binary-rs} with $\kappa_i := \nv_{i+1}$, i.e.\ the code $\mathcal{C}_i := \RS[\E, \dom_i, 2^{\kappa_i}]$ over its domain $\dom_i \subseteq \K$ of size $\blen_i = 2^{\kappa_i + \rexp_i}$ (requires $\kappa_i + \rexp_i \le 64$ and $\ell_i\le2^{\kappa_i}$), and a query count $\nq_i$. (The agreement thresholds appear only in the analysis.)

\smallskip
\textbf{Commit}$(f : \cube{\nv} \to \K)$: $\mathbf{P}$ sends the oracle $\orc^{(0)}[u, \cdot] := \Enc_0(f(u, \cdot))$, $u \in \cube{\ell_0}$, whose entries are $\K$ elements. $\mathbf{V}$ sends $z_0\leftarrow\E^{\nv}$; $\mathbf{P}$ sends $\cood{0}\in\E$ (honestly $\mle{f}(z_0)$). The \emph{OOD claim} $(\eq(z_0,\cdot),\cood{0})$ joins the claims the opening proves.

\smallskip
\textbf{Open}$\bigl(\{(W_k, c_k)\}_{k = 1, \dots, J_0}\bigr)$, $1\le J_0\le512$ weighted claims about $f$: $\mathbf{V}$ sends $\lambda \leftarrow \E$. The running claim is $(W, \rc{0}) := \bigl(\sum_{k} \lambda^{k-1} W_k,\; \sum_{k} \lambda^{k-1} c_k\bigr)$, on the lanes $P_u:=\mpoly_{f(u,\cdot)}$, $u\in\cube{\ell_0}$. For $i = 0, 1, \dots, \nlev - 1$, with $\kappa:=\kappa_i$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{(fold rounds)} For $j = 1, \dots, \ell_i$, with running claim $(W, \rc{j-1})$, $W:\cube{\ell_i-j+1}\times\cube{\kappa}\to\E$, and lanes $P$ with $\ell_i-j+1$ lane variables, split as $F$, $G$ by the first:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5pt,topsep=1pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item $\mathbf{P}$ sends $u, v, \tabx{0}, \tabx{1} \in \E$, honestly $u = \inner{W_0}{G}$, $v=\inner{W_0+W_1}{F}$ and $\tabx{\iota}=\inner{W_\iota}{XF}$, with $W_\iota:=W(\iota,\cdot)$.
\item $\mathbf{V}$ sends $\afa_j, \afb_j \leftarrow \E$.
\item Both set $\rc{j} := u+\afa_j\bigl(u+\rc{j-1}\bigr)+\afa_j^2v+\afb_j\tabx{0}+\afa_j\afb_j\bigl(\tabx{0}+\tabx{1}\bigr)$ and $W \gets \mle{W}(\afa_j,\cdot)$; $\mathbf{P}$ sets $P \gets P_{\afa_j,\afb_j} = G+(\afa_j+\afb_jX)F$.
\end{enumerate}
After the rounds, $P$ is one polynomial of degree below $2^{\kappa}+\ell_i$. Its low $2^\kappa$ coefficients are a message $f^{(i+1)}:\cube{\nv_{i+1}}\to\E$ and its high ones are $\phigh\in\E^{\ell_i}$: $P=\mpoly_{f^{(i+1)}}+\Wn{\kappa}\sum_{j<\ell_i}\phigh_j\novel_j$. The running claim $(W,\rc{\ell_i})$ is a weighted claim about $f^{(i+1)}$.
\item If $i < \nlev - 1$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5pt,topsep=1pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{(commit)} $\mathbf{P}$ sends the oracle $\orc^{(i+1)}[u, \cdot] := \Enc_{i+1}(f^{(i+1)}(u, \cdot))$, $u \in \cube{\ell_{i+1}}$, then $\phigh$.
\item \emph{(ood)} $\mathbf{V}$ sends $z_{i+1} \leftarrow \E^{\nv_{i+1}}$; $\mathbf{P}$ sends $\cood{i+1} \in \E$ (honestly $\mle{f^{(i+1)}}(z_{i+1})$).
\item \emph{(query)} $\mathbf{V}$ sends $\nq_i$ i.i.d.\ columns $\qset_i = (x_q)_{q < \nq_i} \leftarrow \dom_i$ and $\lambda\leftarrow\E$, one message.
\item \emph{(read)} $\mathbf{V}$ reads $\orc^{(i)}[\cdot, x_q]$ and folds it as the rounds folded the lanes, $c_q := \orc^{(i)}_{\afa_1,\afb_1,\dots}[x_q]$ (honestly $P(x_q)$), then removes the high part, $c'_q := c_q + \Wn{\kappa}(x_q)\sum_{j<\ell_i}\phigh_j\,\novel_j(x_q)$ (honestly $\Enc_i(f^{(i+1)})[x_q]$).
\item \emph{(next level)} The $J_{i+1}:=\nq_i+2$ claims of level $i+1$, the residual, the OOD and the $\nq_i$ consistency claims, are batched into one running claim on the lanes $P_u:=\mpoly_{f^{(i+1)}(u,\cdot)}$:
\[
W \gets W + \lambda\,\eq(z_{i+1},\cdot) + \lambda^2\textstyle\sum_q \lambda^{q} W^{(i)}_{x_q},
\qquad
\rc{0} := \rc{\ell_i} + \lambda\,\cood{i+1} + \lambda^2\textstyle\sum_{q}\lambda^{q}c'_q,
\]
where $W^{(i)}_x$ are the column weights of $\Enc_i$ (\S\ref{sec:binary-rs}).
\end{enumerate}
\item If $i = \nlev - 1$: \emph{(final)} $\mathbf{P}$ sends the $2^{\nv_\nlev}+\ell_i$ coefficients of $P$. $\mathbf{V}$ sends $\nq_{\nlev-1}$ i.i.d.\ columns $\qset_{\nlev-1} \leftarrow \dom_{\nlev-1}$, computes $c_q$ from $\orc^{(\nlev-1)}$ as in 2(d) and checks $c_q\qeq P(x_q)$. Then $\nv_\nlev$ sumcheck rounds of degree two (Fact~\ref{fact:sumcheck}) reduce $\sum_x W(x)\,f^{(\nlev)}(x)\qeq\rc{\ell_i}$ to a point $\fc\in\E^{\nv_\nlev}$, and $\mathbf{V}$ checks the last running claim against $\mle{W}(\fc)\cdot\mle{f^{(\nlev)}}(\fc)$, from the coefficients it holds.
\end{enumerate}
$\mathbf{V}$ evaluates $\mle{W}$ itself, at the point $(\afa,\fc)$ of all rounds' $\afa_j$ and tail challenges: every weight is MLE-friendly, and $\mle{W}$ there is the sum of the input weights at that point and of each later level's batched weights at its suffix. $\mathbf{V}$ accepts iff every check above passed.
\end{protocol}

At level $0$ the lanes are $\K$-valued, so a table entry pairs an $\E$-valued weight with $\K$-valued coefficients; from the first fold on everything is in $\E$. The verifier never forms the weight of $XF$: the prover sends its values $\tabx{\iota}$, and the analysis binds it (Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list}).

Completeness is perfect. In characteristic $2$, $\inner{\mle{W}(\afa_j,\cdot)}{H}=\inner{W_0}{H}+\afa_j\inner{W_0+W_1}{H}$ for every $H$, and applied to $G+\afa_jF+\afb_jXF$ this is the formula of step 1(c), as honestly $\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}=\rc{j-1}$. The folded column is $P(x_q)$, as evaluation commutes with the fold, and the correction leaves $P_{\mathrm{low}}(x_q)=\inner{W^{(i)}_{x_q}}{f^{(i+1)}}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:colweight}). The final checks are identities.

\subsection{Why an affine fold}\label{sec:affine-intuition}
%=====================================================================

Let $f,g:\dom\to\E$ be two words and $u,v$ two codewords, with errors $e:=f-u$ and $e':=g-v$. Say $x$ is a \emph{bad point} if $e(x)\neq0$ or $e'(x)\neq0$. A fold challenge \emph{repairs} a bad point $x$ if the folded error is zero at $x$.

\paragraph{Scalar fold.} The folded error of $f+\fc g$ is $e+\fc e'$, so $\fc$ repairs $x$ exactly when $e'(x)\neq0$ and $\fc=-e(x)/e'(x)$. Each bad point has at most one repairing challenge, and the prover chooses it through $f$ and $g$. Suppose $f,g$ agree with $u,v$ on the same $\agr-1$ points, and no pair of codewords agrees with $f,g$ on $\agr$ common points. The prover gives each of the $\blen-\agr+1$ bad points a different repairing challenge. Each such challenge brings the fold to agreement $\agr$ with $u+\fc v$, so with probability $(\blen-\agr+1)/|\E|$ the fold looks close although $f,g$ are not. Tolerating a loss of $s$ coordinates does not help: the prover gives $s+1$ bad points the same repairing challenge, and the error stays at least $\bigl(1+\lfloor(\blen-\agr)/(s+1)\rfloor\bigr)/|\E|$ \cite[Prop.~6.1]{CYWZ26}, after \cite[Remark~1.1]{BCIKS20}.

\paragraph{Affine fold.} The folded error of $f+(\afa+\afb x)g$ is $e+(\afa+\afb x)e'$, so $(\afa,\afb)$ repairs $x$ exactly when $e'(x)\neq0$ and $\afa+x\afb=-e(x)/e'(x)$. These challenges form a line in $\E^2$, so a random challenge repairs $x$ with probability $1/|\E|$. The prover sets the line's position, but $x$ sets its slope. So the lines of two different points cross at exactly one challenge. A random challenge thus repairs one bad point with probability up to $(\blen-\agr+1)/|\E|$, but two with probability at most $\binom{\blen}{2}/|\E|^2$.

\subsection{Round-by-round soundness}\label{sec:soundness}
%=====================================================================

\paragraph{Thresholds.} At level $i$, with $\blen:=\blen_i$ and $\ell:=\ell_i$, every round is analysed at the level's final degree $\dgr$, and round $j$ at the agreement $\agr_j$, for $0\le j\le\ell$:
\[
\dgr := 2^{\kappa_i}-1+\ell,
\qquad
\agr_j := \agr_\ell-(\ell-j),
\qquad
\agr_{\ell}:=\bigl\lceil(\sqrt{\dgr/\blen}+\slack_i)\,\blen\bigr\rceil\le\blen,
\]
for a slack $\slack_i>0$. The honest lanes start at degree $2^{\kappa_i}-1$ and gain one degree per round, so they stay within $\dgr$; each round loses one coordinate of agreement. Write $\agr^{(i)}_j$, $\dgr^{(i)}$ when the level matters, and $L_i:=\jl(\agr^{(i)}_0,\dgr^{(i)})$, the size of the list the level starts on (Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved}).

Fix a commitment $\mathsf{cm} = \orc^{(0)}$ and input claims $\{(W_k, c_k)\}_k$, and define the relation
\[
\relopen \;:=\; \Bigl\{ \bigl(\orc^{(0)}, \{(W_k, c_k)\}_k\bigr) \;:\; \exists\, U \in \lst\bigl(\orc^{(0)};\agr^{(0)}_0,\dgr^{(0)}\bigr) \text{ with } \inner{W_k}{g_U} = c_k \text{ for all } k \Bigr\}.
\]
The commitment's list is $\lst(\orc^{(0)};\agr^{(0)}_0,\dgr^{(0)})$, of size at most $L_0$, so its members decode to at most $L_0$ messages. Their multilinears are pairwise distinct at $z_0$ except with probability at most $\binom{L_0}{2}\nv/|\E|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sz}), which the grind before $z_0$ brings below $2^{-125}$. Then at most one message answers the OOD claim, so the commitment is binding, and every challenge drawn after it and before the opening faces a single candidate.

\begin{theorem}[RBR soundness]\label{thm:rbr}
Suppose that $(\agr^{(i)}_0)^2>\blen_i\dgr^{(i)}$ for every level $i$, so that the Johnson counts below are defined (as $\agr^{(i)}_j-1\ge\agr^{(i)}_0$ for $j\ge1$), and let $\epsaff$ be the affine MCA error of Definition~\ref{def:epsaff}. Then the opening phase of Protocol~\ref{fig:protocol} for $\relopen$ has the following RBR soundness error at each verifier message, with $\blen:=\blen_i$, $\agr_j:=\agr^{(i)}_j$, $\dgr:=\dgr^{(i)}$:
\[
\begin{array}{llcl}
\textnormal{batching } \lambda \textnormal{ of level } 0: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{batch}} &=& \dfrac{(J_0 - 1)\, L_0}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{fold } (\afa_j,\afb_j), \textnormal{ level } i: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{fold}}_{i,j} &=& \epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr_j) + \dfrac{2\,\jl(\agr_j-1,\dgr)}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{OOD point } z_i,\ i\ge1: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{ood}}_i &=& \dbinom{L_i}{2} \dfrac{\nv_i}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
(\qset_i,\lambda),\ i<\nlev-1: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{query}}_i &=& \Bigl(\dfrac{\agr^{(i)}_{\ell_i}-1}{\blen_i}\Bigr)^{\nq_i} + \dfrac{(J_{i+1} - 1)\, L_{i+1}}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{final query message } \qset_{\nlev-1}: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{query}}_{\nlev-1} &=& \Bigl(\dfrac{\agr^{(\nlev-1)}_{\ell_{\nlev-1}}-1}{\blen_{\nlev-1}}\Bigr)^{\nq_{\nlev-1}} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{each of the } \nv_\nlev \textnormal{ tail rounds}: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{tail}} &=& \dfrac{2}{|\E|} .
\end{array}
\]
Here $J_0\le512$ is the number of input claims and $J_i = \nq_{i-1} + 2$ for $i \ge 1$. The batching row of level $0$ is $(J_0+1)\,L_0/|\E|$ when $\lambda$ comes with the ring-switching challenges (\S\ref{sec:soundness-outer}). The \emph{RBR error}, which governs the Fiat-Shamir compilation, is the largest row over all rounds, each divided by $2^{b}$ for the grind of $b$ bits before its message.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The opening inside the protocol.}\label{sec:soundness-outer} The sixteen ring-switching challenges (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}) and $\lambda$ are one verifier message. Take a list member that violates some input claim. If it violates a claim $k\ge2$, the batched discrepancy is, for every map, a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $J_0-1$ in $\lambda$; otherwise it is the ring-switched claim's discrepancy alone, which vanishes with probability at most $2/|\E|$ (\S\ref{rs:map}). Lemma~\ref{lem:sz} and a union over both cases and the list give $(J_0+1)\,L_0/|\E|$.

\paragraph{Parameters.} The query grind is a fixed budget. The OOD and batching grinds are kept small so that level 0's list, which the knowledge extractor decodes, stays short. The fold grind is the smallest with which no admitted shape (stack size and rate) needs more queries than with unlimited grinding. The implementation picks the interpolation parameters of each $\epsaff$ by search, and the slack $\slack_i$ that minimizes $\nq_i$ with every ground row at most $2^{-125}$.

The remainder of this section proves the theorem: \S\ref{sec:tools} states the coding-theoretic tools, \S\ref{pcs:proof} the invariant and the round-by-round argument.

\subsubsection{Proximity tools}\label{sec:tools}

The engine is \emph{mutual correlated agreement} (MCA)~\cite{ACFY25} for the affine combiner, from the preprint~\cite{CYWZ26}. We state its error as the finite count in the proof of~\cite[Lemma~4.1]{CYWZ26}, with the interpolation parameters left free, as in~\cite[\S5.1, Finite bounds]{CYWZ26}.

\begin{definition}[Affine MCA error]\label{def:epsaff}
Let $1\le\dgr<\agr\le\blen$ be integers. A triple $(\imul,\iyz,\ithr)$ of positive integers is \emph{admissible} if, with $\iz:=\min\bigl(\iyz,\lfloor(\imul\agr-\iyz-1)/\dgr\rfloor\bigr)$,
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item $\iz\ge1$;
\item the \emph{interpolation inequality} holds:
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\iz}\max(0,\imul\agr-\iyz-\dgr j)\,(\iyz-j+1)\;>\;\blen\sum_{s=0}^{\min(\imul-1,\iz)}(\imul-s)(\iyz-s+1);
\]
\item $\dgr+1<\ithr<\agr$ and $\ithr^2>\blen(\dgr+1)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)\;:=\;\min_{(\imul,\iyz,\ithr)\ \mathrm{admissible}}\ \frac{\iyz^2+\jl(\ithr,\dgr+1)\cdot\max\bigl(|\E|,\binom{\blen}{2}\bigr)+4\dgr^2\iz^2\iyz^3\bigl(\blen/(\agr-\ithr)\bigr)^2}{|\E|^2}.
\]
\end{definition}

The three terms bound the exceptional coefficient pairs $(\afa,\afb)$: those where reconstructing the interpolated polynomial fails, those with a persistent rational explanation, and the rest \cite[Lemmas~4.3, 4.5, 4.7]{CYWZ26}. The interpolation inequality is the dimension count of \cite[Lemma~4.2]{CYWZ26}, more unknowns than equations, checked in exact integers.

\begin{theorem}[Affine MCA; {\cite[Thm.~1.1, Lemma~4.1, \S5.1]{CYWZ26}}]\label{thm:mca-affine}
Let $\dom\subseteq\K$ have size $\blen$, let $1\le\dgr<\agr\le\blen$, $h\ge1$, and let $f,g:\dom\to\E^h$ be words of $h$ lanes. For $\afa,\afb\in\E$ and an $h$-tuple $P$ of polynomials let $\aset_{\afa,\afb}(P):=\{x\in\dom:P(x)=f(x)+(\afa+\afb x)g(x)\}$. Then, for uniform $\afa,\afb\leftarrow\E$, except with probability at most $\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)$: every $h$-tuple $P$ of degree at most $\dgr$ with $|\aset_{\afa,\afb}(P)|\ge\agr$ has $h$-tuples $U$, $V$ with $\deg U\le\dgr$, $\deg V\le\dgr-1$ and $P=U+(\afa+\afb X)V$, such that $U=f$ and $V=g$ on all but at most one point of $\aset_{\afa,\afb}(P)$ (agreement loss one).
\end{theorem}

For $h=1$ this is \cite[Thm.~1.1]{CYWZ26}, with the finite count of its Lemma~4.1 in place of its closed form. The count holds for every admissible triple, as the proofs of \cite[Lemmas~4.2 to 4.7]{CYWZ26} use the specific $\imul$, $\iyz$ and $\ithr$ only through (i) to (iii). For $h>1$, \cite[\S5.1, Interleaving and shared agreement]{CYWZ26} packs the $h$ lanes into one word over the degree-$h$ extension of $\E$, with $(\afa,\afb)$ still drawn from $\E$ and acting lane by lane. Interpolation and the degree bounds are unchanged over the extension, a nonzero affine constraint on $(\afa,\afb)$ still has at most $|\E|$ solutions, and the Johnson count depends only on agreement sets, so the same error bounds simultaneous agreement of all lanes, with no union over lanes.

\begin{lemma}[An affine fold round]\label{lem:fold-list}
Let $\orc$ be an interleaved word with $\ell\ge1$ lane variables over a domain of size $\blen$, $f:=\orc[(0,\cdot),\cdot]$ and $g:=\orc[(1,\cdot),\cdot]$ its two halves, of $h=2^{\ell-1}$ lanes each. Let $1\le\dgr<\agr\le\blen$ with $(\agr-1)^2>\blen\dgr$, and $L:=\jl(\agr-1,\dgr)$. Let $W:\cube{\ell}\times\cube{\kappa}\to\E$ be a weight, $W_\iota:=W(\iota,\cdot)$, $c\in\E$, and suppose every tuple of $\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$, as a pair $(F,G)$ of halves, has $\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}\neq c$. Let $u,v,\tabx{0},\tabx{1}\in\E$ be arbitrary. Except with probability at most $\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)+2L/|\E|$ over uniform $(\afa,\afb)$, every $P\in\lst(\orc_{\afa,\afb};\agr,\dgr)$ has
\[
\inner{\mle{W}(\afa,\cdot)}{P}\;\neq\;u+\afa(u+c)+\afa^2v+\afb\tabx{0}+\afa\afb(\tabx{0}+\tabx{1}).
\]
\end{lemma}

This is \cite[Thm.~5.1, Cor.~5.2]{CYWZ26} adapted to the protocol: one claim on the sum $\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}$ instead of two, the weight folded at $\afa$ instead of a challenge of its own, and no premix. \cite{CYWZ26} analyses round $j$ of a level at degree $2^\kappa-1+j$ and premixes the halves with an extra challenge, so that both come back at degree $2^\kappa-2+j$. At a degree fixed for the whole level, Theorem~\ref{thm:mca-affine} already returns both halves within it.

\begin{proof}
The list $\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$ has at most $L$ members (Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved}) and does not depend on any challenge. There are two bad events over $(\afa,\afb)$. $B_1$: the event of Theorem~\ref{thm:mca-affine} for the words $g$ and $f$, at degree $\dgr$ and agreement $\agr$, so $\Pr[B_1]\le\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)$. $B_2$: some $(F,G)\in\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$ has $\inner{\mle{W}(\afa,\cdot)}{P_{F,G}}=u+\afa(u+c)+\afa^2v+\afb\tabx{0}+\afa\afb(\tabx{0}+\tabx{1})$ for $P_{F,G}:=G+(\afa+\afb X)F$. Fix such a pair. As in the completeness argument, $\inner{\mle{W}(\afa,\cdot)}{P_{F,G}}$ is the same expression in the pair's true values: $u^\ast=\inner{W_0}{G}$, $v^\ast=\inner{W_0+W_1}{F}$, ${\tabx{\iota}}^\ast=\inner{W_\iota}{XF}$ and $c^\ast=\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}\neq c$. The difference of the two sides is a polynomial of degree at most $2$ in $(\afa,\afb)$, with constant term $u^\ast-u$ and coefficient of $\afa$ equal to $(u^\ast-u)+(c^\ast-c)$. If it were the zero polynomial, both would vanish and $c^\ast=c$. So it is nonzero, and zero with probability at most $2/|\E|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sz}). A union bound over at most $L$ pairs gives $\Pr[B_2]\le2L/|\E|$.

Suppose neither occurs, and let $P\in\lst(\orc_{\afa,\afb};\agr,\dgr)$, agreeing with the fold on $\aset$, $|\aset|\ge\agr$. As $B_1$ failed, $P=U+(\afa+\afb X)V$ with $\deg U\le\dgr$, $\deg V\le\dgr-1$, $U=g$ and $V=f$ on all but one point of $\aset$. Put $F:=V$ and $G:=U$: then $(F,G)\in\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$ and $P=P_{F,G}$. As $B_2$ failed, $P$ violates the new claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OOD separation]\label{lem:ood}
Let $\orc$ be an interleaved word with $|\lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr)| \le L$, decoding to $\nv$-variable messages. For uniform $z \leftarrow \E^{\nv}$, $\Pr[\exists\, U, U' \in \lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr) : g_U\neq g_{U'},\ \mle{g_U}(z) = \mle{g_{U'}}(z)] \le \binom{L}{2}\, \nv / |\E|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The list decodes to at most $L$ messages, and distinct messages have distinct multilinear extensions. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sz} two of them agree at $z$ with probability at most $\nv/|\E|$; a union bound over the pairs concludes.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Proof of the main theorem}\label{pcs:proof}

At level $i$, write $\orc^{(i)}_{j}$ for the oracle folded by the first $j$ rounds ($\orc^{(i)}_0=\orc^{(i)}$), so that $c_q=\orc^{(i)}_{\ell_i}[x_q]$, and $(W^{(j)},\rc{j})$ for the running claim after round $j$, a weighted claim about polynomial tuples with $\ell_i-j$ lane variables (Definition~\ref{def:tuple-claim}). A tuple \emph{violates} the claim if $\inner{W^{(j)}}{P}\neq\rc{j}$.

We now define the invariant required by Definition~\ref{def:rbr}, a condition on the partial transcripts of the opening. In one phrase: \emph{every polynomial tuple close to the current fold violates the current claim}. Precisely, the list below gives the condition just after each verifier message; a transcript ending with prover messages inherits the condition of its last verifier message. Every condition also includes one implicit disjunct, the \emph{escape clause}: \emph{``some verifier check has already failed''} (such transcripts are rejected no matter what follows).
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Before any opening message:} every $U \in \lst(\orc^{(0)};\agr_0,\dgr)$ violates at least one input claim.
\item \emph{After the batching challenge $\lambda$ of level $i$ (alone at level $0$, with the queries $\qset_{i-1}$ otherwise), and after each fold message $(\afa_j,\afb_j)$:} every $P \in \lst(\orc^{(i)}_{j};\agr_j,\dgr)$ violates the round-$j$ claim ($\lambda$ being the case $j = 0$).
\item \emph{After the OOD point $z_{i+1}$:} the round-$\ell_i$ condition holds, and no two distinct messages decoded from $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)})$ have multilinear extensions agreeing at $z_{i+1}$.
\item \emph{After the final query message $\qset_{\nlev-1}$:} the low part $f^{(\nlev)}$ of the sent $P$ violates the running claim $(W,\rc{\ell_{\nlev-1}})$.
\item \emph{After each of the $\nv_\nlev$ tail rounds (complete transcript):} $f^{(\nlev)}$ violates the running sumcheck claim. The terminal one $\mathbf{V}$ checks against $\mle{f^{(\nlev)}}$ itself, so it rejects.
\end{itemize}
Conditions (i), (ii), (iv) of Definition~\ref{def:rbr} hold by inspection: on a false statement, the first item is exactly the failure of $\relopen$; a prover message changes nothing an instance depends on, except possibly failing a check, which only switches the escape clause on; and the last item is precisely ``some check failed'', so such a complete transcript is rejected. The separation clause of the OOD instance is there because the next message consumes it. It remains to bound the escape probabilities (iii); in the proof below we assume all checks so far pass, since otherwise the escape clause already holds.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}]
If the statement lies outside $\relopen$, then before any opening message is sent every $U \in \lst(\orc^{(0)};\agr^{(0)}_0,\dgr^{(0)})$ violates at least one input claim, so the invariant holds. We follow the protocol and bound, for each verifier message, the probability that the invariant fails to carry over.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Batching challenge $\lambda$.}
Assume that every $U \in \lst(\orc^{(i)};\agr_0,\dgr)$ violates at least one of the $J_i$ claims $(W_k, c_k)$: the input claims at level $0$, the claims of step 2(e) at a later level, as the query step below shows. Fix such a $U$. Its discrepancy against the batched claim is $\sum_{k} \lambda^{k - 1} d_k$ with $d_k := \inner{W_k}{g_U} - c_k$, a polynomial in $\lambda$ of degree less than $J_i$, nonzero because the $d_k$ are not all zero. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sz} it vanishes with probability at most $(J_i - 1)/|\E|$, so a union bound over the at most $L_i$ list members leaves every $U$ violating the round-$0$ claim except with probability $(J_i - 1) L_i / |\E|$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Fold message $(\afa_j,\afb_j)$.}
Assume that every $P \in \lst(\orc^{(i)}_{j-1};\agr_{j-1},\dgr)$ violates the round-$(j{-}1)$ claim. As $\agr_{j-1}=\agr_j-1$, this is the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list} for the word $\orc^{(i)}_{j-1}$, the weight $W^{(j-1)}$ and $c=\rc{j-1}$, at degree $\dgr$ and agreement $\agr_j$. Whatever table the prover sends, the lemma makes every $P\in\lst(\orc^{(i)}_{j};\agr_j,\dgr)$ violate the round-$j$ claim, $W^{(j)}=\mle{W^{(j-1)}}(\afa_j,\cdot)$ and $\rc{j}$ as in step 1(c), except with probability $\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr_j)+2\jl(\agr_j-1,\dgr)/|\E|$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{The level interface.}
After the last fold round of level $i < \nlev - 1$, every polynomial $P$ of degree at most $\dgr$ agreeing with the single word $w := \orc^{(i)}_{\ell_i} \in \E^{\dom_i}$ on at least $\agr_{\ell_i}$ coordinates violates the residual claim $(W,\rc{\ell_i})$. The prover now sends the new oracle $\orc^{(i+1)}$ and $\phigh$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{OOD point $z_{i+1}$.}
After it, two properties must hold: the one above, which does not involve it; and that no two distinct messages decoded from $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)})$, a list already determined by the prover's message, have multilinear extensions agreeing at $z_{i+1}$. The latter fails with probability at most $\binom{L_{i+1}}{2}\, \nv_{i+1} / |\E|$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:ood}.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Query message $(\qset_i,\lambda)$.}
Assume both properties of the previous step; the prover has since sent its answer $\cood{i+1}$. By the separation property, at most one message $g^\ast$ decoded from $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)})$ can satisfy $\mle{g^\ast}(z_{i+1}) = \cood{i+1}$: every member decoding to another message violates the OOD claim $(\eq(z_{i+1}, \cdot),\, \cood{i+1})$, whatever $\qset_i$ is. If no such $g^\ast$ exists, we are done. Otherwise the members decoding to $g^\ast$ make its claims, as claims weigh the low part only. Put $P^\ast:=\mpoly_{g^\ast}+\Wn{\kappa_i}\sum_{j<\ell_i}\phigh_j\novel_j$, of degree at most $\dgr$, with low part $g^\ast$, and distinguish two cases.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{$P^\ast$ agrees with $w$ on at least $\agr_{\ell_i}$ coordinates.} Then $P^\ast$ violates the residual claim by assumption, and $\inner{W}{P^\ast}=\inner{W}{g^\ast}$ as the claim does not weigh the high part: $g^\ast$ violates the residual claim, whatever $\qset_i$ is.
\item \emph{Otherwise.} A uniform column $x \leftarrow \dom_i$ satisfies $P^\ast(x) = w(x)$ with probability at most $(\agr_{\ell_i}-1)/\blen_i$, so all $\nq_i$ i.i.d.\ queries do so simultaneously with probability at most $((\agr_{\ell_i}-1)/\blen_i)^{\nq_i}$; and any disagreeing query $x_q$ makes $g^\ast$ violate the consistency claim $(W^{(i)}_{x_q},\, c'_q)$, since $\Enc_i(g^\ast)[x_q]$ and $c'_q$ are $P^\ast(x_q)\neq w(x_q)=c_q$, each corrected by the same high part.
\end{itemize}
Except with probability $((\agr_{\ell_i}-1)/\blen_i)^{\nq_i}$ over $\qset_i$, every member of $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)})$ thus violates at least one of the $J_{i+1}$ claims. These claims are fixed once $\qset_i$ is: the opened columns are the committed oracle's. So, conditioned on $\qset_i$, $\lambda$ is uniform, and the batching step at level $i+1$ adds $(J_{i+1}-1)L_{i+1}/|\E|$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Final level.}
At level $\nlev - 1$ the fold rounds end as above: every polynomial of degree at most $\dgr$ agreeing with $w := \orc^{(\nlev-1)}_{\ell}$ on at least $\agr_{\ell}$ coordinates violates the running claim. The prover sends $P$, of degree at most $\dgr$. If it agrees with $w$ on at least $\agr_\ell$ coordinates, it violates the claim, so its low part $f^{(\nlev)}$ does; otherwise, except with probability $((\agr_\ell-1)/\blen_{\nlev-1})^{\nq_{\nlev-1}}$, some query has $c_q\neq P(x_q)$ and the verifier rejects. No list is involved: $P$ is transmitted. The $\nv_\nlev$ rounds that follow are a plain sumcheck (Fact~\ref{fact:sumcheck}) of degree two on a polynomial that $\mathbf{V}$ holds, $2/|\E|$ per round, so the terminal check against $\mle{f^{(\nlev)}}$ fails.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optimizations}\label{sec:pcs-opt}
%=====================================================================

The committed $f$ is the stack of \S\ref{sec:stacking}: $\nstack$ field elements in $\K$, then zeros up to $2^{\nv}$. Let the row index be the stack's most significant $\ell_0$ coordinates: $f(u, x) := q(x, u)$ for $u \in \cube{\ell_0}$ and $x \in \cube{\nv - \ell_0}$, \S\ref{app:ntt}. Row $u$ is then the block of $2^{\nv - \ell_0}$ consecutive elements of $q$ at offset $\langle u \rangle 2^{\nv - \ell_0}$, the padding is whole rows, and only $\nrows=\lceil\nstack/2^{\nv-\ell_0}\rceil$ of the $2^{\ell_0}$ rows hold data. Claims on the stack, the commitment's OOD claim included, are taken in $q$'s variable order and rotated to $f$'s. So we get the following optimizations at commitment:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item the FFT-encoder only runs on $\nrows$ rows (and not the full $2^{\ell_0}$), since FFT-ing zeros gives zeros;
\item a Merkle leaf only contains $\nrows$ elements of $\K$ (proof size optimization), we keep the convention of hashing the full $2^{\ell_0}$ values for simplicity, but we hash them in reverse order, starting from the padding zeros;
\item the prover can thus preprocess the sponge state after hashing the whole $136$-byte blocks of those zeros, and then hash the rest of each leaf starting from this state.
\end{itemize}

Conclusion: at commitment, both the FFT and the Merkle leaf hashing cost are proportional to $\nstack$ (not its next power of two). Unfortunately we still pay for the padding inside the $\nstack$ field elements, because some instruction counts are not powers of two. This could be prevented using the Jagged PCS \cite{jagged_pcs}.

At opening, the prover never materializes the level-$0$ weight over the stack. A round's table entries are inner products of the weight with $G$, $H$ and $XH$, linear in the lanes, so the prover keeps the weight factored and projects the lanes, and $X$ times them (Lemma~\ref{lem:mulx}), onto its factors. The table needs only the low $2^\kappa$ coefficients of $XH$; the high part is one coefficient per round, kept to evaluate the folded lane.

\subsection{The Johnson bound}\label{app:johnson}
%=====================================================================

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved} in its general form (arbitrary code).

\begin{theorem}[Johnson bound, agreement form]\label{thm:johnson-general}
Let $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \Sigma^\blen$ be a code, over any alphabet $\Sigma$, in which two distinct codewords agree on at most $\dgr$ coordinates, and let $\agr$ be an integer with $\agr^2>\blen\dgr$. Then every word $\mathbf{c} \in \Sigma^\blen$ has at most $\jl(\agr,\dgr)=\bigl\lfloor\blen(\agr-\dgr)/(\agr^2-\blen\dgr)\bigr\rfloor$ codewords agreeing with it on at least $\agr$ coordinates. With $\rate:=\dgr/\blen$ and $\agr\ge(\sqrt{\rate}+\slack)\blen$, $\slack \in (0, 1 - \sqrt{\rate})$, this is at most $\tfrac{1}{2\slack\sqrt{\rate}}$.
\end{theorem}

Polynomial tuples of degree at most $\dgr<\blen$ are such a code over $\Sigma=\E^{2^\ell}$: two distinct ones differ in some lane, and two distinct polynomials of degree at most $\dgr$ agree on at most $\dgr$ points. This gives Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved}.

\begin{proof}
Let $U_1, \dots, U_L$ be distinct codewords, each agreeing with $\mathbf{c}$ on at least $\agr$ coordinates. For each $j$, fix a set $\aset_j$ of exactly $\agr$ coordinates on which $U_j$ agrees with $\mathbf{c}$, and write $\varphi := \agr/\blen$ and $\rate:=\dgr/\blen$. For a coordinate $i$, let $d_i := |\{ j : i \in \aset_j \}|$; then $\sum_i d_i = L \agr = L \varphi \blen$.

Wherever two codewords both agree with $\mathbf{c}$ they agree with each other, so $|\aset_j \cap \aset_{j'}| \le \dgr=\rate \blen$ for $j \neq j'$, and counting ordered pairs,
\[
\sum_i d_i (d_i - 1) \;=\; \sum_{j \neq j'} |\aset_j \cap \aset_{j'}| \;\le\; L(L-1)\, \rate \blen .
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz on the vectors $(d_1, \dots, d_\blen)$ and $(1, \dots, 1)$ gives $\bigl(\sum_i d_i\bigr)^2 \le \blen \sum_i d_i^2$, i.e.\ $\sum_i d_i^2 \ge (L \varphi \blen)^2 / \blen = L^2 \varphi^2 \blen$; the left side above, which equals $\sum_i d_i^2 - \sum_i d_i$, is therefore at least $L^2 \varphi^2 \blen - L \varphi \blen$. Dividing by $L\blen$ and rearranging,
\[
L \varphi^2 - \varphi \;\le\; (L-1)\, \rate
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
L\, (\varphi^2 - \rate) \;\le\; \varphi - \rate .
\]
As $\varphi^2>\rate$, $L\le(\varphi-\rate)/(\varphi^2-\rate)=\blen(\agr-\dgr)/(\agr^2-\blen\dgr)$, and $L$ is an integer. Finally, if $\varphi\ge\sqrt{\rate}+\slack$, then $\varphi^2 - \rate \ge 2\slack\sqrt{\rate} + \slack^2 > 2\slack\sqrt{\rate}$ while $\varphi - \rate \le 1$, so $L \le \tfrac{1}{2\slack\sqrt{\rate}}$.
\end{proof}
