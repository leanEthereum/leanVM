\section{The polynomial commitment scheme: WHIR \slash Ligerito with affine folds}\label{annex:pcs}

A self-contained description of the WHIR\slash Ligerito polynomial commitment scheme \cite{ACFY25,NA25} over binary fields, with a round-by-round soundness analysis in the Johnson list-decoding regime. The two schemes coincide when WHIR is instantiated with interleaved codes and Ligerito with the Reed-Solomon codes of Annex~\ref{app:ntt}, which gives the novel polynomial basis and additive NTT used for encoding. Following Flock~\cite{Flock26}, we do not use out-of-domain (OOD) sampling at commitment, so our PCS is only list binding. The two fields are those of \S\ref{sec:vm}: we commit to a $\K$-valued multilinear, and opening claims are in $\E$, with $|\E|=2^{128}$.

A fold round does not take a random scalar combination of two halves, as the original schemes do: it takes the \emph{affine combiner} of Chiesa, Yogev, Weissenberg and Zheng~\cite{CYWZ26}, a combination whose coefficient is a random polynomial $\afa+\afb X$ of degree one. Its mutual correlated agreement error is of order $1/|\E|$ instead of $\blen/|\E|$ for block length $\blen$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:mca-affine} and Remark~\ref{rem:scalar-fold}), which is what lets a $128$-bit field carry the $120$-bit target. The price is one coordinate of agreement per fold round, one degree per fold round, and a few more field elements per round.

\subsection{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelims}
%=====================================================================

\subsubsection{Codes, interleaving, and list decoding}\label{sec:codes}

We use the Reed-Solomon code $\RS[\E,\dom,\kdim]$ of Definition~\ref{def:rs-code}, with $|\dom|=\blen$, dimension $\kdim=2^\kappa$, and rate $\rate=\kdim/\blen$. Messages are indexed by $\cube{\kappa}$; the linear encoder $\Enc$ of \S\ref{sec:binary-rs} maps them onto the code and preserves $\K$-valued words. A polynomial is written in the novel basis $(\novel_c)_c$ of Annex~\ref{app:ntt}, and a polynomial of degree below $2^\kappa$ is the same thing as a message $g:\cube{\kappa}\to\E$, its coefficient on $\novel_{\langle u\rangle}$ being $g(u)$. A fold round raises degrees by one, so the analysis also uses tuples of polynomials of degree at most some $\dgr\ge2^\kappa$.

\begin{definition}[Interleaved words, lists]\label{def:interleaved}
An \emph{interleaved word} is a matrix $\orc \in \E^{\cube{\ell} \times \dom}$, of $2^\ell$ \emph{lanes}. A \emph{polynomial tuple} $U=(U_u)_{u\in\cube{\ell}}$ has degree at most $\dgr$ if every $U_u$ does; it \emph{agrees} with $\orc$ on $\aset\subseteq\dom$ if $U_u(x)=\orc[u,x]$ for all $u$ and all $x\in\aset$. For an integer threshold $\agr$, $\lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr)$ is the set of polynomial tuples of degree at most $\dgr$ agreeing with $\orc$ on at least $\agr$ coordinates. A tuple of degree below $2^\kappa$ \emph{decodes} to the message $g_U:\cube{\ell+\kappa}\to\E$, $g_U(u,\cdot)$ the coefficients of $U_u$; distinct tuples decode to distinct messages, hence distinct $\mle{g_U}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weighted claims on tuples]\label{def:tuple-claim}
For a weight $W:\cube{\ell}\times\cube{\kappa}\to\E$ and a polynomial tuple $P$ whose lane $P_u$ has coefficients $p_{u,c}$ on $\novel_c$, put $\inner{W}{P}:=\sum_{u\in\cube{\ell}}\sum_{c<2^\kappa}W(u,c)\,p_{u,c}$: the coefficients of index $2^\kappa$ and above carry no weight. On a tuple of degree below $2^\kappa$ it is $\inner{W}{g_P}$, the inner product of Definition~\ref{def:inner}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Affine fold]\label{def:affine-fold}
Let $\orc$ have $\ell\ge1$ lane variables, and split a lane index as $(\iota,u')$, $\iota\in\{0,1\}$ the variable folded first. For $\premix,\afa,\afb\in\E$, the \emph{affine fold} is the interleaved word with $\ell-1$ lane variables
\[
\orc_{\premix,\afa,\afb}[u',x]\;:=\;\orc[(1,u'),x]+(\afa+\afb x)\bigl(\orc[(0,u'),x]+\premix\,\orc[(1,u'),x]\bigr),
\]
and the same formula with the indeterminate $X$ for $x$ folds a polynomial tuple $U$ into $U_{\premix,\afa,\afb}$. Evaluation commutes with it: $U_{\premix,\afa,\afb}(x)$ is the fold of the values $U(x)$, so a tuple agreeing with $\orc$ on $\aset$ folds to one agreeing with $\orc_{\premix,\afa,\afb}$ on $\aset$. Folding raises the degree by at most one.
\end{definition}

In a fold round, $F:=U_{(0,\cdot)}$ and $G:=U_{(1,\cdot)}$ are the lanes whose folded bit is $0$ and $1$, the premixed lanes are $H:=F+\premix G$, and the fold is $G+(\afa+\afb X)H$. A degree-one coefficient on a lane of degree $\dgr-1$ keeps the result within degree $\dgr$; the premix $\premix$ lets both $F$ and $G$ have degree $\dgr-1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list}).

\begin{theorem}[Johnson bound]\label{thm:johnson-interleaved}
For every interleaved word $\orc$ over a domain of size $\blen$, every degree bound $\dgr<\blen$ and every threshold $\agr$ with $\agr^2>\blen\dgr$: $\ |\lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr)| \le \jl(\agr,\dgr) := \bigl\lfloor \blen(\agr-\dgr)/(\agr^2-\blen\dgr) \bigr\rfloor$. If $\agr\ge(\sqrt{\rate}+\slack)\blen$ with $\rate:=\dgr/\blen$, this is at most $\frac{1}{2\slack\sqrt{\rate}}$. (Proof in \S\ref{app:johnson}: distinct tuples of degree at most $\dgr$ agree on at most $\dgr$ coordinates.)
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{PCS, round-by-round soundness, and grinding}\label{sec:iopcs}

\begin{definition}[$L$-list binding, informal]\label{def:listbinding}
A \emph{polynomial commitment scheme} (PCS) is \emph{$L$-list binding} if every commitment transcript $\mathsf{cm}$ determines a set $\polyset_{\mathsf{cm}}$ of at most $L$ multilinear polynomials, not necessarily efficiently computable, such that for every unbounded prover and every collection of opened claims: if no member of $\polyset_{\mathsf{cm}}$ satisfies all the claims, the verifier rejects except with small probability. $L = 1$ is standard binding.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[RBR soundness, informal; cf.~\cite{CCHLRR19,ACFY25}]\label{def:rbr}
A public-coin protocol is \emph{round-by-round (RBR) sound with errors} $(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \dots)$, one per verifier message, if there is an \emph{invariant} on partial transcripts (not necessarily efficiently computable) marking the positions where a cheating prover has already lost:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item on a false statement, the invariant holds before any message is sent;
\item the prover alone cannot escape it: it survives every prover message;
\item the $i$-th verifier message lets it escape with probability at most $\varepsilon_i$;
\item if it survives to the end of the interaction, the verifier rejects.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Interactive soundness error is then at most $\sum_i \varepsilon_i$. After Fiat-Shamir, it is $\max_i \varepsilon_i$ per random-oracle query~\cite{CCHLRR19,BCS16}.

\paragraph{Grinding.} A verifier message may be preceded by a grind of $b$ bits (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}): the prover sends a nonce, and the challenge drawn right after it, used for nothing else, must have its low $b$ bits zero. A prover that wants a fresh draw of the message then pays $2^{b}$ random-oracle queries on average, so the message's RBR error counts as $\varepsilon_i\,2^{-b}$ against the queries it costs. The security target is $\varepsilon_i\,2^{-b_i}\le2^{-120}$ for every verifier message $i$, $b_i$ the bits ground before it ($0$ for none).

\subsection{Reed-Solomon codes over binary fields}\label{sec:binary-rs}
%=====================================================================

The encoder $\Enc_{\kappa,\rexp}$ of Definition~\ref{def:enc} evaluates $2^\kappa$ novel-basis coefficients on an additive domain of size $\blen=2^{\kappa+\rexp}$. Here $\rexp\ge1$ and $\kappa+\rexp\le64$. The protocol uses three properties, proved in Annex~\ref{app:ntt}:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{fast encoding, for the prover:} $\Enc_{\kappa,\rexp}$ is computed by the additive NTT with $\tfrac12 \kappa \blen$ multiplications and fewer than $(\kappa + 1)\, \blen$ additions (Proposition~\ref{prop:ntt});
\item \emph{MLE-friendly columns, for the verifier:} for every $x \in \dom$, the weight $W_x : \cube{\kappa} \to \K$, defined by $\Enc_{\kappa,\rexp}(g)[x] = \inner{W_x}{g}$ (the relation being valid for arbitrary $g$), admits an MLE efficiently evaluable anywhere in $\E^{\kappa}$ in $O(\kappa)$ field operations (Lemma~\ref{lem:colweight});
\item \emph{multiplication by $X$, for the prover:} $X\cdot\novel_c$ is a short combination of $\novel_{c+1}$ and the $\novel_{c'}$, $c'\le c$, obtained by clearing low bits of $c$, and it never moves a coefficient of index at least $2^\kappa$ below $2^\kappa$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mulx}). So the low $2^\kappa$ coefficients of $XH$, the only ones a claim weighs, depend only on the low $2^\kappa$ coefficients of $H$.
\end{itemize}
A polynomial of degree below $2^\kappa+2^\kappa$ splits as $P=P_{\mathrm{low}}+\Wn{\kappa}\cdot\sum_{j<2^\kappa}p_{2^\kappa+j}\,\novel_j$, since $\novel_{2^\kappa+j}=\Wn{\kappa}\,\novel_j$ for $j<2^\kappa$ (Definition~\ref{def:enc}): its low part $P_{\mathrm{low}}$ is a message of the code, and its high part costs one evaluation of $\Wn{\kappa}$ and of a few $\novel_j$.

\subsection{The protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
%=====================================================================

\begin{protocol}[WHIR/Ligerito with affine folds]\label{fig:protocol}
\ \par\smallskip
\emph{Parameters.} The scheme commits to $f : \cube{\nv} \to \K$ across $\nlev \ge 1$ \emph{levels} $i = 0, \dots, \nlev-1$: variable counts $\nv = \nv_0 > \nv_1 > \dots > \nv_\nlev \ge 0$ with fold factors $\ell_i := \nv_i - \nv_{i+1} \ge 1$ and $\nv_\nlev$ small (e.g.\ $\nv_\nlev \le 5$); per level, a rate $\rate_i = 2^{-\rexp_i}$, $\rexp_i \ge 1$, giving the encoder $\Enc_i := \Enc_{\kappa_i, \rexp_i}$ of \S\ref{sec:binary-rs} with $\kappa_i := \nv_{i+1}$, i.e.\ the code $\mathcal{C}_i := \RS[\E, \dom_i, 2^{\kappa_i}]$ over its domain $\dom_i \subseteq \K$ of size $\blen_i = 2^{\kappa_i + \rexp_i}$ (requires $\kappa_i + \rexp_i \le 64$, and $\ell_i\le2^{\kappa_i}$); a query count $\nq_i$; and the grinding bits $\grind{fold}$, $\grind{batch}$, $\grind{ood}$, $\grind{query}$. (The agreement thresholds appear only in the analysis.)

\smallskip
\textbf{Commit}$(f : \cube{\nv} \to \K)$: $\mathbf{P}$ sends the oracle $\orc^{(0)}[u, \cdot] := \Enc_0(f(u, \cdot))$, $u \in \cube{\ell_0}$, whose entries are $\K$ elements. No OOD sample is taken.

\smallskip
\textbf{Open}$\bigl(\{(W_t, c_t)\}_{t = 1, \dots, J_0}\bigr)$, $1\le J_0\le512$ weighted claims about $f$: $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{batch}$ bits and sends $\lambda \leftarrow \E$ (in the stacked opening of \S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled} the grind comes before the sixteen challenges of the ring-switching map, Annex~\ref{annex:rs}, which with $\lambda$ form one verifier message, one input claim's weight and value depending on them); the running claim is $(W, \rc{0}) := \bigl(\sum_{t} \lambda^{t-1} W_t,\; \sum_{t} \lambda^{t-1} c_t\bigr)$, on the lanes $P_u:=\mpoly_{f(u,\cdot)}$, $u\in\cube{\ell_0}$. For $i = 0, 1, \dots, \nlev - 1$, with $\kappa:=\kappa_i$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{(fold rounds)} For $j = 1, \dots, \ell_i$, on the running claim $(W, \rc{j-1})$, $W:\cube{\ell_i-j+1}\times\cube{\kappa}\to\E$, and lanes $P$ with $\ell_i-j+1$ lane variables, split as $F$, $G$ by the first:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5pt,topsep=1pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{fold}$ bits and sends $\premix_j \leftarrow \E$.
\item $\mathbf{P}$ sends $u_0, v_0, \tabx{0}, v_1, \tabx{1} \in \E$, honestly $u_\iota = \inner{W_\iota}{G}$, $v_\iota=\inner{W_\iota}{H}$, $\tabx{\iota}=\inner{W_\iota}{XH}$, where $H := F+\premix_j G$ and $W_\iota:=W(\iota,\cdot)$. $\mathbf{V}$ sets $u_1 := \rc{j-1} + v_0 + \premix_j u_0$, which is the check $(v_0+\premix_ju_0)+u_1\qeq\rc{j-1}$ solved for $u_1$.
\item $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{fold}$ bits and sends $\wch_j, \afa_j, \afb_j \leftarrow \E$, in this order.
\item Both set $\rc{j} := \sum_{\iota\in\{0,1\}} \eq(\wch_j,\iota)\,\bigl(u_\iota+\afa_jv_\iota+\afb_j\tabx{\iota}\bigr)$ and $W \gets \mle{W}(\wch_j,\cdot)$; $\mathbf{P}$ sets $P \gets P_{\premix_j,\afa_j,\afb_j} = G+(\afa_j+\afb_jX)H$.
\end{enumerate}
After the rounds, $P$ is one polynomial of degree below $2^{\kappa}+\ell_i$, $P_{\mathrm{low}}$ its low $2^\kappa$ coefficients, read as $f^{(i+1)}:\cube{\nv_{i+1}}\to\E$, and $\phigh\in\E^{\ell_i}$ its high ones: $P=\mpoly_{f^{(i+1)}}+\Wn{\kappa}\sum_{j<\ell_i}\phigh_j\novel_j$. The running claim $(W,\rc{\ell_i})$ is a weighted claim about $f^{(i+1)}$.
\item If $i < \nlev - 1$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5pt,topsep=1pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{(commit)} $\mathbf{P}$ sends the oracle $\orc^{(i+1)}[u, \cdot] := \Enc_{i+1}(f^{(i+1)}(u, \cdot))$, $u \in \cube{\ell_{i+1}}$, then $\phigh$.
\item \emph{(ood)} $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{ood}$ bits and sends $z \leftarrow \E^{\nv_{i+1}}$; $\mathbf{P}$ sends $\cood \in \E$ (honestly $\mle{f^{(i+1)}}(z)$).
\item \emph{(query)} $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{query}$ bits and sends $\nq_i$ i.i.d.\ columns $\qset_i = (x_q)_{q < \nq_i} \leftarrow \dom_i$.
\item \emph{(batch)} $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{batch}$ bits and sends $\lambda\leftarrow\E$.
\item \emph{(read)} $\mathbf{V}$ reads $\orc^{(i)}[\cdot, x_q]$, folds it as the rounds folded the lanes, $c_q := \orc^{(i)}_{\premix_1,\afa_1,\afb_1,\dots}[x_q]$ (honestly $P(x_q)$), and corrects it to $c'_q := c_q + \Wn{\kappa}(x_q)\sum_{j<\ell_i}\phigh_j\,\novel_j(x_q)$ (honestly $\Enc_i(f^{(i+1)})[x_q]$).
\item The claim for level $i+1$ is $\rc{0} := \rc{\ell_i} + \lambda\,\cood + \lambda^2\sum_{q}\lambda^{q}c'_q$ with weight $W + \lambda\,\eq(z,\cdot) + \lambda^2\sum_q \lambda^{q} W^{(i)}_{x_q}$, where $W^{(i)}_x$ are the column weights of $\Enc_i$ (\S\ref{sec:binary-rs}), on the lanes $P_u:=\mpoly_{f^{(i+1)}(u,\cdot)}$: $J_{i+1}:=\nq_i+2$ claims, the residual, the OOD and the $\nq_i$ consistency claims, in one.
\end{enumerate}
\item If $i = \nlev - 1$: \emph{(final)} $\mathbf{P}$ sends all $2^{\nv_\nlev}+\ell_i$ coefficients of $P$; $\mathbf{V}$ grinds $\grind{query}$ bits, sends $\nq_{\nlev-1}$ i.i.d.\ columns $\qset_{\nlev-1} \leftarrow \dom_{\nlev-1}$, computes $c_q$ from $\orc^{(\nlev-1)}$ as in 2(e) and checks $c_q\qeq P(x_q)$. Then $\nv_\nlev$ sumcheck rounds (Fact~\ref{fact:sumcheck}, degree two) reduce $\sum_x W(x)\,f^{(\nlev)}(x)\qeq\rc{\ell_i}$ to one point $\fc\in\E^{\nv_\nlev}$, and $\mathbf{V}$ closes by checking the last running claim against $\mle{W}(\fc)\cdot\mle{f^{(\nlev)}}(\fc)$, from the coefficients it holds.
\end{enumerate}
$\mathbf{V}$ evaluates the weights itself at the end: every weight in the protocol is MLE-friendly, so $\mle{W}$ at the point $(\wch,\fc)$ of all weight challenges and tail challenges is a sum of the input weights at that point and of each glued claim's weight at its suffix. $\mathbf{V}$ accepts iff every check above passed.
\end{protocol}

At level $0$ the lanes are $\K$-valued and their table entries pair an $\E$-valued weight with $\K$-valued coefficients; from the first fold on everything is in $\E$. The verifier never forms the weight of $XH$: the prover sends its value $\tabx{\iota}$, and the analysis binds it (Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list}).

Completeness is perfect: the honest table satisfies $(v_0+\premix_ju_0)+u_1=\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}=\rc{j-1}$ in characteristic $2$; by linearity $\inner{\mle{W}(\wch_j,\cdot)}{G+(\afa_j+\afb_jX)H}=\sum_\iota\eq(\wch_j,\iota)(u_\iota+\afa_jv_\iota+\afb_j\tabx{\iota})$; the folded column is $P(x_q)$ because evaluation commutes with the fold (Definition~\ref{def:affine-fold}); the correction removes the high part, leaving $P_{\mathrm{low}}(x_q)=\inner{W^{(i)}_{x_q}}{f^{(i+1)}}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:colweight}); and the final checks are identities.

\subsection{Round-by-round soundness}\label{sec:soundness}
%=====================================================================

\paragraph{Thresholds.} At level $i$, with $\blen:=\blen_i$ and $\ell:=\ell_i$, round $j\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ is analysed against the degree bound $\dgr_j := 2^{\kappa_i}-1+j$ (the lanes start at $\dgr_0=2^{\kappa_i}-1$). Fix a slack $\slack_i>0$, an end threshold $\agr_{\ell}:=\lceil(\sqrt{\dgr_\ell/\blen}+\slack_i)\,\blen\rceil\le\blen$, and $\agr_j := \agr_\ell-(\ell-j)$ for $0\le j\le\ell$: one coordinate of agreement is lost per round, so the level starts at $\agr_0=\agr_\ell-\ell$. Write $\agr^{(i)}_j$, $\dgr^{(i)}_j$ when the level matters, and $L_i:=\jl(\agr^{(i)}_0,\dgr^{(i)}_0)$, the size of the list the level starts on (Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved}).

Fix a commitment $\mathsf{cm} = \orc^{(0)}$ and input claims $\{(W_t, c_t)\}_t$, and define the relation
\[
\relopen \;:=\; \Bigl\{ \bigl(\orc^{(0)}, \{(W_t, c_t)\}_t\bigr) \;:\; \exists\, U \in \lst\bigl(\orc^{(0)};\agr^{(0)}_0,\dgr^{(0)}_0\bigr) \text{ with } \inner{W_t}{g_U} = c_t \text{ for all } t \Bigr\}.
\]
The commitment binds the prover to the list $\polyset_{\mathsf{cm}} := \{\mle{g_U} : U \in \lst(\orc^{(0)};\agr^{(0)}_0,\dgr^{(0)}_0)\}$, of size at most $L_0$.

\begin{theorem}[RBR soundness]\label{thm:rbr}
Suppose that, for every level $i$ and round $j$, $(\agr^{(i)}_0)^2>\blen_i\dgr^{(i)}_0$ and $(\agr^{(i)}_j-1)^2>\blen_i\dgr^{(i)}_j$, so that the Johnson counts are defined, and let $\epsaff$ be the affine MCA error of Definition~\ref{def:epsaff}. Then Protocol~\ref{fig:protocol} is an $L_0$-list binding PCS, and its opening phase for $\relopen$ has the following RBR soundness error at each verifier message, with $\blen:=\blen_i$, $\agr_j:=\agr^{(i)}_j$, $\dgr_j:=\dgr^{(i)}_j$:
\[
\begin{array}{llcl}
\textnormal{batching } \lambda \textnormal{ entering level } i: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{batch}}_i &=& \dfrac{(J_i - 1)\, L_i}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{premix } \premix_j, \textnormal{ level } i: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{mix}}_{i,j} &=& \dfrac{\jl(\agr_j-1,\dgr_j)}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{fold } (\wch_j,\afa_j,\afb_j), \textnormal{ level } i: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{fold}}_{i,j} &=& \epsaff(\blen,\dgr_j,\agr_j) + \dfrac{2\,\jl(\agr_j-1,\dgr_j)}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{OOD challenge entering level } i \ge 1: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{ood}}_i &=& \dbinom{L_i}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\nv_i}{|\E|} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{query message } \qset_i: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{query}}_i &=& \Bigl(\dfrac{\agr^{(i)}_{\ell_i}-1}{\blen_i}\Bigr)^{\nq_i} , \\[2ex]
\textnormal{each of the } \nv_\nlev \textnormal{ tail rounds}: & \varepsilon^{\mathrm{tail}} &=& \dfrac{2}{|\E|} .
\end{array}
\]
Here $J_0\le512$ is the number of input claims and $J_i = \nq_{i-1} + 2$ for $i \ge 1$. In the stacked opening the level-$0$ message also carries the ring-switching map, and its row is $(J_0-1+129)\,L_0/|\E|$ (\S\ref{sec:soundness-outer}). The grinds of Protocol~\ref{fig:protocol} divide these by $2^{\grind{batch}}$, $2^{\grind{fold}}$, $2^{\grind{fold}}$, $2^{\grind{ood}}$ and $2^{\grind{query}}$ (\S\ref{sec:iopcs}); the tail rounds are not ground. The \emph{RBR error}, which governs the Fiat-Shamir compilation, is the maximum of the ground entries over all rounds.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The opening inside the protocol.}\label{sec:soundness-outer} Two terms of the analysis come from the protocol around the opening (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}). First, the stacked opening draws the sixteen ring-switching challenges (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}) and $\lambda$ as one message, after one grind of $\grind{batch}$ bits. For a list member that violates some input claim, the discrepancy of the batched claim is a nonzero polynomial in the seventeen challenges: the ring-switched claim's discrepancy has degree at most $129$ in the map's challenges and is multiplied by a power of $\lambda$, so the total degree is at most $J_0-1+129$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:sz} with a union over the list gives $(J_0-1+129)\,L_0/|\E|$. Second, level $0$ takes no OOD sample, so the commitment binds a list of up to $L_0$ polynomials (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}). The claims the protocol raises on them sit at points drawn after the commitment, so as in Lemma~\ref{lem:ood} at most one list member can agree with the first such claim, except with probability $L_0\,\nv/|\E|$ ($\nv=\nv_0$ variables, no grind). The analysis keeps this term, and it is what bounds the level-$0$ slack $\slack_0$ from below.

The parameters meet the $120$-bit target of \S\ref{sec:iopcs} by construction: the code fixes $\grind{fold}=4$, $\grind{query}=17$, $\grind{batch}=7$ and $\grind{ood}=15$, the smallest batch and OOD values that make every row of every admitted shape (stack size and rate) at most $2^{-120}$, picks the interpolation parameters of each $\epsaff$ by search, and chooses the slack $\slack_i$ that minimizes $\nq_i$ subject to every row. The tail rows are $2^{-127}$.

\begin{remark}[Why not a scalar fold]\label{rem:scalar-fold}
A scalar fold, $\orc[(0,u'),\cdot]+\fc\,\orc[(1,u'),\cdot]$ or the multilinear fold of the original schemes, cannot have an MCA error much below $\blen/|\E|$, at any agreement loss: for every $s\ge0$ with $\agr-s-1>\dgr$ there are words for which a uniform scalar challenge gives an exceptional candidate with probability at least $\bigl(1+\lfloor(\blen-\agr)/(s+1)\rfloor\bigr)/|\E|$ \cite[Prop.~6.1]{CYWZ26} (a construction of \cite[Remark~1.1]{BCIKS20}). For $s=0$ and a constant agreement fraction that is a constant times $\blen/|\E|$: over $|\E|=2^{128}$, a fold round on a block of $\blen=2^{20}$ or more would need more than $10$ bits of grinding to reach $2^{-120}$, the more the longer the block, and the bound of the original analysis \cite[Thm.~4.6]{BCHKS25}, also proportional to $\blen$, more than that. The affine combiner's error is of order $1/|\E|+\blen^2/|\E|^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:mca-affine}), and a few bits cover its constant. The one coordinate of agreement loss is not a weakness of the analysis: with no loss, affine combiners also have error at least $(\blen-\agr+1)/(2|\E|)$ \cite[Prop.~6.2]{CYWZ26}.
\end{remark}

The remainder of this section proves the theorem: \S\ref{sec:tools} states the coding-theoretic tools, \S\ref{pcs:proof} the invariant and the round-by-round argument.

\subsubsection{Proximity tools}\label{sec:tools}

The engine is \emph{mutual correlated agreement} (MCA)~\cite{ACFY25} for the affine combiner, from~\cite{CYWZ26}, a preprint. We state its error as the finite count in the proof of~\cite[Lemma~4.1]{CYWZ26}, with the interpolation parameters left free, as \cite[\S5.1, Finite bounds]{CYWZ26} uses it.

\begin{definition}[Affine MCA error]\label{def:epsaff}
Let $1\le\dgr<\agr\le\blen$ be integers. A triple $(\imul,\iyz,\ithr)$ of positive integers is \emph{admissible} if, with $\iz:=\min\bigl(\iyz,\lfloor(\imul\agr-\iyz-1)/\dgr\rfloor\bigr)$,
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item $\iz\ge1$;
\item the \emph{interpolation inequality} holds: $\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\iz}\max(0,\imul\agr-\iyz-\dgr j)\,(\iyz-j+1)\;>\;\blen\sum_{s=0}^{\min(\imul-1,\iz)}(\imul-s)(\iyz-s+1)$;
\item $\dgr+1<\ithr<\agr$ and $\ithr^2>\blen(\dgr+1)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)\;:=\;\min_{(\imul,\iyz,\ithr)\ \mathrm{admissible}}\ \frac{\iyz^2+\jl(\ithr,\dgr+1)\cdot\max\bigl(|\E|,\binom{\blen}{2}\bigr)+4\dgr^2\iz^2\iyz^3\bigl(\blen/(\agr-\ithr)\bigr)^2}{|\E|^2}.
\]
\end{definition}

The three terms count the coefficient pairs $(\afa,\afb)$ that can be exceptional: at most $\iyz^2$ where the interpolated polynomial's reconstruction fails, at most $\jl(\ithr,\dgr+1)\max(|\E|,\binom{\blen}{2})$ with a persistent rational explanation, and at most $4\dgr^2\iz^2\iyz^3(\blen/(\agr-\ithr))^2$ otherwise \cite[Lemmas~4.3, 4.5, 4.7]{CYWZ26}. The interpolation inequality is the dimension count of \cite[Lemma~4.2]{CYWZ26}, more unknowns than equations, checked in exact integers. For $\blen=2^{20}$, $\dgr=2^{18}$, $\agr=576717$ and $|\E|=2^{128}$, the triple $(3,49,567980)$ is admissible, with $\iz=6$, $\jl(\ithr,\dgr+1)=6$, and its first and last terms together below $2^{-182}$ \cite[\S5.1]{CYWZ26}.

\begin{theorem}[Affine MCA; {\cite[Thm.~1.1, Lemma~4.1, \S5.1]{CYWZ26}}]\label{thm:mca-affine}
Let $\dom\subseteq\K$ have size $\blen$, let $1\le\dgr<\agr\le\blen$, $t\ge1$, and let $f,g:\dom\to\E^t$ be words of $t$ lanes. For $\afa,\afb\in\E$ and a $t$-tuple $P$ of polynomials let $\aset_{\afa,\afb}(P):=\{x\in\dom:P(x)=f(x)+(\afa+\afb x)g(x)\}$. Then, for uniform $\afa,\afb\leftarrow\E$, except with probability at most $\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)$: every $t$-tuple $P$ of degree at most $\dgr$ with $|\aset_{\afa,\afb}(P)|\ge\agr$ has $t$-tuples $U$, $V$ with $\deg U\le\dgr$, $\deg V\le\dgr-1$ and $P=U+(\afa+\afb X)V$, such that $U=f$ and $V=g$ on all but at most one point of $\aset_{\afa,\afb}(P)$ (agreement loss one).
\end{theorem}

For $t=1$ this is \cite[Thm.~1.1]{CYWZ26} with the count of its Lemma~4.1 in place of the closed form $\frac{4}{\slack|\E|}+\frac{2^{14}\blen^2}{\rate^3\slack^{10}|\E|^2}$ (which needs $\blen\slack^2\ge4/\rate$, $\rate=\dgr/\blen$, $\agr=\lceil(\sqrt\rate+\slack)\blen\rceil$). The count holds for every admissible triple: the proofs of \cite[Lemmas~4.2 to 4.7]{CYWZ26} use the specific choices of $\imul$, $\iyz$ and $\ithr$ only to establish (i) to (iii). For $t>1$, \cite[\S5.1, Interleaving and shared agreement]{CYWZ26} packs the $t$ lanes into one word over the degree-$t$ extension of $\E$, with $(\afa,\afb)$ still drawn from $\E$ and acting lane by lane: interpolation and the algebraic degree bounds are unchanged over the extension, each nonzero affine constraint on $(\afa,\afb)$ still has at most $|\E|$ solutions, and the Johnson count depends only on agreement sets, so the same error bounds the event with simultaneous agreement of all lanes and no union over lanes. \emph{The preprint states this interleaved extension with a short justification, not a detailed proof; we rely on it as stated.}

\begin{lemma}[An affine fold round]\label{lem:fold-list}
Let $\orc$ be an interleaved word with $\ell\ge1$ lane variables over a domain of size $\blen$, $f:=\orc[(0,\cdot),\cdot]$ and $g:=\orc[(1,\cdot),\cdot]$ its two halves, of $t=2^{\ell-1}$ lanes each. Let $1\le\dgr<\agr\le\blen$ with $(\agr-1)^2>\blen\dgr$, and $L:=\jl(\agr-1,\dgr)$. Let $W:\cube{\ell}\times\cube{\kappa}\to\E$ be a weight, $W_\iota:=W(\iota,\cdot)$, $c\in\E$, and suppose every tuple of $\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr-1)$, as a pair $(F,G)$ of halves, has $\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}\neq c$. Call $\premix\in\E$ \emph{good} if no $(F,G)\in\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$ has a nonzero coefficient of $X^{\dgr}$ in some lane of $G$ while $F+\premix G$ has none in any lane.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item A uniform $\premix$ is good except with probability at most $L/|\E|$.
\item Let $\premix$ be good and let $u_0,v_0,\tabx{0},v_1,\tabx{1}\in\E$ be arbitrary, with $u_1:=c+v_0+\premix u_0$. Except with probability at most $\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)+2L/|\E|$ over uniform $(\wch,\afa,\afb)$, every $P\in\lst(\orc_{\premix,\afa,\afb};\agr,\dgr)$ has $\inner{\mle{W}(\wch,\cdot)}{P}\neq\sum_\iota\eq(\wch,\iota)(u_\iota+\afa v_\iota+\afb\tabx{\iota})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

This is \cite[Thm.~5.1, Cor.~5.2]{CYWZ26} in the form the protocol needs: one claim on the sum $\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}$, from which the verifier derives $u_1$, instead of two separate claims, the weight challenge entering as $\eq(\wch,\cdot)$, and the error split between the two verifier messages.

\begin{proof}
Distinct pairs in $\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$ agree on at most $\dgr$ coordinates, so the list has at most $L$ members (Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved}); it does not depend on any challenge.

(i) For a member with a nonzero coefficient of $X^{\dgr}$ in lane $u'$ of $G$, the coefficient of $X^\dgr$ in lane $u'$ of $F+\premix G$ vanishes for exactly one $\premix$. A union bound over the list gives $L/|\E|$.

(ii) Two bad events over $(\wch,\afa,\afb)$. $B_1$: the event of Theorem~\ref{thm:mca-affine} for the words $g$ and $f+\premix g$, at degree $\dgr$ and threshold $\agr$; for the fixed $\premix$, $\Pr[B_1]\le\epsaff(\blen,\dgr,\agr)$. $B_2$: some $(F,G)\in\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr-1)$ has $\inner{\mle{W}(\wch,\cdot)}{P_{F,G}}=\sum_\iota\eq(\wch,\iota)(u_\iota+\afa v_\iota+\afb\tabx{\iota})$ for $P_{F,G}:=G+(\afa+\afb X)(F+\premix G)$. Fix such a pair and put $H:=F+\premix G$, of degree at most $\dgr-1$, so $XH$ has degree at most $\dgr$, and
\[
\Delta_\iota(\afa,\afb)\;:=\;\bigl(\inner{W_\iota}{G}-u_\iota\bigr)+\afa\bigl(\inner{W_\iota}{H}-v_\iota\bigr)+\afb\bigl(\inner{W_\iota}{XH}-\tabx{\iota}\bigr).
\]
The pair's discrepancy is $\sum_\iota\eq(\wch,\iota)\Delta_\iota=\Delta_0+\wch(\Delta_0+\Delta_1)$ (characteristic $2$). If all six table entries were right for the pair, then $(v_0+\premix u_0)+u_1=\inner{W_0}{F}+\inner{W_1}{G}\neq c$, contradicting the definition of $u_1$; so $\Delta_0,\Delta_1$ are not both the zero polynomial, and neither are $\Delta_0$ and $\Delta_0+\Delta_1$. Hence at most one $\wch$ makes the discrepancy the zero polynomial in $(\afa,\afb)$, and for every other $\wch$ it is a nonzero affine polynomial, zero with probability at most $1/|\E|$. A union bound over at most $L$ pairs gives $\Pr[B_2]\le2L/|\E|$.

Suppose neither occurs, and let $P\in\lst(\orc_{\premix,\afa,\afb};\agr,\dgr)$, agreeing with the fold on $\aset$, $|\aset|\ge\agr$. As $B_1$ failed, $P=U+(\afa+\afb X)V$ with $\deg U\le\dgr$, $\deg V\le\dgr-1$, $U=g$ and $V=f+\premix g$ on all but one point of $\aset$. Put $G:=U$ and $F:=V+\premix U$: then $F=f$ and $G=g$ there, so $(F,G)\in\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr)$, and $F+\premix G=V$ has no $X^\dgr$ term. As $\premix$ is good, $\deg G\le\dgr-1$, hence $\deg F\le\dgr-1$, $(F,G)\in\lst(\orc;\agr-1,\dgr-1)$, and $P=P_{F,G}$. As $B_2$ failed, $P$ violates the new claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[OOD separation]\label{lem:ood}
Let $\orc$ be an interleaved word with $|\lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr)| \le L$, $\dgr$ below $2^\kappa$, decoding to $\nv$-variable messages. Then $\Pr_{z \leftarrow \E^{\nv}}[\exists \text{ distinct } U, U' \in \lst(\orc;\agr,\dgr) : \mle{g_U}(z) = \mle{g_{U'}}(z)] \le \binom{L}{2} \nv / |\E|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Distinct tuples have distinct multilinear extensions; apply Lemma~\ref{lem:sz} to each difference and union bound.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Proof of the main theorem}\label{pcs:proof}

At level $i$, write $\orc^{(i)}_{j}$ for the oracle folded by the first $j$ rounds ($\orc^{(i)}_0=\orc^{(i)}$), so that $c_q=\orc^{(i)}_{\ell_i}[x_q]$, and $(W^{(j)},\rc{j})$ for the running claim after round $j$, a weighted claim about polynomial tuples with $\ell_i-j$ lane variables (Definition~\ref{def:tuple-claim}). A tuple \emph{violates} the claim if $\inner{W^{(j)}}{P}\neq\rc{j}$.

We now define the invariant required by Definition~\ref{def:rbr}, a condition on the partial transcripts of the opening. In one phrase: \emph{every polynomial tuple close to the current fold violates the current claim}. Precisely, the list below gives the condition just after each verifier message; a transcript ending with prover messages inherits the condition of its last verifier message. Every condition also includes one implicit disjunct, the \emph{escape clause}: \emph{``some verifier check has already failed''} (such transcripts are rejected no matter what follows).
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Start of level $i$} (before any message for $i = 0$, after the query message $\qset_{i-1}$ otherwise): every $U \in \lst(\orc^{(i)};\agr_0,\dgr_0)$ violates at least one of the level's $J_i$ claims (the input claims for $i = 0$, the claims of step 2(f) of Protocol~\ref{fig:protocol} otherwise).
\item \emph{After the batching challenge $\lambda$ of level $i$, and after each fold message $(\wch_j,\afa_j,\afb_j)$:} every $P \in \lst(\orc^{(i)}_{j};\agr_j,\dgr_j)$ violates the round-$j$ claim ($\lambda$ being the case $j = 0$).
\item \emph{After the premix $\premix_j$:} the round-$(j{-}1)$ condition holds, and $\premix_j$ is good for $\orc^{(i)}_{j-1}$ at degree $\dgr_j$ and threshold $\agr_j$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list}).
\item \emph{After the OOD challenge $z$ entering level $i+1$:} the round-$\ell_i$ condition holds, and the multilinear extensions decoded from $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)}_0)$ are pairwise distinct at $z$.
\item \emph{After the final query message $\qset_{\nlev-1}$:} the low part $f^{(\nlev)}$ of the sent $P$ violates the running claim $(W,\rc{\ell_{\nlev-1}})$.
\item \emph{After each of the $\nv_\nlev$ tail rounds (complete transcript):} $f^{(\nlev)}$ violates the running sumcheck claim. The terminal one $\mathbf{V}$ checks against $\mle{f^{(\nlev)}}$ itself, so it rejects.
\end{itemize}
Conditions (i), (ii), (iv) of Definition~\ref{def:rbr} hold by inspection: on a false statement, the first item is exactly the failure of $\relopen$; a prover message changes nothing an instance depends on, except possibly failing a check, which only switches the escape clause on; and the last item is precisely ``some check failed'', so such a complete transcript is rejected. The goodness of $\premix_j$ and the separation clause of the OOD instance are there because the next message consumes them. It remains to bound the escape probabilities (iii); in the proof below we assume all checks so far pass, since otherwise the escape clause already holds.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}]
If the statement lies outside $\relopen$, then before any message is sent every $U \in \lst(\orc^{(0)};\agr^{(0)}_0,\dgr^{(0)}_0)$ violates at least one input claim, so the invariant holds. We follow the protocol and bound, for each verifier message, the probability that the invariant fails to carry over.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Batching challenge $\lambda$.}
Assume that every $U \in \lst(\orc^{(i)};\agr_0,\dgr_0)$ violates at least one of the $J_i$ claims $(W_t, c_t)$. Fix such a $U$. Its discrepancy against the batched claim is $\sum_{t} \lambda^{t - 1} d_t$ with $d_t := \inner{W_t}{g_U} - c_t$, a polynomial in $\lambda$ of degree less than $J_i$, nonzero because the $d_t$ are not all zero. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sz} it vanishes with probability at most $(J_i - 1)/|\E|$, so a union bound over the at most $L_i$ list members leaves every $U$ violating the round-$0$ claim except with probability $(J_i - 1) L_i / |\E|$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Premix $\premix_j$ and fold message $(\wch_j,\afa_j,\afb_j)$.}
Assume that every $P \in \lst(\orc^{(i)}_{j-1};\agr_{j-1},\dgr_{j-1})$ violates the round-$(j{-}1)$ claim. As $\agr_{j-1}=\agr_j-1$ and $\dgr_{j-1}=\dgr_j-1$, this is the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list} for the word $\orc^{(i)}_{j-1}$, the weight $W^{(j-1)}$ and $c=\rc{j-1}$, at degree $\dgr_j$ and threshold $\agr_j$. Part (i) makes $\premix_j$ good except with probability $\jl(\agr_j-1,\dgr_j)/|\E|$. Given a good $\premix_j$ and whatever table the prover sends, part (ii) makes every $P\in\lst(\orc^{(i)}_{j};\agr_j,\dgr_j)$ violate the round-$j$ claim, $W^{(j)}=\mle{W^{(j-1)}}(\wch_j,\cdot)$ and $\rc{j}$ as in step 1(d), except with probability $\epsaff(\blen,\dgr_j,\agr_j)+2\jl(\agr_j-1,\dgr_j)/|\E|$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{The level interface.}
After the last fold round of level $i < \nlev - 1$, every polynomial $P$ of degree at most $\dgr_{\ell_i}$ agreeing with the single word $w := \orc^{(i)}_{\ell_i} \in \E^{\dom_i}$ on at least $\agr_{\ell_i}$ coordinates violates the residual claim $(W,\rc{\ell_i})$. The prover now sends the new oracle $\orc^{(i+1)}$ and $\phigh$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{OOD challenge $z$.}
After $z$, two properties must hold: the one above, which does not involve $z$; and pairwise distinctness at $z$ of the multilinear extensions decoded from $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)}_0)$, a list already determined by the prover's message. The latter fails with probability at most $\binom{L_{i+1}}{2} \nv_{i+1} / |\E|$ over $z$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:ood}.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Query message $\qset_i$.}
Assume both properties of the previous step; the prover has since sent its answer $\cood$. The values $\mle{g_U}(z)$ are pairwise distinct across $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)}_0)$, so at most one member $U^\ast$ can satisfy $\mle{g_{U^\ast}}(z) = \cood$: every other member violates the OOD claim $(\eq(z, \cdot),\, \cood)$, whatever $\qset_i$ is. If no such $U^\ast$ exists, we are done. Otherwise write $g^\ast := g_{U^\ast}$, a message on $\cube{\nv_{i+1}}$, and $P^\ast:=\mpoly_{g^\ast}+\Wn{\kappa_i}\sum_{j<\ell_i}\phigh_j\novel_j$, of degree at most $\dgr_{\ell_i}$, with low part $g^\ast$; distinguish two cases.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{$P^\ast$ agrees with $w$ on at least $\agr_{\ell_i}$ coordinates.} Then $P^\ast$ violates the residual claim by assumption, and $\inner{W}{P^\ast}=\inner{W}{g^\ast}$ as the claim does not weigh the high part: $g^\ast$ violates the residual claim, whatever $\qset_i$ is.
\item \emph{Otherwise.} A uniform column $x \leftarrow \dom_i$ satisfies $P^\ast(x) = w(x)$ with probability at most $(\agr_{\ell_i}-1)/\blen_i$, so all $\nq_i$ i.i.d.\ queries do so simultaneously with probability at most $((\agr_{\ell_i}-1)/\blen_i)^{\nq_i}$; and any disagreeing query $x_q$ makes $g^\ast$ violate the consistency claim $(W^{(i)}_{x_q},\, c'_q)$, since $\Enc_i(g^\ast)[x_q]=P^\ast(x_q)-\Wn{\kappa_i}(x_q)\sum_j\phigh_j\novel_j(x_q)\neq c_q-\Wn{\kappa_i}(x_q)\sum_j\phigh_j\novel_j(x_q)=c'_q$.
\end{itemize}
Except with probability $((\agr_{\ell_i}-1)/\blen_i)^{\nq_i}$, every member of $\lst(\orc^{(i+1)};\agr^{(i+1)}_0,\dgr^{(i+1)}_0)$ thus violates at least one of the $J_{i+1}$ claims, which is exactly the assumption of the batching step at level $i + 1$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Final level.}
At level $\nlev - 1$ the fold rounds end as above: every polynomial of degree at most $\dgr_{\ell}$ agreeing with $w := \orc^{(\nlev-1)}_{\ell}$ on at least $\agr_{\ell}$ coordinates violates the running claim. The prover sends $P$, of degree at most $\dgr_\ell$. If it agrees with $w$ on at least $\agr_\ell$ coordinates, it violates the claim, so its low part $f^{(\nlev)}$ does; otherwise, except with probability $((\agr_\ell-1)/\blen_{\nlev-1})^{\nq_{\nlev-1}}$, some query has $c_q\neq P(x_q)$ and the verifier rejects. No list is involved: $P$ is transmitted. The $\nv_\nlev$ rounds that follow are a plain sumcheck (Fact~\ref{fact:sumcheck}) of degree two on a polynomial that $\mathbf{V}$ holds, $2/|\E|$ per round, so the terminal check against $\mle{f^{(\nlev)}}$ fails.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optimizations}\label{sec:pcs-opt}
%=====================================================================

The committed $f$ is the stack of \S\ref{sec:stacking}: $\nstack$ field elements in $\K$, then zeros up to $2^{\nv}$. Let the row index be the stack's most significant $\ell_0$ coordinates: $f(u, x) := q(x, u)$ for $u \in \cube{\ell_0}$ and $x \in \cube{\nv - \ell_0}$, \S\ref{app:ntt}. Row $u$ is then the block of $2^{\nv - \ell_0}$ consecutive elements of $q$ at offset $\langle u \rangle 2^{\nv - \ell_0}$, the padding is whole rows, and only $\nrows=\lceil\nstack/2^{\nv-\ell_0}\rceil$ of the $2^{\ell_0}$ rows hold data. So we get the following optimizations at commitment:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item the FFT-encoder only runs on $\nrows$ rows (and not the full $2^{\ell_0}$), since FFT-ing zeros gives zeros;
\item a Merkle leaf only contains $\nrows$ elements of $\K$ (proof size optimization), we keep the convention of hashing the full $2^{\ell_0}$ values for simplicity, but we hash them in reverse order, starting from the padding zeros;
\item the prover can thus preprocess the sponge state after hashing the whole $136$-byte blocks of those zeros, and then hash the rest of each leaf starting from this state.
\end{itemize}

Conclusion: at commitment, both the FFT and the Merkle leaf hashing cost are proportional to $\nstack$ (not its next power of two). Unfortunately we still pay for the padding inside the $\nstack$ field elements, because some instruction counts are not powers of two. This could be prevented using the Jagged PCS \cite{jagged_pcs}.

At opening, the level-$0$ rounds never materialize the input weight over the stack: the table entries of a round are inner products of the weight with $G$, $H$ and $XH$, and each is linear in the lanes, so the prover keeps the weight in factored form and projects the lanes (and $X$ times them, Lemma~\ref{lem:mulx}) onto its factors. Only the low $2^\kappa$ coefficients of $XH$ are needed for the table; its high part is one coefficient per round, kept for the evaluation of the folded lane.

\subsection{The Johnson bound}\label{app:johnson}
%=====================================================================

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved} in its general form (arbitrary code).

\begin{theorem}[Johnson bound, agreement form]\label{thm:johnson-general}
Let $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \Sigma^\blen$ be a code, over any alphabet $\Sigma$, in which two distinct codewords agree on at most $\dgr$ coordinates, and let $\agr$ be an integer with $\agr^2>\blen\dgr$. Then every word $\mathbf{c} \in \Sigma^\blen$ has at most $\jl(\agr,\dgr)=\bigl\lfloor\blen(\agr-\dgr)/(\agr^2-\blen\dgr)\bigr\rfloor$ codewords agreeing with it on at least $\agr$ coordinates. With $\rate:=\dgr/\blen$ and $\agr\ge(\sqrt{\rate}+\slack)\blen$, $\slack \in (0, 1 - \sqrt{\rate})$, this is at most $\tfrac{1}{2\slack\sqrt{\rate}}$.
\end{theorem}

Polynomial tuples of degree at most $\dgr<\blen$ are such a code over $\Sigma=\E^{2^\ell}$: two distinct ones differ in some lane, and two distinct polynomials of degree at most $\dgr$ agree on at most $\dgr$ points. This gives Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-interleaved}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:fold-list} applies it to pairs of tuples.

\begin{proof}
Let $U_1, \dots, U_L$ be distinct codewords, each agreeing with $\mathbf{c}$ on at least $\agr$ coordinates. For each $j$, fix a set $\aset_j$ of exactly $\agr$ coordinates on which $U_j$ agrees with $\mathbf{c}$, and write $\varphi := \agr/\blen$ and $\rate:=\dgr/\blen$. For a coordinate $i$, let $d_i := |\{ j : i \in \aset_j \}|$; then $\sum_i d_i = L \agr = L \varphi \blen$.

Wherever two codewords both agree with $\mathbf{c}$ they agree with each other, so $|\aset_j \cap \aset_{j'}| \le \dgr=\rate \blen$ for $j \neq j'$, and counting ordered pairs,
\[
\sum_i d_i (d_i - 1) \;=\; \sum_{j \neq j'} |\aset_j \cap \aset_{j'}| \;\le\; L(L-1)\, \rate \blen .
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz on the vectors $(d_1, \dots, d_\blen)$ and $(1, \dots, 1)$ gives $\bigl(\sum_i d_i\bigr)^2 \le \blen \sum_i d_i^2$, i.e.\ $\sum_i d_i^2 \ge (L \varphi \blen)^2 / \blen = L^2 \varphi^2 \blen$; the left side above, which equals $\sum_i d_i^2 - \sum_i d_i$, is therefore at least $L^2 \varphi^2 \blen - L \varphi \blen$. Dividing by $L\blen$ and rearranging,
\[
L \varphi^2 - \varphi \;\le\; (L-1)\, \rate
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
L\, (\varphi^2 - \rate) \;\le\; \varphi - \rate .
\]
As $\varphi^2>\rate$, $L\le(\varphi-\rate)/(\varphi^2-\rate)=\blen(\agr-\dgr)/(\agr^2-\blen\dgr)$, and $L$ is an integer. Finally, if $\varphi\ge\sqrt{\rate}+\slack$, then $\varphi^2 - \rate \ge 2\slack\sqrt{\rate} + \slack^2 > 2\slack\sqrt{\rate}$ while $\varphi - \rate \le 1$, so $L \le \tfrac{1}{2\slack\sqrt{\rate}}$.
\end{proof}
