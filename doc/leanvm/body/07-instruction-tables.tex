\section{The instruction tables}\label{sec:tables}

The instructions are grouped into eleven \emph{classes}, and each class has its own table (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}). All eleven tables are the same table: a row does the plumbing that every instruction needs, the state step, the bytecode read, the register accesses and the memory accesses, and what the class computes is a Boolean circuit proven by Flock (Annex~\ref{annex:flock}), one instance per row, whose words the row puts on the bus. The exception is \texttt{EXT}, the extension-field product, which is of degree two over $\K$: its table has no class circuit and states the product as identities instead. A second circuit per table, its clock circuit (\S\ref{sec:clock}), orders the row's accesses in time. The tables differ in which words their circuit has, whether the class has control flow, and how it touches memory.

A read of a lookup array (\S\ref{sec:lookup}) is the single flush \pull\ $\tup{\sep,\addr,\val_0,\val_1,\dots}$; the array's producer pushes the entry back as often as it is read.

Write $\acc{k}\tup{\sep,a;\,v_{\mathrm{old}}\to v_{\mathrm{new}}}$ for the access, in slot $k$ of a row with clock $\ts$ (\S\ref{sec:memchan}), of the cell at address $a$ of the array under $\sep$: the flush pair
\[
  \pull\ \tup{\sep,a,\tsprev_k,v_{\mathrm{old}}},\qquad \push\ \tup{\sep,a,\ts+\intk{k},v_{\mathrm{new}}},
\]
where $\tsprev_k$ is the access's previous timestamp, a port of the row's clock circuit, which checks \eqref{eq:order} on it. A read is written $\acc{k}\tup{\sep,a;\,v}$, for $v_{\mathrm{old}}=v_{\mathrm{new}}=v$. A table has no constraint of its own but \texttt{EXT}'s three identities: what a row asserts, its circuits assert.

\subsection{The class table}\label{sec:tab-class}

\paragraph{The circuit's words.} A class's circuit (\S\ref{flock:classes}) reads and writes whole $64$-bit words, its \emph{ports}: the register values $v_1,v_2$, the bytecode's flags and immediate, the result, a memory cell, and so on, as the class needs. Flock packs the circuit's witness $64$ bits to a $\K$-element, so a port IS one word of the class's packed witness $\qcls{j}$, committed once (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}). The table commits none of these words again: they are \emph{virtual} columns, and a claim the bus raises on one of them at a row is routed to the same word of that row's instance in $\qcls{j}$ (\S\ref{sec:stacking}). Putting a port in a bus tuple is therefore what binds the circuit to the machine: the value it reads from a register is what the register tuple carries, the flags it decodes on are what the bytecode tuple carries, the result it computes is what the register write pushes. No constraint ties the two, because they are one committed value.

\paragraph{Columns.} The state $\pc$ and the clock $\ts$, a port of the clock circuit; the bytecode entry's register number $a_1$ and successor $\pc^{+}$; the value read, $v_1$, a port; the flags, a port, for a class that has them; then, for a class that reads a second register, $a_2$ and the port $v_2$; for a class that writes a register, $a_d$, the old value $v_d^{\mathrm{old}}$ and the result $\mathit{out}$ (a port); for a class with control flow, the bytecode's $\Delta,\mathit{link},\mathit{jalr},\exitflag$ and the port $b$; the immediate $\mathit{imm}$ (a port) if the circuit takes one; the memory words below; each access's previous timestamp $\tsprev_k$ and the clock circuit's $\mathit{step}$, ports of the clock circuit. A word is $64$ bits and a register holds one, so every port that reaches a tuple is a $\K$-element as it stands. The same holds of the clock circuit's ports, packed in $\qclk{j}$: the clock, the accesses' previous timestamps and the step are virtual columns too.

\paragraph{Flushes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{State:} \pull\ $\tup{\dsep{ST},\pc,\ts,0}$, \push\ $\tup{\dsep{ST},\pc',\ts+\mathit{step},\exitflag\,(\ts+\mathit{step})}$, where the next $\pc$ is the class's (below): $\pc'=\pc^{+}$ unless said otherwise.
  \item \textbf{Bytecode:} \pull\ $\tup{\dsep{BC},\pc,\opc{j},\mathit{flags},a_1,a_2,a_d,\mathit{imm},\pc^{+},\Delta,\mathit{link},\mathit{jalr},0,\exitflag}$, the control fields for the control class only, and a constant in place of a column the row has not: $0$ for $\mathit{flags}$, $0$ for $a_2$, $\intk{\sink}$ for $a_d$ and $0$ for $\mathit{imm}$, which the entry then has to hold (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}).
  \item \textbf{Registers:} $\acc{0}\tup{\dsep{REG},a_1;\,v_1}$, then $\acc{1}\tup{\dsep{REG},a_2;\,v_2}$ for a class that reads a second register, and $\acc{3}\tup{\dsep{REG},a_d;\,v_d^{\mathrm{old}}\to v_d}$ for a class that writes one, with $v_d=\mathit{out}$ unless said otherwise. A class makes a register access exactly when its circuit has the word, a second read when it takes $v_2$ and a write when it gives $\mathit{out}$, or when it moves the word between the register and its cell (\texttt{LD}, \texttt{SD}). The other accesses would prove nothing, a read of $\regs[0]$ or a write of the sink, and a row that skips one leaves its slot unused.
  \item \textbf{Memory:} the class's accesses, in slot $2$ and up.
\end{itemize}
A padding row (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}) is a row of the class's no-op instruction on zero registers, its circuit instance an honest one.

\subsection{The classes}\label{sec:tab-classes}

The instructions of rv64im and the custom ones are decoded into the eleven classes below, an entry's flags selecting one function of its class (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}); \texttt{lui}, \texttt{auipc} and \texttt{jal} are constants added to $\regs[0]$, \texttt{fence} is an addition of zeros, and \texttt{ecall} a jump to the halt slot. The reference for every function is the specification~\cite{riscv}; \S\ref{flock:classes} gives the circuits.

\paragraph{\texttt{ALU}} (arithmetic, comparisons, bitwise logic, branches and jumps). Ports $v_1,v_2,\mathit{imm},\mathit{flags}\to\mathit{out},b$. The second operand is $v_2\oplus\mathit{imm}$, the decoder making one of the two zero, and $b\in\{0,1\}$ is whether the branch is taken, a whole port so that Flock's empty rows force its other $63$ bits to zero. This is the one class with control flow, and the row derives what it pushes rather than committing it (\S\ref{sec:m3}):
\[
  \pc'=\pc^{+}+b\,\Delta+\mathit{jalr}\,(\mathit{out}+\pc^{+}),\qquad v_d=\mathit{out}+\mathit{link}\,(\mathit{out}+\pc^{+}),
\]
each of degree two, where the bytecode's $\Delta$ is the branch target XOR $\pc^{+}$, so that $\pc^{+}+\Delta$ is the target, and $\mathit{link},\mathit{jalr}\in\{0,1\}$ select \texttt{jal}'s link and \texttt{jalr}'s computed target, both zero elsewhere.

\paragraph{\texttt{LOAD}} (\texttt{lb}, \texttt{lbu}, \texttt{lh}, \texttt{lhu}, \texttt{lw}, \texttt{lwu}). Ports $v_1,\mathit{imm},\mathit{flags},\mathit{cell}\to\mathit{address},\mathit{out}$: the circuit adds the address, extracts the bytes the width and the offset select from the cell and extends them, and hands the bus the word's address with the misalignment bits in it (\S\ref{sec:exp}). A load reads one register, so it has no $a_2,v_2$ columns and reads $0$ off the entry. One access, $\acc{2}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{address};\,\mathit{cell}}$.

\paragraph{\texttt{STORE}} (\texttt{sb}, \texttt{sh}, \texttt{sw}). Ports $v_1,v_2,\mathit{imm},\mathit{flags},\mathit{cell}\to\mathit{address},\mathit{cell}'$, the new cell being the old one with the stored bytes replaced. A store writes no register, so it has no $a_d,v_d^{\mathrm{old}},\mathit{out}$ columns and reads $\intk{\sink}$ off the entry. One access, $\acc{2}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{address};\,\mathit{cell}\to\mathit{cell}'}$.

\paragraph{\texttt{LD}, \texttt{SD}} (\texttt{ld}, \texttt{sd}). Ports $v_1,\mathit{imm}\to\mathit{address}$, the adder alone: a doubleword is its whole cell, so there are no bytes to select and the bus address is the sum itself (\S\ref{sec:exp}). Neither has flags, so both read $0$ off the entry for them. The word moved is a committed column, the same in the register's tuple and in the cell's, so no constraint is needed to say they agree. \texttt{LD} reads no second register and has no $a_2,v_2$ columns; its $\mathit{cell}$ is committed, and it is what $\regs[a_d]$ receives: $\acc{2}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{address};\,\mathit{cell}}$ and $\acc{3}\tup{\dsep{REG},a_d;\,v_d^{\mathrm{old}}\to\mathit{cell}}$. \texttt{SD} writes no register and has no $a_d,v_d^{\mathrm{old}}$ columns; its $v_2$ is committed, and it is what the cell receives: $\acc{1}\tup{\dsep{REG},a_2;\,v_2}$ and $\acc{2}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{address};\,\mathit{cell}\to v_2}$.

\paragraph{\texttt{SHIFT}, \texttt{MUL}, \texttt{MULH}.} Ports $v_1,v_2,\mathit{imm},\mathit{flags}\to\mathit{out}$ for the shifts (the amount being the low bits of $v_2\oplus\mathit{imm}$), $v_1,v_2,\mathit{flags}\to\mathit{out}$ for the low and the high word of a product.

\paragraph{\texttt{DIV}} (division and remainder). Ports $v_1,v_2,\mathit{flags},q,r\to\mathit{out},\mathit{bad}$. The circuit does not divide: the prover supplies a quotient and a remainder, $q$ and $r$, as \emph{hints}, ports that are in the witness and in no column, and the circuit checks $v_1=q\cdot v_2+r$ over the integers with $r<|v_2|$, and the specification's cases for a zero divisor and the overflow. The check's verdict is the word $\mathit{bad}$, nonzero on a wrong hint, which the row puts in slot $12$ of its bytecode tuple: the program is zero there, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:omc} a row with $\mathit{bad}\neq0$ reads no entry.

\paragraph{\texttt{HASH}} (\texttt{blake2s}). Ports $v_2,\mathit{flags},h_0,\dots,h_3,m_0,\dots,m_7\to\mathit{out}_0,\dots,\mathit{out}_3$: the counter is the second register and the finalization word the flags (\S\ref{sec:vm}). The row writes no register, so it has no $a_d,v_d^{\mathrm{old}},\mathit{out}$ columns and reads $\intk{\sink}$ off the entry, and it has no immediate. Its block is sixteen accesses at $v_1+\intk{8k}$ (\S\ref{sec:exp}), in slots $2$ to $17$: reads of the chaining value and the message, whose words are ports, and writes of the result, whose old values $\mathit{out}^{\mathrm{old}}_k$ are four committed columns like $v_d^{\mathrm{old}}$:
\begin{align*}
  &\acc{2+k}\tup{\dsep{MEM},v_1+\intk{8k};\,h_k}\quad(k<4),\qquad
  \acc{2+k}\tup{\dsep{MEM},v_1+\intk{8k};\,m_{k-8}}\quad(8\le k<16),\\
  &\acc{2+k}\tup{\dsep{MEM},v_1+\intk{8k};\,\mathit{out}^{\mathrm{old}}_{k-4}\to\mathit{out}_{k-4}}\quad(4\le k<8).
\end{align*}
The result cannot be written over the chaining value, because a padding row must rewrite what it writes (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}) and no chaining value is a fixed point of the compression.

\paragraph{\texttt{EXT}} (\texttt{extmul}, \texttt{extmac}, \texttt{extmulk}, \texttt{extmack}). No class circuit: the table states the product as identities, and its clock circuit computes what is Boolean about the row.

The three registers hold the addresses of $a$, $b$ and $c$ (\S\ref{sec:vm}). The row reads all three and writes none. So the destination is a read, $\acc{3}\tup{\dsep{REG},a_d;\,v_d}$, with $v_d$ a port of the clock circuit. The row has columns $a_d$ and $v_d$, and no $v_d^{\mathrm{old}}$ or $\mathit{out}$.

The limbs as the row finds them, $a_0,a_1,a_2,b_0,b_1,b_2,c_0,c_1,c_2$, and the new limbs $c'_0,c'_1,c'_2$ are twelve committed columns. Then come nine accesses, one per limb, in slots $4$ to $12$. Limb $k$ is at $\mathit{addr}_k$, with $\mathit{addr}_0=v_1$, $\mathit{addr}_3=v_2$ and $\mathit{addr}_6=v_d$:
\begin{align*}
  &\acc{4+k}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{addr}_k;\,a_k},\qquad
  \acc{10+k}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{addr}_{6+k};\,c_k\to c'_k}\qquad(k<3),\\
  &\acc{7}\tup{\dsep{MEM},\mathit{addr}_3;\,b_0},\qquad
  \acc{7+k}\tup{\mathit{sep},\mathit{addr}_{3+k};\,b_k}\qquad(k=1,2),
\end{align*}
with $\mathit{sep}=\dsep{MEM}+\mathit{base}\,(\dsep{MEM}+\dsep{REG})$.

The clock circuit (\S\ref{sec:clock}) takes four more input ports, $v_1,v_2,v_d,\mathit{flags}$, and gives eight more output ports. The first two are the flags' bits, $\mathit{acc}$ and $\mathit{base}$, each a whole port, so each is $0$ or $1$ in $\K$. Bit $0$ accumulates: $c'=c+a\cdot b$ instead of $a\cdot b$. Bit $1$ makes $b$ a base-field element. The other six are the addresses $\mathit{addr}_1,\mathit{addr}_2,\mathit{addr}_4,\mathit{addr}_5,\mathit{addr}_7,\mathit{addr}_8$, $p+8$ and $p+16$ for each pointer $p$, which the circuit computes with incrementers (\S\ref{sec:exp}).

A base-field $b$ has one limb in memory. Its two high limbs are reads of $\regs[0]$: the clock circuit gates $\mathit{addr}_4$ and $\mathit{addr}_5$ to $0$, and $\mathit{base}=1$ makes $\mathit{sep}=\dsep{REG}$; otherwise $\mathit{sep}=\dsep{MEM}$. This is the one bus tuple whose separator is not a constant. It is a form of degree one in the port $\mathit{base}$, which the bytecode's flags fix. Every other access to a register is a read, or the write at the entry's $a_d$, and no entry has $a_d=0$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}), so nothing writes $\regs[0]$ and its seed's zero is what those reads return. Then $b=b_0$, and the product is $a\cdot b_0$.

The identities say the product. $y^3+y+1$ has its coefficients in $\Ftwo$, so with $d_m=\sum_{i+j=m}a_ib_j$, a sum of products in $\K$, the reductions $y^3=y+1$ and $y^4=y^2+y$ give each new limb as a sum of products of columns:
\[
  c'_0=\mathit{acc}\,c_0+d_0+d_3,\qquad c'_1=\mathit{acc}\,c_1+d_1+d_3+d_4,\qquad c'_2=\mathit{acc}\,c_2+d_2+d_4.
\]
The table's three constraints are the differences of the two sides, each of degree two with every coefficient one, and the table sumcheck proves that they vanish on every row, a padding row's included (\S\ref{sec:air}).

The reads of $a$ and $b$ come before the rewrite of $c$, in slots. So $c$ may be $a$ or $b$.

A padding row multiplies zeros at the address $0$. It writes $0$ over $0$, so each of its accesses pulls the tuple it pushes, and its identities hold.

\paragraph{Sizes.} A circuit's instance occupies $2^{\kblock}$ bits: $8$ for \texttt{LD} and \texttt{SD}, $10$ for \texttt{ALU}, \texttt{LOAD}, \texttt{STORE} and \texttt{SHIFT}, $12$ for \texttt{MUL}, $13$ for \texttt{MULH} and \texttt{DIV}, and $14$ for \texttt{HASH}, \texttt{EXT} having none; $9$ for the clock circuit of a row with three accesses, every class's but \texttt{HASH}'s and \texttt{EXT}'s, $11$ for \texttt{HASH}'s eighteen, and $12$ for \texttt{EXT}'s twelve, which also computes the limbs' addresses (\S\ref{flock:classes}). Flock proves a batch of at least $2^{13}$ bits and at least eight instances, so a table has at least $2^{3}$ rows, and at least $2^{13-\kblock}$ for the smallest of its circuits: $2^{5}$ for \texttt{LD} and \texttt{SD}, $2^{3}$ for \texttt{HASH} and \texttt{EXT}, and $2^{4}$ for the others. Padding rows (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}) supply them.
