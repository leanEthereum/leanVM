\section{Recursion: the verifier as a circuit}\label{annex:rec}

A recursion proof shows that leanVM proofs verify. It is not a run of the RISC-V machine: a second machine, the \emph{recursion machine}, proves a fixed list of rows that check the proofs, with the same bus (\S\ref{sec:gp}, \S\ref{sec:gkr}), table sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:air}), Flock (Annex~\ref{annex:flock}) and stacked opening (\S\ref{sec:stacking}, Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}). The rows run the verifier's core, the protocol of \S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled} short of the evaluations only the program or the circuits fix; those, and a few others the outer verifier is better placed to make, leave the circuit as claims in the outer statement, which the outer verifier settles after the outer proof (\S\ref{rec:statement}). Aggregation trees (\S\ref{rec:tree}) compose recursion proofs, reducing those claims at every node so that only the root's verifier evaluates them.

\subsection{Shapes and circuits}\label{rec:shape}

A \emph{shape} is a program $P$ with its digest, the eleven table heights $\tau_j$ and the rate. It fixes every loop bound of the core: the layout of every stack, the bus's depth $\mu$, the rounds of every sumcheck, the WHIR ladder, its query counts and the depth of every Merkle path. A \emph{circuit} $\rcirc$ for a list of shapes is the list of rows the core executes on one proof of each, and which of their slots carry the same value. It is a function of the shapes alone: the verifier builds it from them, never from a proof, by the same code the prover runs, with every value read from the proof replaced by zero. What depends on a proof is only the prover's assignment of values. In particular no row exists or is skipped according to a value: a query index selects nothing, it is a list of bits that the Merkle rows take as the direction of each level, and the paths are hashed in full rather than pruned.

\subsection{Wires and copy cycles}\label{rec:wires}

A \emph{wire} holds one of three kinds of value: a word of $\K$, an element of $\E$ as its three limbs, or a digest as four words. Every row of every table names one wire per slot, and a slot $s$ of row $z$ carries its wire's value $v$ as the tuple $\tup{\slotkey,v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3}$, unused limbs zero, where $\slotkey(s,z)$ is the integer $\intk{2^{32}g+z}$, $g$ the slot's index among all tables' slots: an index column (\S\ref{sec:idxcol}). The slots naming one wire form a cycle in a fixed order, and the slot \pull{}s its tuple at its own key and \push{}es it at $\slotnext(s,z)$, the key of the next slot of the cycle, a public column of the circuit.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rec-cycles}
If the bus balances, every slot of a cycle carries the same value.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every key is pulled once and pushed once, by distinct slots, so the multiset equality matches the tuple pushed at a key with the one pulled there: the value slot $s$ carries is the value its successor carries. Around a cycle they are all one.
\end{proof}

A wire on one slot is a cycle of one, whose tuple cancels itself: a free value, which is how a scalar the prover sends enters, and how a hint does. An equality the verifier checks merges two wires' cycles into one. A constant, and a word of the statement, is a row of the \textsf{PUB} table, a framework block (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}) whose every coordinate is public: the verifier knows its value. Padding rows have $\slotnext=\slotkey$ in every slot, so whatever they carry cancels, and satisfy their table's identities with zero columns or, for a hash row, as an honest compression.

\subsection{Tables}\label{rec:tables}

Each table's slots are degree-two forms over its columns (\S\ref{sec:m3}), so an output need not be committed: it rides the bus as the form that computes it.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textsf{EMUL}: $c=a\cdot b+d$ over $\E$. Columns $a,b,d$, nine words of $\K$. The slot of $c$ carries $c_0=a_0b_0+a_1b_2+a_2b_1+d_0$, $c_1=a_0b_1+a_1b_0+a_1b_2+a_2b_1+a_2b_2+d_1$, $c_2=a_0b_2+a_1b_1+a_2b_0+a_2b_2+d_2$, the product modulo $y^3+y+1$.
\item \textsf{EXK}: $c=a\cdot k+d$ with $k\in\K$. Seven columns; $c_i=a_ik+d_i$.
\item \textsf{HASH}: one BLAKE2s compression, its words ports of a packed witness of the \textsf{HASH} class circuit (\S\ref{flock:classes}), which Flock proves, plus a selector bit $b$, with the identity $b^2=b$. Its slots are the chaining value $h$, the counter and the finalization word, the message's first half through the selector ($m_k+b(m_k+m_{4+k})$, the left half when $b=0$ and the right one when $b=1$), $b$, the message words $m_4,m_5,m_6$ as an element of $\E$, $m_7$, every message word alone, the output, and the output's first three words as an element of $\E$. A transcript step wires $h$ to the parameter IV and the counter to $(64,\text{final})$; a Merkle node too, its child on the selector slot and its sibling free; a block of a leaf's hash wires $h$ to the previous block's output and its counter to the block's. Every hash row, whatever it computes, is a row of this one table, so one Flock reduction proves them all.
\item \textsf{SPLIT}: a word and its 64 bits, with $w=\sum_i\intk{2^i}\,b_i$ and $b_i^2=b_i$. Read one way it splits a word, read the other it packs bits into one.
\item \textsf{CAST}: four words seen as a digest, as an element of $\E$, as two elements of $\E$ with a zero top limb (a digest's two halves on the stream), and as four words.
\end{itemize}
Each table's summand in the table sumcheck is its two bus forms, weighted $1$ and $\xi$ as the RISC-V tables', and its identities, each with a power of $\xi$ of its own from $\xi^2$, the tables' ranges disjoint, so that the target pins each identity's sum to zero.

\subsection{The core in rows}\label{rec:core}

The Fiat-Shamir chain is a chain of \textsf{HASH} rows: absorbing a scalar is a compression of the chaining value with the scalar and the observe tag (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}), and a challenge is the first three words of a compression with the squeeze tag. The scalars hashed are the wires the arithmetic uses, so what is hashed is what is used. A round polynomial's fixed coefficient is derived from the claim, the others read and absorbed in order; a proof of work is two compressions and a split of the digest's first word, its low bits held to zero. The announced heights and rate are held to the shape's; the final clock is split and held to a live clock at slot zero. The sumchecks, the bus's GKR, its decomposition and the table sumcheck's final identity are \textsf{EMUL} and \textsf{EXK} rows; the tables' bus forms are evaluated at the claimed column values, a product coordinate as a product of two claimed values. Each Flock reduction is replayed up to its matrices. The opening's query indices are the bits of challenges, a query's bits possibly straddling two limbs; each query's leaf is hashed from the shared zero prefix of the absent lanes (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) and its path in full, the direction of each level the query's bit there, and the root is merged with the one on the stream. The query's index as a word, for the induced weights, is packed from its bits.

\subsection{The outer statement}\label{rec:statement}

The outer statement is, for each inner proof, its output, its shape, and what the core leaves:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item the program claim: the table sumcheck's final identity short of the bytecode producer's program columns and of RAM's image, as one evaluation of the stacked bytecode table at the sumcheck's point and the fingerprint's, twisted by the multiplicity bits' Frobenius powers, and one of the image;
\item per Flock circuit, its matrix form $\erow^{\top}(A_0+\lcalpha B_0)\wcol$ with its points and slices;
\item the ring-switched claims' shares of the opening's target and of its terminal weight (\S\ref{sec:ringswitch}), with the map's and the batching challenges, the terminal point, each Flock claim's outer coordinates and the multiplicity bits, which with the matrix forms locate every ring-switched claim.
\end{itemize}
Every word of it is a \textsf{PUB} row on the wire the core computed. The outer transcript's initial state is a digest of the shapes, its first block a digest of the statement. The outer verifier rebuilds $\rcirc$ from the shapes, checks the outer proof against the statement, then evaluates each program claim on the program, each matrix form on its circuit, and each ring-switched share from the claims by the native closed forms.

\paragraph{Soundness.} If the outer proof verifies then, except with the probability its own soundness allows, the bus balances, every identity holds and every hash row is a compression (Annex~\ref{annex:flock}). By Lemma~\ref{lem:rec-cycles} the values form one consistent assignment of the circuit's wires. The free wires on the stream's scalars and the Merkle hints are then an inner proof on which every check of the core holds, with challenges that are the transcript's compressions of exactly those scalars, leaving exactly the claims the statement holds; the outer verifier's settlement of those claims is the rest of the inner verifier. So each inner proof verifies, against the output and the program the statement names. As for every recursive composition of Fiat-Shamir proofs, knowledge soundness of the composition rests on the transcript hash behaving as a random oracle.

\subsection{Aggregation trees}\label{rec:tree}

An aggregation tree proves many leanVM proofs of one program $P$ with one proof that a native verifier checks once. Its leaves are leanVM proofs of one shape. A \emph{lift} node is a recursion proof whose circuit $\rlift$ verifies one leaf in rows (\S\ref{rec:core}); a \emph{first-level node} is a recursion proof whose circuit $\rfirst$ verifies $\arity$ lifts in rows, and a \emph{node} one whose circuit $\rnode$ verifies $\arity$ nodes of either kind in rows (\S\ref{rec:child}); the root is a node, or the first-level node of a tree of $\arity$ leaves, or the lift of a tree of one leaf, and the native verifier checks it alone. This is the shape RISC Zero's lift and join, SP1's compress and OpenVM's leaf and internal circuits have: the RISC-V verifier appears in one circuit, at the bottom, and every node above the first level verifies proofs of the recursion machine with one circuit design.

Every proof of a tree, lift or node, states the same words:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item its kind $\rkind$: $0$ for a lift, $2$ for a first-level node and $3$ for a node, its high bit saying whether it is a node and its low bit whether its children are nodes;
\item its digest $\tdig$ of the leaves' outputs under it: a lift's is the compression of a tag and its leaf's output, a node's the chained compressions of a tag, $\arity$ and its children's digests in order;
\item one claim on each of four \emph{dense} polynomials at prefixes of one point $\fdense$: the stacked bytecode table $\bctab$ of $P$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}), RAM's image $\imgpoly$ zero padded to a power of two, and the fixed polynomials $\fixlift$ and $\fixnode$ (\S\ref{rec:child});
\item for each of the 22 Flock circuits $f$, the values of its matrices $A_0$ and $B_0$ (\S\ref{flock:rows}) at the prefixes of length $k_f$ of one row point $\frow$ and one column point $\fcol$, $2^{k_f}$ being the circuit's instance width.
\end{itemize}
Their number is fixed by $P$ and the circuits, whatever the tree's size. A node's statement is constrained by its rows: the kind is a constant of the circuit, the digest is computed from the leaf's output or the children's digests, and the claims are the outputs of the reduction of \S\ref{rec:reduce}.

\paragraph{Shapes.} $\rlift$ is fixed by the leaf shape and the statement layout, and $\rfirst$ and $\rnode$ by the heights, rate and statement layout of their children and by $\arity$. The lift has heights of its own, $\taulift$, which $\rfirst$ verifies, so a lift commits what its rows need whatever the arity. $\rfirst$ and $\rnode$ share their tables' heights $\taufix$: each table of each is padded to the larger, so that one node circuit verifies a child of either kind of node with the same rows. $\taufix$ is the least fixed point of $\tau\mapsto\max(\tau,\tau(\rfirst),\tau(\rnode(\tau)))$, $\tau(\rnode(\tau))$ the heights of the node verifying children of heights $\tau$, computed together with $\taulift$, on which $\rfirst$ depends and which depends on $\taufix$ only through the statement's length; it exists because a node's rows grow only with the logarithm of its children's size. The shapes a tree admits are then four: the leaf's, the lift's, the first-level node's and the node's for $\arity$, and the root's verifier knows all four. Every recursion proof's transcript starts from one digest of the program, the leaf shape, $\arity$, $\taulift$, $\taufix$, the rate and the statement's length, and its first block is the chained compression of its statement's limbs.

\subsection{Verifying a recursion proof in rows}\label{rec:child}

A node replays the recursion machine's verifier on each child: its commitment root, the bus's GKR and decomposition (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}), the table sumcheck with the tables' bus forms and identities of \S\ref{rec:tables}, the \textsf{HASH} table's Flock reduction up to its matrices, and the stacked opening with its ring-switched claim (\S\ref{sec:ringswitch}), whose share of the target and of the terminal weight the rows compute from the map's six challenges in the Frobenius form, as the native verifier does. The \textsf{HASH} matrix claim is a claim on the same circuit as the RISC-V machine's \textsf{HASH} class circuit, and joins its reduction.

What the native verifier evaluates of the child's circuit are its public bus columns: each slot's $\slotnext$, and the \textsf{PUB} rows' values. The child's statement occupies the first \textsf{PUB} rows, so the rows evaluate their share of the value columns at the bus point from the statement's wires: $\sum_{z<S}\eq(z,\zeta)\sum_j w_{1+j}\,v_j(z)$, the words' limbs weighted by the fingerprint, in rows linear in the statement. Every other public column is fixed by the child's circuit: the $\slotnext$ columns and the \textsf{PUB} constants. Each circuit's fixed columns are stacked, largest first at aligned offsets, into one table of $2^{\fixdim}$ words. The lift's table is the fixed polynomial $\fixlift$; the two node circuits share their heights, hence their layout, and the fixed polynomial $\fixnode$ is $\rfirst$'s table and $\rnode$'s under one more variable, the kind's low bit: $\fixnode(x,0)$ is $\rfirst$'s and $\fixnode(x,1)$ is $\rnode$'s. The bus's decomposition needs $\sum_c c_c\,W(p_c)$ over the fixed columns, $W$ the child's circuit's fixed polynomial, each $p_c$ the bus point's prefix of the column's height followed by its offset's bits (and, on $\fixnode$, the child's kind bit), $c_c$ its weight in the bus (its block's selector times its fingerprint weight, times $\xi$ on the pull side). The prover supplies that sum as a hint, the rows use it, and the claim joins the node's reduction. The child's kind is a word $k$ of its statement. A first-level node holds it to $0$, so that its children are checked against $\fixlift$; a node computes $b=k+2$, the sum in $\K$, and holds $b$ Boolean, so that $k\in\{2,3\}$ and its children are checked against $\fixnode(\cdot,b)$. Every circuit holds its own kind word to its constant through the bus, so a child's kind is the circuit whose fixed columns it is checked against.

A node circuit refers to the fixed columns of the circuits it verifies, $\rnode$ to its own among them, only through such claims, so it is well defined. No commitment to a circuit is needed: the claims are settled against the circuits the root's verifier builds.

\subsection{The per-node reduction}\label{rec:reduce}

A lift's rows leave, from the leaf's core: the program claim, $\sum_i\mu_i\,\varphi^i(\bctab(\varphi^{-i}(\chi),\vec\alpha))+w\,\mathrm{image}(p)$ (\S\ref{rec:statement}), and each Flock circuit's matrix form. The program claim is turned into point claims first: the prover supplies $D_i=\bctab(\varphi^{-i}(\chi),\vec\alpha)$ for each multiplicity bit $i$ and $I=\imgpoly(p_{\mathrm{lo}})$, the rows check $\sum_i\mu_iD_i^{2^i}+w\prod_{j\ge m}(1+p_j)\,I=v$, $m$ the image's variables (RAM is zero above the image), and compute each $\varphi^{-i}(\chi_j)=\chi_j^{2^{192-i}}$ as $(\chi_j^{2^{128}})^{2^{64-i}}$, the first power being two Frobenius maps on the limbs. A node's rows leave, for each child, the claim on its circuit's fixed polynomial and the \textsf{HASH} matrix form, and read each child's carried claims off its statement.

The claims are then reduced, one point per polynomial. A transcript of its own, started from a label, absorbs each child's final transcript state and the child's hints (the lift's: the leaf's final state, the $D_i$ and $I$) before anything is sampled; the child's statement, carried claims included, is bound by its own transcript's first block. Then:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item The dense reduction. With a challenge $\rbat$, the claims $\sum_t c_t\,Q(p_t)=v$ on $Q\in\{\bctab,\imgpoly,\fixlift,\fixnode\}$ become $\sum_Q\sum_x \Omega_Q(x)\,Q(x)=\sum_c\rbat^c v_c$, $\Omega_Q(x)=\sum_c\rbat^c\sum_t c_t\,\eq(p_t,x)$, proven by one degree-two sumcheck over the most variables $n$, a polynomial of $n_Q<n$ variables lifted by $\prod_{i\ge n_Q}x_i$ (back-loaded batching, \S\ref{sec:air}, the low variables bound first here). Its end is $\sum_Q\prod_{i\ge n_Q}\fc_i\,\Omega_Q(\fc_{<n_Q})\,v_Q$ against the prover's values $v_Q$, which become the claims $Q(\fdense_{<n_Q})=v_Q$. The rows evaluate each $\Omega_Q$ at the point, a claim on a fixed polynomial sharing one prefix product of its bus point's coordinates among its terms.
\item The matrix reduction. With a fresh challenge, the claims $u^{\top}(a A_0+bB_0)w=v$ on each circuit's matrices, the fresh ones with $u=\erow$, $w=\wcol$ and $(a,b)=(1,\lcalpha)$ (\S\ref{flock:native}), the carried ones with $u=\eq(\frow,\cdot)$, $w=\eq(\fcol,\cdot)$ and $(a,b)$ one of $(1,0),(0,1)$, are batched and proven by a row phase, $\sum_f\sum_x\sum_c\rbat^cu_c(x)\,g_c(x)$ with $g_c=(aA_0+bB_0)w_c$ over $\max_fk_f$ variables, then a column phase, $\sum_f\sum_y A_0(\frow,y)W^A_f(y)+B_0(\frow,y)W^B_f(y)$, the column weights $W_f=\sum_c\rbat^cu_c(\frow)\,(a,b)_c\,w_c$; the circuits share the challenges, each lifted as above. Its end is checked against the prover's $A_0$ and $B_0$ of each circuit at $(\frow_{<k_f},\fcol_{<k_f})$, the rows evaluating each claim's $u$ and $w$ in closed form: $\erow$ through the skip domain's Lagrange weights and an $\eq$, $\wcol$ through the slices and an $\eq$. The prover computes $g_c$ by one forward walk of the circuit and the column phase's $A_0(\frow,\cdot)$, $B_0(\frow,\cdot)$ by two backward walks (\S\ref{flock:matrices}): the matrices are never built.
\end{itemize}
This is leanVM \texttt{main}'s reduction (a batched evaluation sumcheck for the dense polynomials, Spartan's two-phase matrix sumcheck for the circuits), with every circuit's sumcheck sharing one row and one column point.

\subsection{The root}\label{rec:root}

The root's verifier builds $\rlift$, $\rfirst$ and $\rnode$ from the shapes, holds the root's kind to the one its number of leaves gives (a lift for one, a first-level node for $\arity$, a node for more), verifies the root's recursion proof against its statement with its own circuit (natively evaluating that circuit's public columns, \S\ref{rec:statement}), recomputes the digest from the leaves' outputs and the tree's shape, and settles the claims the root carries: $\bctab$, $\imgpoly$, $\fixlift$ and $\fixnode$ at their prefixes of $\fdense$, and each circuit's $A_0$ and $B_0$ at its prefixes of $\frow$ and $\fcol$ by one forward walk. That is one evaluation of each fixed polynomial, whatever the number of leaves.

\paragraph{Soundness.} Suppose the root's verifier accepts. By the soundness of the root's recursion proof and \S\ref{rec:statement}, the root's rows hold on an assignment whose free wires are its children's statements and proofs. Each child's replay in rows is then the recursion machine's verifier accepting that child's proof against its statement, except that the fixed columns' value is a hint, and the child's \textsf{HASH} matrix form is not evaluated: both are claims of the reduction's input. By the reduction's soundness, if any input claim is false then, except with probability $O((N_{\mathrm{claims}}+n)/|\E|)$ over the batching challenge and the sumcheck's challenges, which the transcript samples after every input claim is bound, one of the output claims is false. The root's output claims are settled true, so every claim its children left, carried or fresh, is true: each child's proof verifies against its statement, that statement's carried claims are true, and the child's kind names the circuit it was checked against. By induction down the tree every lift's leaf verifies (its program claim's point claims and matrix forms being true, as \S\ref{rec:statement} needs), against the output its digest commits; the digest chain binds the leaves' outputs in order to the root's. Knowledge soundness of the recursive composition of Fiat-Shamir proofs is, as for one level, heuristic in the random-oracle sense.
