\section{Keccak by GKR}\label{annex:keccak}

This annex proves that every \texttt{KECCAK} step of an execution (\S\ref{sec:tab-keccak}) writes the Keccak-$f[1600]$ permutation~\cite{fips202} of what it reads. It assumes sumcheck, GKR~\cite{GKR08} and multilinear extensions, and nothing about Keccak beyond its round structure.

\subsection{Credits}\label{keccak:credits}

The design is Lev Soukhanov's. The first Hashcaster~\cite{Hashcaster} proves batched Boolean operations over binary fields with a sumcheck whose first rounds run on the bits themselves. Hashcaster~2~\cite{Hashcaster2} is a GKR for Keccak built on two ideas: packing bits into a polynomial ring by the Chinese remainder theorem, so that an AND of many bits is one product, and having the prover send that product as a polynomial that the verifier checks through a projection~\cite{RMFESkip}. The layered reduction is GKR~\cite{GKR08} specialized to the round structure of Keccak, and the packing is a reverse multiplication friendly embedding in the sense of~\cite{CCXY18}. This annex is that design with sixteen bits packed in degree below $24$, the output not committed, its claims coming from the main protocol, and the closing claims opened by the VM's commitment.

\subsection{Overview}\label{keccak:overview}

The protocol rests on four observations.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A round is one GKR layer.} A Keccak round is a linear map followed by chi, a map of degree two. A claim on the output of a linear map is a claim on its input with transposed weights, so the linear map costs nothing, and only chi needs a sumcheck. GKR runs the rounds backwards: each layer turns a claim on the states after a round into a claim of the same shape on the states before it.
\item \textbf{Only the input is committed.} The prover commits each step's input, and nothing else: not the $23$ states in between, and not the output. The output needs no commitment because the main protocol already holds claims on it, the values its table sumcheck ends with on the \texttt{KECCAK} output lanes. So the $24$ layers run from the output claims back to the committed inputs.
\item \textbf{Sixteen steps become one field element.} Chi multiplies bits of the same step. A reverse multiplication friendly embedding~\cite{CCXY18} packs the bits of sixteen steps into one binary polynomial, in such a way that one product of polynomials computes all sixteen products of bits. The prover sends the claim's weighted sum of these products, the verifier evaluates it at a random point, and there the sixteen steps have become one field element.
\item \textbf{The last claim is a ring-switching claim.} After $24$ layers the claim is on the committed inputs, as bits. A short sumcheck turns it into the $64$ bit slices of the committed column at one point, which is exactly what ring switching takes (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}).
\end{enumerate}

The annex follows that order. \S\ref{keccak:statement} fixes the statement and what is committed, \S\ref{keccak:rmfe} the embedding, \S\ref{keccak:claims} the shape of a claim and the first one, \S\ref{keccak:layer} one layer, \S\ref{keccak:openings} the opening at the end, and \S\ref{keccak:summary} collects the protocol. Then come its soundness (\S\ref{keccak:soundness}), its cost (\S\ref{keccak:cost}), how the recursive verifier evaluates the weights (\S\ref{keccak:recursion}), and the optimization the VM runs, which commits one more state to halve the layers (\S\ref{keccak:segments}).

\subsection{Statement and commitment}\label{keccak:statement}

This subsection fixes how we name the bits of a Keccak state, recalls what one round does, and then says what the prover commits and what the reduction must prove.

\paragraph{The state.} A Keccak-$f[1600]$ state is $1600$ bits. FIPS~202~\cite{fips202} arranges them in a $5\times5$ grid of cells, with a stack of $64$ bits in each cell.
\begin{itemize}
\item The $64$ bits in one cell form a \emph{lane}. The grid has $5$ \emph{columns} and $5$ \emph{rows}, and lane number $\ell=5\cdot\text{row}+\text{column}$ is the cell in that column and row, so $\ell$ runs from $0$ to $24$. Conversely, lane $\ell$ is in column $\ell\bmod5$ and in row $\lfloor\ell/5\rfloor$.
\item Bit $z$ of a lane, for $0\le z<64$, is at height $z$ in its stack. The $25$ bits at height $z$, one in each lane, form \emph{slice} $z$.
\end{itemize}
So a bit is given by a lane and a slice. We number it by its \emph{position}
\[
j=z+64\ell\qquad(0\le z<64,\ 0\le\ell<25),
\]
which is bit $z$ of lane $\ell$. The $64$ bits of lane $\ell$ are the consecutive positions $64\ell,\dots,64\ell+63$.

\paragraph{Moving along a row.} Chi, the one nonlinear step of a round, combines each bit with two bits of the same row and the same slice: the one in the next column and the one two columns along, counting columns modulo $5$. We name these neighbours. For a position $j$ and $0\le k<5$, let $\along{j}{k}$ be the position in the same row and the same slice as $j$, but $k$ columns further along. Since a lane is one cell of the grid, a different column in the same row is a different lane: if $j$ is bit $z$ of lane $\ell$, then $\along{j}{k}$ is bit $z$ of lane
\[
5\lfloor\ell/5\rfloor+\bigl((\ell+k)\bmod5\bigr),
\]
the row $\lfloor\ell/5\rfloor$ unchanged and the column $\ell\bmod5$ moved by $k$. For example, with $k=1$, bit $z$ of lane $3$ goes to bit $z$ of lane $4$, and bit $z$ of lane $4$ wraps around to bit $z$ of lane $0$. Moving $k$ columns along and then $5-k$ more comes back to the start, so $j\mapsto\along{j}{k}$ is a permutation of the positions, and its inverse is $j\mapsto\along{j}{5-k}$.

\paragraph{A round.} Keccak-$f[1600]$ is $24$ rounds. Round $R$, for $1\le R\le24$, maps a state $a$ to a new state $a'$ in two stages.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A linear map $\klin$: theta, then rho, then pi~\cite{fips202}.
\begin{itemize}
\item Theta adds to each bit two parities. Each is the sum of the five bits of one column at one slice, one bit per row. The first is from the previous column, at the same slice; the second from the next column, at the previous slice.
\item Rho rotates each lane by a fixed amount: bit $z$ of lane $\ell$ moves to bit $z+\koff{\ell}\bmod64$ of the same lane.
\item Pi moves each lane, whole, to another cell of the grid.
\end{itemize}
Each of the three only adds bits or moves them, so $\klin$ is linear over $\Ftwo$.
\item A nonlinear map: chi, then iota. Chi adds to each bit the product of the next bit along its row, negated, and the bit after that. Iota then adds the $64$ bits of the round constant $\krc_R\in\cube{64}$ to lane $0$, that is to the positions $j<64$.
\end{enumerate}
In one formula, with $b$ the state between the two stages:
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:round}
b=\klin(a),\qquad a'[j]=b[j]+\bigl(1+b[\along{j}{1}]\bigr)\,b[\along{j}{2}]+[\,j<64\,]\,\krc_R[j].
\end{equation}
The only product is chi's, of degree two. So a round is linear except for one product, and that product is the only part of a round that needs a sumcheck.

\paragraph{The steps.} The VM's \texttt{KECCAK} table has one row per step, and a step is one permutation: it reads a state and writes Keccak-$f[1600]$ of it. The table has $2^{\kbatch}$ rows, where $\kbatch$ is its log-height $\tau$, at least $9$ (\S\ref{sec:arith}). We index the steps by $t\in\cube{\kbatch}$. The reduction proves every row, padding rows included. An honest prover fills each padding row with the permutation of the zero state (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}), so padding rows are valid steps too.

\paragraph{What is committed.} The prover commits the input state of each step, and nothing else. It stores a lane as one element of $\K$: bit $z$ of the lane is the coefficient of $x^z$. The $25$ input lanes of all steps form one function
\[
\qkeccak:\cube{\kbatch}\times\cube{5}\to\K,
\]
committed as one column of the VM's witness (a column of the commitment, not of the grid). Its first argument is the step $t$. Its second argument is a \emph{slot}: slot $\ell$ holds input lane $\ell$, for $\ell<25$, and the other seven slots hold zero. So $\qkeccak$ has $\nkeccak=\kbatch+5$ variables, and it is one region of the stack (\S\ref{sec:stacking}).

The reduction works on bits, so we also name the bits of $\qkeccak$. Its \emph{bit slices} are the Boolean functions $Q_0,\dots,Q_{63}$ on the same domain with $\qkeccak=\sum_{z<64}Q_z\,x^z$ (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}). Then bit $z$ of input lane $\ell$ in step $t$ is $Q_z(t,\ell)$.

\paragraph{What the main protocol holds.} The \texttt{KECCAK} table has no value columns of its own (\S\ref{sec:tab-keccak}). Its $25$ input lanes and $25$ output lanes are virtual columns, defined from $\qkeccak$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Input lane $\ell$ is slot $\ell$ of $\qkeccak$, with one exception. Before permuting, the step adds the last bit of the padding into lane $16$. So the committed slot $16$ holds the lane the machine reads, plus $x^{63}$.
\item Output lane $\ell$ is the function $\kout{\ell}:\cube{\kbatch}\to\K$ that gives, for each step, lane $\ell$ of the permutation of its committed input. It is determined by the commitment, but nobody commits to it.
\end{itemize}
The table sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}) ends with one claim on each output lane, all at the same point $\fc_{<\kbatch}\in\E^{\kbatch}$. We split this point as $(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})$: $r_{\mathrm{low}}$ is its first four coordinates and $r_{\mathrm{pack}}$ the other $\kbatch-4$. For each $\ell<25$, the prover has sent a value $\koutv{\ell}\in\E$ and claims $\koutv{\ell}=\mle{\kout{\ell}}(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})$. These $25$ claims are where the reduction starts.

\paragraph{The statement.} For $0\le e\le24$, let $a_{e}(j;t)\in\Ftwo$ be the bit at position $j$ after $e$ rounds \eqref{keccak:eq:round}, starting from the committed input of step $t$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $a_0$ is the committed input: $a_{0}(z+64\ell;t)=Q_z(t,\ell)$;
\item every other $a_e$ is determined by the commitment, but not committed;
\item the output lanes are read off $a_{24}$: $\kout{\ell}(t)=\sum_{z<64}a_{24}(z+64\ell;t)\,x^z$.
\end{itemize}
The reduction proves that the claims on the output lanes are true:
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:statement}
\koutv{\ell}=\mle{\kout{\ell}}(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})\quad\text{for all }\ell<25.
\end{equation}
In words: the values the main protocol holds for the output lanes are those of the permutation of the committed inputs. Nothing more is needed, because the input lanes are committed and the bus binds them to memory (\S\ref{sec:tab-keccak}).

\subsection{The embedding}\label{keccak:rmfe}

Chi multiplies bits of the same step. To multiply the bits of many steps with one operation, the protocol groups sixteen consecutive steps into a \emph{pack}: a step index splits as $t=(i,p)$, with $i\in\cube{4}$ its low four bits and $p\in\cube{\kbatch-4}$ the pack. The bits a pack holds at one position are a vector of $\Ftwo^{16}$, and the lemma below moves it into a polynomial ring where a bitwise AND is one product.

\begin{lemma}[Embedding]\label{lem:rmfe}
There are $\Ftwo$-linear maps $\kemb:\Ftwo^{16}\to\Ftwo^{\le23}[X]$ and $\kproj:\Ftwo^{\le46}[X]\to\Ftwo^{16}$, on polynomials of degree at most $23$ and $46$, such that, for all $u,v\in\Ftwo^{16}$ and with $\wedge$ the coordinatewise product,
\[
\kproj\bigl(\kemb(u)\cdot\kemb(v)\bigr)=u\wedge v.
\]
\end{lemma}

The idea is the Chinese remainder theorem: a polynomial is cut into its residues modulo a few small coprime polynomials, each residue holding a few bits as its values, and a product of polynomials multiplies the residues, hence the bits, as long as no residue wraps around its modulus.

\begin{proof}
Take the seven pairwise coprime polynomials
\[
X,\quad X+1,\quad X^3+X+1,\quad X^3+X^2+1,\quad X^5+X^2+1,\quad X^5+X^3+1,\quad X^5+X^3+X^2+X+1,
\]
holding $1,1,2,2,3,3,3$ bits respectively, fifteen in all, and let $f$ be their product, of degree $23$. A factor holding $n$ bits receives the polynomial of degree below $n$ that takes those bits as its values at $0$ and $1$ and, when $n=3$, as its coefficient of $X^2$ (its value at infinity). By the Chinese remainder theorem one polynomial of degree at most $22$ has these seven residues. $\kemb(u)$ is that polynomial for the first fifteen bits of $u$, plus $u_{15}\,f$.

A product $\kemb(u)\kemb(v)$ has degree at most $46$. Since $f$ vanishes modulo every factor, its residue modulo a factor is the product of the two residues, which has degree at most $2(n-1)$, below the factor's degree, and so is not reduced: its values at $0$ and $1$ and its leading coefficient are the products of those of the two residues. The coefficient of $X^{46}$ is $u_{15}v_{15}$, both other terms having degree at most $45$. $\kproj$ reads the first fifteen bits off the seven residues and the last off the coefficient of $X^{46}$, all of which are $\Ftwo$-linear in the $47$ coefficients.
\end{proof}

Write $\kemb_i$ for the embedding of the $i$-th unit vector and $\kemb_{\mathbf 1}=\sum_i\kemb_i$ for that of the all-ones vector, so $\kproj(\kemb_{\mathbf 1}\cdot\kemb(u))=u$: multiplying by $\kemb_{\mathbf 1}$ is how a single embedding, not a product, is read back. Each coordinate of $\kproj$ is the parity of a fixed set of coefficients, and extends to an $\E$-linear functional on $\E^{\le46}[X]$. For $\kw\in\E^{16}$ put
\[
\kproj_{\kw}(G)=\sum_{i\in\cube{4}}\kw_i\,\kproj(G)_i\qquad(G\in\E^{\le46}[X]),
\]
the projection's sixteen bits combined with the weights $\kw$.

\subsection{Claims}\label{keccak:claims}

Every claim of the reduction, from the first to the last, has one shape, so that a layer can take one in and give one out.

\begin{definition}[Claim]\label{def:keccak-claim}
A \emph{claim at depth $d$}, for $0\le d\le24$, is a tuple $(c,\kw,r,\kpw)$ of a value $c\in\E$, \emph{instance weights} $\kw\in\E^{16}$, a point $r\in\E^{\kbatch-4}$ and \emph{position weights} $\kpw\in\E^{1600}$. It asserts
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:claim}
c=\sum_{p\in\cube{\kbatch-4}}\ \sum_{i\in\cube{4}}\ \sum_{j<1600}\eq(r,p)\,\kw_i\,\kpw_j\;a_{24-d}\bigl(j;(i,p)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Write $\kwsum=\sum_i\kw_i$.
\end{definition}

So a claim is a weighted sum of the bits of the state $d$ rounds before the output, and a bit's weight is a product of three factors, each with its own origin:
\begin{itemize}
\item an equality table $\eq(r,\cdot)$ over the packs, which is what a sumcheck over them leaves;
\item arbitrary weights $\kw$ over the sixteen steps of a pack, which the embedding sets (step 2 of a layer);
\item arbitrary weights $\kpw$ over the $1600$ positions, which the transposed linear map scrambles (step 6 of a layer).
\end{itemize}
A claim at depth $0$ speaks of the output, and a claim at depth $24$ of the input, which is committed.

\paragraph{The first claim.} It is made at the output claims' point. The verifier samples $r_{\mathrm{lane}}\in\E^{5}$, and the first claim is $(c,\kw,r,\kpw)$ at depth $0$ with $r=r_{\mathrm{pack}}$,
\[
\kw_i=\eq(r_{\mathrm{low}},i),\qquad \kpw_{z+64\ell}=\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)\,x^{z},\qquad c=\sum_{\ell<25}\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)\,\koutv{\ell}.
\]
Weighting bit $z$ of a lane by $x^z$ adds the lane's bits back up into the lane as a $\K$-element, so by Fact~\ref{fact:eq} the right side of \eqref{keccak:eq:claim} is $\sum_{\ell}\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)\,\mle{\kout{\ell}}(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})$: the first claim is the random combination, with coefficients $\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)$, of the $25$ claims on the output lanes, and it holds if they all do. The verifier computes its value $c$ from the $\koutv{\ell}$.

\subsection{A layer}\label{keccak:layer}

Layer $d$, for $0\le d<24$, turns a claim $(c,\kw,r,\kpw)$ at depth $d$ into a claim at depth $d+1$. It undoes round $24-d$. Let $b=\klin(a_{23-d})$ be the state that round applies chi to, so that by \eqref{keccak:eq:round}
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:chi}
a_{24-d}(j;t)=b(j;t)+\bigl(1+b(\along{j}{1};t)\bigr)\,b(\along{j}{2};t)+[\,j<64\,]\,\krc_{24-d}[j].
\end{equation}
At a glance, a layer is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l@{\qquad}l@{\qquad}l}
step & who & what \\\hline
1 & prover & sends the product polynomial $G$ ($47$ elements) \\
& verifier & checks the projection of $G$ against $c$ \\
2 & verifier & samples $\kpt$; at $\kpt$ a pack is one field element \\
3 & both & sumcheck over packs ($2(\kbatch-4)$ elements) \\
4 & both & sumcheck over positions ($33$ elements) \\
& prover & sends $\kev_0,\kev_1,\kev_2$ \\
& verifier & checks the last sumcheck equation \\
5 & verifier & samples $\kbeta$, batching the three values into one claim \\
6 & both & pull the claim through $\klin$ by transposing its weights
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{1. Product polynomial.} Chi multiplies bits of one step, and the embedding (Lemma~\ref{lem:rmfe}) multiplies the bits of sixteen steps at once. So the prover embeds every pack of $b$, writing $S_{p,j}$ for the embedding of pack $p$ at position $j$. Since NOT $u$ is $1+u$, and the constant $1$ embeds as $\kemb_{\mathbf 1}$, the polynomial $G_{p,j}$ below projects to the pack's sixteen bits of \eqref{keccak:eq:chi} at position $j$, less the round constant:
\[
S_{p,j}=\kemb\Bigl(\bigl(b(j;(i,p))\bigr)_{i\in\cube{4}}\Bigr),\qquad
G_{p,j}=\kemb_{\mathbf 1}\,S_{p,j}+\bigl(\kemb_{\mathbf 1}+S_{p,\along{j}{1}}\bigr)\,S_{p,\along{j}{2}}.
\]
The prover sends the $47$ coefficients of the claim's weighted sum of these,
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:product}
G(X)=\sum_{p\in\cube{\kbatch-4}}\ \sum_{j<1600}\eq(r,p)\,\kpw_j\,G_{p,j}(X)\ \in\E^{\le46}[X].
\end{equation}
Applying $\kproj_{\kw}$ to \eqref{keccak:eq:product} gives the right side of \eqref{keccak:eq:claim} without its round constant, which is public and the same in every step. Since $\sum_p\eq(r,p)=1$, the verifier checks
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:projection}
\kproj_{\kw}(G)+\kwsum\sum_{z<64}\krc_{24-d}[z]\,\kpw_z\ \qeq\ c.
\end{equation}
The claim is now a claim about $G$: that it is the polynomial \eqref{keccak:eq:product}.

\paragraph{2. Evaluation.} The verifier samples $\kpt\in\E$, and the claim about $G$ becomes the claim that $G(\kpt)$ is the value of \eqref{keccak:eq:product} at $\kpt$. At $\kpt$ an embedding is a single field element, linear in the sixteen bits it embeds, with the unit embeddings at $\kpt$ for coefficients. These are the next claim's instance weights:
\[
\kw'_i=\kemb_i(\kpt),\qquad \kwsum'=\sum_i\kw'_i=\kemb_{\mathbf 1}(\kpt).
\]
So $S_{p,j}(\kpt)=\sum_i\kw'_i\,b(j;(i,p))$. Write $b^{\kpt}_j(p)$ for it, a function on $\cube{\kbatch-4}$ for each position $j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:at-s}
G(\kpt)=\sum_{p\in\cube{\kbatch-4}}\eq(r,p)\sum_{j<1600}\kpw_j\Bigl(\kwsum'\,b^{\kpt}_j(p)+\bigl(\kwsum'+b^{\kpt}_{\along{j}{1}}(p)\bigr)\,b^{\kpt}_{\along{j}{2}}(p)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
a sumcheck claim whose left side the verifier computes from the coefficients it holds. This is the heart of the protocol: the sixteen steps of a pack have become one field element, at the price of new instance weights $\kw'$, and the right side has the form of chi again, on field elements instead of bits.

\paragraph{3. Packs.} The parties run sumcheck on \eqref{keccak:eq:at-s} over its $\kbatch-4$ variables, with each $b^{\kpt}_j$ replaced by its multilinear extension. The summand is $\eq(r,\cdot)$ times a cofactor of degree two, so a round costs two $\E$-elements (\S\ref{sec:prelim}). With $\fc\in\E^{\kbatch-4}$ the challenges, it ends on the claim
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:after-packs}
\rc{\kbatch-4}=\eq(r,\fc)\sum_{j<1600}\kpw_j\Bigl(\kwsum'\,\mle{b^{\kpt}_j}(\fc)+\bigl(\kwsum'+\mle{b^{\kpt}_{\along{j}{1}}}(\fc)\bigr)\,\mle{b^{\kpt}_{\along{j}{2}}}(\fc)\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{4. Positions.} What is left is a sum over the $1600$ positions, and a second sumcheck binds them. The positions sit in $\cube{11}$, the low six coordinates the slice and the high five the lane, and every table indexed by a position is zero on the $448$ points whose lane is $25$ or more. The parties run sumcheck on \eqref{keccak:eq:after-packs} over these eleven variables. Its summand is a product of three multilinears, the position weights being one of them, so a round costs three $\E$-elements. With $\fc^{\mathrm{pos}}\in\E^{11}$ the challenges, the prover sends
\[
\kev_k=\sum_{j<1600}\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}},j)\,\mle{b^{\kpt}_{\along{j}{k}}}(\fc)\qquad(k=0,1,2),
\]
the embedded state at the positions $0$, $1$ and $2$ columns along from one point. The verifier evaluates the weights there itself, $\mle{\kpw}(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}})=\sum_{j<1600}\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}},j)\,\kpw_j$, and checks the sumcheck's final claim:
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:terminal}
\eq(r,\fc)\;\mle{\kpw}(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}})\;\bigl(\kwsum'\,\kev_0+(\kwsum'+\kev_1)\,\kev_2\bigr)\ \qeq\ \rc{\kbatch+7}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{5. Batching.} The prover's three values are three claims on one state, at shifted positions. The verifier samples $\kbeta\in\E$ and they become one, of value $c'=\kev_0+\kbeta\,\kev_1+\kbeta^{2}\,\kev_2$. Moving each shift from the state onto the weights, $k$ columns along being undone by $5-k$,
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:flat}
c'=\sum_{j<1600}\kpw^{\flat}_j\,\mle{b^{\kpt}_j}(\fc),\qquad
\kpw^{\flat}_j=\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}},j)+\kbeta\,\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}},\along{j}{4})+\kbeta^{2}\,\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}},\along{j}{3}),
\end{equation}
and by Fact~\ref{fact:eq}, $\mle{b^{\kpt}_j}(\fc)=\sum_{p,i}\eq(\fc,p)\,\kw'_i\,b(j;(i,p))$. So $(c',\kw',\fc,\kpw^{\flat})$ is a claim of the shape \eqref{keccak:eq:claim}, on $b$ in place of $a_{24-d}$.

\paragraph{6. Linear map.} $b(\cdot;t)=\klin\bigl(a_{23-d}(\cdot;t)\bigr)$ in every step, so with $\klin^{\top}$ the transpose of $\klin$, extended to $\E^{1600}$,
\[
\sum_{j<1600}\kpw^{\flat}_j\,b(j;t)=\sum_{j<1600}\bigl(\klin^{\top}\kpw^{\flat}\bigr)_j\,a_{23-d}(j;t).
\]
The layer outputs the claim $(c',\,\kw',\,\fc,\,\klin^{\top}\kpw^{\flat})$ at depth $d+1$, with no further interaction. It has the shape of the claim the layer took in, so the layers chain.

\subsection{The opening}\label{keccak:openings}

Twenty-four layers take the first claim to a claim $(c,\kw,r,\kpw)$ at depth $24$, on the committed input: $a_{0}(z+64\ell;t)=Q_z(t,\ell)$. Its weights are general, so it is a claim on the $64$ bit slices:
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:in}
c=\sum_{i\in\cube{4}}\kw_i\sum_{o\in\cube{5}}\sum_{z<64}\kpw^{\mathrm{in}}(o,z)\;\mle{Q_z}\bigl(i,r,o\bigr),\qquad
\kpw^{\mathrm{in}}(\ell,\,z)=\kpw_{z+64\ell},
\end{equation}
with $\kpw^{\mathrm{in}}$ zero on the slots from $25$ on. The parties run sumcheck over the four variables of $i$, with challenges $\fc^{\mathrm{low}}\in\E^{4}$, then over the five of the slot, with challenges $\fc^{\mathrm{in}}\in\E^{5}$, both of degree two (two $\E$-elements a round). The prover sends $s_0,\dots,s_{63}\in\E$ and the verifier checks
\[
\mle{\kw}(\fc^{\mathrm{low}})\sum_{z<64}\mle{\kpw^{\mathrm{in}}}(\fc^{\mathrm{in}},z)\,s_z
\]
against the final claim, extending $\kpw^{\mathrm{in}}$ in the slot only. What is left is the family
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:in-claim}
s_z=\mle{Q_z}\bigl(\fc^{\mathrm{low}},r,\fc^{\mathrm{in}}\bigr)\qquad(0\le z<64),
\end{equation}
$64$ bit slices at one shared point of $\E^{\nkeccak}$, which is exactly what ring switching takes (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}).

\subsection{Protocol summary}\label{keccak:summary}

\begin{protocol}[Keccak by GKR]\label{keccak:protocol}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier samples $r_{\mathrm{lane}}$, which fixes the first claim (\S\ref{keccak:claims}).
\item For $d=0,\dots,23$, layer $d$ (\S\ref{keccak:layer}): the prover sends $G$; the verifier checks \eqref{keccak:eq:projection} and samples $\kpt$; the parties run the $\kbatch-4$ rounds over packs and the $11$ rounds over positions; the prover sends $\kev_0,\kev_1,\kev_2$; the verifier checks \eqref{keccak:eq:terminal} and samples $\kbeta$.
\item The parties open the last claim (\S\ref{keccak:openings}): four rounds, five rounds, then the prover sends $s_0,\dots,s_{63}$.
\end{enumerate}
What is left for the commitment scheme is the family \eqref{keccak:eq:in-claim}, its one ring-switched claim (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}). The claims on the output lanes leave the main protocol's pool: the reduction has consumed them.
\end{protocol}

\subsection{Soundness analysis}\label{keccak:soundness}

The argument has the usual GKR shape. If the statement fails, the first claim is false; a layer turns a false claim into a true one only with small probability; and a false claim at the end contradicts a true opening of the commitment.

The commitment determines $\qkeccak$, hence every $a_{e}$ and every $\kout{\ell}$, before any challenge is drawn, and the commitment scheme proves the claims opened on $\qkeccak$. So take \eqref{keccak:eq:in-claim} to be true, up to the error of the commitment scheme and the $2^{-160}$ of Annex~\ref{annex:rs}, and suppose \eqref{keccak:eq:statement} fails. Call a claim at depth $d$ \emph{true} if \eqref{keccak:eq:claim} holds.

\paragraph{The first claim.} The difference between its value and the right side of \eqref{keccak:eq:claim} is
\[
B(r_{\mathrm{lane}})=\sum_{\ell<25}\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)\bigl(\koutv{\ell}+\mle{\kout{\ell}}(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})\bigr),
\]
a polynomial of degree at most five in $r_{\mathrm{lane}}$, fixed before $r_{\mathrm{lane}}$ is drawn, and nonzero as soon as \eqref{keccak:eq:statement} fails. So the first claim is false except with probability $5/|\E|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sz}).

\paragraph{A layer.} Let the claim entering layer $d$ be false. The polynomial of \eqref{keccak:eq:product} satisfies \eqref{keccak:eq:projection} with the true value in place of $c$, so a prover passing that check sent some other polynomial of degree at most $46$, and the two agree at $\kpt$ with probability at most $46/|\E|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sz}). Otherwise \eqref{keccak:eq:at-s} is a false sumcheck claim. Both sumchecks have summands of degree three, the equality factor included, so the false claim survives their $\kbatch+7$ rounds with probability at most $3(\kbatch+7)/|\E|$, and otherwise \eqref{keccak:eq:terminal} fails for the true $\kev_k$: one of the three sent is wrong. The combination $c'$ is then wrong except with probability $2/|\E|$, a nonzero polynomial of degree two in $\kbeta$ having two roots. Steps 5 and 6 are identities. A false claim therefore leaves a layer true with probability at most $(3\kbatch+69)/|\E|$.

\paragraph{The opening.} If the first claim is false, the claim at depth $24$ is false too, except with the layers' probability, at most $24(3\kbatch+69)/|\E|$. The sumcheck on \eqref{keccak:eq:in} then starts from a false claim, survives its nine rounds with probability at most $18/|\E|$, and otherwise its final check forces a wrong $s_z$, contradicting \eqref{keccak:eq:in-claim}.

\paragraph{Total.} A union bound gives
\[
\frac{5+24\,(3\kbatch+69)+18}{|\E|}=\frac{72\,\kbatch+1679}{|\E|}<2^{-180}
\]
for $\kbatch\le32$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), next to the errors of ring switching and of the commitment scheme.

\paragraph{Completeness.} An honest prover that does not abort is always accepted. The prover computes in the basis of the powers of $\kpt$, which exists unless $\kpt$ lies in a proper subfield of $\E$, and aborts in that case. The proper subfields have at most $2^{96}$ elements each and fewer than $2^{97}$ together, so this has probability below $2^{-95}$ a layer.

\subsection{Cost}\label{keccak:cost}

\paragraph{Proof.} A layer costs $47+2(\kbatch-4)+33+3=2\kbatch+75$ elements of $\E$, and the opening $82$. With $\kbatch=16$ that is about $2650$ elements in all.

\paragraph{Prover.} The work is in step 1 and in the first rounds of step 3, and is kept on bits as long as possible.
\begin{itemize}
\item The witness is bit-sliced: a machine word holds one position of $64$ steps, four packs, so building it is running Keccak on $64$ steps at a time.
\item $G$ is computed before $\kpt$ exists, on bits: a pack and a position cost one carry-less product of two binary polynomials of degree $23$, and field arithmetic starts only when the products meet their weights.
\item The first four rounds of step 3 also run on bits~\cite{Hashcaster}: the prover evaluates the summand on $\{0,1,\infty\}^4$, where every value is a sum of products of bit-level combinations of packs.
\item After $\kpt$, the prover computes in the basis of the powers of $\kpt$, in which a pack's value $b^{\kpt}_j$ is its embedding's bits and multiplying by it is a carry-less product.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verifier.} A layer's only real work is the $1600$ position weights: applying $\klin^{\top}$, and $\mle{\kpw}(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}})$. \S\ref{keccak:recursion} shows how a verifier that cannot afford them one by one evaluates them from two points.

\subsection{Evaluating the weights in the recursion}\label{keccak:recursion}

A native verifier holds the $1600$ position weights of each claim and applies $\klin^{\top}$ to them. The recursive verifier (\S\ref{sec:recursion}) never writes them out. Every claim after the first has weights $\klin^{\top}\kpw^{\flat}$, and by \eqref{keccak:eq:flat} $\kpw^{\flat}$ factors as a lane table times a slice table, a shift along the columns moving lanes only:
\[
\kpw^{\flat}_{z+64\ell}=\kpw^{\mathrm{ln}}_{\ell}\,\kpw^{\mathrm{sl}}_{z},\qquad
\kpw^{\mathrm{sl}}_z=\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}}_{<6},z),\qquad
\kpw^{\mathrm{ln}}_\ell=\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}}_{\ge6},\ell)+\kbeta\,\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}}_{\ge6},\along{\ell}{4})+\kbeta^{2}\,\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}}_{\ge6},\along{\ell}{3}),
\]
the shift of a lane being that of its positions. A claim is thus carried as $25$ lane weights and a point of $\E^{6}$. The verifier needs its weights in three places, and each is a pairing $\inner{\klin^{\top}\kpw^{\flat}}{g}=\inner{\kpw^{\flat}}{\klin(g)}$ against a $g$ that factors the same way, $g_{z+64\ell}=g^{\mathrm{ln}}_\ell\,g^{\mathrm{sl}}_z$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mle{\kpw}(\fc^{\mathrm{pos}})$ in \eqref{keccak:eq:terminal} of the next layer, where $g$ is the equality table of that layer's point;
\item the sum against $s_z$ that closes \eqref{keccak:eq:in}, where $g^{\mathrm{sl}}_z=s_z$ and $g^{\mathrm{ln}}_\ell=\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{in}},\ell)$;
\item the round constants of \eqref{keccak:eq:projection}, where $g$ is a single position, and only the seven slices $0,1,3,7,15,31,63$ of lane $0$ can be set in a round constant.
\end{itemize}
Each step of $\klin$ respects the factorization: pi moves lanes, rho rotates the slices of lane $\ell$ by its offset $\koff{\ell}$, and theta adds column sums, rotated by at most one slice. With $\kto{\ell}$ the lane pi moves lane $\ell$ to, $v_\ell=\ell\bmod5$ its column, and $g^{\mathrm{ln}}_{[v]}=\sum_{k<5}g^{\mathrm{ln}}_{v+5k}$ the sum of $g^{\mathrm{ln}}$ over column $v$, indices modulo $5$,
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:form}
\inner{\kpw^{\flat}}{\klin(g)}=\sum_{\ell<25}\kpw^{\mathrm{ln}}_{\kto{\ell}}\Bigl(\bigl(g^{\mathrm{ln}}_{\ell}+g^{\mathrm{ln}}_{[v_\ell-1]}\bigr)\,\krot_{\koff{\ell}}+g^{\mathrm{ln}}_{[v_\ell+1]}\,\krot_{\koff{\ell}+1}\Bigr),\qquad
\krot_n=\sum_{z<64}g^{\mathrm{sl}}_z\,\kpw^{\mathrm{sl}}_{(z+n)\bmod64}.
\end{equation}
The slices enter only through the rotation sums $\krot_n$, for the few $n$ that occur. When $g^{\mathrm{sl}}$ is an equality table too, $\krot_n$ is the multilinear extension of the predicate $[\,z'=z+n\bmod64\,]$ at the two points of $\E^{6}$, which a ripple of the carry over the six bits evaluates with a constant number of multiplications a bit. The first claim's weights factor as well, with $x^z$ for slice table, and need no $\klin^{\top}$.

\subsection{Optimization for recursion: committing the middle state}\label{keccak:segments}

A claim passes through the layers one after another, so the proof and the verifier's work grow with the number of rounds between two committed states. In the recursion that work is itself proved, so the VM shortens the chain: it also commits each step's state after twelve rounds. This costs the prover $25$ more committed lanes a step and halves the number of layers. Only the two ends of the reduction change.

\paragraph{Commitment and statement.} The $24$ rounds are cut into two \emph{segments} of twelve, segment $m<2$ being rounds $12m+1,\dots,12m+12$. Slot $25m+\ell$ of $\qkeccak$ holds lane $\ell$ of segment $m$'s input, so the slot has six variables, $\nkeccak=\kbatch+6$, and slots $50$ to $63$ are zero. Let $a_{m,e}(j;t)$ be bit $j$ of the state after $e$ rounds of segment $m$, run from that segment's committed input, so that $a_{m,0}(z+64\ell;t)=Q_z(t,25m+\ell)$ and $\kout{\ell}(t)=\sum_{z<64}a_{1,12}(z+64\ell;t)\,x^z$. The statement is \eqref{keccak:eq:statement}, on this $\kout{\ell}$, together with the requirement that the first segment ends on the committed middle state:
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:chain}
a_{0,12}(z+64\ell;\,t)=Q_z\bigl(t,\,25+\ell\bigr)\quad\text{for all }t\in\cube{\kbatch},\ \ell<25,\ z<64.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Claims and layers.} Both segments ride one claim: the segment index is one more variable next to the pack. A claim at depth $d\le12$ has a point $r=(r_{\mathrm{pack}},r_{\mathrm{seg}})\in\E^{\kbatch-4}\times\E$, and \eqref{keccak:eq:claim} becomes a sum over $m<2$ as well, with $\eq\bigl(r,(p,m)\bigr)$ in place of $\eq(r,p)$ and $a_{m,12-d}$ in place of $a_{24-d}$. Layer $d$ undoes round $12(m+1)-d$ in segment $m$, for both segments at once: its round constants enter \eqref{keccak:eq:projection} weighted by $\eq(r_{\mathrm{seg}},m)$, and its sumcheck over packs gains one round, for the segment. Twelve layers take a claim on both segment outputs to a claim on both segment inputs, which are committed.

\paragraph{The first claim.} The verifier samples $r_{\mathrm{seg}}\in\E$ as well as $r_{\mathrm{lane}}$. The first claim has the weights of \S\ref{keccak:claims}, now on both segment outputs. Its part on the second segment is $r_{\mathrm{seg}}\sum_\ell\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)\,\koutv{\ell}$, given by the output claims as before. Its part on the first segment is on the committed middle state, and the prover sends it as a value $c_{\mathrm{st}}$. The first claim's value is their sum, $c=c_{\mathrm{st}}+r_{\mathrm{seg}}\sum_\ell\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell)\,\koutv{\ell}$.

\paragraph{The two openings.} The value $c_{\mathrm{st}}$ speaks of committed lanes as words, at the point $(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})$:
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:out}
c_{\mathrm{st}}\ \qeq\sum_{o\in\cube{6}}\kpw^{\mathrm{out}}(o)\;\mle{\qkeccak}\bigl(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}},o\bigr),\qquad
\kpw^{\mathrm{out}}(25+\ell)=\eq(r_{\mathrm{seg}},0)\,\eq(r_{\mathrm{lane}},\ell),
\end{equation}
$\kpw^{\mathrm{out}}$ being zero on every other slot. A sumcheck of degree two over the six slot variables, with challenges $\fc^{\mathrm{out}}\in\E^{6}$, reduces it to one value $c_{\mathrm{out}}$ the prover sends: the verifier checks $\mle{\kpw^{\mathrm{out}}}(\fc^{\mathrm{out}})\,c_{\mathrm{out}}$ against the final claim, and what is left is the ordinary column claim
\begin{equation}\label{keccak:eq:out-claim}
\mle{\qkeccak}\bigl(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}},\fc^{\mathrm{out}}\bigr)=c_{\mathrm{out}},
\end{equation}
which joins the main protocol's pool. The last claim is opened as in \S\ref{keccak:openings}, with $\kpw^{\mathrm{in}}(25m+\ell,z)=\eq(r_{\mathrm{seg}},m)\,\kpw_{z+64\ell}$ and six slot rounds instead of five. In \S\ref{keccak:recursion}, its lane table becomes $g^{\mathrm{ln}}_\ell=\sum_{m<2}\eq(r_{\mathrm{seg}},m)\,\eq(\fc^{\mathrm{in}},25m+\ell)$.

\paragraph{Cost.} A layer is $47+2(\kbatch-3)+33+3=2\kbatch+77$ elements, and the openings $13$ (six rounds and $c_{\mathrm{out}}$) and $84$ (ten rounds and the $64$ values $s_z$). With $\kbatch=16$ that is about $1400$ elements, against $2650$, and the verifier runs half as many layers.

\paragraph{Soundness.} The argument of \S\ref{keccak:soundness} carries over with three changes. A layer has $\kbatch+8$ sumcheck rounds, so it lets a false claim through with probability at most $(3\kbatch+72)/|\E|$, and there are twelve. The opening of $c_{\mathrm{st}}$ adds $12/|\E|$, and the last opening's ten rounds $20/|\E|$. And the first claim must now also catch a failure of \eqref{keccak:eq:chain}. If \eqref{keccak:eq:out} holds, the first claim's value differs from its true value by
\[
P(r_{\mathrm{seg}},r_{\mathrm{lane}})=\eq(r_{\mathrm{seg}},0)\,A(r_{\mathrm{lane}})+r_{\mathrm{seg}}\,B(r_{\mathrm{lane}}),
\]
with $B$ as in \S\ref{keccak:soundness} and $A$ the multilinear extension, at $(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}},r_{\mathrm{lane}})$, of
\[
D(i,p,\ell)=\sum_{z<64}x^{z}\Bigl(a_{0,12}\bigl(z+64\ell;(i,p)\bigr)+Q_z\bigl((i,p),25+\ell\bigr)\Bigr)\in\K,
\]
set to zero for $\ell\ge25$. The powers $x^z$ are a basis of $\K$ over $\Ftwo$, so $D$ is nonzero as soon as \eqref{keccak:eq:chain} fails in one bit, and its extension in its first $\kbatch$ variables is then nonzero at $(r_{\mathrm{low}},r_{\mathrm{pack}})$ except with probability $\kbatch/|\E|$. If \eqref{keccak:eq:chain} or \eqref{keccak:eq:statement} fails, $P$ is thus a nonzero polynomial of degree at most six, fixed before $r_{\mathrm{seg}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{lane}}$ are drawn, which vanishes there with probability at most $6/|\E|$. In total
\[
\frac{\kbatch+6+12\,(3\kbatch+72)+12+20}{|\E|}=\frac{37\,\kbatch+902}{|\E|}.
\]

\paragraph{More segments.} Nothing above depends on there being two segments: any number $M$ of segments that divides $24$ works, the segment index then taking $\lceil\log_2M\rceil$ variables. The crate takes any (\texttt{keccak::DEPTH} rounds a segment); the VM uses two.
