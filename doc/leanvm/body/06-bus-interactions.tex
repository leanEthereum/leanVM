\section{Bus interactions}\label{sec:omc}

The machine runs five interactions on the bus, differentiated by their domain separators (\S\ref{sec:m3}): the VM state, the register log, the memory log (RAM and the advice, which share a log and never share an address), the public bytecode, and the padding tuples. The bytecode and the padding tuples are read-only, read in the form of \S\ref{sec:lookup}; the registers and the memory are read-write, each checked by a log of its accesses (\S\ref{sec:memchan}).

Two encodings of an integer $i$ coexist on the bus. What the machine indexes (a register, a memory cell, an instruction) and what it holds (a register's value, an address) is the integer itself, $\intk{i}$ (\S\ref{sec:vm}). When something happens is a power of $\gen$: a row's time and its memory position (\S\ref{sec:state}). The powers of $\gen$ appear otherwise only as constants: the domain separators and the class tags. Nothing adds two integers on the bus: every address a row names is a field of its bytecode entry, a word its circuit computed, or an XOR (\S\ref{sec:exp}), and every time and position a row pushes is the one it pulled times a constant power of $\gen$.

\subsection{VM State}\label{sec:state}

\paragraph{Time and position.} A row's \emph{time} is $\tm=\gen^{c}$, on cycle $c$ of the run, counted from $0$, and its memory \emph{position} is $\pos=\gen^{m}$, $m$ the memory accesses the run made before it. The generator $\gen$ of $\K^{*}$ has order $2^{64}-1$, so the powers $\gen^{i}$, $i<2^{64}-1$, are pairwise distinct and none is zero. A row's memory access $k$, from $0$, is at position $\pos\,\gen^{k}$, and its successor's position is $\pos\,\gen^{n}$, $n$ the row's accesses: none for most classes, one for a load or a store, sixteen for \texttt{HASH}, nine for \texttt{EXT}, and seven for \texttt{EXT} with a base-field $b$, whose two high limbs are no access (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}). A padding row has $\tm=\pos=0$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}).

The state interaction uses domain separator $\dsep{ST}=\gen^{0}$ and carries the program counter, the time, the position and an exit marker. Each row pulls $\tup{\dsep{ST},\pc,\tm,\pos,0}$ and pushes $\tup{\dsep{ST},\pc',\gen\,\tm,\gen^{n}\pos,\exitflag}$, where $\exitflag$ is the bytecode's exit selector. Both products are a constant times a column, but \texttt{EXT}'s position, which is linear in $\pos$ and a column of its own (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}). Only \texttt{ecall} has $\exitflag=1$.

The boundary tuples are
\begin{align*}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{initial}} &= \tup{\dsep{ST},\intk{\pc_{\mathrm{entry}}},1,1,0},\\
  \sigma_{\mathrm{final}} &= \tup{\dsep{ST},\intk{\tbase+4(\nprog-1)},\gen^{\ncyc},\gen^{\nacc},1},
\end{align*}
with $\ncyc$ and $\nacc$ the run's cycles and memory accesses, which the prover announces (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}): the logs' live rows (\S\ref{sec:memchan}). The layout's final tuple holds zeros at the two positions, and the verifier adds their share to the pull side's total itself: a leaf is affine in each coordinate, so with $w_2,w_3$ the fingerprint weights of the two coordinates and $e$ the final block's selector weight, the share is $e\,(w_2\gen^{\ncyc}+w_3\gen^{\nacc})$. It forms $\gen^{N}$ from the bits $b_i$ of $N$ as $\prod_i\bigl(1+b_i(\gen^{2^{i}}+1)\bigr)$, which a recursive verifier computes on the bits as wires (Annex~\ref{annex:rec}). Every row but an exit row pushes the marker $0$, so only an exit row can meet the final tuple: a jump to the halt address is insufficient. An exit's padding row pushes the marker at time $0$, and $\gen^{\ncyc}$ is not zero.

\begin{proposition}[Balance implies the existence of a valid execution]\label{prop:walk}
Write $\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{final}}$ for the two boundary states. Let $\mathcal R$ be a finite multiset of rows, each pulling a state $\bpull(r)$ and pushing a state $\bpush(r)$, and suppose the state channel balances:
\[
  \mset{\bpush(r):r\in\mathcal R}\uplus\mset{\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}}}
  \;=\;\mset{\bpull(r):r\in\mathcal R}\uplus\mset{\sigma_{\mathrm{final}}} .
\]
Then $\mathcal R$ splits into rows $r_1,\dots,r_L$ with
\[
  \bpull(r_1)=\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}},\qquad \bpush(r_i)=\bpull(r_{i+1}),\qquad \bpush(r_L)=\sigma_{\mathrm{final}},
\]
and rows forming closed walks.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Build the directed multigraph $G$ on the states: one edge $\bpull(r)\to \bpush(r)$ per row, plus one edge $e$ from $\sigma_{\mathrm{final}}$ to $\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}}$.

Fix a state $x$. Its in-degree in $G$ counts the rows with $\bpush(r)=x$, plus $e$ if $x=\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}}$. Its out-degree counts the rows with $\bpull(r)=x$, plus $e$ if $x=\sigma_{\mathrm{final}}$. Those two counts are the multiplicities of $x$ on the two sides of the hypothesis, so they are equal: $G$ is balanced at every state.

A finite balanced multigraph is a union of edge-disjoint closed walks: follow unused edges from any vertex, which balance always permits on entering one, until a vertex repeats; remove the closed walk found and repeat. Deleting $e$ from the closed walk that contains it leaves $r_1,\dots,r_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Time tells the run from the rest]\label{cor:time}
In Proposition~\ref{prop:walk}, let every row push $\gen\,\tm$ and $\gen^{n}\pos$ for the $\tm$ and $\pos$ it pulls, $n$ its accesses, let $\sigma_{\mathrm{initial}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{final}}$ be as above, and let fewer than $2^{64}-1$ rows and accesses be on the bus. Then $L=\ncyc$, row $r_i$ has time $\gen^{i-1}$ and position $\gen^{m_i}$, $m_i$ the accesses of $r_1,\dots,r_{i-1}$, those accesses number $\nacc$ in all, and every row on a closed walk has time $0$, and position $0$ if the walk makes an access.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Along $r_1,\dots,r_L$ the time starts at $1=\gen^{0}$ and each row multiplies it by $\gen$, so $r_i$ pulls $\gen^{i-1}$ and $r_L$ pushes $\gen^{L}=\gen^{\ncyc}$; both exponents are below $2^{64}-1$, so $L=\ncyc$. The positions are the same argument, with $\gen^{n}$ per row. A closed walk of $k\ge1$ rows returns to the time it starts from, $\tm\,\gen^{k}=\tm$, and $\gen^{k}\neq1$ for $0<k<2^{64}-1$, so $\tm=0$, and every time on the walk is the first one times a power of $\gen$. Likewise $\pos\,\gen^{n}=\pos$ with $n$ the walk's accesses, so $\pos=0$ when $n>0$.
\end{proof}

No other bound on the rows enters: one commitment holds fewer than $2^{28}$ rows (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}), far below the order of $\gen$.

\begin{remark}
  Closed walks at time $0$ are what lets a table be filled to a power of two (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}): a padding row's register and memory tuples are at time and position $0$, which no log row has, so such a row can pull only the public padding tuples (\S\ref{sec:memchan}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The lookup protocol}\label{sec:lookup}

A read pulls the entry it reads and nothing else; the array's table side, its \emph{producer}, pushes every entry as often as it is read, that number being committed as one word whose bits are one-bit columns of the bus. This is the \texttt{LookupProducer} of Binius M3~\cite{m3}, in place of the per-read running counts of~\cite[Protocol~4.27]{DP23}.

\paragraph{Setting.} A \emph{lookup array} $\lut$ is read-only and holds $2^{\kappa}$ entries, entry $\lut[i]$ being a tuple of $\ncomp$ elements of $\K$, possibly none ($\ncomp=0$). Its \emph{components} $\lut_0,\dots,\lut_{\ncomp-1}$ are the multilinears of height $2^{\kappa}$ holding one coordinate of the entry each: they are either committed or public. Entry $i$ sits at a public address $\addr_i\in\K$, the $2^{\kappa}$ addresses pairwise distinct: the integer $\intk{\tbase+4i}$ for the bytecode (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}). A \emph{read} is a row of a table (\S\ref{sec:m3}) naming an address $a\in\K$ and a value $v\in\K^{\ncomp}$, and is correct when $a=\addr_i$ and $v=\lut[i]$ for some $i<2^{\kappa}$. Write $e_i=\tup{\sep,\addr_i,\lut[i]}$ for entry $i$ as a tuple.

\paragraph{Tuple and columns.} The bus interaction uses a tuple of the form $\tup{\sep,\addr,\val}$: a domain separator naming the array, the address, then the entry in coordinates $2,\dots,\ncomp+1$. Every higher coordinate is zero, so $\ncomp\le m-2$ for the bus width $m$ (\S\ref{sec:m3}). The parts of a read are columns of the reading table, or forms of degree at most $d$ in them: its address and the $\ncomp$ components of the value read. Committed on top of $\lut$ is one \emph{multiplicity} column $\mlt$ of height $2^{\kappa}$, word $i$ being the integer $\intk{m_i}$, with $m_i$ the number of reads of entry $i$. Its bits $\mbit{0},\dots,\mbit{\nbit-1}$ are the producer's one-bit columns: $\mbit{k}(i)\in\{0,1\}$ is bit $k$ of $m_i$. The number of bits $\nbit$ is the bit length of $\nrd$, the most reads the announced tables can make of the array: $\nrd=\sum_j2^{\tau_j}$ for the bytecode, which every row reads once, and $16$ times that for the padding tuples (\S\ref{sec:memchan}). That bound serves completeness alone: an honest $m_i$ is at most $\nrd<2^{\nbit}$, so $\nbit$ bits hold it, and nothing below depends on how many reads there are.

\paragraph{Flush rules.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Read} of $v$ at $a$: \pull\ $\tup{\sep,a,v}$, a flush of the reading row.
\item \textbf{Produce:} for each bit $k<\nbit$, a block of $2^{\kappa}$ push leaves owned by no table, leaf $i$ being
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prodleaf}
  1+\mbit{k}(i)\,\pubp{k}(i),\qquad \pubp{k}(i)=\bigl(\beta-\pi_\alpha(e_i)\bigr)^{2^{k}}-1 .
\end{equation}
These are leaves rather than tuples: by Lemma~\ref{lem:prodbits} they multiply to the factors of entry $i$ pushed $m_i$ times, so for the product check the producer is the push of the multiset $\mset{e_i^{\,m_i}:i<2^{\kappa}}$, entry $i$ with multiplicity $m_i$. \S\ref{sec:leafstack} and \S\ref{sec:air} prove them in the table sumcheck, as an air of height $2^{\kappa}$ whose columns are the bits.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Completeness.} An honest prover commits $\mlt[i]=\intk{m_i}$, the number of reads of entry $i$, which is below $2^{\nbit}$. Each correct read pulls one copy of an entry $e_i$, and the producer pushes $e_i$ exactly $m_i$ times, so both sides carry the same multiset at the separator $\sep$ and the bus balances.

\begin{lemma}[A producer's leaves are its multiplicities]\label{lem:prodbits}
Let $P$ be an element of a commutative ring and $b\in\{0,1\}$. Then $1+b\,(P^{2^{k}}-1)=P^{\,b\,2^{k}}$. Hence for an integer $m=\sum_{k<\nbit}b_k2^{k}$ with bits $b_k$, $\prod_{k<\nbit}\bigl(1+b_k(P^{2^{k}}-1)\bigr)=P^{m}$, and the $\nbit2^{\kappa}$ leaves \eqref{eq:prodleaf} of a producer multiply to $\prod_{i<2^{\kappa}}(\beta-\pi_\alpha(e_i))^{m_i}$; as polynomials in the formal $A,X$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:gp}, with $X-\pi_A(e_i)$ for $\beta-\pi_\alpha(e_i)$, to $\Pi_{\mset{e_i^{\,m_i}}}(A,X)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $b=0$ both sides are $1$; for $b=1$ both are $P^{2^{k}}$. The product over $k$ is then $\prod_kP^{b_k2^{k}}=P^{\sum_kb_k2^{k}}=P^{m}$. The leaves of entry $i$ are the case $P=\beta-\pi_\alpha(e_i)$, $m=m_i$, and the identity holds in the polynomial ring as well, being one of the ring.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lookup correctness]\label{thm:omc}
Let the reads and the producer be the only flushes carrying $\lut$'s separator, and write $(a_j,v_j)$ for the address and value that read $j$ evaluates to. If the bus balances, with the producer counted as the multiset $\mset{e_i^{\,m_i}}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:prodbits}, then every read is correct, $(a_j,v_j)=(\addr_i,\lut[i])$ for some $i<2^{\kappa}$, and entry $i$ is read exactly $m_i$ times.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Balance equates the multiset of pushed tuples with the multiset of pulled ones, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:gp} that is what the product check tests, the producer's factors being those of $\mset{e_i^{\,m_i}}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:prodbits}. Keep in each multiset only the tuples carrying $\lut$'s separator; the two are still equal. On the push side what survives is $\mset{e_i^{\,m_i}:i<2^{\kappa}}$, on the pull side the reads $\mset{\tup{\sep,a_j,v_j}}$. So every read's tuple is some $e_i$, that is $(a_j,v_j)=(\addr_i,\lut[i])$, and since the addresses $\addr_i$ are pairwise distinct the $e_i$ are too, so exactly $m_i$ reads equal $e_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Addresses are in range]\label{cor:addrrange}
Under the same hypotheses every read address is an entry address $\addr_i$, $i<2^{\kappa}$: no read at any other address, $0$ included, occurs in an accepted proof (except with negligible soundness error).
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{The bits are bits.} Lemma~\ref{lem:prodbits} needs every $\mbit{k}(i)$ in $\{0,1\}$, and no constraint asks it: the $\mbit{k}$ are the bit slices of the committed words $\mlt$, so they are Boolean by construction. The prover's claims on them at the table sumcheck's point are bound to $\mlt$ by ring switching (\S\ref{sec:ringswitch}), the $64$ slices of the word being the $\nbit$ sent bit values and then zeros, which are not sent. A committed word at or above $2^{\nbit}$ thus fails the opening, and an honest one never is.

\paragraph{What is left to check.} Nothing beyond balance. There is no count, so no nonzero product and no bound on the number of reads: Theorem~\ref{thm:omc} holds for any number of them. What the size of a producer does change is the product check's soundness: Theorem~\ref{th:product_check} counts the linear factors of a side with multiplicity, and the leaves of bit $k$ stand for up to $2^{\kappa}2^{k}$ of them, so a producer adds up to $2^{\kappa}(2^{\nbit}-1)$ factors to the push side, each of total degree at most $4$. The bus's challenges are drawn before the opening, so they must also hold against every polynomial of the commitment's list, which costs up to $12$ bits (\S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}). Every layout one commitment holds has fewer than $2^{28}$ rows, so $\nbit\le28$, and a program of at most $2^{21}$ entries keeps the degree below $2^{52}$, within that margin. Each further bit of entries doubles the degree, so a larger program, up to the text region's $2^{26}$ entries, grinds one bit of proof of work per bit past $2^{21}$ before the bus's challenges (\S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}); the native verifier also refuses an announced layout whose degree exceeds the margin at its grinding. The logs' blocks and the padding producer add their rows to the push side too, the latter a fixed number of entries, so the program's size remains what moves the degree. No other argument needs the caps: the memory argument needs no bound on the rows but the order of $\gen$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:time}).

\paragraph{The verifier's public columns.} The producer's air (\S\ref{sec:air}) ends on a claim on each column at a point $r\in\E^{\kappa}$. The prover sends the bits' values $\widetilde{\mbit{k}}(r)$; the verifier evaluates $\widetilde{\pubp{k}}(r)$ itself. Write the fingerprint of an entry as $\pi_\alpha(e_i)=\sum_sw_sc_s(i)$, with $w_s=\eq(\alpha,s)$ and $c_s$ the column of coordinate $s$. Squaring is additive in characteristic $2$, so
\[
  \bigl(\beta-\pi_\alpha(e_i)\bigr)^{2^{k}}=\beta^{2^{k}}+\sum_sw_s^{2^{k}}\,c_s(i)^{2^{k}},
\]
and $\widetilde{\pubp{k}}(r)$ is $\beta^{2^{k}}-1$ plus $\sum_sw_s^{2^{k}}$ times the extension at $r$ of the column $i\mapsto c_s(i)^{2^{k}}$. Each kind of coordinate stays of its kind (\S\ref{sec:idxcol}): a constant $c$ becomes $c^{2^{k}}$; the integer index column $\intk{\mathrm{base}}+\sum_tu_tx^{t+\sigma}$, $u$ the bits of $i$, becomes $\intk{\mathrm{base}}^{2^{k}}+\sum_tu_t(x^{t+\sigma})^{2^{k}}$, affine in the bits still. The program's public columns are summed once into $c(i)=\sum_sw_sc_s(i)$ over them, and $c(i)^{2^{k}}$ is raised entry by entry, at a cost of the program's size times $\nbit$. The program's polynomial $P$ of \S\ref{sec:e2e-bc} is thus never evaluated by the bus; it is what the digest binds.

\paragraph{Relaxing the hypotheses.} A bus entry may be any polynomial of degree at most $d = 2$ in the row's columns (\S\ref{sec:m3}), so neither the address nor the value need be committed. Example: the next $\pc$ a branch pushes is $\pc^{+}+\mathit{jump}$, the sum of two columns, the second a word its circuit computes, and no column of its own (\S\ref{sec:tables}).

\subsection{Read-write arrays}\label{sec:memchan}

Three arrays are read-write: the registers $\regs$, $2^{6}$ cells (\S\ref{sec:vm}), RAM $\mem$, $\nmem=2^{\kmem}$ cells, and the advice $\adv$, $2^{\kadv}$ cells. What a cell holds depends on when it is read, so the lookup protocol of \S\ref{sec:lookup} does not apply to them. Each is checked by a \emph{log}, as in Twist~\cite{Twist}: the run's accesses in the order it makes them, one row each, a row naming its cell by one-hot words and its write by an \emph{increment}, so that what a cell holds at a row is a sum over the rows before it. There are two logs: the \emph{register log}, one row per cycle, and the \emph{memory log}, one row per memory access, which RAM and the advice share, since they never share an address (\S\ref{sec:vm}).

\paragraph{Cells and values.} A log has $2^{\kappa}$ rows, of which the first $\nlive$ are \emph{live}, $\live(j)=[j<\nlive]$, with $\nlive=\ncyc$ for the register log and $\nacc$ for the memory log; the others are dead. Row $j$ names a cell in each of the log's \emph{groups} $g$, the one-hot $\ra{g}(k,j)=[\text{row }j\text{ names cell }k\text{ in group }g]$, and has an increment $\inc(j)\in\K$; its last group is the cell it writes, $\wa=\ra{g}$ for that group. With $\mathrm{init}$ what the cells hold before the run,
\[
  \Val(k,j)=\mathrm{init}(k)+\sum_{j'<j}\wa(k,j')\,\inc(j'),
\]
row $j$ reads $\Val(k,j)$ at the cell $k$ of each group, and leaves $\Val(k,j)+\inc(j)$ in the cell it writes: $\K$ has characteristic two, so the increment is the old value plus the new. The register log has three groups, the two registers read and the cell written, over the $2^{6}$ register cells, which hold zero before the run. The memory log has one group, the cell accessed, read and written in one row, a read having $\inc=0$.

\paragraph{The tuples.} Live row $j$ pushes one tuple, at time or position $\gen^{j}$ in coordinate $2$; a dead row pushes nothing, its leaf being $1$. The register log's row pushes
\[
  \tup{\dsep{REG}+\ptr(j)(\dsep{REG}+\dsep{PTR}),\;k_0,\;\gen^{j},\;k_1,\;k_2,\;\Val(k_0,j),\;\Val(k_1,j),\;\Val(k_2,j)+\inc(j)},
\]
$k_g$ the cell of group $g$ and $\ptr(j)$ a committed flag, set on a pointer read: an \texttt{EXT} row reads the address of its result from the register $a_d$ and writes no register (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}), so its tuple is under $\dsep{PTR}=\gen^{4}$, and its row must write nothing; every other row's is under $\dsep{REG}=\gen^{3}$. The memory log's row pushes
\[
  \tup{\dsep{MEM},\;\addr(k_0),\;\gen^{j},\;\Val(k_0,j),\;\Val(k_0,j)+\inc(j)},\qquad \dsep{MEM}=\gen^{1},
\]
$\addr(k)$ the byte address of memory cell $k$. A table's row pulls the same tuples (\S\ref{sec:tables}): one register tuple, at its time, and one memory tuple per access $k$, at position $\pos\,\gen^{k}$.

\paragraph{The cells' words.} A cell is cut into chunks of $6$ bits, $\nchunk$ of them, and chunk $c$ of a row's cell in group $g$ is committed as the one-hot word $x^{c'}$, $c'$ the chunk's value: a $\K$-word with that one bit set, whose $64$ bit slices are the one-hot $\ra{g,c}(\cdot,j)$, Boolean by construction as a multiplicity's bits are (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), and $\ra{g}(k,j)=\prod_c\ra{g,c}(k_c,j)$. A register is one chunk. A memory cell has $\nchunk\le4$: its top chunk names a \emph{symbol}, RAM's among $0$ to $31$ and the advice's among $32$ to $63$, each symbol $2^{\klow}$ consecutive words of its region with $\klow=6\lceil(\max(\kmem,\kadv)-5)/6\rceil$, and its low chunks the word within it. So $\addr(k)$ is the symbol's first byte address plus $8$ times the low bits, a public table of $64$ words plus an index column (\S\ref{sec:idxcol}), whose extension the verifier evaluates in $O(\klow)$. Committed per log: each group's chunks' words, then the increments, a column of $2^{\kappa}$ words each; for the register log the flags, $64$ rows a word; and the advice's initial words $\advinit$.

\paragraph{The read-write sumcheck.} The bus leaves the log's block one claim at its point $\zeta$ (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}): the prover sends $V=\sum_j\eq(\zeta,j)L(j)$, the block's leaves $L(j)=\live(j)(\beta-\pi_\alpha(\btup_j))+1-\live(j)$ at $\zeta$. The verifier forms $\Lambda=\sum_j\eq(\zeta,j)\live(j)$ and $\Lambda_{\gen}=\sum_j\eq(\zeta,j)\live(j)\gen^{j}$ from the bits of $\nlive$ in $O(\kappa)$ operations, and takes off the slots it knows, the constant ones and the time, leaving
\[
  \sum_{k,j}\eq(\zeta,j)\live(j)\sum_g\ra{g}(k,j)\Bigl(w^{\val}_g\,\Val(k,j)+w^{\addr}_g\addr(k)+[g\text{ written}]\,w^{\inc}\inc(j)+[g=0]\,w^{\ptr}\ptr(j)\Bigr),
\]
the $w$ the fingerprint weights of each slot, gathered. One sumcheck over the cell's bits, then the row's, proves it, and leaves $\Val(\fc_{\mathrm{cell}},\fc_{\mathrm{row}})$ at its point, which the prover sends, and the committed columns at $\fc_{\mathrm{row}}$: each word's $64$ slices, the increment and the flags' slices.

\paragraph{The evaluation sumcheck.} A second sumcheck, over the rows, proves
\[
  \Val(\fc_{\mathrm{cell}},\fc_{\mathrm{row}})=\mathrm{init}(\fc_{\mathrm{cell}})+\sum_j\mathrm{LT}(j,\fc_{\mathrm{row}})\,\wa(\fc_{\mathrm{cell}},j)\,\inc(j),
\]
$\mathrm{LT}(j,j')=[j<j']$, whose extension the verifier evaluates in $O(\kappa)$, batched with the checks below at the weights $\lw{\cdot}$ drawn after $\fc_{\mathrm{row}}$, each check zero on every row and weighted by $\eq(\fc_{\mathrm{row}},j)$, and leaves the committed columns at its own point $\fc_{\mathrm{ev}}$. The register log's statement adds its outputs: $\regs[17]$ ends on $\intk{93}$ and $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$ on the output, each $\sum_j\live(j)\wa(o,j)\inc(j)$ for its cell $o$, at the weight $\lw{o}$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}). Its flag check is $\lw{\ptr}\,\ptr(j)\inc(j)$, zero on every row: a pointer read writes nothing.

\paragraph{One-hot in characteristic two.} The slices are bits, and the verifier checks that a word's slices sum to $1$ at both points, so every row's word has an odd number of bits set. In characteristic two that is all a sum of bits says, and a word with three bits set would read the sum of three cells and name the XOR of their addresses, which can be a fourth cell's. The \emph{collision check} closes this: with $A_j(r)=\sum_k\ra{g,c}(k,j)\eq(r,k)$ for one word, a row with one bit set at $k$ has
\[
  A_j(r)^3=\eq(r,k)^3=\colsc\,\eq(r',k)=\colsc\,A_j(r'),\qquad\colsc=\prod_i(1+r_i+r_i^2),\quad r'_i=r_i^3/(1+r_i+r_i^2),
\]
since $(1+r_i)^3=(1+r_i+r_i^2)(1+r'_i)$ and $r_i^3=(1+r_i+r_i^2)\,r'_i$: Twist's collision polynomial with the cube taken through the Frobenius. For a set $S$ of two or more bits, $A_j(r)^3+\colsc A_j(r')=\sum_{k\neq l\in S}\eq(r,k)\eq(r^2,l)$, squaring being additive and $\eq$ having coefficients in $\Ftwo$; that is $Q(r,r^2)$ for the polynomial $Q(r,s)=\sum_{k\neq l\in S}\eq(r,k)\eq(s,l)$, nonzero and multilinear in $(r,s)$, and $s=r^2$ maps its distinct monomials to distinct ones, so it is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $18$ in $r$. The check is $\lw{g,c}^{3}\bigl(A_j(\colpt)^3+\colsc A_j(\colpt')\bigr)$, zero on every row, at the point $\colpt\in\E^{6}$ drawn after the commitment. With the parity, every word has exactly one bit set.

\paragraph{Regions.} In the memory log, a symbol of neither region names no cell: the verifier checks its slices are zero at both points. A region of fewer words than a symbol, $\kmem<\klow$ or $\kadv<\klow$, takes one symbol of which only its first words are cells, and the check $\lw{\mathrm{part}}\,\ra{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{first},j)\bigl(1+\prod_c\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_c}\ra{c}(v,j)\bigr)$, zero on every row, holds a row naming that symbol to them, $\mathcal{V}_c$ the values the region's words take in low chunk $c$. So every row names a cell of RAM or the advice, and an access at any other address, a misaligned one included (\S\ref{sec:exp}), has no tuple to meet.

\paragraph{The initial memory.} The registers start at zero. The memory log's $\mathrm{init}$ is RAM's image in RAM's symbols, then zeros, and $\advinit$ in the advice's. The prover sends $\advinit$'s value at the advice's point in $\fc_{\mathrm{cell}}$, a claim for the opening, and the verifier adds it to the target, times the factor the advice's fixed cell bits give. RAM's image is public but costs its length to evaluate, so the evaluation sumcheck runs without it: the term is constant in $j$, so it is the all-ones row's at weight $\prod_i\fc_{\mathrm{ev},i}$, and the final value leaves a claim on the image at RAM's point in $\fc_{\mathrm{cell}}$ at that weight, times RAM's factor. That claim joins the program's (\S\ref{sec:e2e-unrolled}) at a fresh weight $\lw{\mathrm{img}}$ drawn after both: summed with weight one, a prover could add one offset to both, which cancels in characteristic two.

\paragraph{Completeness.} An honest prover writes each log from the run, its dead rows naming the first live row's cells, or cell $0$, with increment $0$, and every check holds: the words are one-hot, the values are the running sums, a pointer read leaves its register as it found it, and the registers end on the exit's values.

\begin{theorem}[Memory consistency]\label{thm:rwmem}
Let the logs' tuples be the only pushes under $\dsep{REG}$, $\dsep{PTR}$ and $\dsep{MEM}$ at a nonzero time or position, the tables' accesses the pulls, and let the times and positions be as in Corollary~\ref{cor:time}. If the bus balances and both logs' sumchecks and checks accept, then, but for their errors, every access of a row $r_1,\dots,r_L$ is at a cell of its array, reads what the accesses before it in the run left there, or its initial value, and a pointer read writes nothing; the registers end on $\intk{93}$ in $\regs[17]$ and the output in $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:time} the register tuples of $r_1,\dots,r_L$ are at the pairwise distinct times $\gen^{0},\dots,\gen^{\ncyc-1}$ and their memory tuples at the distinct positions $\gen^{0},\dots,\gen^{\nacc-1}$, one each, and every other row's are at time or position $0$, where no log row pushes. The register log's live rows push one tuple at each of those times, the memory log's one at each of those positions, so balance, keeping the tuples at one time or position, equates the tuple of the run's cycle $j$ with that of the log's row $j$, and of the run's access $m$ with that of the log's row $m$. The read-write sumcheck makes the log's leaves those of its one-hot words, values $\Val$, increments and flags; the evaluation sumcheck makes $\Val$ the running sum from $\mathrm{init}$, makes a flagged row's increment zero and the outputs the cells' last values; the parity and the collision checks make each word one-hot, and the regions' checks make each memory cell one of RAM's or the advice's, whose address $\addr$ names it alone. So each pulled tuple carries the cell's running value, which is the claim.
\end{proof}

RAM and the advice share $\dsep{MEM}$: no address is in both, and $\addr$ is injective on the cells of the two regions, so the memory log keeps them apart.

\paragraph{The padding tuples.} A padding row is at time and position $0$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}), so its register and memory tuples meet no log row. They are a lookup array of their own (\S\ref{sec:lookup}): the distinct tuples the fill blocks' padding rows pull, computed from the program by both parties, with $\tup{\dsep{MEM},0,0,0,0}$, the \emph{absent limb} a base-field $b$'s high limbs pull (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}). Its producer pushes each as often as its committed multiplicity says, at most $16$ per row of the tables, and the table is small, so the verifier evaluates its columns itself rather than deferring them. A row of the run, at a nonzero time, can pull no padding tuple, and a padding row can pull nothing but one.

\subsection{Bytecode}\label{sec:bytecode}

The bytecode is a lookup array (\S\ref{sec:lookup}) of $\nprog = 2^{\kbc}$ entries, entry $i$ at the address $\intk{\tbase+4i}$, the byte address of the instruction. Bus interaction uses domain separation $\sep=\dsep{BC} = \gen^{2}$. The program is public, and so is its decoding: an entry is not the instruction word but the fields a row needs of it, decoded once by both parties (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}). So $\ncomp=10$: the class tag, the flags, the three register numbers, the immediate, the successor $\intk{\tbase+4i+4}$, which every row pushes as its next $\pc$ unless it jumps, the jump offset of a branch, a \texttt{jal} or an \texttt{ecall}, a zero in slot $10$, where the row of a division puts its verdict (\S\ref{sec:tables}), and the exit selector. With the separator and the address ahead of them, this is the widest entry on the bus at $12$ coordinates, which is what forces the four index bits, hence the $m=16$ slots of \S\ref{sec:m3}. Its producer's multiplicity column $\mlt$ has height $2^{\kbc}$, entry $i$'s word counting the rows at $\pc=\intk{\tbase+4i}$. A jump's target needs no check of its own: the row it leads to reads the bytecode there, and Corollary~\ref{cor:addrrange} makes it an instruction. A row's three register numbers are not committed as words: they are bit fields of a committed word, one per row for the tables of one height, read through its bit slices as $\mlt$'s are (\S\ref{sec:regpack}). Coordinate $2$ is the tag, naming the instruction's class and its table:
\begin{gather*}
  \opc{ALU}=\gen^{0},\quad \opc{LOAD}=\gen^{1},\quad \opc{STORE}=\gen^{2},\quad \opc{LD}=\gen^{3},\quad \opc{SD}=\gen^{4},\quad \opc{SHIFT}=\gen^{5},\\
  \opc{MUL}=\gen^{6},\quad \opc{MULH}=\gen^{7},\quad \opc{DIV}=\gen^{8},\quad \opc{HASH}=\gen^{9},\quad \opc{EXT}=\gen^{10}.
\end{gather*}
A slot of the text that holds no instruction the machine defines has the tag $0$, which is no table's: a run reaching it has no proof.

\subsection{Addresses}\label{sec:exp}

Integer addition is no low-degree operation of $\K$, and none is needed on the bus. A register's address is its number, a field of the public bytecode entry. A load's or a store's address is $\intk{\regs[\mathit{rs1}]+\mathrm{imm}}$, and computing it is the business of the row's circuit (\S\ref{sec:tables}), which also puts the misalignment bits back into the word it hands the bus: an aligned access names its cell's address, a misaligned one an address no cell has, so Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem} refuses it with no constraint of its own. A doubleword's misalignment bits are all three low ones, so for \texttt{ld} and \texttt{sd} that word is the sum itself. The words of a \texttt{blake2s} block are at $\regs[\mathit{rs1}]\oplus8k$ by definition (\S\ref{sec:vm}), and the field sum $\intk{\regs[\mathit{rs1}]}+\intk{8k}$ is that XOR: the address is a column plus a constant.

The limbs of an extension-field element are at $p+8$ and $p+16$ (\S\ref{sec:vm}), integer sums with no alignment to make them XORs. \texttt{EXT} has no class circuit, so its operand circuit computes them, by incrementers (\S\ref{flock:classes}), as a load's circuit computes its address. A pointer off its word leaves its low bits in every limb's address, and a pointer near the end of memory leaves a limb outside it: either way the limb names no cell of the memory log (Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem}).

\subsection{The index columns}\label{sec:idxcol}

A producer needs its address column, raised to powers $2^{k}$, at its own point (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), and a log its cells' addresses at its cell point (\S\ref{sec:memchan}). Write $w$ for the bits of $i$. Every region's cell $i$ sits at $\mathrm{base}\oplus(i\ll\sigma)$ with $\sigma=3$ for the words of RAM and the advice, $2$ for the instructions and $0$ for the registers (base $0$), and this \emph{integer index column} is affine in the bits, $\intk{\mathrm{base}}+\sum_k w_k\,x^{k+\sigma}$, so its extension is that same sum at $\zeta$:
\[
  \intk{\mathrm{base}}+\sum_{k=0}^{\kappa-1}\zeta_k\,x^{k+\sigma}.
\]
