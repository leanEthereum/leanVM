\section{The VM state and the lookups}\label{sec:interactions}

Rows interact in two ways. The VM state rides the bus (\S\ref{sec:m3}). The memory and the bytecode are arrays that rows \emph{read}: a lookup, proven by Shout~\cite{ST25} in its binary-field form~\cite{Posen}.

\subsection{VM State}\label{sec:state}

The state interaction carries the 2 registers, program counter and frame pointer: $\tup{\pc,\fp}$. Each instruction row pulls its current state and pushes its successor $\tup{\nxt{\pc},\nxt{\fp}}$; the bus is preseeded by pushing the initial state $\tup{\pc_{\mathrm{initial}},\fp_{\mathrm{initial}}} = \tup{\gen^0,\gen^0}$ and pulling the final state $\tup{\pc_{\mathrm{final}},\fp_{\mathrm{final}}} = \tup{\gen^{\nprog - 1},\gen^0}$ (boundary conditions).
\begin{proposition}[Balance implies the existence of a valid execution]\label{prop:walk}
Let $\mathcal R$ be a finite multiset of rows, each pulling a state $\bpull(r)$ and pushing a state $\bpush(r)$, and suppose the state channel balances:
\[
  \mset{\bpush(r):r\in\mathcal R}\uplus\mset{(\pc_{\mathrm{initial}},\fp_{\mathrm{initial}})}
  \;=\;\mset{\bpull(r):r\in\mathcal R}\uplus\mset{(\pc_{\mathrm{final}},\fp_{\mathrm{final}})} .
\]
Then $\mathcal R$ splits into rows $r_1,\dots,r_L$ with
\[
  \bpull(r_1)=(\pc_{\mathrm{initial}},\fp_{\mathrm{initial}}),\qquad \bpush(r_i)=\bpull(r_{i+1}),\qquad \bpush(r_L)=(\pc_{\mathrm{final}},\fp_{\mathrm{final}}),
\]
and rows forming closed walks.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Build the directed multigraph $G$ on the states: one edge $\bpull(r)\to \bpush(r)$ per row, plus one edge $e$ from $(\pc_{\mathrm{final}},\fp_{\mathrm{final}})$ to $(\pc_{\mathrm{initial}},\fp_{\mathrm{initial}})$.

Fix a state $x$. Its in-degree in $G$ counts the rows with $\bpush(r)=x$, plus $e$ if $x=(\pc_{\mathrm{initial}},\fp_{\mathrm{initial}})$. Its out-degree counts the rows with $\bpull(r)=x$, plus $e$ if $x=(\pc_{\mathrm{final}},\fp_{\mathrm{final}})$. Those two counts are the multiplicities of $x$ on the two sides of the hypothesis, so they are equal: $G$ is balanced at every state.

A finite balanced multigraph is a union of edge-disjoint closed walks: follow unused edges from any vertex, which balance always permits on entering one, until a vertex repeats; remove the closed walk found and repeat. Deleting $e$ from the closed walk that contains it leaves $r_1,\dots,r_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Tolerating closed walks is what lets a table be filled to a power of two (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The lookup protocol}\label{sec:shout}

\paragraph{Setting.} A \emph{lookup array} $\lut$ holds $2^{\kappa}$ entries, entry $\lut[k]$ being a tuple of $\ncomp$ elements of $\K$. Its \emph{components} $\lut_0,\dots,\lut_{\ncomp-1}$ are the multilinears in $\kappa$ variables holding one coordinate of the entry each: they are either committed or public. A \emph{read} is made by every row of a table (\S\ref{sec:m3}): it names an address $a\in\K$ and a value $v\in\K^{\ncomp}$, each a polynomial of degree at most $d=2$ in the row's columns, and is correct when $a=\gen^{k}$ and $v=\lut[k]$ for some $k<2^{\kappa}$. Number the reads of all the tables $p=0,\dots,\nread-1$; read $p$ belongs to a table of log height $\tau_p$, and $a_p(x)$, $v_p(x)$ are its address and value at row $x\in\cube{\tau_p}$.

\paragraph{Fingerprints.} A read is the tuple $\tup{1,a,0,v}$: a one, the address, an unused slot, then the value in coordinates $3,\dots,\ncomp+2$, which is where the bytecode's encoding already keeps it (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}). The fingerprint $\pi_\alpha$ of \S\ref{sec:gp} maps it to one element of $\E$, at the same $\alpha$ as the bus. Write
\[
  \rfp_p(x)=\pi_\alpha\tup{1,a_p(x),0,v_p(x)},\qquad \afp(k)=\pi_\alpha\tup{1,\gen^{k},0,\lut[k]}\quad(k<2^{\kappa})
\]
for the fingerprint of read $p$ at row $x$, a polynomial of degree at most $d$ in the row's columns, and for the fingerprint of the array's own entry $k$.

\paragraph{One-hot addresses.} Fix the number of chunks $\nchunk=8$, a power of two, and a chunk width $\kchunk$ with $\nchunk\,\kchunk\ge\kappa$. An index $k<2^{\kappa}$ is cut into $\nchunk$ chunks, chunk $c$ being its bits $c\,\kchunk,\dots,(c+1)\,\kchunk-1$, those past $\kappa$ read as zero: $k=(k^{(0)},\dots,k^{(\nchunk-1)})$ with $k^{(c)}\in\cube{\kchunk}$. For every read $p$ and chunk $c$ the prover commits a table of bits
\[
  \ra_{p,c}:\cube{\kchunk}\times\cube{\tau_p}\to\{0,1\},\qquad \ra_{p,c}(u,x)=[\,u=k_p(x)^{(c)}\,],
\]
where $k_p(x)$ is the index row $x$ reads: one \emph{chunk table} per chunk per read, each of its rows $\ra_{p,c}(\cdot,x)$ holding a single $1$. They define, on boolean points,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ra}
  \ra_p(k,x)=\prod_{c=0}^{\nchunk-1}\ra_{p,c}\bigl(k^{(c)},x\bigr),
\end{equation}
the indicator of $k=k_p(x)$ for an honest prover. Nothing is assumed of a malicious one: the chunk tables are arbitrary bits, which is what a commitment to bits gives for free (\S\ref{sec:shout-open}), and $\ra_p$ is whatever \eqref{eq:ra} makes of them.

\paragraph{What is proven.} Three statements, for every read $p$ and row $x$.
\begin{align}
  \rfp_p(x)&=\sum_{k<2^{\kappa}}\ra_p(k,x)\,\afp(k) &&\text{(read-checking)},\label{eq:readcheck}\\
  \ra_p(k,x)&=\textstyle\prod_c\ra_{p,c}(k^{(c)},x)\ \text{ for all }k &&\text{(the chunk product)},\notag\\
  \ra_{p,c}(\cdot,x)&\ \text{holds exactly one }1 &&\text{(one-hot)}.\notag
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Lookup correctness]\label{thm:shout}
If the three statements hold for every $\alpha\in\E^{4}$, every read is correct: $(a_p(x),v_p(x))=(\gen^{k},\lut[k])$ for some $k<2^{\kappa}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a read and a row. Each $\ra_{p,c}(\cdot,x)$ is the indicator of one position $u_c\in\cube{\kchunk}$, so by \eqref{eq:ra} $\ra_p(\cdot,x)$ is the indicator of the one index $k$ whose chunks are the $u_c$, among all $\nchunk\,\kchunk$-bit indices. Both sides of \eqref{eq:readcheck} are multilinear in $\alpha$, so they agree slot by slot. Slot $0$ reads $1=\sum_{k'<2^{\kappa}}\ra_p(k',x)$, so $k<2^{\kappa}$. Slot $1$ then reads $a_p(x)=\gen^{k}$, and the remaining slots $v_p(x)=\lut[k]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Addresses are in range]\label{cor:addrrange}
Under the same hypotheses every read address is an entry address $\gen^{k}$, $k<2^{\kappa}$: no read at any other address, $0$ included, occurs in an accepted proof (except with negligible soundness error).
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Why the one.} A chunk's positions cover $\nchunk\,\kchunk\ge\kappa$ bits, so one-hot chunks name an index that may lie past the array. The leading $1$ of the tuple makes read-checking count the ones of $\ra_p(\cdot,x)$ inside the array, at no cost: an index past it leaves none.

\subsubsection{Read-checking}

The arrays are handled one at a time; fix one, with $\zeta$ the terminal point of the bus (\S\ref{sec:gkr}), so that $\zeta_{<\tau_p}$ is uniformly random and drawn after the commitment.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item The verifier samples $\lrd\in\E$. The prover sends $S\in\E$, claimed to be $\sum_p\lrd^{\,p}\sum_{x\in\cube{\tau_p}}\eq(\zeta_{<\tau_p},x)\,\rfp_p(x)$, the sum over the reads $p$ of this array.
\item By \eqref{eq:readcheck} extended multilinearly in $x$, that value is a sum over the array's entries,
\[
  S\;\qeq\;\sum_{k\in\cube{\kappa}}\Bigl(\sum_p\lrd^{\,p}\,\mle{\ra_p}(k,\zeta_{<\tau_p})\Bigr)\,\afp(k),
\]
which a sumcheck of $\kappa$ rounds and degree $2$ reduces to its summand at one point $\fcp{\lut}\in\E^{\kappa}$. The prover sends $\mle{\ra_p}(\fcp{\lut},\zeta_{<\tau_p})$ for every read $p$ of the array, settled by the cycle sumcheck below. The array's side is
\[
  \mle{\afp}(\fcp{\lut})=\eq(\alpha,0)+\eq(\alpha,1)\,\mle{\mathrm{idx}}(\fcp{\lut})+\sum_{i<\ncomp}\eq(\alpha,3+i)\,\mle{\lut_i}(\fcp{\lut}),
\]
slots written as integers, with $\mathrm{idx}$ the index column of \S\ref{sec:idxcol}. A public component the verifier evaluates; for a committed one the prover sends the evaluation, a claim deferred to the PCS (\S\ref{sec:stacking}).
\item The reads' side joins the table sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:air}): it is one more sum over each table's rows, against the weight $\eq(\zeta_{<\tau_j},\cdot)$, of a polynomial of degree at most $d$ in its columns,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:read_poly}
  \rfp^{\mathrm{rd}}_j(x)=\sum_{p\,\in\,T_j}\lrd^{\,p}\,\rfp_p(x),
  \qquad
  \sum_{j=1}^{\ntab}\ \sum_{x\in\cube{\tau_j}}\eq(\zeta_{<\tau_j},x)\,\rfp^{\mathrm{rd}}_j(x)\;\qeq\;\sum_{\lut}S_{\lut},
\end{equation}
the powers of $\lrd$ running over the reads of both arrays.
\end{enumerate}
A wrong $S$ fails one of its two checks, so \eqref{eq:readcheck} holds at the random $\zeta$, batched by $\lrd$ and fingerprinted at $\alpha$: by Corollary~\ref{cor:idtest} it holds for every read, row and slot, up to an error of $(\nread+\taumax+4)/|\E|$ on top of the two sumchecks'.

\subsubsection{The cycle sumcheck}\label{sec:shout-cycle}

It settles the values $\mle{\ra_p}(\fcp{\lut},\zeta_{<\tau_p})$ against the chunk tables, and proves every chunk row one-hot. Write $\fcp{\lut,(c)}\in\E^{\kchunk}$ for chunk $c$ of $\fcp{\lut}$, zero past $\kappa$ like an index's.

\paragraph{The chunk product.} The product \eqref{eq:ra} is multilinear in $k$ but has degree $\nchunk$ in $x$, so $\mle{\ra_p}(\fcp{\lut},\zeta)$ is \emph{not} the product of the chunks' extensions at $\zeta$. Both sides of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virt}
  \mle{\ra_p}(\fcp{\lut},\zeta_{<\tau_p})=\sum_{x\in\cube{\tau_p}}\eq(\zeta_{<\tau_p},x)\prod_{c=0}^{\nchunk-1}\mle{\ra_{p,c}}\bigl(\fcp{\lut,(c)},x\bigr)
\end{equation}
are multilinear in both arguments and agree with \eqref{eq:ra} on boolean points, so the identity holds, and it is a sum over the table's rows.

\paragraph{One-hot rows.} Write $\vone+X$ for $(1+X_1,\dots,1+X_{\kchunk})$.

\begin{lemma}[One-hot as a product]\label{lem:onehot}
A multilinear $f\in\E[X_1,\dots,X_{\kchunk}]$ is $\eq(\cdot,u)$ for some $u\in\cube{\kchunk}$ exactly when $f(X)\,f(\vone+X)=\prod_h X_h(1+X_h)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Coordinate $h$ of $\eq(X,u)\,\eq(\vone+X,u)$ is $(1+X_h+u_h)(X_h+u_h)=X_h(1+X_h)$ for either bit $u_h$. Conversely, the right side is a product of $2\kchunk$ distinct irreducibles and $f$ divides it, so $f$ is a constant $c$ times some of them. Being multilinear it holds at most one of $X_h$, $1+X_h$ for each $h$, and $X\mapsto\vone+X$ swaps the two, so $f(X)f(\vone+X)=c^{2}\prod_{h\in H}X_h(1+X_h)$ over the coordinates $H$ that $f$ holds. Then $H$ is all of them and $c^{2}=1$, so $c=1$ in characteristic $2$: $f$ is one factor per coordinate, which is $\eq(X,u)$ with $u_h=1$ where the factor is $X_h$.
\end{proof}

Nothing is asked of the values of $f$ on the cube: the identity forces them. A whole table $A(u,j)$, $j\in\cube{n}$, has every row $A(\cdot,j)$ with $j$ in a set $J$ one-hot exactly when
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}\eq(Y,j)\,\mle{A}(X,j)\,\mle{A}(\vone+X,j)=\prod_h X_h(1+X_h)\sum_{j\in J}\eq(Y,j)
\]
as polynomials in $(X,Y)$, both sides being multilinear in $Y$ and the lemma at $Y=j$. It is checked at one random point, at the cost of $(2\kchunk+n)/|\E|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sz}), the left side by sumcheck. For a point $\rhot=(r_1,\dots,r_{\kchunk})$ put $\rhotb=\vone+\rhot$ and $\chot=\prod_h r_h(1+r_h)$.

\paragraph{The batch.} Let $T_j$ make $n_j$ reads, and number its chunk tables $s=\nchunk\,p+c$, with $p<n_j$ the read's rank in its table: $s$ ranges over the \emph{selector cube} $\cube{\varsigma}$, $\varsigma=\max_j\lceil\log_2(\nchunk\,n_j)\rceil$, a chunk table past the last one being zero. As $\nchunk$ is a power of two, the low $\log_2\nchunk$ bits of $s$ are the chunk and the rest the read. Write $\iota(s)$ for the indicator that the chunk is $0$, and $\lut_s$ for the array the read of $s$ looks up. The verifier samples the one-hot point $\rhot\in\E^{\kchunk}$, then $\rsel\in\E^{\varsigma}$ and $\lcyc\in\E$. Table $T_j$ owes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle}
\begin{aligned}
  \sum_{x\in\cube{\tau_j}}\eq(\zeta_{<\tau_j},x)\sum_{s\in\cube{\varsigma}}\eq(\rsel,s)&\Bigl(\lcyc^{\,2j-2}\,\iota(s)\prod_{c}\mle{\ra_{s+c}}\bigl(\fcp{\lut_s,(c)},x\bigr)\\
  &+\lcyc^{\,2j-1}\,\mle{\ra_s}(\rhot,x)\,\mle{\ra_s}(\rhotb,x)\Bigr)\\
  &\qeq\;\lcyc^{\,2j-2}\sum_{p<n_j}\eq(\rsel,\nchunk\,p)\,\mle{\ra_p}(\fcp{\lut_p},\zeta_{<\tau_j})\\
  &\phantom{\qeq}\;+\lcyc^{\,2j-1}\,\chot\sum_{s<\nchunk n_j}\eq(\rsel,s).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first sum is \eqref{eq:virt}, read by read, and the second is the one-hot test of the table's chunk tables, at $X=\rhot$ and $Y=(\zeta_{<\tau_j},\rsel)$, every row of every chunk table being owed. Both sides are multilinear in $\rsel$, so the weights $\eq(\rsel,\cdot)$ also batch the reads of a table at the cost of $\varsigma/|\E|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sz}), and $\lcyc$ batches the tables and keeps the two kinds apart. The eq point over the rows is the bus point again, as for the constraints (\S\ref{sec:air}): it only has to be random and chosen after the commitment.

The tables are batched into one sumcheck exactly as in \S\ref{sec:air}, back-loaded over $\taumax$ rounds to a point $\fcp{cyc}\in\E^{\taumax}$, the summand of individual degree $\max(\nchunk,2)+1$. The same sumcheck then goes on over $s$, for $\varsigma$ more rounds, to a point $\fcp{sel}$. In these rounds the factor of chunk $c$ is, at $s$, the chunk table of that chunk of the read of $s$: it does not depend on the chunk bits of $s$, so its extension at $\fcp{sel}$ reads the upper coordinates $\fcp{sel}_{\ge\log_2\nchunk}$ only. The prover sends $\nchunk+2$ values per table,
\[
  f_{j,c}=\sum_{p<n_j}\eq\bigl(\fcp{sel}_{\ge\log_2\nchunk},p\bigr)\,\mle{\ra_{\nchunk p+c}}\bigl(\fcp{\lut_p,(c)},\fcp{cyc}_{<\tau_j}\bigr),\qquad
  g_j=\sum_{s}\eq(\fcp{sel},s)\,\mle{\ra_s}(\rhot,\fcp{cyc}_{<\tau_j}),
\]
and $h_j$, which is $g_j$ at $\rhotb$. The final value is $\eq(\rsel,\fcp{sel})$ times the sum over the tables, each with its weight of \S\ref{sec:air}, of $\lcyc^{\,2j-2}\,\mle{\iota}(\fcp{sel})\prod_c f_{j,c}+\lcyc^{\,2j-1}\,g_j\,h_j$, with $\mle{\iota}(\fcp{sel})=\prod_{i<\log_2\nchunk}(1+\fcp{sel}_i)$.

\subsection{Opening the one-hot bits}\label{sec:shout-open}

The cycle sumcheck leaves $\nchunk+2$ values per table, at points sharing their row coordinates. They are reduced to a single claim on the commitment.

\paragraph{Packing.} Let $T_j$ make $n_j$ reads and write $\varsigma_j=\lceil\log_2(\nchunk\,n_j)\rceil$. Its chunk tables are one table of bits $\hot_j(x,u,s)$, with $x\in\cube{\tau_j}$ the row, $u\in\cube{\kchunk}$ the position and $s\in\cube{\varsigma_j}$ the chunk table, zero past the last one. It is committed packed, $64$ rows to a $\K$ element (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}): the low six bits of the row are the packing prefix, and the stack (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) holds one column per table, in $\kchunk+(\tau_j-6)+\varsigma_j$ variables: the position, the rest of the row, then the chunk table. Committed entries are bits by construction, with no check. This needs $\tau_j\ge6$, so every table is filled to at least $64$ rows (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}).

\paragraph{One evaluation per table.} Put $\cubetab_j(u,s)=\mle{\hot_j}(\fcp{cyc}_{<\tau_j},u,s)$, a table over the \emph{chunk cube} $\cube{\kchunk+\varsigma_j}$. Every value the cycle sumcheck left on $T_j$ is a sum of $\cubetab_j$ against a weight the verifier evaluates: $\eq(\fcp{\lut_p,(c)},u)\,\eq(\fcp{sel}_{\ge\log_2\nchunk},p)$ on the chunk tables of chunk $c$ for $f_{j,c}$, and $\eq(\rhot,u)\,\eq(\fcp{sel},s)$, $\eq(\rhotb,u)\,\eq(\fcp{sel},s)$ for $g_j$ and $h_j$. The verifier samples $\lcube\in\E$, weights each of the $(\nchunk+2)\,\ntab$ values by its own power, and one sumcheck of degree $2$ over the cube, all tables together (a smaller cube extended by zeros to the largest), reduces them to $\mle{\cubetab_j}(\fcp{cube})$ for one point $\fcp{cube}$. The prover sends those $\ntab$ values $t_j$.

\paragraph{One family of slices.} Each $t_j=\mle{\hot_j}(\fcp{cyc}_{<\tau_j},\fcp{cube})$ is a claim on packed bits, and all of them have the same packing prefix $\fcp{cyc}_{<6}$. The verifier samples $\lhot\in\E$, and the prover sends the $64$ slices of the batch: slice $b$ is the sum, over the tables $j$ and the packed entries of $\hot_j$, of bit $b$ of the entry times $\lhot^{\,j-1}$ times the $\eq$ weight of its position, row and chunk table against $(\fcp{cube},\fcp{cyc}_{6\le\cdot<\tau_j})$. The verifier checks
\[
  \sum_{b\in\cube{6}}\eq(\fcp{cyc}_{<6},b)\,s_b\;\qeq\;\sum_{j=1}^{\ntab}\lhot^{\,j-1}\,t_j ,
\]
and ring switching binds the slices to the commitment (Annex~\ref{annex:rs}, \S\ref{rs:parts}): one claim for all the one-hot bits of the proof.

\subsection{Memory}\label{sec:memchan}

The memory is an array of $\nmem = 2^{\kmem}$ cells, $16\le\kmem\le32$ (\S\ref{sec:vm}). A memory word is $192$-bit (i.e. one $\E$-element), so $\ncomp=3$: memory is committed as three $\K$-valued multilinears $\mem_0,\mem_1,\mem_2$ in $\kmem$ variables, with
\[
  \mem=\mem_0+y\,\mem_1+y^2\,\mem_2 .
\]
Read-checking leaves the three claims $\mle{\mem_i}(\fcp{mem})$.

\subsection{Bytecode}\label{sec:bytecode}

The bytecode is an array of $\nprog = 2^{\kbc}$ instructions, $\kbc \le 32$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}). An instruction is an opcode plus seven operands, so $\ncomp=8$: with the one, the address and the unused slot ahead of them, this is the widest tuple at $11$ coordinates, which is what forces the four index bits, hence the $m=16$ slots of \S\ref{sec:m3}. Coordinate $3$ is the opcode, naming the instruction:
\[
  \opc{XOR}=\gen^{0},\quad \opc{MUL}=\gen^{1},\quad \opc{SET}=\gen^{2},\quad \opc{DRF}=\gen^{3},\quad \opc{JMP}=\gen^{4},\quad \opc{B2S}=\gen^{5}.
\] The program is public, i.e. not committed: its whole share of $\mle{\afp}(\fcp{bc})$ is one evaluation of the encoding, at $(\fcp{bc},\alpha)$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-bc}). A read names its table's opcode as a constant, so a row only reads an instruction of its own kind.

\paragraph{The chunk width.} Both arrays use the same $\kchunk$, announced by the prover with the other sizes: the verifier checks $\nchunk\,\kchunk\ge\max(\kmem,\kbc)$ and $\kchunk\le4$. A read commits $\nchunk\,2^{\kchunk}$ bits per row, so the honest choice is the smallest; a larger one is sound.

\subsection{The index column}\label{sec:idxcol}

An array's fingerprint carries its addresses $\gen^{0},\dots,\gen^{2^{\kappa}-1}$, so read-checking needs the extension of that \emph{index column} $\mathrm{idx}$ at its point (\S\ref{sec:shout}), written $\zeta$ here. Writing $w$ for the bits of $i$, $\gen^{\,i}=\prod_k(\gen^{\,2^{k}})^{w_k}$, whose $k$-th factor is $1+w_k(1+\gen^{\,2^{k}})$, which gives the efficient MLE formula:
\[
  \mle{\mathrm{idx}}(\zeta)\;=\;\prod_{k=0}^{\kappa-1}\bigl(1+\zeta_k\,(1+\gen^{\,2^{k}})\bigr),
\]
