\paragraph{Result and boundary.} A fixed library of valid BLAKE2s compressions suffices to statistically hide the actual $64$-value univariate-skip message. The proof uses exact finite-field rank and fair choices between valid executions, not pseudorandomness of BLAKE2s. This is a marginal theorem for the first Flock message with independent honest interactive coins. Its joint distribution with commitments, the preceding VM reductions, subsequent Flock messages and ring switching is not covered.

\section{The public space containing every first message}

Follow Annex C of the leanVM specification. The quirky R1CS polynomial $P(Y)$ has degree at most $126$ and vanishes on the $64$-point additive space $\skipdom$. Its message is its evaluations on $\skipcoset$, the other coset in a $128$-point additive space. The barycentric denominators on that larger space are constant, so the coefficient of degree $127$ is a nonzero constant times the sum of its evaluations. Consequently every valid message lies in the public space
\[
 \mathcal W=\{v\in\E^{64}:\textstyle\sum_i v_i=0\},
 \qquad \dim_{\E}\mathcal W=63.
\]
Conversely every vector in $\mathcal W$, extended by zeros on $\skipdom$, interpolates to a polynomial of degree at most $126$.

The remaining within-block coordinates have length eight. Seven equality coordinates are the protocol's fixed constants; call the eighth random coordinate $u$. For one valid compression witness $z$, let $p_z(u)\in\mathcal W$ be its contribution before the batch equality weight. Splitting its $256$ groups of $64$ rows into two halves gives
\[
 p_z(u)=(1+u)p_z(0)+u p_z(1).
\]
Thus its message is affine in $u$, although it is nonlinear in the compression's inputs.

\section{A finite valid library spans the whole message space}

Let $z_*$ be the compression witness for BLAKE2s-256 of $64$ zero bytes, with the standard initial chaining value, byte counter $64$, final-block flag all ones and last-node flag zero. The audit fixes $64$ other $64$-byte messages, generated by \texttt{Random(151)}, and constructs their full Boolean compression witnesses $z_0,\ldots,z_{63}$. Define
\[
 d_b(u)=p_{z_b}(u)+p_{z_*}(u).
\]
At $u=0$ the $64$ difference vectors have exact extension-field rank $63$. This certifies a nonzero $63$-row minor of their matrix in any fixed public coordinates on $\mathcal W$. Since each entry is affine in $u$, the minor has degree at most $63$. It follows that the differences span $\mathcal W$ except with probability at most $63/2^{192}$ over uniform $u$.

The certificate checks every one of the $65$ witnesses against the actual Python verifier's $A_0,B_0$ circuit walk, verifies $Az\cdot Bz=z$ and the constant wire, and compares each output with \texttt{hashlib.blake2s}. It also compares interpolation of the transmitted coset values with direct quirky R1CS evaluation at independent field coins. A finite rank evaluation is sufficient here because it certifies an ordinary polynomial minor of controlled degree, not the generic rank of a binary matrix.

\section{Turn library directions into extension-field noise}

Use a public batch of $2^{18}$ compressions. Write its index as $(s,b)$ with twelve low coordinates $s$ and six bank coordinates $b$. In each of the $64$ banks reserve the $448$ positions of the support $S_{12}$ from \path{zk-stacked-padding.tex}. At a reserved position independently choose $z_*$ or $z_b$ with one fair bit. Fill all other positions with arbitrary fixed valid compression witnesses. There are $28672$ random-choice positions; the public power-of-two batch size is part of this construction, not an optimized cost claim.

Let $r_{\rm low}\in\E^{12}$ and $r_{\rm bank}\in\E^6$ be the batch equality coins. The random part of the first message is
\[
 \sum_{b=0}^{63}\eq(r_{\rm bank},b)d_b(u)U_b,
 \qquad
 U_b=\sum_{s\in S_{12}}\eq(r_{\rm low},s)\,\varepsilon_{b,s}.
\]
On the shared sparse-span event the $64$ evaluations $U_b$ are independent uniform elements of $\E$. Its failure probability is at most $\delta_{\mathrm{span}}+12/2^{192}$. Excluding Boolean bank coins costs another $12/2^{192}$ and makes every bank weight nonzero. The library minor then makes the random contribution uniform on $\mathcal W$. Every fixed complementary contribution also lies in $\mathcal W$, so it only translates this same uniform distribution.

\begin{theorem}[First Flock message]
For this padding and independent honest interactive coins, Flock's first $64$-value message has a witness-independent simulator with statistical error at most
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{span}}+\frac{87}{2^{192}}<2^{-157},
\]
uniformly over the fixed valid compression witnesses at the other positions. The simulator samples $63$ independent uniform extension values and sets the last value to their sum.
\end{theorem}

All randomness is chosen before the verifier coins. Each library choice is implementable as a closed two-instruction execution: one \texttt{BLAKE2S} followed by a \texttt{JUMP} to its start. Use fresh private memory for its message and correct digest. Its bytecode, addresses, state transitions and read multiplicities do not depend on the choice; only private memory values do. The cycle audit checks both choices with fixed bytecode, addresses and counter exponents. This establishes local VM realizability, not joint hiding of the resulting bus messages.

\paragraph{Next obligation.} The same choice bits affect Flock's quadratic zerocheck, lincheck and its $64$ terminal slices. The theorem does not permit conditioning on these later observations or on the earlier VM transcript. The next useful test is the joint map of these observations, including nonlinear cross terms between compression choices.
