\begin{abstract}

LeanSPHINCS is inspired by SLH-DSA~\cite{FIPS205}: it runs on Keccak-256, with WOTS$^+$C~\cite{HK22C} one-time signatures and a three-layer hypertree. It follows FIPS~205 except where Section~\ref{sec:overview} says otherwise.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{NIST security level~1}~\cite{NISTPQC}: a target of 127 bits of classical security in the random-oracle model and about 64 bits in the quantum random-oracle model (Section~\ref{sec:security}).
  \item \textbf{Public key: 32 bytes.}
  \item \textbf{Signature: 7740 bytes.}
  \item \textbf{Verification: 515 permutations} of Keccak-f.
  \item \textbf{Lifetime: $2^{30}$ signatures per key pair.}
\end{itemize}
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview and differences from FIPS 205}
\label{sec:overview}

The message digest selects a FORS instance and one leaf in each of its trees; FORS signs the digest, and a hypertree of WOTS$^+$C keys signs the FORS public key up to $\pkroot$. The table lists every departure from FIPS~205; everything else is FIPS~205's: the address and its types $0$ to $6$; the input layouts of $\mathsf F$, $\mathsf H$, $\mathsf T_\ell$, $\PRF$ and $\PRFmsg$; FORS, its secrets, its trees and the compression of its roots by $\mathsf T_k$; the hashing of the hypertree's nodes; the split of the digest into FORS leaves, tree index and leaf index; pure SLH-DSA's framing of the message for $\PRFmsg$; deterministic signing with $\mathit{opt\_rand}=\pkseed$; the public key $\pkseed\concat\pkroot$; and the order of a signature.

\section{Hashing and parameters}
\label{sec:parameters}

Write $\bits r$ for $r$-bit strings, $\concat$ for concatenation, $\BE_r(z)$ for the unsigned $r$-bit big-endian encoding of $z$ (FIPS~205's $\mathsf{toByte}(z,r/8)$), $\BE^{-1}$ for its inverse ($\mathsf{toInt}$), and $x\getsr A$ for a uniform sample from $A$. Indices start at zero; $\bot$ denotes failure.

Let $\keccak:\bits{*}\to\bits{256}$ be Keccak-256~\cite{keccak}, Ethereum's hash: FIPS~202's~\cite{fips202} 256-bit hash with first padding byte $\mathtt{0x01}$ instead of $\mathtt{0x06}$. $\Truncate_r$ keeps the first $r$ bytes of its output. With $\addr$ the 32-byte address of a call (Appendix~\ref{sec:address}),
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Th(\pkseed,\addr,M)&=\Truncate_n\!\left(\keccak(\pkseed\concat\addr\concat M)\right),\\
  \PRF(\pkseed,\skseed,\addr)&=\Th(\pkseed,\addr,\skseed),\\
  \PRFmsg(\skprf,\mathit{opt\_rand},M)&=\Truncate_n\!\left(\keccak(\skprf\concat\mathit{opt\_rand}\concat M)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
$\Th$ is FIPS~205's $\mathsf F$, $\mathsf H$ and $\mathsf T_\ell$ alike, and the text keeps those names. As in pure SLH-DSA (FIPS~205 Algorithms~22 and~24), $\PRFmsg$ takes the 45-byte framed message, with $\mathit{ctx}$ the 11 ASCII bytes of \texttt{leanSPHINCS}. The framing only separates $\PRFmsg$ from the other calls: verification never computes $\PRFmsg$, so no signature depends on $\mathit{ctx}$.
\[
  M'=\BE_8(0)\concat\BE_8(|\mathit{ctx}|)\concat\mathit{ctx}\concat M.
\]
$\Hmsg$ takes the message itself, so that it stays on 16-byte boundaries. It is $\mathsf{MGF1}$~\cite{RFC8017} on Keccak-256, two calls of 84 bytes each, with $Z=R\concat\pkseed\concat\pkroot\concat M$:
\[
  \Hmsg(R,\pkseed,\pkroot,M)=\Truncate_m\!\left(\keccak(Z\concat\BE_{32}(0))\concat\keccak(Z\concat\BE_{32}(1))\right).
\]

The message $M$ is 32 bytes. $\pkseed$, $\pkroot$, the randomizer $R$, chain values and Merkle nodes are $n$ bytes; $\skseed$ and $\skprf$ are 32 bytes.

\paragraph{Keys.}
\label{sec:keys}
The secret key is $\skseed$ alone; $\pkseed$ and $\skprf$ are its hashes under the scheme's 11-byte ASCII name, $\skprf$ keeping 32 bytes so that predicting randomizers is as hard as guessing $\skseed$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \pkseed&=\Truncate_n\!\left(\keccak\!\left(\text{\texttt{leanSPHINCS}}\concat\skseed\concat\BE_8(0)\right)\right),\\
  \skprf&=\keccak\!\left(\text{\texttt{leanSPHINCS}}\concat\skseed\concat\BE_8(1)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Below, functions take $\skseed$ and $\pkseed$ implicitly.

\section{\texorpdfstring{WOTS$^+$C}{WOTS+C}: signing with hash chains}
\label{sec:ots}

A one-time key $K=(l,\tau,e)$ is named by its layer $l<d$ from the bottom, its tree $\tau$ on that layer and its leaf $e<2^{h_l}$, FIPS~205's key pair address. It has $v$ chains of $2^w=4$ positions, computed as in FIPS~205's WOTS$^+$: for $0\leq i<v$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  C_{i,0}&=\PRF\!\left(\pkseed,\skseed,\addr_{\mathrm{prf}}(K,i)\right),\\
  C_{i,\mu+1}&=\mathsf F\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{chain}}(K,i,\mu),C_{i,\mu}\right),
    &&0\leq\mu<2^w-1,
\end{aligned}
\]
a step's address holding the position it leaves. The key's public key, its Merkle leaf, compresses the endpoints in chain order:
\[
  X^{l,\tau}_{0,e}=\mathsf T_v\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{pk}}(K),C_{0,3}\concat\cdots\concat C_{v-1,3}\right).
\]

To sign an $n$-byte $M$ (a FORS public key, Section~\ref{sec:fts}, or a root of the layer below), the signer encodes it as positions $x_0,\ldots,x_{v-1}$ and reveals each $C_{i,x_i}$; the verifier hashes these forward to the endpoints. In place of WOTS$^+$'s checksum, every valid encoding has the same sum:
\[
  \mathcal C=\left\{x\in\{0,\ldots,2^w-1\}^{v}:\textstyle\sum_{i}x_i=T\right\}.
\]
Two distinct encodings of equal sum are incomparable, so hashing forward from one never yields another.

\paragraph{Encoding.}
$\Enc(K,M,c)$ hashes a 68-byte input,
\[
  D=\Th\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{enc}}(K),M\concat\BE_{32}(c)\right),
\]
and reads it as FIPS~205's WOTS$^+$ reads a message, $x=\basetwo(D,w,v)$, most significant bits first: with $D_j$ byte $j$ of $D$,
\[
  x_i=\left\lfloor D_{\lfloor i/4\rfloor}/2^{6-2(i\bmod4)}\right\rfloor\bmod 4,\qquad 0\leq i<v.
\]
It returns $x$ if $x\in\mathcal C$, and $\bot$ otherwise.

\paragraph{Signing.}
$\WotsSign(K,M)$ tries $c=0,\ldots,\cmax-1$ in order and, at the first $c$ with $x=\Enc(K,M,c)\neq\bot$, returns $(c,\sigma)$ with $\sigma_i=C_{i,x_i}$, or $\bot$ if every counter fails. Taking the least counter makes signing $M$ under $K$ twice reveal the same positions; different positions would let an adversary combine the smaller ones of both.

\paragraph{Recovering the leaf.}
$\WotsLeaf(K,M,c,\sigma)$ computes $x=\Enc(K,M,c)$ and returns $\bot$ if it fails. Otherwise it sets $C'_{i,x_i}=\sigma_i$, applies the chain recurrence up to position $3$, and returns $\mathsf T_v\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{pk}}(K),C'_{0,3}\concat\cdots\concat C'_{v-1,3}\right)$.

\section{The hypertree: authenticating one-time keys}
\label{sec:layer}

Layer $l$ has $2^{h-(h_0+\cdots+h_l)}$ trees of height $h_l$: $2^{18}$ on the bottom layer, $2^{10}$ on the middle one, and one top tree. The leaves of tree $\tau$ of layer $l$ are the $X^{l,\tau}_{0,e}$, and its inner nodes are those of FIPS~205's $\mathsf{xmss\_node}$,
\[
  X^{l,\tau}_{z,j}=\mathsf H\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{node}}(l,\tau,z,j),X^{l,\tau}_{z-1,2j}\concat X^{l,\tau}_{z-1,2j+1}\right),\qquad 1\leq z\leq h_l,\ 0\leq j<2^{h_l-z}.
\]
The tree's root is $X^{l,\tau}_{h_l,0}$; the top tree's root is $\pkroot$.

An index $\idx<2^h$ names one key on every layer: with $\beta_l=h_0+\cdots+h_{l-1}$ the index bits below layer $l$, its key there is
\[
  K_l(\idx)=\left(l,\ \left\lfloor\idx/2^{\beta_l+h_l}\right\rfloor,\ \left\lfloor\idx/2^{\beta_l}\right\rfloor\bmod2^{h_l}\right),
\]
as in FIPS~205's $\mathsf{ht\_sign}$ with each layer's own height for $h'$. The key of layer $l+1$ signs the root of the tree holding the key of layer $l$.

The authentication path of the key at leaf $e$ is $P=(P_0,\ldots,P_{h_l-1})$, from the leaf up, with $P_z=X^{l,\tau}_{z,\lfloor e/2^z\rfloor\oplus1}$ the sibling at height $z$, where $\oplus1$ flips the low bit of the node index.

$\TreeFold(K,Q_0,P)$ recovers the root from a leaf value $Q_0$ and its path: for $0\leq z<h_l$, with $j=\lfloor e/2^{z+1}\rfloor$,
\[
  Q_{z+1}=\begin{cases}
    \mathsf H\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{node}}(l,\tau,z+1,j),Q_z\concat P_z\right)
      &\text{if bit $z$ of $e$ is zero},\\[2pt]
    \mathsf H\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{node}}(l,\tau,z+1,j),P_z\concat Q_z\right)
      &\text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\]
and it returns $Q_{h_l}$.

\section{FORS: signing the digest}
\label{sec:fts}

The FORS instance of an index is FIPS~205's at the bottom key $K_0(\idx)=(0,\tau,e)$: its addresses hold layer $0$, tree $\tau$ and key pair address $e$. It has $k$ trees of height $a$, whose leaves hash secrets. Node indices run across the trees: in tree $\kappa<k$, node $j<2^{a-z}$ at height $z$ has tree index $\kappa2^{a-z}+j$. For leaf $s<2^a$ and $1\leq z\leq a$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathit{sk}_{\kappa,s}&=\PRF\!\left(\pkseed,\skseed,\addr_{\mathrm{fprf}}(\idx,\kappa2^a+s)\right),\\
  Y^{\kappa}_{0,s}&=\mathsf F\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{fors}}(\idx,0,\kappa2^a+s),\mathit{sk}_{\kappa,s}\right),\\
  Y^{\kappa}_{z,j}&=\mathsf H\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{fors}}(\idx,z,\kappa2^{a-z}+j),Y^{\kappa}_{z-1,2j}\concat Y^{\kappa}_{z-1,2j+1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The instance's public key compresses the trees' roots:
\[
  \ForsKey(\idx)=\mathsf T_k\!\left(\pkseed,\addr_{\mathrm{roots}}(\idx),Y^{0}_{a,0}\concat\cdots\concat Y^{k-1}_{a,0}\right).
\]

\paragraph{Opening.}
The digest selects a leaf $s_\kappa$ in each tree. $\ForsSign(\idx,s)$ reveals, per tree, the secret $\mathit{sk}_{\kappa,s_\kappa}$ and the path $(Y^{\kappa}_{z,\lfloor s_\kappa/2^z\rfloor\oplus1})_{0\leq z<a}$, from the leaf up: $1+a$ values per tree.

\paragraph{Recovering the key.}
$\ForsRec(\idx,s,\text{opening})$, FIPS~205's $\mathsf{fors\_pkFromSig}$, hashes each revealed secret into its leaf, folds it to its tree's root as $\TreeFold$ does, with the bits of $s_\kappa$ and the tree's node addresses, and returns $\mathsf T_k$ of the $k$ roots as $\ForsKey$ does.

An instance may sign several digests: short of breaking the hash, a forgery at it needs, in every tree, a selected leaf whose secret is already revealed.

\paragraph{The digest.}
The digest $\Hmsg(R,\pkseed,\pkroot,M)$ has $m=34$ bytes $g_0,\ldots,g_{33}$, which FIPS~205's Algorithm~19 splits into $\mathit{md}$, the first $\lceil ka/8\rceil=30$ bytes, then $\lceil(h-h_0)/8\rceil=3$ bytes for the tree and $\lceil h_0/8\rceil=1$ byte for the leaf, read most significant bit first:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  (s_0,\ldots,s_{k-1})&=\basetwo(\mathit{md},a,k),\\
  \idx&=2^{h_0}\left(\BE^{-1}(g_{30}\concat g_{31}\concat g_{32})\bmod2^{h-h_0}\right)+\left(g_{33}\bmod2^{h_0}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every digest is valid, since every index names a bottom leaf.

\section{The signature scheme}
\label{sec:scheme}

\subsection{Key generation}

$\Gen$ samples $\skseed\getsr\bits{256}$, derives $\pkseed$ and $\skprf$ (Section~\ref{sec:keys}), builds the top tree as FIPS~205's $\mathsf{slh\_keygen\_internal}$ does, and returns
\[
  \PK=(\pkseed,\pkroot),\qquad \SK=\skseed.
\]

\subsection{Signing}

$\Sig(\SK,M)$, on a 32-byte message $M$, is deterministic:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Derive $\pkseed$ and $\skprf$ from $\SK$, and $\pkroot$ from the top tree.
\item Compute the randomizer $R=\PRFmsg(\skprf,\pkseed,M')$, and extract $\idx$ and $s$ from $\Hmsg(R,\pkseed,\pkroot,M)$.
\item Compute the opening $\ForsSign(\idx,s)$ and $M_0=\ForsKey(\idx)$.
\item For $l=0,1,2$: compute $(c_l,\sigma_l)=\WotsSign(K_l(\idx),M_l)$, returning $\bot$ on failure, and the path $P_l$ of $K_l(\idx)$ in its tree, and let $M_{l+1}$ be that tree's root.
\item Return $R$, the opening, and $(c_l,\sigma_l,P_l)$ for each layer.
\end{enumerate}
Signing is stateless and deterministic. A key signs at most $2^{30}$ messages.

\subsection{Verification}

$\Ver(\PK,M,\sigma)$ rejects a signature of the wrong length. Otherwise it parses $\PK=(\pkseed,\pkroot)$ and $\sigma$ (Section~\ref{sec:costs}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item Extract $\idx$ and $s$ from $\Hmsg(R,\pkseed,\pkroot,M)$.
\item Set $M_0=\ForsRec(\idx,s,\text{opening})$.
\item For $l=0,1,2$: compute $X_l=\WotsLeaf(K_l(\idx),M_l,c_l,\sigma_l)$, rejecting if $X_l=\bot$, and $M_{l+1}=\TreeFold(K_l(\idx),X_l,P_l)$.
\item Accept exactly when $M_3=\pkroot$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Serialization and cost}
\label{sec:costs}

The public key is $\pkseed\concat\pkroot$ and the secret key $\skseed$, 32 bytes each. A signature is $R$, then the FORS opening tree by tree, each tree's secret then its path from the leaf up, then the layers from the bottom up, each $\BE_{32}(c_l)$, then its $v$ chain values in chain order, then its path from the leaf up:
\[
  \underbrace{n}_{R}
  +\underbrace{k\,(1+a)\,n}_{\text{FORS}}
  +\underbrace{d\,(4+v\,n)}_{\text{WOTS$^+$C}}
  +\underbrace{h\,n}_{\text{paths}}
  =16+4224+3084+416=7740\text{ bytes}.
\]

Costs count permutations of Keccak-f: an input of $r$ bytes costs $\lfloor r/136\rfloor+1$. An addressed input starts with the 48 bytes of $\pkseed\concat\addr$. Thus $\mathsf F$ (64 bytes), $\mathsf H$ and $\PRF$ (80), an encoding (68), $\PRFmsg$ (93) and each call of $\Hmsg$ (84) cost one permutation, $\mathsf T_k$ (432) four, and $\mathsf T_v$ (1072) eight. A verification walks $(2^w-1)v-T=64$ chain steps per layer, so it takes exactly
\[
  \underbrace{2}_{\Hmsg}
  +\underbrace{k\,(1+a)}_{\text{FORS trees}}
  +\underbrace{4}_{\mathsf T_k}
  +\underbrace{d}_{\text{encodings}}
  +\underbrace{d\bigl((2^w-1)v-T+8\bigr)}_{\text{WOTS$^+$C}}
  +\underbrace{h}_{\text{paths}}
  =2+264+4+3+216+26=515
\]
permutations.

A one-time leaf takes $v$ calls of $\PRF$, $(2^w-1)v=192$ chain steps and $\mathsf T_v$: 264 permutations. Key generation takes $264\cdot2^{10}+(2^{10}-1)+2=271\,361$, the last two deriving $\pkseed$ and $\skprf$. Signing takes, in expectation,
\[
  \underbrace{5}_{\pkseed,\,\skprf,\,\PRFmsg,\,\Hmsg}
  +\underbrace{271\,359}_{\text{top tree}}
  +\underbrace{73\,708}_{\text{FORS}}
  +\underbrace{135\,678}_{\text{lower trees}}
  +\underbrace{d\,(v+T+1/p)}_{\text{WOTS$^+$C}}
  \approx5.25\cdot10^5,
\]
where a one-time signature takes $v$ calls of $\PRF$, $T$ chain steps and $1/p\approx14\,670.4$ expected one-permutation encoding attempts, with $p$ of Section~\ref{sec:completeness}. A signer may keep the top tree from key generation instead of rebuilding it.

\section{Security}
\label{sec:security}

Model $\keccak$ as one shared random oracle. In the strong-unforgeability game, a classical probabilistic adversary gets $\PK$, queries the oracle and adaptively requests signatures on at most $q_s$ messages of its choice, then returns $(M^*,\sigma^*)$; it wins exactly when $\Ver(\PK,M^*,\sigma^*)$ accepts and no signing request for $M^*$ returned $\sigma^*$. With $q$ bounding every oracle call of the game, including key generation, signing and the final verification, the target is a winning probability of at most $q/2^{127}$ for $q_s=2^{30}$.

\paragraph{The Lean proof.}
The Lean~4 theorem \texttt{FORS.h26\_layered} in \texttt{formal/lean\_sphincs/} proves this bound for $q_s=1\,182\,000\,000\approx2^{30.14}$. Its model fixes every hash input's bytes as this document does, checked against the implementation by reading both. Its $q$ counts the calls of its model signer, which recomputes some values the implementation's signer reuses.

\paragraph{Hash domains.}
Distinct calls of one key never share an input. The unaddressed calls, $\PRFmsg$, those of $\Hmsg$ and the derivations of $\pkseed$ and $\skprf$, have inputs of 93, 84 and 44 bytes, which no addressed call has, its payload being 16, 20, 32, 384 or 1024 bytes; the two calls of $\Hmsg$, like the two derivations, differ in their last byte. An addressed input is $\pkseed$ then an address whose type word names the call (a FORS tree's height word telling its leaves from its nodes) and whose fields name its position. The secrets enter only the derivations, $\PRF$ and $\PRFmsg$, which the verifier never computes. The game has one key; across keys, calls differ in $\pkseed$, which two keys share with probability $2^{-128}$.

\paragraph{Quantum security.}
About 64 bits in the quantum random-oracle model is a target only; no quantum bound is proved.

\section{Completeness}
\label{sec:completeness}

Signing fails only if a counter search is exhausted, and verification accepts every signature it returns. A uniform 128-bit $D$ gives a valid encoding with probability
\[
  p=\frac{[y^{128}](1+y+y^2+y^3)^{64}}{2^{128}}
  =\frac{23195210617839120199658819909693679}{2^{128}}
  \geq2^{-13.85},
\]
where $[y^r]$ denotes the coefficient of $y^r$, so all $\cmax=2^{32}$ counters of a layer fail with probability $(1-p)^{\cmax}\leq\exp(-2^{18})$.
